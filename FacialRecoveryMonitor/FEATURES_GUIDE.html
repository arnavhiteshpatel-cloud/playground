1. Who it is for and what it does

Facial Recovery Monitor helps people recovering from conditions that affect the facial nerve — Bell's palsy, facial nerve injury, facial nerve reconstruction or reanimation, acoustic neuroma surgery and similar — to:

  • complete standardised, guided facial check-ins with a webcam;
  • record symptoms with a structured survey;
  • keep an organised, dated history on their own computer;
  • review charts over time and export records to discuss with their clinician.

The long-term idea is that computer vision could make home recordings more consistent and quantifiable. In this prototype the numbers are demonstrations of what facial landmarks can capture — not clinical measurements.

The five standardised expressions

# Expression Instruction
1 Neutral position "Relax your facial muscles and look directly at the camera." — the reference for all movements.
2 Gentle smile "Give a gentle smile without forcing the movement."
3 Eyebrow movement "Raise your eyebrows gently."
4 Eye closure "Gently close your eyes." Press Capture first, then close your eyes for about 5 seconds; listen for the alert sound.
5 Lip pucker (optional) "Gently pucker your lips as if about to whistle." Skip it if uncomfortable or not relevant.

What happens when you press Capture: a 3-second countdown, then the app analyses each camera frame for ~1.6 seconds on your computer, keeps the median of the landmark measurements and discards the frames. If fewer than 5 frames contained a face, the step is marked No face detected and you can retry or skip. If your head was turned more than 15°, you are warned.

5. The experimental measurements explained

The app uses the MediaPipe Face Landmarker, which locates 478 points on the face in each camera frame. For each captured expression it:

  1. Straightens the points so the line between the eyes is horizontal (removes head tilt).
  2. Normalises distances by the inter-ocular distance (IOD) — the distance between the centres of the two eyes — so sitting closer or further away does not change the values.
  3. Takes the median across all frames of the capture (robust to blinks and small movements).
  4. Compares each movement with your neutral capture from the same session.
Group Value How it is calculated Unit
Resting position Eye opening — L/R similarity smaller ÷ larger resting eye opening ratio (1.00 = equal)
Brow height difference (L − R) left minus right brow height above each eye line % IOD
Mouth-corner height difference (L − R) positive = left corner sits lower % IOD
Smile Right / Left movement how much closer each mouth corner moved toward the outer corner of the eye on the same side % IOD
Eyebrow raise Right / Left movement increase in brow height compared with rest % IOD
Eye closure Right / Left closure reduction of eye opening (lid gap ÷ eye width) compared with rest; 100% ≈ lids meet % of resting opening
Lip pucker Right / Left movement how far each mouth corner moved toward the facial midline % IOD
Each movement L/R similarity smaller side's movement ÷ larger side's movement (1.00 = equal). Only calculated if at least one side moved enough (≥1% IOD, or ≥10% for eye closure). ratio
  • Right / Left = your own right and left side (not the side of the screen).
  • Head pose (turn, nod, tilt) is estimated and stored as a quality indicator.
  • Model "blendshape" scores (e.g. smile, blink) are stored with each expression for future research but are not displayed as results.
  • Missing ≠ zero. If a value cannot be calculated it is reported as missing with the reason, never as 0 or a guess.

Limitations: values change with lighting, camera height/distance, head turn, glasses, facial hair and camera quality; a 2-D webcam cannot fully correct head rotation; the landmark model was not developed or tested specifically on faces with facial palsy; synkinesis and muscle tone are not measured. The values have not been compared with clinical grading systems such as House-Brackmann, Sunnybrook or eFACE.

12. Your data: privacy, storage, export, deletion

  • Everything stays on your computer in a SQLite database. No account, no cloud, no internet connection is used by the app. The face model is bundled and runs locally.
  • Camera: only on during a check-in you started; turns off when you finish, cancel or leave.
  • No video is ever recorded. By default only numbers derived from landmarks are stored.
  • Still images: only if you switch them on for a session and press Save Session.
Operating system Data folder
Windows %APPDATA%\FacialRecoveryMonitor\
macOS ~/Library/Application Support/FacialRecoveryMonitor/
Linux ~/.local/share/FacialRecoveryMonitor/

Inside: facial_recovery_monitor.sqlite3 (your records), snapshots/ (only if you saved images), logs/app.log (technical log; no measurements or answers).

CSV export columns: record_type (profile / facial_checkin / symptom_survey), record_id, date, time, section, item, value, unit, data_source (experimental_measurement, user_entered or capture_status), notes. Opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets. JSON export keeps the full nested structure, including per-expression landmark features.

Facial images and facial measurements are sensitive health-related data: protect your computer account and any exported files (they are not encrypted).

Missing Python packages or Tkinter Message explaining exactly what to install.
Any unexpected error Something went wrong dialog; your saved records are unaffected; technical details go to the log file (shown on screen only in developer mode, FRM_DEV=1).

14. Tips for consistent check-ins

  • Same place, same time of day, same lighting (face a window or lamp; avoid bright light behind you).
  • Camera at eye level, about an arm's length away, face filling a good part of the frame.
  • Look straight at the camera; keep your head still and upright.
  • Gentle, natural movements — do not force or strain. Skip anything uncomfortable.
  • Watch the quality line after each capture (frames with a face, head-turn angle); recapture if needed.
  • Use session notes to record anything unusual (tired, different camera, new glasses…).

15. Frequently asked questions

Does the app tell me whether I am recovering? No. It records and organises information; your clinician interprets it.

Why does a value say "missing"? It needs a successful neutral capture and the relevant movement, and a face visible in at least 5 frames. Ratios also need enough movement on at least one side.

Why did my values change although I feel the same? Lighting, camera position, distance, head angle and expression intensity all affect landmark geometry. Keep the set-up consistent and look at longer-term patterns, together with your clinician.

Is my face uploaded anywhere? No. Nothing leaves your computer unless you export a file and share it yourself.

Can I use it without a camera? Yes — the survey, history, analytics and profile work without one; check-in measurements need a camera.

Can I edit a saved record? You can update a check-in's notes right after saving (Update notes). Otherwise records are kept as recorded; you can delete them in History.

16. Technical reference

Component Technology
Language Python 3.10–3.13 (3.12 bundled/recommended)
User interface CustomTkinter (modern Tk widgets), custom design system (app/theme.py), icons drawn with Pillow
Camera OpenCV VideoCapture on a background thread (DirectShow on Windows, AVFoundation on macOS)
Face landmarks MediaPipe Face Landmarker (Tasks API, 478 landmarks, head-pose matrix, blendshapes), model bundled in assets/models/
Charts Matplotlib embedded in Tk, with hover tooltips
Storage SQLite (WAL mode, foreign keys, parameterised queries)
Numerical NumPy

Database tables: profile · checkin_sessions · expression_records (per expression: status, frame counts, head pose, median landmark features, blendshapes, optional image path) · session_metrics (derived experimental values) · symptom_surveys · survey_responses · app_settings. Child tables cascade on delete. The schema is created automatically on first start and existing records are never overwritten.

Source layout: main.py (start-up checks) · app/ui.py (window, navigation, error handling) · app/pages/*.py (one file per page) · app/camera.py · app/facial_analysis.py · app/database.py · app/export.py · app/charts.py · app/survey_definitions.py · app/theme.py · app/dialogs.py · app/icons.py · app/config.py · app/utils.py.
