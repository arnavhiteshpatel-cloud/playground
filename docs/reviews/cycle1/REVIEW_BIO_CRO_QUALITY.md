# Review cycle 1: bone biology, CRO operations, quality and regulatory

Scope: the documents and code listed in the cycle-1 brief: README, FOUNDER_HANDOFF, FORENSIC_AUDIT, EVIDENCE_LEDGER,
SCIENTIFIC_EVIDENCE, EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY, BENCHMARK_SCORECARD, RELEASE_READINESS, `docs/science/`, `validation/`,
`docs/quality/` (URS, FS, IQ/OQ/PQ, gap analysis, SOPs, traceability), `business/` (revenue model, pricing plan,
templates), the three sample DefectFill reports in `reports_samples/`, `osteoquant/report/defectfill.py`, and the
code paths those documents point to (`osteoquant/api/bundle.py`, `osteoquant/advisor/separability.py`,
`osteoquant/advisor/integration.py`). Grant and investor files were spot-checked for compliance and validation
language. There is no `docs/science/KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md`; the knowledge base is `BONE_BIOLOGY_AND_GRAFTS.md`.

Reviewer perspectives:
- **BIO**: bone biologist and histomorphometrist (healing biology, graft materials, defect models, histology reference standards).
- **CRO**: study director at a preclinical CRO buying analysis for sponsor studies (turnaround, SOP fit, audit expectations, willingness to pay, reasons to reject).
- **QA-REG**: quality and regulatory reviewer (21 CFR 58, 21 CFR Part 11, OECD GLP No. 17, computerised-system validation, document control). The main question here is whether anything claims compliance, validation or regulatory acceptance that does not exist.

Method: read-only. All work was reading documents and code, plus text extraction from the sample PDFs. No
experiments were run. Line numbers refer to the working tree on 2026-11-27.

Severity: **critical** means a sponsor-facing number or claim would be wrong or misleading as delivered.
**High** means it would block a CRO pilot, invalidate a validation argument, or mislead by omission.
**Medium** means a biology, design or documentation weakness to fix before real-data work. **Low** means
consistency or hygiene.

## Verdict

The project is unusually honest about its own status. The RUO, not-validated and no-Part-11 banners appear in the
README, the URS/FS headers, every SOP, the report generator and the landing page. Removing the GLP offering
(REVENUE_MODEL O5, SOW §6) was the right call.

The problems sit in three places:

1. **Biological edge cases of the advisor and the endpoints.** These are the cases that decide what a sponsor's
   headline number means: bone formed inside a resorbing graft footprint, DBM, periosteal callus, and woven-bone
   ranges.
2. **A gap between what the documents promise a report contains and what the PDF actually prints.** The PDF has
   no support status, no conditions, no evidence stage, no acquisition parameters, and no mineral volume.
3. **A scattering of stale or inconsistent numbers, plus a few grant sentences** that read as validation claims.

None of this is fatal. All of it would be found in a sponsor QA audit or in a first pilot debrief.

## Five things that would stop a CRO from piloting this

1. **There is no real three-class evidence, and the pilot design cannot produce any.** Every graft and new-bone
   number is synthetic (FA-01). The pilot template judges success by agreement with the customer's own
   threshold analysis. The project's own experiments say that method fails in exactly the overlapping materials
   the product targets. A study director cannot tell whether a disagreement means OsteoQuant is wrong or the
   legacy method is wrong (CRO-01).
2. **The DefectFill PDF delivers conditional numbers without their conditions.** The PDF never prints the
   advisor's status, the conditions on a "supported with conditions" endpoint, or the stage of evidence. One
   example: for a resorbable graft, bone formed inside the day-0 footprint is counted as graft. Bias of this
   kind would not survive a sponsor's QA audit of the report (QA-REG-01, BIO-01).
3. **Abstention is presented as a post-hoc refusal, not as a study-design gate.** The SOW assumes the graft is
   separable and "confirms during D1". The CRO finds out the primary endpoint is "not measured" only after the
   animals are done. No contract term covers fees, scope or deliverables when that happens (CRO-03).
4. **The quality package describes a different product.** The URS, FS, IQ/OQ and gap analysis describe a hosted,
   multi-tenant FastAPI web server; none of them mentions the offline desktop application that is now the
   product. No verification has been executed (12 of 81 traceability rows VERIFIED). There is no QMS, no
   supplier-qualification pack and no legal entity. A CRO cannot run a vendor qualification (QA-REG-02,
   QA-REG-04, CRO-07).
5. **Coverage stops short of the studies CROs mostly run.** Several things are not supported:
   - day-0 designs in large animals (no in vivo micro-CT exists for sheep, goats or minipigs);
   - metal implants (osseointegration and bone–implant contact);
   - segmental defects with fixation hardware;
   - bone-derived grafts and DBM;
   - GLP studies.

   What remains is mainly small-animal non-GLP ceramic studies. That segment is smaller than the 510(k)-driven
   market framing implies (CRO-04, CRO-05, QA-REG-09).
