# Cycle 1 Review: Investor Due Diligence and IP/Licensing (perspectives 11 and 12)

**Date:** 2026-11-27. **Scope:** README, FOUNDER_HANDOFF, FORENSIC_AUDIT, EVIDENCE_LEDGER, SCIENTIFIC_EVIDENCE,
BENCHMARK_SCORECARD, RISK_REGISTER, `business/` (revenue model, workbook, outputs JSON, pricing plan, segmentation,
market inputs, templates), `investor/` (funding strategy, one-pager, founder intro, `build_decks.py` and the three
`.pptx` decks as committed), `deliverables/` PDF, `docs/research/COMPETITOR_MATRIX.md`, `docs/ip/*`,
`docs/security/SBOM.csv`, `sbom.cdx.json`, `IP_PROVENANCE.md`, `pyproject.toml`, dataset registries,
`models/registry.json` and model cards, `osteoquant/ml/train.py`, `osteoquant/licensing/`, and the built desktop bundle
`packaging/dist/OsteoQuant/` (including `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES/INDEX.json`). Installed-package licences were listed with
`pip-licenses` in this environment.

**Reviewer stance.** (1) A seed-stage investor doing diligence on a pre-revenue, unincorporated, single-founder
(undergraduate) preclinical imaging-software project. (2) An IP/licensing counsel *perspective*. This is **not legal
advice**; every IP item is a question to put to a registered patent attorney, licensing counsel and UIUC OTM.

**Severity.** Critical = would fail diligence or create a live legal exposure now; High = must be fixed before any
external sharing, investment or paid pilot; Medium = weakens the case or creates avoidable risk; Low = hygiene.

**Headline.** The honesty labelling inside the Markdown files is unusually good. The problems are (a) **the investor
artefacts actually on disk do not match the current model** (stale decks and report PDF overstate 2031 revenue by
2.8–4.5× and understate equity need by about 4×); (b) a **closed-source desktop bundle that ships a GPL-only Qt module and
omits the LGPL texts**; (c) a **public repository** that defeats the IP strategy's own trade-secret plan, exposes a
non-privileged FTO triage, and undercuts licence revenue; (d) unresolved **ownership** (university policy, AI-generated
code); and (e) zero customer evidence.

## 4. Strongest honest pitch

Bone-graft makers have to show that their material resorbs and is replaced by new bone, yet the standard micro-CT
workflow (manual ROIs and global thresholds) stops working exactly when it matters: once a resorbing ceramic reaches
bone-like density. OsteoQuant is an offline, auditable analysis workstation built around that failure mode. Before a
study, it tells the sponsor whether micro-CT can separate their graft from new bone at their scan settings. When it
cannot, it says so and recommends a day-0 baseline scan or histology instead of reporting a number. Every human
correction is recorded in a tamper-evident trail. On real public data its morphometry tracks Scanco IPL (8 samples,
convention to be confirmed on held-out data). Synthetic pre-training plus a single labelled real scan nearly matches a
model trained on ten real scans, which is early support for a "few customer labels per material" onboarding path.
The graft-versus-new-bone results are simulation-only today. The founder is pre-revenue and is spending the next
90 days on a physical phantom study and 20 buyer conversations, deliberately before raising equity. The realistic
near-term business is a founder-scale analysis service plus licences in a niche worth roughly $1–4M a year in the US.
Venture scale depends on evidence, not yet gathered, from adjacent modules and non-US sponsors.
