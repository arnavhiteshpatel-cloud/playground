# NSF vs NIH SBIR/STTR Eligibility for OsteoQuant

**Prepared:** 2026-11-27. Labels are [V]/[V-old]/[CC]/[INF]/[HYP]/[UNR], as defined in FUNDING_OPPORTUNITIES.md.
**Founder situation:** full-time UIUC undergraduate. No company, no employees, no customers.

> **Bottom line:** NSF SBIR, NSF STTR and NIH SBIR all require the PI to be **primarily employed (>50%) by the small business at award and for the whole project**. Of the four programs, **only NIH STTR** lets the PD/PI be primarily employed by the partner research institution (for example, a UIUC faculty member) instead of the company. None of the primary documents says "full-time students are ineligible". However, NSF's archived FAQ says that **anything** preventing >50% legal employment, "including residency status or university policy", disqualifies the PI. Whether a full-time undergraduate can also be >50% employed by their own startup is therefore the critical open question ([UNR]; ask the program officer and UIUC).

## 2. Answers to the specific questions

**(a) NSF SBIR PI primary employment.** The PI must be ≥51% employed by the small business at award and for the full award. Outside employment above 19.6 h/week conflicts, and the test does not apply at submission. [V]

**(b) NSF STTR PI rules.** STTR **does not** remove the requirement at NSF. Per 26-510 the PI "must be an employee of the proposing small business for at least 51% of his/her time" [V]. The *co-PI* must come from the partner research institution [V]. The common assumption that the STTR PI may be primarily employed by the university is **true at NIH but not at NSF** [V]. Can a full-time student be the NSF STTR PI? There is no explicit bar. Under the archived FAQ, a student qualifies only if they are legally >50% employed by the company at award and nothing (such as university policy or visa status) prevents it. [V-old] [INF]

**(c) NIH SBIR vs STTR PI rules and multiple PIs.**
- **NIH SBIR:** The PD/PI must be primarily employed (>½ time) by the SBC at award and during the project [V]. "Deviations from this requirement for Phase I SBIR projects are allowable under exceptional circumstances" (GPS 18.5) [V]. With multiple PIs, "at least one must meet the primary employment requirement" [V]. One practical structure is therefore a **hired full-time scientist as contact PD/PI**, with the founder as a second PD/PI if allowed [INF; confirm with NIH].
- **NIH STTR:** The PD/PI may be primarily employed by the SBC or the partnering non-profit RI, must commit ≥10% effort, and must have a formal appointment with or commitment to the SBC [V]. **A UIUC faculty member can be the PD/PI.** The founder could serve as a company-side PD/PI in an MPI application. Whether a full-time student meets the "primary employment with either" condition is [UNR], so ask NIBIB.

**(d) Does full-time student status disqualify?** No primary source says so [V: no such statement in 26-510, PA-27-100/102 or GPS 18.5]. The binding test is employment:
- NSF counts any condition that prevents >50% legal employment, explicitly including "university policy", as disqualifying [V-old].
- A full-time undergraduate course load plus ≥20.4 h/week of paid company employment is legally possible for a US citizen or permanent resident [INF]. It may conflict with UIUC employment rules for students, with F-1 visa work rules, and with credibility in review [INF/UNR].
- The clean paths are:
  1. The founder graduates or goes part-time before the award date.
  2. The founder takes a leave of absence during the award.
  3. A hired full-time PI is used (NSF/NIH SBIR).
  4. A faculty PD/PI is used (NIH STTR only).

**(e) Reauthorization.** The programs lapsed Sep 30, 2025 and were reauthorized Apr 13, 2026 through Sep 30, 2031 (P.L. 119-83). Both agencies are accepting applications again [V].
