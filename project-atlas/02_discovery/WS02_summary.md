# WS02 — Insurance, payments & financial administration: discovery summary

**Records:** 34 opportunities (WS02-001…034), 30 sources (WS02-S001…S030). Access date 2026-11-28.
**Evidence base:** 7 A-level primary sources: IDOI and NIPR licensing pages, Illinois statutes via FindLaw, and the SEC 10-Ks of Goosehead, TWFG, Priority Technology, SelectQuote, Paychex and CorVel. Plus B/C sources: Brown & Brown and Baldwin results, the Encova commission schedule, OPTIS, Reagan/Big "I", Sica|Fletcher and ATRI. Most new-entrant economics are still **E**.

## Landscape
- **Getting licensed is cheap.** Illinois producer licensing needs 20 hours of pre-licensing per line (7.5 of them classroom), General and State exams through Pearson VUE, and $215 per roughly 2-year term. The applicant must be 18 or older. An agency entity costs $150 and needs a designated licensed producer [A, S001–S003].
  - Getting P&C licensed takes about 1–3 months and under $1k.
  - We found no Illinois statute requiring E&O, but carriers and networks expect it. TWFG branches pay their own E&O [A, S012].
  - Retail agents can avoid a surplus-lines license by placing E&S business through wholesalers. The surplus-lines tax is 3.5%, plus a 0.04% stamping fee [A/C, S004–S005].
- **The barrier is market access.** A start-up agency realistically needs an aggregator or network (SIAA, Smart Choice), a branch/MGA platform (TWFG recruits start-ups and passes about 60% of commission income to its agents) or E&S wholesalers [A/D, S012–S015]. Fees, splits, departure terms and Illinois availability are **unverified**.
- **Commission pools (B):** a published regional-carrier schedule pays 16% on all commercial lines except workers' comp, 15–20% on GL/property/auto/BOP, and 5–10% on WC (tiered down by premium size). New and renewal rates are the same [S016].
- **Books are valuable, which is the wealth-creation evidence.**
  - Deals for agencies above $1M EBITDA averaged about 11.4x EBITDA in 2025 [C, S007].
  - There were 695 deals in 2025. About 30,000 agencies under $1.25M revenue lack a succession path [C, S006].
  - Best-practice agencies earn 23–31% EBITDA margins, and 42.5% for the top quartile of the under-$1.25M band [C, S008].
  - Incumbent economics, not new-entrant economics: Brown & Brown 35.9% adjusted EBITDAC margin with 2.8% organic growth; Baldwin 22.7% margin with 7% organic growth [B, S009–S010].
  - Small books trade far below the 11.4x figure. The sub-$1M EBITDA multiple is unverified.
- **Payments:** an acquirer's 10-K shows a working residual economy. Priority buys ISO residual streams and amortizes them over 3–9 years (6-year weighted average), which means a merchant book is a *wasting* asset [A, S019].
- **Payroll incumbent (Paychex):** 38.6% operating margin, 82–83% client retention, and more than 50% of payroll clients come from CPA, broker and bank referrals [A, S022].

## Strongest candidates (evidence-adjusted)
1. **WS02-028 Vertical bookkeeping** (construction job-costing/WIP, trucking cost-per-mile/IFTA), score 6.11.
   - No license and almost no capital; billed monthly in advance; standard delegable roles.
   - AI categorization and reconciliation is a real, durable saving.
   - Ceiling is moderate (gross margin roughly 45–65%, an assumption). It acts as the relationship hub for selling insurance and payroll.
2. **WS02-001 Trucking/commercial-auto niche agency**, score 5.91.
   - Coverage is mandated and tickets are high: about 10.6¢/mile in premium, about $10k per truck at an assumed 100k miles; small fleets pay roughly double per mile [C, S017].
   - Prospects are identifiable from public FMCSA data.
   - Risks: churn among new-venture carriers, saturated lead lists, scarce carrier capacity, and 2026 FMCSA vetting of new authorities that may shrink volume [C, S030].
3. **WS02-026 Niche payroll bureau** (construction certified payroll, restaurants), score 5.90.
   - Paid at each pay run; incumbent retention data is A-level.
   - Risks: tax-deposit fiduciary exposure and SaaS price pressure.
4. **WS02-002 Contractor/trades commercial agency**, score 5.75. Public permit and license data give a lead list, COI demand repeats, and surety bonds (WS02-007) cross-sell.
5. **WS02-018 Agency back-office outsourcing**, score 5.67.
   - Sells to about 37,000 independent agencies averaging 9.9 staff [C, S018].
   - Fastest to first revenue and no license, but AI substitution and price wars cap it.
6. **WS02-004 Chicago small-habitational niche**, score 5.67. Lenders and closing deadlines act as a referral channel; whether carriers will give a start-up capacity is unknown.
7. **WS02-027 401(k) setup/TPA driven by Illinois Secure Choice**, score 5.62, and **WS02-019 COI tracking service**, score 5.58. Both are good bundles, but their pricing and mandate details are unverified.

**Synthesis:** the strongest WS02 *system* for this founder is probably **one vertical (trucking or contractors) served with back-office (bookkeeping/payroll) plus an insurance agency**.
- The back-office services earn cash in months 1–6 and build trust.
- The renewal book compounds into a sellable asset.
- Premium finance (WS02-022) and an MGA/program (WS02-016) are phase-2 adjacencies.

This cross-sell assumption (E) is the main thing to validate.

## Surprises
- Goosehead's 10-K confirms franchise royalties of **20% on new business and 50% on renewals**, and that **Goosehead keeps ultimate ownership of the policies**. Franchise count fell 9% in 2025, with 43 franchises in Illinois [A, S011]. Franchising looks attractive but undermines the wealth thesis.
- Medicare economics are documented: $873 lifetime commission per approved MA policy [A, S020]. But a DOJ False Claims Act intervention against major brokers, and lifetime value falling from $910 to $873 in two years, show policy fragility.
- Merchant residuals can be sold, but acquirers amortize them over about 6 years. The real value of a merchant book is far below what a naive multiple of its residual suggests.

## Looked good, but fails or weakens
- **Rejected for wealth structure:** franchise agencies (015) and captive agencies (014), because the founder does not own the book.
- **Rejected as lead arbitrage:** final-expense telesales (012), with chargebacks and TCPA exposure, and ACA enrollment (011), with thin per-member economics and policy/enforcement risk.
- **Rejected for access or cyclicality:** title (032), cyclical and attorney-locked in Illinois; crop insurance (013), outsider access wall; health TPA (034), credibility and capital wall; currency exchange (033), secular decline and capital needs.
- **Rejected as too thin:** premium audit vendor (020); AP-automation resale (025), a feature rather than a business.
- **Hold:** an MGA (016) and premium finance (022, which needs a $50k minimum net worth plus a funded loan book [A, S025]) are not entry points from zero.

## Evidence gaps (priority order)
1. **Aggregator, network and wholesaler terms for an Illinois start-up:** SIAA, Smart Choice and others. We need fees, splits, minimums and departure fees, plus which networks accept trucking or contractor business.
2. **Realized commission rates:** trucking commission through wholesalers and MGAs, and new-venture premiums per truck.
3. **Small-book valuations:** revenue multiples for sub-$500k agencies, and for bookkeeping and payroll practices.
4. **Illinois Collection Agency Act (225 ILCS 425)** and the Currency Exchange Act. Scope for commercial debt, bond and manager rules are unverified because ilga.gov was unreachable (503/TLS).
5. **Merchant program terms:** ISO residual split and vesting, and annual merchant attrition rates.
6. **Pricing benchmarks:** bookkeeping and payroll fees by vertical, white-label payroll platform costs, and COI-tracking prices.
7. **Illinois Secure Choice:** employer thresholds and deadlines, and federal start-up plan credits.
8. **Other Illinois rules:**
   - Public-adjuster fee caps and solicitation rules.
   - Whether surety needs a separate line of authority.
   - Whether fee-based insurance consulting requires a license (affects 019 and 021).
   - The current CPI-adjusted dram-shop caps.
9. **CMS 2026/2027 MA compensation caps:** the ZIP files were not reviewed.

**Limitations:** the shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so later evidence came from direct fetches of primary pages and SEC EDGAR. The Hartford and FMCSA pages returned 503/403. The B&B/Baldwin multiples and the sub-$1M multiples come partly from search summaries.
