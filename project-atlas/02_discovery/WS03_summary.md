# WS03 — Industrial & Manufacturing: Discovery Summary

**Records:** 35 opportunities, 33 sources. **Verdicts:** 12 advance, 20 hold, 3 reject. Scores are for this founder starting from zero. Access date for all sources: 2026-11-28.

## Landscape (what the evidence says)

- **Owning production is a low-ROIC entry.** In the 2022 Economic Census, machine shops (332710) kept only **~10.0% of shipments** after materials, payroll, benefits, purchased services, depreciation and rent. They hold **~$1.32M of machinery per establishment** [C, S007]. Comparable residuals:
  - sheet metal 18.3%
  - powder coating 21.3%
  - plastics 17.0%
  - PCBA 5.0%
  - dies/molds 1.8%
- **Intermediaries capture more margin per dollar of capital.** Xometry's marketplace gross margin was **34.7%** in FY2025 (81,821 buyers, 4,996 suppliers) [B, S001]. Protolabs' outsourced "network" revenue grew 15.7% against 4.1% for its own factories [B, S002].
  - Scaled platforms still lose money: Xometry's net loss was $61.7M; Fictiv lost money three years running before selling for $350M [C/D, S004–S005]. A small human-led broker is a different business.
- **Industrial services are where PE is consolidating.**
  - PrivSource tracked ~196 industrial-services add-ons in 2025 [D, S017].
  - Crane 1 has 10 add-ons and 30 locations [C, S016].
  - Transcat's calibration service: $217M revenue at 32.5% gross margin, acquisitive [B, S012–S013]. Mistras NDT: 28.2% gross margin [B, S014].
  - Motor-repair roll-ups: MCE/Frontenac and HECO [C, S018–S019].
  - SUSB shows thousands of US machinery-repair (811310) firms at $1–10M receipts, the scale where $100–500k owner profit is plausible [C, S008]. This is the strongest signal that small operators here create sellable value.
- **Illinois buyer density** (CBP 2023) [C, S006]:
  - 12,465 manufacturing establishments; 4,371 have 20+ employees and 2,329 have 50+.
  - By county: Cook 4,350, DuPage 1,534, Lake 794, Kane 782, Will 592, Winnebago 520, McHenry 444. About 70% are in the Chicago metro plus Rockford.
  - Machine shops: 829. Machinery-repair establishments: 873. Warehouses (493110): 765.
- **Tariffs and reshoring (verified to Nov 2026):**
  - The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on 2026-02-20 [S009].
  - A temporary Section 122 10% surcharge lapsed on 2026-07-24 [S033].
  - Section 301 duties of generally 10–12.5% on 60 partners replaced it, with USMCA goods and Section 232 goods largely exempt. These duties are being litigated [S010].
  - Section 232 tariffs of 50% on steel, aluminum and copper remain [S011].
  - Net: tariffs favor domestic/USMCA sourcing and raise metal costs; volatile, so a selling point, not a foundation.

## Strongest candidates

1. **WS03-012 — Overhead crane and hoist inspection and repair** (adjusted score 5.98, evidence C).
   - OSHA requires periodic inspections at 1–12 month intervals with certification records [A, S015].
   - PE platforms show branch economics can be delegated. The founder sells and manages but must hire the lead technician first.
2. **WS03-001 — Contract-manufacturing sourcing broker** (machining and sheet metal; 5.64, C).
   - Lowest-capital route into manufacturing, with public proof that buyers pay ~35% gross margin to intermediaries.
   - The wedge must be complexity, domestic/USMCA content and local audited shops, not price.
   - It is also the honest path to owning equipment: buy machines (WS03-026/027/028) only for proven repeat parts.
3. **WS03-017 — Industrial scale and weighing service** (5.48, D).
   - Calibration is required for legal-for-trade and quality systems.
   - The Illinois barrier is trivial: $100/yr agency and $30/yr serviceperson registration [A, S028].
   - A PE consolidator (Michelli) is acquiring in this niche.
4. **WS03-030 — Reverse engineering and build-to-print of obsolete parts** (5.41, E).
   - Uses the founder's technical background and earns higher margins than generic brokerage.
5. **WS03-025 — Lockout/tagout procedures and machine-guarding compliance** (5.45, E).
   - LOTO is OSHA's #3 and machine guarding its #10 most-cited standard in FY2026 [A, S030].
   - Near-zero capital, and AI drafting materially cuts unit cost.
   - Weak recurrence: the employer's own authorized employee must perform the annual periodic inspection [A, S031]. Best as a door-opener for recurring services.
6. **WS03-014 + WS03-015 — Reliability bundle: infrared thermography and vibration routes.**
   - NFPA 70B-2023 annual inspection expectation [C/D, S026–S027]. Low capital; learnable certifications.
   - How far 70B is enforced is unverified.
7. **WS03-005 + WS03-006 — Obsolete and surplus automation parts: inventory resale plus no-inventory sourcing.**
   - One of the few industrial niches where buyers search by part number.
   - eBay fee 13.6% up to $7,500 [B, S020]. Risks: inventory discipline, counterfeits.
8. **WS03-013 — Pallet-rack inspection and repair.**
   - Annual inspection is expected under ANSI MH16.1/RMI [C, S029], and the Chicago logistics market is dense.
   - Price evidence is missing.

**Natural combination.** Items 1, 3, 6 and 8 share a buyer (plant maintenance/EHS manager), a CBP-identifiable prospect list and the field-tech model. A multi-service "plant compliance inspections" company (cranes, racks, scales, IR) could cross-sell and build a consolidator-friendly asset; test this in screening.

## Surprises

- Illinois powder coating keeps 43% of shipments after materials, payroll and benefits (US: 33%), the best owned-production margin, but local and permit-bound.
- Protolabs' 3D printing fell 4.1% while CNC grew 17.6%: additive bureaus are a reject.
- Consolidators are active locally, e.g., J&S Industrial Services (IL) [D, S017].

## Looked good but fail or weaken

- **PCBA brokerage (004):** materials are 71% of value and maker residual is ~5%, so there is too little margin to share.
- **Hydroblasting/vacuum trucks (022):** G1 capital failure, safety-record walls, and PE roll-ups already in the market.
- **Private-label consumables on marketplaces (009):** a 12% referral fee plus FBA, ads and stacked tariffs leave thin contribution against direct Chinese sellers. Viable only inside direct accounts.
- **Owning a CNC shop first (026):** about 10% residual and about $1.3M of machinery per establishment.
- **Reshoring consulting (002):** demand exists, but it depends on litigated, volatile tariff policy.
- **Calibration lab (016):** demand is excellent, but accreditation, capital and metrology expertise make it a second-stage move.

## Evidence gaps (priority for screening)

- **Service prices in Chicago:** per-crane inspection, per-rack-facility inspection, IR day rates, scale calibrations, LOTO per-machine. Not found. Most service economics are E/D.
- **Technician wages:** BLS OEWS pages and files returned 403. Staffing costs are estimates.
- **Illinois/Chicago licensing:** crane service, electrical work near energized gear (IR), IEPA special-waste permits (coolant, vacuum truck), the Auction License Act (008) and the PE Act boundary (030).
- **Enforcement:** how NFPA 70B and RMI are enforced in practice by insurers and AHJs.
- **Broker willingness:** whether Illinois job shops will quote reliably to a new broker, and at what markup.
- **Unpublished fees and unreviewed sources:** ISO 17025 accreditation cost/time (A2LA quotes only); franchise FDD Item 19s.
