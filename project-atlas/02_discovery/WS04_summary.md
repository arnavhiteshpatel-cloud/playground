# WS04 — Healthcare, dental, veterinary & life-sciences operations: discovery summary

36 opportunities · 37 sources · access date 2026-11-28. Evidence is uneven. Regulatory facts are mostly at level A. Incumbent economics are at level B (Henry Schein, Patterson, R1 RCM, Stericycle, Addus, Accendra/Owens & Minor). Ticket sizes come from federal procurement awards (A as spend evidence, D as a proxy for small-clinic pricing). Most new-entrant margins remain **E**.

## Landscape
The stream splits into four economic families:

1. **Labor-arbitrage care services** (home care, home nursing, NEMT, staffing, adult day; 001–008, 030). There is real, documented spend. Illinois CCP in-home rate is $30.80/h from Jan 2026 on a ~$1.6B program [S002]. Home Instead franchise units averaged $2.61M in 2024 gross sales at about $33 per client hour [S003]. Margins are thin, though. Addus earns only "low 20%" incremental gross margin on Illinois Medicaid hours, and its Illinois DSO hit 54.7 days [S004]. Labor is the binding constraint, and Medicaid exposure is rising: OBBBA work requirements arrive by Jan 2027 [S007], and the 80/20 rule is unresolved [S009].
2. **Asset-light back-office for fragmented practices** (billing/RCM, dental verification, credentialing, IT MSP, compliance, records retrieval; 010–014, 021). Capital needs are near zero, revenue is recurring, and the work fits a tech founder. Competition is crowded and offshore-priced. DSO and corporate-vet consolidation steadily removes independent customers [S026].
3. **Mandated technical and route services** (biomed ISO, instrument sharpening, BSC/cleanroom certification, pipette calibration, regulated medical waste, spore testing; 015–019, 024–026). Federal awards show small private firms winning multi-year contracts [S030–S035]. Demand is compliance-driven and recurring, and licensing is light. The usual constraint is skilled or credentialed labor.
4. **Capital- or credential-heavy clinical models** (Medicare home health/hospice, vet clinic, portable X-ray, adult day; 005, 006, 033, 035). These are poor from-zero fits.

## Strongest candidates (evidence-adjusted, harsh)
1. **WS04-014: HIPAA-focused managed IT for independent dental/vet practices.** Strong founder fit, prepaid monthly contracts, low capital. Consolidation is the main risk. Pricing is unverified (E).
2. **WS04-024: Small-generator regulated medical-waste route.** The service is legally mandated, and an IEPA hauling permit costs $2,000/yr plus $250 per vehicle [S016]. The incumbent reports a 38% gross margin, describes barriers to entry as low, and paid a $295M class settlement over small-customer price increases [S028]. Small Illinois firms win VA work [S031]. The key unknown is access to and pricing at a treatment facility.
3. **WS04-010: Niche RCM for authorization-heavy outpatient specialties** (ABA, therapy, behavioral). The work is asset-light, gets real AI leverage, and carries clear rules on BAAs and percentage fees [S012, S018]. Platforms that bundle billing for free are a real threat.
4. **WS04-015: Biomed ISO for small sites** (ASCs, clinics, dental, vet). Maintenance is mandated, FDA leaves third-party servicing alone [S014], and federal ISO awards exist [S035]. Success depends on hiring BMETs.
5. **WS04-001: Independent private-pay home care.** It has the best-documented revenue path. Item 19 tenure data show 3–4-year units mostly at $1.0–1.5M gross sales [S003]. Margins and labor are the weak points, and evidence is level C for an independent operator.
6. **Next tier:** WS04-018 (BSC/USP cleanroom certification: mandated, with procurement evidence, but needs an NSF-accredited hire because accreditation takes about a year of field work [S015]); WS04-013 (records retrieval plus AI medical chronologies for personal-injury firms); WS04-016 (mobile instrument sharpening, with multi-year VA contracts to independents [S032]); WS04-032 (pet aftercare route for vet clinics, unverified but structurally durable).

**Bundle insight:** 011, 014, 021, 024 and 026 sell to the same buyer, the independent dental or vet office manager, through the same relationship. A single "independent-practice operations" company could stack IT, billing, compliance, waste and spore testing. It would raise revenue per account and give independents what DSOs get centrally. Stage B should test this as one combined thesis rather than as five separate small businesses.

## Surprises
- **Owens & Minor sold its distribution business** (Products & Healthcare Services) for $375M on 31 Dec 2025 and renamed itself Accendra Health, keeping only home-based patient supply [S029]. Large-incumbent distribution economics now rest on Henry Schein and the last public Patterson filing: Dental ~8.4% and Animal Health ~3.4% operating margin [S026].
- Small regional firms, not only Stericycle/WM, win Illinois VA medical-waste and instrument-repair contracts, which suggests the incumbents are not unassailable.
- The Home Instead Item 19 reports gross sales per client hour (~$33), which gives a usable national bill-rate benchmark.
- Medicare DMEPOS competitive bidding restarts no later than 1 Jan 2028 [S008]. That argues for keeping any equipment-rental business on private pay or hospice contracts.

## Looked good but fail or weaken
- **Dental/vet consumables distribution (022):** incumbent operating margins of 3–8% with no wedge. Rejected.
- **Medicare home health/hospice (005):** CY2026 net −1.3% Medicare home-health payment cut [S008], plus capital, clinical leadership and audit risk.
- **IDoA CCP Medicaid home care (003):** documented rate but thin margin, payment-lag risk and 2027+ federal cuts. Hold as a later add-on only.
- **Nurse/CNA staffing (030):** app-disrupted, credit risk from nursing homes, and IDOL enforcement tightened in 2025 [S010].
- **NEMT (008):** high-risk Medicaid provider screening, fingerprinting, three-yearly driver safety recertification with payment recoupment if undocumented [S017], insurance-heavy, commoditized.
- **Practice brokerage (034)** and **clinical-trial recruitment (036):** credibility walls.

## Evidence gaps (priority for Stage B)
- Private-market pricing for dental/vet IT MSP, medical-waste pickup, BSC certification per unit, instrument sharpening, billing fees (% of collections). Nearly all of these are E.
- Northern-Illinois PIMW treatment-facility options and tipping fees, and pollution-liability insurance cost.
- Illinois statutes not retrievable this session (ilga.gov returned 503): 77 IAC 245 caregiver training and manager rules; 735 ILCS 5/8-2001 medical-record copy-fee caps; Veterinary and Dental Practice Act lay-ownership rules; pet-crematory permitting.
- USP <797> recertification interval (6-month cited in practice, not verified); IEMA X-ray service-provider registration.
- Wage data (the BLS OEWS Illinois page was inaccessible) and establishment counts (the Census CBP API requires a key, which we could not register for under the rules).
- Home-care franchise Item 5/6/7 fees, Item 20 closures, and Chicago territory availability.

## Limitations
The shared web-search budget ran out after about 12 searches. After that, research relied on direct fetches of primary sources: CMS, HFS, IDPH, IEPA, NSF, FDA, CDC, Cornell LII regulatory text, SEC EDGAR filings, and the USASpending award API. Federal award values are multi-year contract totals at hospital scale and serve only as spend proxies for small clinics. The Home Instead Item 19 was read from a third-party-hosted extract, not a state FDD database.
