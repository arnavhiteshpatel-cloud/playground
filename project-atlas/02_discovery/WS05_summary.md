# WS05 — Commercial & building services: discovery summary (2026-11-28)

36 opportunity records, 36 sources. Most unit economics are analyst estimates (E). Licensing facts, firm counts and incumbent benchmarks come from primary sources (A).

## Landscape
This workstream has a lot of mandated, recurring spend that is fragmented across small firms. What decides whether a cold start can work is **who may legally do the work**, not demand. The services fall into four tiers by how reachable the licence is for this founder:

| Tier | Services | Illinois/Chicago rule (primary source) |
|---|---|---|
| Founder can license in about 1–4 months | Fire extinguishers and kitchen suppression; pest control | Extinguishers and suppression: OSFM distributor licence (Class A $900, Class B $1,500, both for 3 years) plus an employee licence ($300) backed by an ICC/NAFED certificate. The NAFED exam is open-book, costs $320 and lists no experience prerequisite. Also needed: $1M liability cover and hydrotesting through a PHMSA RIN holder, which can be subcontracted (S001–S003, S025). Pest control: IDPH business licence ($250 per location per year) that requires a certified technician on staff. The technician exam fee is $75 and no experience is required (S011–S013). |
| Needs a hired licensed person | Sprinklers; backflow testing; possibly fire alarm | Sprinklers: since 2024 inspectors need an OSFM licence ($500 for 2 years, age 21+, NICET III ITM, NFPA WBITM, ASSE 15010 or an apprenticeship). Contractors need a Designated Certified Person, meaning a PE or NICET III/IV in layout, plus a $3,000 licence (S009, S016, S017). Backflow: testers must be CCCDIs, which requires plumbing-licence qualification, and a plumbing licence takes at least a 4-year apprenticeship (S005–S008, S029). Fire alarm: the IDFPR alarm contractor licence needs 3 years' experience; a degree can count for 2 years and a certification for 1 (S021). |
| No specific licence found | Hood cleaning, fire-door/damper inspection, janitorial, hygiene routes, dock doors | Codes still apply. NFPA 101 (2015) is adopted statewide with NFPA 80 cited for fire doors (S010). MWRD sets a 250 mg/L fats, oils and grease limit (S027). |
| Effectively closed | Elevators; security guards; high-rise window cleaning | Elevators: OSFM licensing outside Chicago, a separate Chicago regime, union labour and original-equipment makers dominate (S022). Guards: IDFPR licensing plus thin margins. |

**Firm counts (CBP 2023, Illinois, S004):** pest control 395 establishments, janitorial 2,520, other services to buildings 463, elevator/other building equipment 344, security systems 187. In each category most establishments have fewer than 5 employees.

The OSFM licence rolls (dated 11/19/2026) show how small the licensed pool is:
- **Fire equipment:** 184 distributor licences, 858 employee licences and about 75 Chicagoland locations. Half of all locations have only 1–2 licensed employees (S014).
- **Sprinklers:** 193 contractors and only 699 licensed inspectors in the whole state (S015).

**Consolidator signals (A-level, incumbent economics only):**
- **APi Group:** inspection-first strategy, 140 acquisitions, Safety Services segment margin of 16.8% (S019).
- **Rollins:** 52.8% gross margin, 19.3% operating margin, about 75% recurring revenue; 44 acquisitions in 2024 and 25+ in 2025 (S018).
- **Cintas Fire Protection:** revenue grew from $729M to $929M in two years (S024).
- **Others:** Pye-Barker (~246 locations, S020) and Service Logic (64 companies, no revenue floor, S030).

**Route density and pull-through:** No primary source puts a number on inspection-to-repair pull-through. APi says only that inspection dollars "can lead to" service work. The route model in the records is E-level:
- One dense-route technician brings in about $150–250k a year.
- Loaded labour plus vehicle cost about $80–95k.
- That leaves roughly $50–110k contribution per route.
- Repair, replacement and upgrade work (e.g., UL 300 suppression upgrades, communicator swaps, door hardware) carries much of the margin.

## Strongest candidates (evidence-adjusted ranking)
1. **WS05-007 Commercial pest control for food and audited facilities** (adjusted 6.38, C)
   - The founder can obtain the licence personally; contracts are monthly; incumbent margins are high and buyers keep acquiring firms.
   - Chicago's own inspection data shows 2,147 food licences cited for rodent/insect violations in 13 months, out of 10,605 inspected, and inspectors tell them to call a licensed pest firm (S034). That is a public, refreshing prospect list.
2. **WS05-001 Extinguisher + emergency/exit lighting route** (5.80, D)
   - Licence obtainable in weeks; mandated annual demand; sellable to consolidators.
   - Chicago building violations cite extinguishers at 489 addresses a year (S035).
   - This is the natural base for adding suppression (003), alarm (006) and sprinkler (002) lines.
3. **WS05-026 Medical/dental clinic janitorial** (5.84, E)
   - Nearly customer-funded and highly reachable, with a healthcare angle. The industry is crowded; the evidence is weak.
4. **WS05-011 Fire-door inspection (NFPA 80) + hardware repair** (5.53, E)
   - Specialist niche with low licensing barriers and repair pull-through. Institutional sales cycles and pricing are unverified.
5. **WS05-025 Restroom hygiene route** (5.48, E)
   - Simple to delegate; recurring replenishment. Tickets are small and Cintas bundles this service.
6. **WS05-006 Fire alarm ITM + monitoring revenue** and **WS05-002 Sprinkler ITM**
   - Attractive economics, but each needs licensed staff or experience. Best treated as phase-2 lines.

The likely winning structure is a **multi-line fire and life-safety company built inspection-first**: start with extinguishers and emergency lighting, then add kitchen suppression. Add alarm and sprinkler lines once licensed hires are in place. Pest control is the strongest standalone alternative.

## Surprises
- Licence rosters are public and small. Sprinkler inspection is labour-constrained statewide, with 699 inspectors.
- The extinguisher-employee licence has no experience requirement; only an open-book certification is needed.
- The Fire Equipment Act may face a 1 Jan 2028 sunset (Justia note, unverified). This is a regulatory risk, and possibly an opportunity if the law lapses.
- City enforcement data (food inspections, building violations) turns compliance demand into named prospects.

## Looked good but fail
- **Backflow testing:** the mandate is perfect, but the founder can never hold the credential and pricing is commodity.
- **Facilities-management aggregation:** thin pass-through margins, severe working-capital float, and Lessen already runs 34K vendors and 3M work orders a year (S031).
- **Hood cleaning:** mandated but crowded, and franchises charge about $200–247k to enter (S026).
- **Elevators, guards, high-rise windows, snow, sweeping:** closed off by labour, licensing, weather or price.

## Evidence gaps
- No primary pricing for any service: per-extinguisher fees, pest contracts, backflow tests, door inspections.
- No primary figure for the pull-through ratio.
- Chicago municipal code text is unverified (chicago.gov returned 403): extinguisher, hood, bed-bug and grease-interceptor rules.
- Not confirmed: Structural Pest Control Act Section 9 insurance minimums, the adopted sprinkler fee rule, and the status of the 2028 sunset. ilga.gov returned 503 and Justia returned 403.
- CMS/TJC requirements for fire doors, dampers and generators: the federal sites blocked access with a captcha.
- BLS state wage tables could not be parsed, and the Census API needs a key.
- Not verified: manufacturer gatekeeping for kitchen-suppression parts, and whether fire-alarm-only work requires the IDFPR licence.
- The shared web-search budget ran out partway through; later work used direct fetches only.
