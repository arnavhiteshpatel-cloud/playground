# WS07 — Logistics, transport, trade & liquidation: discovery summary

36 records (WS07-001…036), 37 sources. As of 2026-11-28. Evidence levels are in brackets.

## Landscape
- **Brokerage has the most documented spend in this workstream, and the most competition.** CHR's NAST billed $3.83B in Q3 2026 at about 13.5% adjusted gross profit [A, S008]. RXO's brokerage gross margin was 12.6% [A, S010]. Landstar pays agents 7.8% of revenue and buys transport at 78.2% [A, S011]. FMCSA data implies about 33k brokers [A-derived, S001]. The industry is consolidating: CHR is acquiring RXO for $5.8B [A, S010].
- **Freight cycle, late 2026.** Tightening comes from shrinking supply, not rising demand:
  - CHR's carrier cost per mile rose 33.5% against 31.5% for its price [A, S008].
  - DAT October spot vs contract: van $3.05 vs $3.28, reefer $3.60 vs $3.62, flatbed $3.61 vs $4.05. Volumes are down 2–9% year on year [C, S013].
  - CDL and English-proficiency enforcement may have removed about 200k drivers. That figure is contested [C, S014, S015].
- **Broker authority (primary sources):**
  - Registration runs through Motus, with identity proofing and at least 8 business days of review [A, S002].
  - $75k financial security is required. Since 2026-01-16, a BMC-85 trust may hold only cash, ILOCs or Treasuries [A, S001].
  - Authority is suspended if the security falls below $75k and is not refilled within 7 days of FMCSA's notice [A, S001].
  - BOC-3 required [A, S002]. UCR $46 in 2026, $55 in 2027 [A, S003, S004]. OP-1 fee reportedly $300, vendor source only [D, S005].
  - The transparency rule is still a proposal [C, S007].
  - Multi-carrier dispatchers who allocate loads need broker authority [A, S006].
- **Working capital.** CHR carries about 61 days of receivables against about 39 days of payables [A-derived, S009]. Factoring costs about 1–5% per 30 days [D, S017].
- **Fraud.** Cargo-theft losses were about $725M in 2025 (+60%), with thieves increasingly using acquired carriers [C, S016].
- **Chicago.** It is the busiest U.S. rail-truck intermodal hub [C, S019; dated data]. 2026 problems: chassis imbalances at Elwood and Joliet [C, S021], a drayage-driver shortage [C, S022], and tight intermodal capacity [C, S020].
- **Customs and trade.**
  - A customs broker license requires U.S. citizenship, age 21+ and passing the CBLE [A, S025]. Pass rates run 12–30% [C/D, S026, S027].
  - Customs brokers may pay referral fees only to freight forwarders [A, S028].
  - The IEEPA tariffs were struck down on 2026-02-20 [C, S030]. Refunds go through ACE/CAPE [C, S031].
  - De minimis is suspended indefinitely, with repeal in July 2027 [A/C, S032].
  - An OTI license needs a qualifying individual with 3 years of experience, plus a $75k or $50k bond [A, S033, S034].
- **Liquidation.** Returns were about $850B in 2025 [C, S035]. But Liquidity Services earns only about 19% gross margin on goods it buys outright and has moved to consignment (86% of Q4 GMV). Its management warns recovery rates are falling [A, S036].

## Strongest candidates
1. **WS07-002 — Freight agent under a host broker.** Validation costs hundreds of dollars. The host funds carriers and carries the credit risk [A, S012]. 457 Landstar agents each produce over $1M [A, S012]. It depends on the founder, and the split caps earnings.
2. **WS07-004 — Flatbed brokerage for Chicago and NW Indiana steel and building-materials shippers.** Widest contract-spot spread [C, S013], plus a securement and permits knowledge wedge.
3. **WS07-005 — LTL 3PL for small shippers.** Many frequent buyers, spread credit risk, and work that can genuinely be automated.
4. **WS07-036 — Fee-based managed transportation.** No carrier-payment float. It suits an analytical founder, and the rate spike makes freight a CFO problem [A, S008]. The sale is credibility-gated.
5. **WS07-015 — Licensed customs brokerage.** Mandated recurring demand, de minimis has ended [A, S032], and the license is a moat. Licensing takes about 12–24 months (G2 WARN).
6. **WS07-023 — Online consignment liquidation auctions.** No inventory, buyers prepay (float), and volume rises in downturns [A, S036]. Illinois auctioneer licensing is unverified. Coordinate with WS13.
7. **WS07-001 — Own-authority truckload broker.** The graduation path from 002. Well-evidenced economics, but extreme competition and a negative cash cycle.

Suggested sequence: start as an agent (002); add LTL (005) and flatbed (004); then take own authority (001) with managed transportation (036). Keep customs brokerage (015) as a parallel long-gestation option.

## Surprises
- 2026 was a supply shock, not a demand recovery. Volumes are down and rates are at multi-year highs.
- Contract margins are compressing (RXO −90 bp) while CHR cuts headcount with AI (NAST −11.4%) [A].
- A founder with weak credit who uses the BMC-85 trust ties up $75k of roughly $100k in capital.
- Most multi-carrier "dispatch services" are effectively unlicensed brokers.
- 19 CFR 111.36 blocks unlicensed referral partners from customs-broker referral fees.

## Looked good but fails
- **Bin stores and pallets (019).** The incumbent's A-level margin is about 19%, supply is abundant and recovery rates are falling.
- **Amazon DSP (026).** One customer controls price and volume.
- **Dispatch services (011).** Gray zone and a low ceiling.
- **HHG moving brokers (012).** Enforcement and reputation problems.
- **Unlicensed tariff-refund advisory (016).** Refunds flow through CAPE, and there is unlicensed-practice risk.
- **Carrier-vetting SaaS (034).** Funded incumbents dominate.
- **Air forwarding (014) and trade-show freight (035).** No wedge.
- **Asset drayage (008) and transload (009).** Timely, but they carry insurance, capital and lease risk.
