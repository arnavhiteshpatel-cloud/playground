# WS07 — Logistics, transport, trade & liquidation: discovery summary

36 records (WS07-001…036), 37 sources. As of 2026-11-28. Evidence levels are in brackets.

## Landscape
- **Freight brokerage is the deepest pool of documented spend in this workstream, but it is also the most crowded.** C.H. Robinson's NAST billed $3.83B in Q3 2026 at about 13.5% adjusted gross profit [A, S008]. RXO's brokerage gross margin was 12.6% [A, S010]. Landstar pays agents 7.8% of revenue and buys transport at 78.2% of revenue [A, S011]. FMCSA's own estimate implies about 33,000 brokers [A-derived, S001]. The industry is consolidating: CHR agreed on 2026-10-05 to acquire RXO ($5.8B) [A, S010].
- **Freight cycle, late 2026.** The market is tightening sharply because supply is shrinking, not because demand is growing:
  - CHR's carrier cost per mile rose 33.5% year on year, against 31.5% for its price [A, S008].
  - DAT's October van spot rate was $3.05 against a $3.28 contract rate. Reefer spot and contract are almost equal ($3.60 vs $3.62). Flatbed has the widest gap ($3.61 vs $4.05). Volume indices are down 2–9% year on year [C, S013].
  - Enforcement on non-domiciled CDLs and English proficiency may have removed around 200k drivers. That figure is contested [C, S014, S015].
  - A new broker now faces scarce, bargaining-strong carriers and squeezed contract margins.
- **Broker authority (primary sources):**
  - Registration now runs through Motus, with identity proofing (ID photo plus selfie), CLEAR business verification, and at least 8 business days of review [A, S002].
  - $75k financial security is required. Since 2026-01-16, a BMC-85 trust may hold only cash, ILOCs or Treasuries [A, S001].
  - Authority is suspended if the security is drawn below $75k and not refilled within 7 calendar days of FMCSA's notice [A, S001].
  - A BOC-3 filing is required [A, S002]. UCR is $46 in 2026 and $55 in 2027 [A, S003, S004].
  - The OP-1 fee is reportedly $300, but only a vendor source says so [D, S005]. fmcsa.dot.gov returned 403.
  - The broker-transparency supplemental proposal has been at OIRA since 2026-08-27 [C, S007].
  - Dispatch services need broker authority if any of FMCSA's seven factors apply [A, S006].
- **Working capital is the hidden cost.** CHR carries about 61 days of receivables against about 39 days of payables [A-derived, S009]. Shippers pay in 30–60+ days. Factoring costs about 1–5% per 30 days [D, S017], which can take 10–15% of a small broker's gross margin.
- **Fraud.** Cargo-theft losses were about $725M in 2025 (+60%). Thieves increasingly use acquired carriers that have clean histories [C, S016].
- **Chicago.** It is the busiest U.S. rail-truck intermodal hub [C, S019; data from 2012–16]. In 2026 it has chassis imbalances at Elwood and Joliet [C, S021], a drayage-driver shortage [C, S022], and limited committed intermodal capacity [C, S020].
- **Customs and trade.**
  - A broker license requires U.S. citizenship, age 21+ and passing the CBLE [A, S025]. Pass rates have run 12–30% [C/D, S026, S027].
  - Only a freight forwarder may receive referral pay from a customs broker [A, S028].
  - The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on 2026-02-20 [C, S030]. Refunds go to importers through ACE/CAPE [C, S031].
  - De minimis is suspended indefinitely, and the statute is repealed from July 2027 [A/C, S032].
  - An ocean forwarder (OTI) license needs a qualifying individual with 3 years of experience [A, S033], plus a $75k (NVOCC) or $50k (forwarder) bond [A, S034].
- **Liquidation.** Retail returns were about $850B in 2025 [C, S035]. But Liquidity Services earns only about 19% gross margin on goods it buys outright: $295.3M purchase revenue against $240.2M cost of goods. It is moving to consignment (86% of Q4 GMV) and warns that heavy supply is pushing recovery rates down [A, S036].

## Strongest candidates (for this founder)
1. **WS07-002 — Freight agent under a host broker.** It costs only hundreds of dollars to test whether the founder can sell freight. The host funds carrier payments and carries the credit risk [A, S012]. There is a documented path to Landstar-style "Million Dollar Agents" (457 of them produce 95% of Landstar's revenue) [A, S012]. Weakness: it depends on the founder, and the split caps earnings.
2. **WS07-004 — Flatbed/open-deck brokerage for Chicago and NW Indiana steel and building-materials shippers.** It has the widest contract-spot spread [C, S013], a real knowledge wedge (securement, permits), and local industrial density.
3. **WS07-005 — LTL 3PL for small shippers.** Many frequent, short-cycle buyers; spread credit risk; and quoting, BOL and audit work that can genuinely be automated [A context, S008].
4. **WS07-036 — Fee-based managed transportation for mid-size shippers.** Because carriers bill the shipper directly, there is no carrier-payment float, and the work suits an analytical founder. The 2026 rate spike makes freight spend a CFO problem [A, S008]. It is a credibility-gated sale, and the broker-definition boundary must be respected [A, S006].
5. **WS07-015 — Licensed customs brokerage.** Every import needs an entry, so demand is mandated and recurring. De minimis has ended [A, S032] and tariffs keep changing. The license moat suits a research-oriented founder. Cost: about 12–24 months to get licensed (G2 WARN), and fee benchmarks are unverified.
6. **WS07-023 — Online consignment auctions for business liquidations.** No inventory, buyers pay before pickup (float), and volume rises in downturns. This matches the incumbents' shift to consignment [A, S036]. **Illinois auctioneer licensing is unverified.** Coordinate with WS13.
7. **WS07-001 — Own-authority truckload broker.** This is where WS07-002 graduates to. The economics are well evidenced, but competition is extreme and the cash cycle runs negative.

Suggested sequence: start as an agent (002); add LTL (005) and flatbed (004); then take own authority (001) with managed transportation (036); keep customs brokerage (015) as a parallel long-gestation option.

## Surprises
- 2026 brought a *supply* shock rather than a demand recovery. Volumes are down and rates are at multi-year highs. Brokers' contract margins are compressing (RXO −90 bp) while CHR's AI-driven headcount cuts (NAST −11.4%) raise the bar for productivity [A].
- The BMC-85 trust can no longer be funded through loan or finance companies. With ~$100k of capital, a founder with weak credit would tie up 75% of it in the trust.
- FMCSA's 2023 guidance makes most "dispatch services" for multiple carriers de facto unlicensed brokers.
- 19 CFR 111.36 blocks an unlicensed referral partner from being paid by customs brokers, unless that partner is a freight forwarder.

## Looked good but fails
- **Liquidation pallets/bin stores (019).** The A-level incumbent purchase margin is about 19%, supply is abundant and recovery rates are falling.
- **Amazon DSP (026).** The program page was unreadable. A single customer controls price and volume.
- **Dispatch services (011).** Regulatory gray zone and a low ceiling.
- **HHG moving brokers (012).** Enforcement and reputation problems.
- **Unlicensed tariff-refund advisory (016).** Refunds flow to importers through CAPE, and there is unlicensed-practice risk.
- **Carrier-vetting software (034).** Funded incumbents already dominate.
- **Air forwarding (014) and trade-show freight (035).** Closed relationship networks, no wedge.
- **Asset drayage (008) and transload (009).** Timely given Chicago's shortage, but they carry insurance, capital and lease risk.

## Evidence gaps
- Census SUSB data for NAICS 488510, and the survival rates of new broker authorities.
- BMC-84 premiums by credit tier.
- Independent-agent revenue distribution (medians, not just top producers).
- Margins for LTL, expedite, flatbed and reefer brokers, which public companies do not break out.
- Customs per-entry fee benchmarks and the CBLE format/CE rules from CBP's primary text.
- 19 CFR 111.11 (corporate licensing).
- The Illinois Auction License Act and its exemptions.
- Chicago drayage rate indices, current intermodal lift counts, industrial vacancy and rents, office-vacancy and decommissioning demand.
- Amazon DSP terms; pallet, container and final-mile pricing.

Most non-brokerage records rest on E-level evidence.

## Limitations

- fmcsa.dot.gov returned HTTP 403.

- Several CCJ and JOC figures come from search snippets of pages that could not be fetched. The source records note this.
