# WS09 — Publishing, education, information & media: discovery summary

Records: 35 opportunities (`WS09_opportunities.jsonl`), 28 sources (`WS09_sources.jsonl`). Researched 2026-11-28.

## Landscape (honest read)
Scored harshly, this is a weak workstream for this founder. No record reaches a raw score of 6.0. The best ones
land around 5.0–5.8, and most economics evidence is E-level. The stream splits into four groups:

1. **Mandated training and credentialing.** Demand is real and often counted at A level, but much of it is
   already commoditized. Illinois insurance Part-1 exams ran about 16.2k attempts in 2022 (Life 6,071; A&H 3,986;
   Property 3,005; Casualty 3,143; retakes included) [WS09-S001]. FMCSA's registry shows about 35k ELDT completions
   a month (2.09M since Feb 2022) spread across 18,384 active providers [WS09-S023]. Where a mandate can be met by
   self-paced online content, prices fall to commodity levels. Examples: BASSET at $14.99 [WS09-S014], real estate
   pre-license at $399–$699 among about 50 licensed IL providers [WS09-S015, S028], and harassment training, which
   IDHR gives away free [WS09-S003, S024]. Mandates defend margin only where delivery is in person or hands-on, or
   where an approval or authorization wall limits supply. OSHA online outreach, for instance, is closed to all but
   8 providers [WS09-S018].
2. **B2B media and events.** The incumbent precedent is strong. Industry Dive reached about $110m revenue and
   $34m EBITDA and sold for about 11x EBITDA [WS09-S010]. Emerald's trade shows earn about 25% adjusted EBITDA
   [WS09-S011]. But new-entrant ramps take 2–3 years and the outcome is binary.
3. **Public-records data products.** These fit the founder's skills well and need little capital. Raw data is
   often free; Chicago's permit API alone holds 854k records [WS09-S025]. So the value has to come from coverage
   (suburban, FOIA-sourced) and enrichment.
4. **Pure information products** (exam prep, directories, content licensing, generic CE). These are the most
   exposed to generative AI and AI search. Chegg's 2025 revenue fell 39% as search changed [WS09-S012]. I scored
   their resilience 2–4.

## Strongest candidates
1. **WS09-015 — Public-records B2B lead data for Chicagoland vendors.** Low capital, recurring, and buyers are
   visible in the data itself. Cheap-model classification is a durable cost edge. Raw Chicago data is free, so it
   only wins if suburban permit and license data can be gathered at scale and contractors pay $100–500/month.
   WS09-017 (local bid intelligence) is the same platform pointed at procurement. Merge the two in screening.
2. **WS09-008 — On-site workplace-safety training contractor** (forklift, OSHA classroom via authorized trainers,
   bilingual). The mandate plus in-person delivery makes it AI-resistant, and there are many frequent-need
   industrial buyers. It is delegable to trainers. The open questions are local prices and trainer cost. The
   founder cannot personally issue OSHA cards, because trainers need 5 years of safety experience [WS09-S005].
3. **WS09-012 — Regional niche B2B trade show / conference.** This is the most AI-resistant information model.
   Exhibitors prepay, and established shows produce real cash. The first edition is risky. De-risk it by building
   an owned audience first (WS09-010 newsletter) and/or co-hosting with an association.
4. **WS09-023 — CNA training program with employer partnerships.** It meets a federal/state credential mandate in
   a chronically short occupation, with hands-on delivery and employer or workforce funding. Evidence is E-level:
   IDPH and IBHE approval paths, tuition, and exam volumes were not captured.
5. **WS09-007 — Restaurant compliance bundle** (multilingual CFPM classes and proctoring, plus renewal tracking).
   Stacked recurring mandates [WS09-S009] and a buyer base reachable door to door. The ceiling is probably under
   $150k/yr.

Other records worth keeping in view: WS09-010 (newsletter, mainly as the audience engine for events and data),
WS09-011 (paid Illinois sector intelligence, a strong founder fit but small buyer pools and AI-substitution risk),
WS09-001 (IL insurance pre-licensing, with counted demand but a small pool), and WS09-004 (CDL behind-the-wheel
school, with a bigger ticket but capital and insurance questions).

## Surprises
- Illinois still mandates live classroom or webinar hours for insurance pre-licensing (7.5 per line)
  [WS09-S002, S016]. Its insurance exams have been test-center-only since Jan 2025 [WS09-S016]. Seat-time and
  in-person mandates are the main shields against AI.
- OSHA's authorized online-provider list has only 8 names [WS09-S018]. Online OSHA cards are not an open market.
- FMCSA has removed more than 10,000 ELDT providers since Dec 2025 [WS09-S020], and 18,384 remain active
  [WS09-S023]. A federal mandate created a glut rather than an opportunity.
- About 50 licensed real estate pre-license schools in Illinois include many tiny Chicago operators [WS09-S028].
  Small schools can survive, but none of this points to a $250k+/yr business.

## Looked good but fails
- **Harassment-prevention training (005):** a universal annual mandate, but the state supplies it free (A-level).
- **BASSET (006), cannabis Responsible Vendor training (020), universal IDFPR CE topics (021):** real mandates but
  $15-class tickets or tiny pools that 18+ providers already serve.
- **Unmandated exam prep (014), content licensing (027), directories (032):** directly in generative AI's path.
- **Local news (028), consumer expos (013), job boards (019):** ad- or cycle-dependent, with weak wedges.

## Evidence gaps and limitations
- **Research was cut short.** The shared WebSearch budget for the session ran out after about 10 searches. The
  rest of the work used about 30 direct fetches of official or known URLs. That is why most economics are E-level.
  More searching could lift several records to C or B; the user can resume this in a follow-up.
- **Unreachable sites:** the Illinois General Assembly statute site (ilga.gov) and IBHE returned 503 errors
  throughout. As a result these are unverified: BASSET statute text, harassment-training penalties, IDFPR
  universal CE (implicit bias, dementia), the local-government cybersecurity mandate (033), and Private Business &
  Vocational Schools approval rules (023, 024, 004). Several FMCSA and IDOI pages returned 403 or 404.
- **MISSING across records:** price points for CFPM classes, forklift/OSHA sessions, CNA tuition, booth fees,
  sponsorship CPMs and permit-data subscriptions; Illinois broker and CNA licensee or exam counts; ILCC and IDOI
  provider-approval fees; whether new OSHA online providers are accepted at all.
- **Exam-taker counts at A level** were captured only for Illinois insurance (2022) and ELDT (national). Counts
  for trades, CNA, real estate and MLO candidates are still missing.
