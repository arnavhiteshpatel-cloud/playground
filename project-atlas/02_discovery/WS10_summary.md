# WS10 — Construction, building materials & real estate operations: discovery summary

35 opportunities (WS10-001…035) and 35 sources. Research date 2026-11-28. Every score assumes this founder starts from zero.

## Landscape and macro context
- **Housing is weak and rates went up.** The Freddie Mac 30-yr rate was 7.21% on 2026-11-25, against 6.23% a year earlier [S019]. Single-family permits fell 12.9% y/y in Oct 2026, while 5+ unit multifamily starts rose 17.1% y/y [S020, S021]. Chicago issued 32,032 permits in 2025, down from 48,540 in 2019, and 29,067 in 2026 through late November [S010].
- **Incumbents show the same split.** Builders FirstSource's operating margin fell from 12.7% (2023) to 5.2% (2025). IES Residential revenue fell 6.1% (−11.2% in Q1 FY26). Data-center and commercial MEP work is booming: Comfort Systems' backlog is $11.94B, against $5.99B a year earlier [S028, S025, S023]. A newcomer cannot reach the boom segment, because it requires GC prequalification, bonding and often union labor.
- **Specialty-trade economics (Census 2022, analyst ratios).** Across NAICS 238, receipts break down as 25% payroll, 30% materials and 8% subcontracts. Proxy operating margins (before interest, tax and unincorporated owners' draw): about 17% for specialty trades overall, 19.6% roofing, 20% masonry, 25% painting and 13.5% remodelers [S022]. Public comps earn 10–15% operating margins with scale: EMCOR 10.1%, Comfort Systems 14.4%, IBP 13.0% [S024, S023, S028]. These are incumbent numbers. A new entrant should plan on 8–15%.
- **Construction working capital is the hidden tax.** Illinois retainage is capped at 10% until 50% completion and 5% after (P.A. 101-432) [S029]. Add pay-when-paid clauses and 60–90 day receivables. Lien rights need a 90-day notice (60 days for owner-occupied residential) and a 4-month recording deadline [S030]. SBA bond guarantees help, but sureties still underwrite personal credit, which is unknown for this founder [S031]. Owner-paid residential and repair work is far more cash-friendly than GC-subcontract work.
- **Licensing, from primary sources.**
  - Chicago GC licence: no exam. Class E costs $300/yr for projects up to $500k with $1M insurance. It applies even to permit-exempt work [S004].
  - Mason licence: requires a City-exam "supervising mason" (age 21+) and W-2 crews [S005].
  - Electrical licence: covers even low-voltage and alarm wiring, unless the contractor is State-licensed as an alarm contractor [S006].
  - Plumbing: City licence plus IDPH contractor registration ($150/yr) and a licensed plumber of record [S007, S015].
  - Illinois roofing: a qualifying-party exam, a $10k (limited) or $25k (unlimited) bond and insurance. Subcontractors must also be licensed [S001–S003].
  - Expediters: BACP company and individual licences, fingerprints, $300k CGL insurance [S008, S009].
  - Property management: broker licensure under a managing broker, plus written PM agreements [S016].
  - Home inspector: IDFPR licence. The Act was scheduled to sunset on 2027-01-01, status unverified [S018].
  - Wholesaling: a pattern of contract assignments now counts as broker activity [S017].
  - Chicago short-term rentals: $250/yr registration, 4.5% hotel tax plus a 6% surcharge, primary-residence rules in 2–4 unit buildings, and only 3,712 active registrations [S012–S014].

## Strongest candidates (preliminary)
1. **WS10-016 Multifamily unit-turn services.** Recurring, essential B2B demand from property managers. It benefits from the high-rate, keep-renting environment, the roles are delegable, and a Class E licence is enough. Per-turn pricing and the share of turns that managers outsource are unverified.
2. **WS10-010 Chicago masonry/tuckpointing.** Code-driven demand with A-level volume data: 1,779 express tuckpointing permits (median $15,000) and 2,101 exterior-wall plus 663 parapet violations in 2025 [S010, S011]. Census masonry margin is 20%. The supervising-mason requirement can be met by hiring. Insurance cost and skilled labor are the key risks.
3. **WS10-001 Chicago permit expediting.** 6,428 permits listed an expediter in 2025: 47% of renovation/alteration permits and 29% of new-construction permits. The field is fragmented (204 names; the top 10 hold 46%). Capital is low and AI is useful [S010]. The ceiling is local and fees are unknown; the only datapoint is a $350 cap on a 2018 public task order [S009].
4. **WS10-029 Small-landlord property management.** Recurring, resilient, cash-favourable and delegable. It requires a broker licence plus a managing broker, so that path needs verification.
5. **WS10-002 AI-assisted takeoff/estimating for subcontractors.** Asset-light, national and cheap to validate with public bid sets. Software substitution and offshore pricing make it crowded.
6. **WS10-005 GPR concrete scanning / private utility locating.** Low capital, repeat buyers, short sales cycle and no licence identified. Pricing is unverified.
7. **WS10-014 Chicago porch/stair remediation** and **WS10-032 small-landlord code-compliance plans.** Public violation data names motivated buyers (1,199 porch and 863 stair violations). These records work best bundled with #2 as a "Chicago exterior code-compliance" platform.
8. **WS10-007 Commercial firestopping.** A code-mandated specialty with a certification moat (FM 4991 / UL QFC) [S034]. The cash cycle is bad, and union jurisdiction in Chicago is unknown.

## Surprises
- Chicago open data (permits, violations, STR registrations) gives A-level demand counts and a ready lead list. It is most valuable as a lead engine for code-driven trades; as a standalone product (WS10-006) it is weak.
- Chicago's GC licence has no exam (Class E is $300). The real barriers are trade licences, insurance and labor.
- Chicago has only about 3,700 registered STR units, and its low-voltage work requires an electrical licence.

## Looked good but fail or weaken
- **Equipment rental (WS10-022/023).** Herc's dollar utilization is 38.5%: each $1 of fleet earns about $0.39 a year before depreciation. United Rentals spends $4.2B a year on rental capex. A small yard earns thin returns on $100–300k of capital.
- **Prefab/modular (WS10-028).** G1 capital fail into a margin-collapse cycle.
- **Wholesaling (033).** Needs a licence and aggressive seller marketing.
- **Flipping/BRRRR (034).** Capital-heavy, rate-exposed and an investment activity rather than an operating company.
- **STR co-hosting (030).** Rules cap the supply of eligible units.
- **Home inspection (025).** Tied to transactions and capped by the founder's hours.
- **Generic remodeling (017).** No wedge.
- **Specialty distribution (026).** Incumbents earn ~5% operating margins and line cards are uncertain.

## Evidence gaps (largest)
- Private-market prices are missing almost everywhere: expediting fees, per-turn prices, takeoff prices, GPR rates, porch and masonry new-entrant margins, sealcoating rates.
- BACP expediter licence fee and timing.
- Illinois broker and managing-broker requirements, and the exact text of RELA 5-20 exemptions (including whether third-party STR managers fall under the hotel exemption).
- Whether the Roofing Act and Home Inspector Act sunsets (2026/2027) were extended.
- Union jurisdiction (firestopping, commercial cleaning).
- Illinois alarm-contractor licence scope for camera monitoring.
- EPA RRP lead-safe requirements for unit turns.
- Census 2022 margins are proxies that exclude owner draw.
