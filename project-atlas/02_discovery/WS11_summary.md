# WS11 Summary: Agriculture, energy, utilities, environmental and waste

**35 opportunities · 49 sources · validator: 0 errors · researched 2026-11-28**

## Landscape

WS11 splits into four clusters. Their economics differ sharply for a founder with no license who starts from zero.

1. **Agriculture (001–006).** Illinois is huge: 71,123 farms, $17.8B in farm production expenses, 881k cover-crop acres [C, S001]. The trouble is reaching the buyers. Farm inputs and services run through retailers, co-ops and long personal relationships, so an outsider is shut out of seed, inputs and precision-ag dealing. The only ag service an outsider can realistically enter is drone application, and it is getting worse:
   - The FCC Covered List has blocked new foreign drone models since Dec 2025 [C, S005].
   - Competitor count is rising fast [D, S007].
   - The fungicide window lasts about six weeks.
   - Aerial work prices near $13/acre, against $8.39 for ground rigs [C, S006].
2. **Energy and utilities (007–016).** The biggest structural change of 2025–26 is the PJM capacity price, which hit the cap: $329.17/MW-day for 2026/27 and $333.44 for 2027/28 in COMED [A, S020]. That is roughly $121.7k per MW-year, a real cost shock for ComEd commercial and industrial customers. It helps the asset-light, licensable advisory models: brokerage, bill audit, demand response and peak management. Federal incentives moved the other way:
   - 25D and 25C ended 2025-12-31.
   - 30D and 45W ended for purchases after 2025-09-30 [A, S017].
   - 30C (EV chargers) ended 2026-06-30 [A, S018].
   - 179D ended for construction beginning after 2026-06-30 [A, S019].

   As a result, residential solar, LED retrofit, EV charger installation and audit-for-tax-deduction plays all weakened.
3. **Environmental testing and compliance (017–025).** These are mandate-driven and most have obtainable credentials:
   - Stormwater: ILR10 requires inspections every 7 days and after 0.5" of rain, and a free USEPA course qualifies the inspector [A, S040].
   - Asbestos: an IDPH license after training [A, S042].
   - Radon: an IEMA license by course and exam [A, S032].
   - Private utility locating: no license, and a structural gap because JULIE members don't mark private lines [B, S031].
   - Phase I ESAs are gated by the Environmental Professional definition [A, S041].
4. **Waste and recycling (026–035).**
   - **Incumbents are very profitable but asset-heavy.** WM's operating margin was 17.1% and RSG's 19.9% (capex 11.4% of revenue) [A, S008/S009]. Adjusted EBITDA margins are 30–32% [B]. Casella earns about 23% adjusted EBITDA but only 0.4% net income [B, S010]. These are incumbent economics; new-entrant economics are unverified.
   - **Disposal is concentrated.** The Chicago metro has only 4 landfills, with about 12.4 years of life left [A, S037]. A new transfer station needs local siting approval plus IEPA permits [A, S036].
   - **Commodity streams are volatile.** UCO, scrap and OCC look weak: biofuel use of yellow grease fell about 30–45% in 2025 [C, S046], and Darling's operating margin went from 15.8% to 4.5% [A, S016].
   - **Asset-light waste brokerage has thin margins.** Quest Resource runs a 17% gross margin and operating losses [A, S013].

## Strongest candidates (all still E/D on unit economics)

1. **WS11-021 Construction stormwater inspections (adjusted score 5.62).** Weekly recurring work is required by law, and the qualifying credential is a free USEPA course. Capital is under $15k. Every covered site files a public NOI, which doubles as a prospect list. The work runs on checklists, so it delegates well and suits AI-drafted reporting. Adding a BMP-maintenance crew raises revenue per site. Gaps: pricing per inspection and the count of active NOIs.
2. **WS11-007 Commercial electricity brokerage (5.50), bundled with WS11-010 bill audit (5.33) and WS11-008 demand-response origination (5.30).** These are asset-light with recurring residuals, and the capacity shock gives a timely reason to pitch.
   - The ICC ABC license has no fee, needs a $5,000 bond and takes 90–180 days to review [A, S023/S024].
   - The binding constraint is Part 454's managerial and technical experience requirement (the years required were not obtained). The founder may need a hire or partner.
   - Supplier commission rates are MISSING.
   - The market is crowded and telemarketing-heavy. The wedge is analytics plus stacking demand-response revenue and peak-load (PLC) management on top.
3. **WS11-026 Roll-off dumpsters (5.49).** Buyers search for this and switch providers easily, and a multi-truck small operator is a proven model. It is capital-heavy (about $60k–$140k, G1 warning). The margin is squeezed by tipping fees charged by vertically integrated competitors. Tipping fees and municipal hauler licensing are MISSING.
4. **WS11-023 Private utility locating (5.48).** The demand gap is documented by JULIE itself, there is no license, equipment costs about $20k–$40k per technician, and contractors have a frequent, urgent need. Pricing and competitor density are unverified.
5. **WS11-017 Cooling-tower and boiler water treatment (5.45).** This is a recurring consumable sold with service: mandatory, sticky and route-based, with no license. The founder's technical background helps. The barriers are credibility (risk-averse buyers) and supplier access to private-label chemistry. No pricing evidence was obtained, so it is the top priority for evidence work.
6. **Runners-up:**
   - **Portable sanitation (WS11-027):** the IDPH license is $250 plus $50 per technician [A, S028/S029].
   - **Scrap tire hauling (WS11-030):** mandated flow and simple IEPA vehicle registration [A, S034].
   - **Asbestos inspection (WS11-020) and radon (WS11-019):** both licensable within weeks to months. They are crowded (744 licensed asbestos inspectors at 397 firms [A, S044]) but could combine into one environmental-testing platform.

## Surprises

- The **capacity-price spike** is the most important 2025–26 Illinois energy fact for small advisory businesses. COMED cleared more demand response than any other zone (1,515.9 MW). The cap and floor apply only through 2027/28, so pricing after that is uncertain.
- **The FCC Covered List** materially raises equipment risk for any drone business (spraying or inspection) built on Chinese platforms.
- **Asset-light waste brokerage is not a good business** on the public-comp evidence: about 17% gross margin and losses. The contingency-audit variant is better.
- **Illinois certifies EV charger maintenance and repair**, not just installation (Part 469). That adds friction for a founder without an electrician.

## Looked good but fails

- **Drone spraying:** seasonality, FCC equipment risk and rising competition (hold).
- **Residential solar after 25D, and community-solar subscriber acquisition:** enforcement-heavy (21 warnings and 11 suspensions in program year 2025–26 [B, S045]).
- **LED retrofit:** a saturating market.
- **UCO, scrap and OCC brokerage:** commodity exposure.
- **Food-scrap hauling:** no mandate.
- **Ag inputs:** thin margins and grower credit.
- **Seed dealing:** relationship-locked.
- **Phase I ESAs:** require an Environmental Professional (EP) hire and lender-panel approval.
- **Drone inspection:** commoditized.
