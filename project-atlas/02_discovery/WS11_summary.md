# WS11 Summary: Agriculture, energy, utilities, environmental and waste

**35 opportunities · 49 sources · validator 0 errors · researched 2026-11-28**

## Landscape

Four clusters with sharply different economics for a no-license founder starting from zero.

1. **Agriculture (001–006).** Illinois is large (71,123 farms; $17.8B farm production expenses; 881k cover-crop acres [C, S001]), but access is the problem: inputs and services run through retailers, co-ops and long relationships, shutting an outsider out of seed, inputs and precision-ag dealing. Drone application is the only realistic entry and it is deteriorating: FCC Covered List blocks new foreign drone models since Dec 2025 [C, S005]; competitor count rising [D, S007]; ~6-week fungicide window; aerial pricing ~$13/acre vs $8.39 ground [C, S006].
2. **Energy & utilities (007–016).** The key 2025–26 change: PJM capacity cleared at the cap — $329.17/MW-day (2026/27) and $333.44 (2027/28, incl. COMED) [A, S020], ~$121.7k per MW-year. This favors asset-light, licensable advisory models (brokerage, bill audit, demand response, peak management). Federal incentives moved the other way: 25D/25C ended 2025-12-31; 30D/45W ended for purchases after 2025-09-30 [A, S017]; 30C ended 2026-06-30 [A, S018]; 179D ended for construction beginning after 2026-06-30 [A, S019]. Residential solar, LED retrofit, EV-charger install and tax-deduction audit plays all weakened.
3. **Environmental testing & compliance (017–025).** Mandate-driven, mostly obtainable credentials: ILR10 stormwater inspections every 7 days and after 0.5" rain, qualified via a free USEPA course [A, S040]; IDPH asbestos license after training [A, S042]; IEMA radon license via course + exam [A, S032]; private utility locating needs no license and fills a gap JULIE leaves (members don't mark private lines) [B, S031]. Phase I ESAs are gated by the Environmental Professional definition [A, S041].
4. **Waste & recycling (026–035).** Incumbents are profitable but asset-heavy: operating margin WM 17.1%, RSG 19.9% (capex 11.4% of revenue) [A, S008/S009]; adjusted EBITDA 30–32% [B]; Casella ~23% adjusted EBITDA but 0.4% net income [B, S010]. These are incumbent, not new-entrant, economics. Disposal is concentrated: Chicago metro has 4 landfills with ~12.4 years of life [A, S037], and new transfer stations need local siting + IEPA permits [A, S036]. Commodity streams look weak (yellow-grease biofuel use fell ~30–45% in 2025 [C, S046]; Darling operating margin 15.8%→4.5% [A, S016]). Asset-light waste brokerage is thin (Quest Resource: 17% gross margin, operating losses [A, S013]).

## Strongest candidates (unit economics still E/D)

1. **WS11-021 Construction stormwater inspections (adj. 5.62).** Legally required weekly recurring work; free qualifying credential; <$15k capital; public NOI filings double as a prospect list; checklist work delegates and suits AI-drafted reports; BMP-maintenance crews add revenue. Gaps: per-inspection pricing, active-NOI counts.
2. **WS11-007 Electricity brokerage (5.50) bundled with WS11-010 bill audit (5.33) and WS11-008 demand-response origination (5.30).** Asset-light recurring residuals with a timely capacity-shock pitch. ICC ABC license: no fee, $5,000 bond, 90–180-day review [A, S023/S024]. Binding constraint: Part 454 managerial/technical experience (years not obtained) — may need a hire/partner. Commission rates MISSING. Crowded, telemarketing-heavy; wedge = analytics + DR and PLC stacking.
3. **WS11-026 Roll-off dumpsters (5.49).** Buyers search and switch; multi-truck small operators are a proven model. Capital-heavy (~$60k–$140k, G1 warning); tipping fees set by integrated competitors squeeze margin. Tipping fees and municipal hauler licensing MISSING.
4. **WS11-023 Private utility locating (5.48).** Gap documented by JULIE; no license; ~$20k–$40k equipment per technician; frequent urgent contractor need. Pricing and competitor density unverified.
5. **WS11-017 Cooling-tower/boiler water treatment (5.45).** Recurring consumable + service, sticky, route-based, no license; technical background helps. Barriers: credibility with risk-averse buyers, private-label chemistry access. No pricing evidence — top evidence priority.
6. **Runners-up:** portable sanitation WS11-027 (IDPH license $250 + $50/technician [A, S028/S029]); scrap-tire hauling WS11-030 (mandated flow, simple IEPA registration [A, S034]); asbestos WS11-020 and radon WS11-019 (licensable in weeks–months; crowded — 744 licensed asbestos inspectors at 397 firms [A, S044]; could combine into one environmental-testing platform).

## Surprises

- The capacity-price spike is the most important 2025–26 Illinois energy fact for small advisory businesses; COMED cleared the most DR of any zone (1,515.9 MW). Cap/floor applies only through 2027/28, so later pricing is uncertain.
- The FCC Covered List materially raises equipment risk for any Chinese-platform drone business (spraying or inspection).
- Asset-light waste brokerage is not a good business on public-comp evidence; the contingency-audit variant is better.
- Illinois certifies EV charger maintenance/repair, not just installs (Part 469) — friction without an electrician.

## Looked good but fails

Drone spraying (seasonality, FCC risk, competition — hold); residential solar post-25D; community-solar subscriber acquisition (enforcement-heavy: 21 warnings, 11 suspensions in PY2025–26 [B, S045]); LED retrofit (saturating); UCO/scrap/OCC brokerage (commodity exposure); food-scrap hauling (no mandate); ag inputs (thin margins, grower credit); seed dealing (relationship-locked); Phase I ESAs (EP hire + lender panels); drone inspection (commoditized).
