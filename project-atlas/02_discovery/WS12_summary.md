# WS12 — Human capital & professional/business services: discovery summary

34 records (WS12-001 to 034) and 28 sources, as of 2026-11-28. All composite scores are harsh. The best evidence-adjusted score is about 5.5 out of 10, and the best-evidenced record is WS12-014 (level C).

## Landscape

**1. Staffing is a game of payroll float and SG&A.** A high gross margin does not mean the business makes money.
- Robert Half (FY2025): contract talent gross margin 39.0% but operating margin −0.8%. Permanent placement gross margin 99.8% but operating margin only 2.9%. The firm as a whole earned 1.4% [B, S007].
- Kforce: Flex gross margin 25.8%, SG&A 23.0%, operating margin 3.8%. Technology bill rate about $89/hr; finance and accounting about $53/hr [A, S009].
- TrueBlue (light industrial): gross margin 22.8%, SG&A 23.0%, adjusted EBITDA 0.7%. PeopleReady made a $6.5M segment profit on $884M of revenue [B, S010].
- BGSF (property-management staffing): about 35–36% gross margin, yet a segment operating loss [B, S011].
- A new entrant will not match these gross margins. It can only beat them by running with very little overhead.

**2. Illinois law (DTLSA, 820 ILCS 175).** I checked this against primary sources. Here is where it stands:
- **Equal pay** now starts after more than 720 hours at the same client within 12 months, not after 90 days. PA 103-1030 made this change, with the clock running from April 1, 2024. Agencies may use the BLS median wage as the comparator. After 4,160 hours in 48 months, pay must reach the 75th percentile [C, S003].
- **Equal benefits** also start at 720 hours; the agency may pay an hourly cash equivalent instead. The March 2024 injunction covered the original clause. On **May 22, 2025, the N.D. Ill. denied an injunction against the amended clause**, so it can be enforced [A, S001]. I could not confirm any appellate ruling after that.
- **Section 67 "interested party" suits** were held unconstitutional by the Cook County circuit court on Mar 6 and May 11, 2026, and are on appeal to the Illinois Supreme Court. Meanwhile IDOL is not issuing right-to-sue notices [A, S004; C, S005].
- **Fixed costs and training:** annual registration is $3,000 per agency plus $750 per branch, with a surety bond [A, S004]. Agencies must also provide free, paid safety training once a year for each client [A, S006].
- **Impact:** the extra cost falls on long assignments, which are exactly the "permatemp" margin pool. Clients face joint exposure, so expect them to cap assignments near 720 hours, convert workers, or demand higher markups. For a new light-industrial agency this is decisive: WS12-001 is rejected.
- Agencies placing only clerical or professional workers may be exempt from registration [A, S004]. That pushes a new entrant toward professional niches.

**3. Direct-hire recruiting** needs an IDOL Private Employment Agency license. The fee is $250–500 a year plus a $5,000 bond, and the agency's forms must be approved [A, S015]. Fees are "generally a percentage of the new employee's annual compensation" [A, S008]. Kforce records direct-hire revenue net of a fallout reserve, with a fallout window of 90 days or less [A, S009]. Realized collections therefore run below billed fees; I assume 80–90% [E].

**4. Licensing walls in legal support** (all checked against primary sources):
- Process serving as a business effectively needs a private-detective license or agency, which requires 3 years of investigative experience [A, S018–S020].
- Court reporting requires an individual CSR license [A, S021], and BLS projects 0% growth, citing AI transcription [C, S022].
- ASL interpreters need individual Illinois licenses [A, S028].
- The private-detective licensing exemption for background screeners covers only creditworthiness work, so criminal-record screening is legally unresolved [A, S020].
- The Collection Agency Act exemption is narrow. It covers only notices sent in the creditor's name [A, S027].

**5. WOTC has lapsed** for hires after Dec 31, 2025 (IRS page reviewed Jul 2026) [A, S025]. WS12-019 is rejected.

## Strongest candidates

1. **WS12-014 Contingency direct-hire recruiting in a technical niche** (advance).
   - Needs almost no capital, has no payroll float, and ROIC above 500% once the desk is producing.
   - The founder's research and tech background lets him assess candidates that generalist recruiters can't.
   - Year-3 profit estimate $80–350k [E].
   - Weak points: highly cyclical (Robert Half placements fell 13.6% in 2025 [A]) and dependent on the founder.
2. **WS12-022 Vertical bookkeeping and monthly close** (advance, top adjusted score).
   - Recurring, often prepaid, easy to delegate.
   - AI cuts the cost of categorization and reconciliation durably, at ordinary prices.
   - No license needed (not checked: CPA attest limits).
   - Evidence is E; there is no primary pricing or churn data.
3. **WS12-012 Offshore dedicated remote staff for one SMB vertical** (advance).
   - Billed by seat, monthly in advance, with gross margins of 35–55% [E].
   - Workers sit outside Illinois worksites, so no DTLSA registration.
   - The main risk is AI agents replacing the admin seats themselves.
4. **WS12-016 Embedded recruiter / RPO subscription** (advance).
   - A recurring, easier-to-delegate version of #1. Pairs naturally with it as one firm.
5. **WS12-006 Scientific, lab, quality and engineering contract staffing** (advance).
   - The best staffing fit for this founder: hands-on regulated work with low AI substitution, plus a technical-credibility wedge.
   - Payroll float and the unclear DTLSA status of lab technicians need work.

Also worth watching (hold):
- WS12-005 IT contract staffing: real scale path, but float, VMS gating and AI eroding demand for developers.
- WS12-024 records retrieval for PI law firms.
- WS12-021 Illinois HR-compliance subscription.
- WS12-007 property-management staffing.

The likely best structure is **one technical-niche talent firm**: start with a contingency desk (014), add a retainer/RPO product (016), and later run contract staffing in the same niche (006/005) funded by retained earnings.

## Surprises

- High gross margin is not profit. BGSF earns 35% gross margin and still loses money; Robert Half's 99.8% permanent-placement gross margin produces a 2.9% operating margin. In recruiting, the recruiter's pay is the cost of goods.
- Illinois's equal-benefits rule survived its second court challenge (May 2025). Many summaries still describe it as "enjoined" based on the 2024 ruling.
- The Section 67 private-suit mechanism, the biggest litigation threat to staffing agencies, was struck down at the trial level in 2026.

## Looks good but fails

- **Light-industrial staffing:** about 0.7% EBITDA at the incumbent, payroll float, WC losses, and Illinois equal pay and benefits.
- **On-demand shift app:** venture-scale burn.
- **Payrolling/EOR:** 4–10% gross margin carried on full payroll float.
- **AI substitution has largely arrived:** transcription, translation, answering services, outsourced SDR work, volume hourly screening, grant drafting.
- **Process serving and court reporting:** licensing walls plus low ceilings.
- **Retained executive search:** good economics but no access without a track record.
- **WOTC services:** the credit has lapsed.

## AI substitution, judged honestly

- **High (demand at risk):** transcription, translation, answering services, SDR work, hourly screening, clerical and accounting temps (Robert Half lists AI as a risk factor [A, S008]; Kforce finance and accounting revenue fell 12.3% [A]), and leasing-agent and front-office staffing.
- **Moderate:** IT contract developers, bookkeeping (both a tool and a threat), background screening, interpreting (phone and video).
- **Low:** skilled trades, lab bench work, on-site ASL interpreting, final candidate persuasion in recruiting.
