# WS12 — Human capital & professional/business services: discovery summary

34 records, 28 sources (2026-11-28). Harsh scoring: best evidence-adjusted composite ≈5.5; best-evidenced record WS12-014 (C).

## Landscape (key facts)

**Staffing = payroll float + SG&A; gross margin ≠ profit.** FY2025 incumbent economics:
- Robert Half: contract GM 39.0% but contract operating margin −0.8%; perm GM 99.8%, operating margin 2.9%; total 1.4% [B, S007].
- Kforce: Flex GM 25.8%, SG&A 23.0%, operating margin 3.8%; Tech bill rate ~$89/hr, F&A ~$53/hr [A, S009].
- TrueBlue (light industrial): GM 22.8%, SG&A 23.0%, adj. EBITDA 0.7%; PeopleReady $6.5M profit on $884M [B, S010].
- BGSF property-management staffing: GM ~35–36% yet segment operating loss [B, S011].
New entrants will not beat these gross margins; only lean overhead differs.

**Illinois DTLSA (820 ILCS 175), verified status:**
- Equal pay now triggers after >720 hours at one client in 12 months (PA 103-1030; clock from Apr 1, 2024), with BLS-median option and 75th percentile after 4,160 hours [C, S003].
- Equal benefits (or hourly cash equivalent) after 720 hours: the 2024 injunction covered the old clause; **on May 22, 2025 N.D. Ill. denied an injunction of the amended clause — enforceable** [A, S001]. Later appellate history not found.
- Section 67 "interested party" suits held unconstitutional by Cook County circuit court (Mar 6 and May 11, 2026), on appeal; IDOL not issuing right-to-sue notices [A, S004; C, S005].
- Registration $3,000/yr + $750/branch + bond [A, S004]; annual per-client paid safety training [A, S006].
- Impact: the cost lands on long ("permatemp") assignments, the margin pool, plus joint client liability → expect 720-hour caps, conversions or higher markups. Decisive against a new light-industrial agency (WS12-001 rejected). Clerical/professional-only agencies may be exempt from registration [A, S004], pushing entrants toward professional niches.

**Direct hire** needs an IDOL Private Employment Agency license: $250–500/yr, $5,000 bond, approved forms [A, S015]. Fees are a percentage of annual compensation [A, S008]; Kforce books direct hire net of a ≤90-day fallout reserve [A, S009]. Assume realized collection ~80–90% of billed [E].

**Licensing walls (primary-verified):**
- Process serving as a business effectively needs PI licensure (3-yr experience) [A, S018–S020].
- Court reporting requires individual CSR licenses [A, S021]; BLS projects 0% growth citing AI [C, S022].
- ASL interpreters need individual licenses [A, S028].
- The PI-Act exemption for screeners covers only creditworthiness, so criminal-record screening is unresolved [A, S020].
- Collection Agency Act exemption is narrow (creditor-name notices only) [A, S027].

**WOTC lapsed** for hires after Dec 31, 2025 (IRS, reviewed Jul 2026) [A, S025] → WS12-019 rejected.

## Strongest candidates

1. **WS12-014 Technical-niche contingency recruiting** (advance): near-zero capital, no float, ROIC >500% once producing; founder can technically assess research/data/engineering candidates; yr-3 $80–350k [E]. Weaknesses: cyclical (RH placements −13.6% in 2025 [A]), founder-dependent.
2. **WS12-022 Vertical bookkeeping/monthly close** (advance; top adjusted score): recurring, often prepaid, delegable, durable AI cost leverage; no license (CPA attest limits unverified). Evidence E.
3. **WS12-012 Offshore dedicated remote staff for one SMB vertical** (advance): seat-based, billed in advance, GM 35–55% [E], no DTLSA exposure; key risk is AI agents displacing admin seats.
4. **WS12-016 Embedded recruiter/RPO subscription** (advance): recurring, delegable complement to #1.
5. **WS12-006 Scientific/lab/quality/engineering contract staffing** (advance): best staffing fit; hands-on regulated work with low AI substitution plus a credibility wedge; float and lab-tech DTLSA status unresolved.

Holds: WS12-005 IT contract staffing (scale path but float, VMS gating, AI eroding developer demand), WS12-024 PI-firm records retrieval, WS12-021 IL HR-compliance subscription, WS12-007 property-management staffing.
Likely best structure: **one technical-niche talent firm**: contingency desk (014) → retainer/RPO (016) → contract staffing in the same niche (006/005), funded from earnings.

## Surprises

- GM is not profit: BGSF's 35% GM loses money; RH's 99.8% perm GM yields 2.9% operating margin. In recruiting, recruiter pay is the COGS.
- Illinois equal benefits survived the second challenge (May 2025); many summaries still call it "enjoined."
- Section 67, the biggest litigation threat to agencies, was struck at trial level in 2026.

## Looks good but fails

- Light-industrial staffing: ~0.7% EBITDA, float, WC, IL equal pay/benefits.
- On-demand shift app: venture burn.
- Payrolling/EOR: 4–10% GM on full float.
- Already AI-substituted: transcription, translation, answering services, outsourced SDR, volume hourly screening, grant drafting.
- Process serving/court reporting: licensing walls, low ceilings.
- Retained search: no access without track record.
- WOTC: lapsed.

## AI substitution (honest)

- **High:** transcription, translation, answering, SDR, hourly screening, clerical/accounting temps (RH lists AI as a risk factor [A, S008]; Kforce F&A −12.3% [A]), leasing-agent and front-office staffing.
- **Moderate:** IT contract developers, bookkeeping (tool and threat), background screening, phone/video interpreting.
- **Low:** skilled trades, lab bench work, on-site ASL, final candidate persuasion.
