# WS13 Summary: Underexplored "quiet money" niches

**Records:** 40 opportunities (8 advance, 21 hold, 11 reject) · **Sources:** 37 · **Evidence mix:** B 1, C 4, D 9, E 26 · Access date 2026-11-28.

## Method and limitations (read first)
- **Structural screen (C):** I downloaded Census SUSB 2022 (6-digit NAICS by enterprise receipts size, plus state tables). For every industry I computed: the number of firms with $1–15M in receipts, the share of receipts held by $100M+ enterprises (a consolidation measure), payroll as a share of receipts, and the Illinois firm count. 227 industries have at least 120 firms in the $1–15M band and less than 40% of receipts at $100M+ firms. Example: mat/uniform laundry (812332) has 86% of receipts at $100M+ firms (a plant-scale game); repair codes (811490 ~6%, 811420 0%) are fragmented but tiny.
- **Incumbent economics (A):** 10-K filings from Cintas, UniFirst, Ecolab, WillScot, McGrath, Accel, SCI, Carriage, Matthews and Rollins.
- **Search budget:** The web-search budget for this turn was used up before I started. All research was direct fetches of known primary sources, plus sitemaps used to find pages.
- **Blocked sources:** ilga.gov (Illinois statutes), ilsos.gov, eCFR, BLS, APHIS, OSHA and BizBuySell were unreachable or refused access. As a result:
  - There are **no D-level business-for-sale earnings proxies**.
  - Several Illinois licensing questions remain MISSING: whether pet crematories need a license, who may legally remove decedents, Secretary of State interlock rules, and apostille fees.
  - Most ticket sizes are analyst estimates (E).

## Landscape
The quiet money in this space comes from three structures:
1. **Consumable or equipment-as-a-service routes** sold to fragmented small buyers: dishmachine chemicals, first aid, ice, water and hygiene. Large incumbents earn 23–25% operating margins on these.
   - Cintas First Aid & Safety: $1.39B revenue, 57.7% gross margin, 25.4% operating margin [A].
   - Ecolab Institutional: 22.8% operating margin [A].
   - The route model is open to a newcomer because capital per account is small and buyers can switch easily.
2. **Mandated or non-discretionary services in fragmented markets** that an acquirer is actively rolling up:
   - private utility locating (GPRS, 13 acquisitions)
   - lake and pond management (a Rentokil-family brand)
   - pet and human cremation (the cremation rate was 62.8% in 2025 and is projected at 69.1% for 2030) [C]
   - wildlife control (Rollins)
3. **Scarce permitted real estate,** such as truck parking. Illinois and Chicago are among the most-cited truck-parking shortage areas in the federal Jason's Law survey [C].

## Strongest candidates
1. **WS13-004, first-aid and safety van route** (adjusted score 6.10; C). A-level incumbent margins and minimal capital (under $25k). It is the natural base of a multi-product route that can add hygiene (005), water (003) and AED programs. Cintas's pending acquisition of UniFirst [A] may push dissatisfied accounts to switch. The wedge is service quality, which is easily copied.
2. **WS13-030, low-cost direct cremation** (5.71; D). The structural shift to cremation and customers paying before service give an excellent cash cycle. A licensed funeral director must be employed. Illinois's crematory oversight law HB 4695 takes effect in 2027 and favors careful operators.
3. **WS13-028, pet cremation for vet clinics** (5.65; D). Non-discretionary, growing and recurring business with clinics. The industry is described as "largely self-regulated" [C]. It can start asset-light using another firm's crematory, then add its own cremation unit. Plausibly $1M/yr. The key gap is Illinois pet-crematory law.
4. **WS13-001, dishmachine lease plus chemical route** (5.63; D). A privately run operator, Auto-Chlor, claims about 140 branches and over 100k customers. Chemicals are a weekly consumable and the visit cadence is fixed. Ice machines (002) and kitchen wear parts (009) can be bundled onto the same visits.
5. **WS13-014, private utility locating and GPR concrete scanning** (5.61; D). The national leader GPRS was founder-started in 2001 with a rented scanner. It now claims 400+ project managers across 54 metros and is buying firms. Buyers are frequent, short-cycle contractors. It fits a technical founder.
6. **WS13-018, multifamily doorstep trash pickup** (5.61; E). Near-zero capital and easy to delegate. Per-unit pricing is unverified.
7. **WS13-016, lake and pond management** (5.54; D). The license is exam-based ($300 for 3 years, with an aquatic category) [A]. Annual contracts, with a large acquirer active in the market.
8. **WS13-021, Chicago truck-parking yard** (5.44; D). Demand is documented and rent is prepaid. Zoning is both the main risk and the moat.

Honorable mentions (all E): 038 wildlife control, 015 K-12 Chromebook repair (founder fit), 012 generator compliance, 013 rack inspection.

## Surprises
- Route businesses selling to restaurants and workplaces have A-level evidence of the high margins that "boring" consumables earn.
- The apostille business is **shrinking structurally**. China (2023), Canada (2024), Viet Nam (2026) and Thailand (2027) joined the Convention [A].
- Accel paid about **$0.84M per Illinois gaming location** when it acquired 16 locations for $13.5M [A]. Illinois VGT routes are genuine quiet money, but only for incumbents.
- WillScot's portable-storage utilization fell from 60% to 51.5% in 2025 [A]. Container rental is more cyclical than its reputation.

## Looked good but fail
- **VGT operator (026):** needs gaming board licensing and more than $100k before first revenue (G1), locations are already under contract, and there are gambling-harm ethics.
- **Ignition interlock service center (025):** the best-evidenced record (B), but calibrations pay about $20 each, so the ceiling is tiny.
- **Mat rental without a plant (006):** an extreme-concentration industry.
- **Lumper services (023):** buyers are locked into incumbents.
- **Air and vacuum machines (024):** the dominant incumbent installs machines free.
- **ATMs (027):** cash use is in secular decline.
- **Donation bins (040):** commodity prices plus the risk of implying a charity.
- **Flags (039), band rentals (037) and beer-line cleaning (010):** ceilings too low.

## Legality and ethics judgments
- **Bail-adjacent work:** Illinois has long barred commercial bail bonding, and the Pretrial Fairness Act ended cash bail. I could not re-verify this from the statute this session, but the space is effectively closed, so I recorded nothing.
- **Unlicensed "skill"/sweepstakes machines, private-lot booting and relocation towing, consumer fireworks:** excluded. They are either legally contested, predatory, or restricted in Illinois.
- **Direct cremation and decedent removal:** must go through licensed funeral directors. IDFPR warns against unlicensed "curbstone" funeral directing [A].

## Biggest evidence gaps
1. Small-account pricing for dishmachine/chemical, first-aid, hygiene and doorstep-trash routes. No price lists are public, so field validation is needed.
2. Illinois statutes: pet crematories, decedent removal by unlicensed staff, BAIID service centers, nuisance-wildlife permits, and tied-house rules on beer-line cleaning.
3. Code text for NFPA 110 fuel and load-bank testing, and for the ANSI/RMI rack-inspection standard.
4. Business-for-sale earnings proxies for every niche (BizBuySell blocked).
5. Chicagoland truck-parking rates and zoning pathways.
