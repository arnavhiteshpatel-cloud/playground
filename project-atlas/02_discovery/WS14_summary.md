# WS14 — Mechanism-first discovery: summary

**Records:** 35 opportunities (6 advance, 16 hold, 13 reject) · **Sources:** 29 · Access date 2026-11-28.
**Evidence mix:** A 3, B 1, C 1, D 7, E 23. The economics of most records are weakly evidenced (see "Evidence gaps").

## Landscape
I sorted the records by how each business captures value, not by industry:

- **Contingency recovery and audit (17 records):** parcel, freight, telecom/SaaS, sales/use tax, property tax, unclaimed property, AP recovery, retailer deductions, Amazon reimbursements, settlement claims, lease CAM, IEEPA refunds, duty drawback, multi-category expense reduction, WOTC, R&D credits, medical bills, public adjusting.
- **Aggregation (4):** non-healthcare GPO, vet/dental buying group, Illinois energy brokerage, print brokerage.
- **Consignment and waste-to-value (6):** estate sales, rack-jobbing, lab decommissioning, office-furniture decommissioning, IT asset disposition (ITAD), dental scrap.
- **Distressed or surplus assets (2):** government surplus, tax liens.
- **Rental fleets and inventory availability (2):** surge climate/drying rental, test-and-measurement rental.
- **Time-sensitive work (2):** passport expediting, ticket brokerage.
- **Marketplace supply side (1):** peer-to-peer car-sharing host.

The pattern across the recovery cluster is the same every time:
- Fees are high, typically 15–30% or more of recovered money [D].
- Capital needs are close to zero.
- The hard parts are credibility, getting the client's data, and waiting months to get paid.
- Regulation, not competition, decides who may practise. Two of the biggest pools are legally closed to this founder: Cook County property-tax appeals and Illinois unclaimed property.

## Strongest candidates (5–8)
1. **WS14-001 Parcel audit plus carrier contract renegotiation on gain-share.**
   - Asset-light, recurring and data-heavy, which fits the founder's background.
   - Operators charge "a share of actual monthly savings" [D, S019].
   - The old late-delivery refund niche is mostly gone: UPS restored guarantees only on next-day air [C, S016], and ground services have none [D, S017]. Value now comes from renegotiating contracts, not chasing refunds.
2. **WS14-002 LTL/truckload freight audit and claims.** This is the natural companion to 001.
   - Unaudited programs lose 3–5% of freight spend to billing errors.
   - Audit firms charge 15–25% of recoveries or a per-invoice fee [D, S021].
   - Combined, 001 and 002 make one "transportation cost recovery" firm.
3. **WS14-004 Sales/use tax reverse audit.**
   - The key legal point is verified: Illinois IL-2848 (R-07/26) accepts an "other" (non-attorney, non-CPA) representative. Only hearings and the Tax Tribunal require an attorney [A, S008].
   - Engagements are high-ticket. CPA-firm marketing claims manufacturers average $75k+ a year in overpaid tax [D, S009].
   - The weak point is cash: payment can lag 6–18 months [E].
4. **WS14-008 Retailer and distributor deduction recovery for emerging consumer-packaged-goods (CPG) brands.**
   - Buyers are identifiable, the pain recurs with every shipment, and AI cuts the cost of classifying deductions and building dispute packets.
   - Retailers' own recovery auditors, such as PRGX's retail profit recovery, generate many of the claims against suppliers [D, S026]. This service is the supplier-side counterpart.
   - Fee levels are unverified [E].
5. **WS14-013 Duty drawback with a licensed customs-broker partner.**
   - Tariff policy has created large refundable duty pools.
   - Providers charge 15–30% contingency and either file directly or hand off to licensed brokers [D, S015]. The broker hand-off is the legal workaround for this founder.
   - The risk is tariff policy reversing. Score held down to resilience 4.
6. **WS14-019 Niche non-healthcare GPO.** This is the best-evidenced mechanism in the stream.
   - Vendor admin fees average 1–2% of purchase volume, 3% is the most common rate, and about 70% is passed back to members [B, S023, GAO].
   - Premier's net admin fees fell 11% as the share returned to members rose. Premier sold its non-healthcare contracts to OMNIA [A, S024].
   - Economics need roughly $20M+ of member purchasing [E]. Hold; test via a national-GPO affiliate.
7. **WS14-027 ITAD** and **WS14-026 office-furniture decommissioning.** Both are waste-to-value businesses where the client pays a fee and the operator also earns on resale.
   - Green Standards runs a paid decommissioning model: 2,000+ projects, typically around 100k sq ft [D, S027]. That leaves an apparent gap for mid-size Midwest jobs.

## Surprises
- **IEEPA tariffs were struck down on 20 February 2026.** About $166B in refunds is flowing through CBP's new CAPE system, with 126k claims by May 2026 [C, S011].
  - A one-time window already crowded by brokers and law firms [C, S012, S013]. Reject.
  - Its durable cousin is duty drawback (WS14-013).
- **WOTC is lapsed.** The IRS page, updated July 2026, still shows authorization only through 31 December 2025 [A, S025].
- **Illinois unclaimed-property finders face tight rules:** a Treasurer license, a fidelity bond of up to $100k, a $500 application fee, and a fee cap of 10% [A, S004, S005].
- **The Cook County Board of Review is attorney-only.** "Only licensed attorneys can practice before the Board of Review" [A, S001]. A pending bill, HB4931, would allow non-attorney representatives for business entities [A, S003]. Worth watching.

## Looked good but fail
- **Property-tax appeals (005):** fails the license gate (G2 FAIL); fee-splitting with lawyers is barred.
- **Unclaimed property (006):** the 10% cap makes it uneconomic.
- **IEEPA refunds (012):** one-time window.
- **WOTC (015):** lapsed.
- **Amazon reimbursements (009):** depend entirely on Amazon's platform rules.
- **Rack-jobbing consignment (024):** the founder carries the retailer's inventory risk.
- **Tax liens (030):** returns are competed away, and it is not an operating business.
- **Tickets, car-sharing, passports, dental scrap, print:** low ceilings, platform dependence or decline.
- **Settlement claims filing (010):** real information asymmetry, but payouts take years.
