# WS18 — Local & Consumer Services: Discovery Summary

**Records:** 35 opportunities (`WS18_opportunities.jsonl`), 23 sources (`WS18_sources.jsonl`). Researched 2026-11-28.

## Landscape
Household services are easy to enter, pay at or before the job, and need little capital, so cash conversion and ROIC look good on paper. The binding limits are the **labor supply** (caregivers, cleaners, technicians), **local density**, and **owner margins** that are thinner than franchise revenue figures suggest. FDD Item 19 data shows the pattern clearly:

- **Handyman.** Ace Handyman single-territory units took in $250k–$775k of revenue in 2025 but kept only **2–11% as owner discretionary income**. Top quartile: $49.8k on $775k. Multi-territory groups reached 15–19% [B, WS18-S009].
- **Junk removal.** Junk King franchisees earn a **56.3% gross profit** after paying 13% in franchise and call-centre fees. The average unit sells $554k (median $436k) at $399 per job [B, WS18-S001].
- **Residential cleaning.** MaidPro units average $431k (median $380k), at about $178 per job [B, WS18-S004]. 91% of Molly Maid cleans are for recurring customers [B, WS18-S002].
- **Lawn fertilization.** Mature Weed Man units average **$2.04M** (median $1.69M) [B, WS18-S007].
- **Senior home care.** Home Instead units average **$2.61M** (median $2.26M) at about $33 per client-hour [B, WS18-S005]. Younger A Place At Home outlets keep **42–64% gross profit after caregiver labor**. Their average revenue climbs from $510k (months 12–24) to $1.52M (months 37–48) [B, WS18-S006].

Removing royalties (typically 5–8%, plus 1–5% brand and call-centre fees) improves an independent operator's margin by roughly that amount. An independent then has to replace the brand, lead flow and software out of its own pocket, so I assumed only part of the savings reaches profit.

## Strongest candidates (evidence-adjusted ranking)
1. **WS18-002 Non-medical home care agency (IDPH Home Services licence).** Best ceiling and delegation in the workstream. Demand is driven by need and demographics: aide jobs are projected to grow 18% over 2025–35 [C, WS18-S021]. There is a public payer floor: Illinois CCP pays $30.80/hr from 1/1/2026 [A, WS18-S020]. The ramp is documented [B]. Risks are caregiver supply, payroll float and licensing lead time.
2. **WS18-004 Lawn fertilization subscription route.** Recurring and auto-renewing, with density building over time. The IDOA licence is exam-based ($300 applicator, $240 operator, 3 years) [A, WS18-S019]. Mature units are large [B]. Chicago's seasonality pushes toward bundling with mosquito control (006) and holiday lighting (027).
3. **WS18-001 Recurring residential cleaning.** Cheapest validation, very high access and cash conversion, and good delegation. The ceiling is moderate and limited by labor.
4. **WS18-007 Residential pest control plans.** Essential, recurring and recession-resistant. IDPH certification is exam-based: one certified technician per business, 70% passing score [A, WS18-S018]. Economics are E-level only.
5. **WS18-011 Radon testing & mitigation (IEMA licence).** A licensed niche created by regulation: the Radon Awareness Act requires sellers to give buyers a disclosure and pamphlet [A, WS18-S017]. The licence needs a course plus an NRPP/NRSB professional exam [A, WS18-S016]. Capital is low and referrals come from inspectors. Prices, volume and competitor counts are unverified (E).
6. **WS18-030 Senior move management & estate sales.** Commission is withheld from sale proceeds, so working capital is negative. Demand is demographic, and it shares a referral network with home care (002).
7. **WS18-003 Junk removal.** The best-evidenced cost structure here, but commoditized by three national brands.

Possible combination for later stages: **home care + senior move/estate services** under one referral engine, or **fertilization + mosquito + holiday lighting + exterior cleaning** as one route company running all year.

## Surprises
- **Child care licensing moved from DCFS to the new Illinois Department of Early Childhood (IDEC) on 1 July 2026.** The rules were renumbered from 89 IAC 406/407/408 to 23 IAC 2007/2008/2009 [A, WS18-S015]. Older guides citing DCFS are out of date.
- **Handyman fails on profit despite strong revenue** (Ace FDD P&L above).
- **The Home Repair and Remodeling Act excludes landscaping, carpet cleaning and maintenance under $500.** Lawn and cleaning routes therefore avoid its contract, pamphlet and insurance formalities. Trades that do fall under it (handyman, painting, garage doors, HVAC, radon) must carry $100k/$300k bodily-injury, $50k property-damage and $10k improper-repair insurance. These amounts are from the as-enacted text [A, WS18-S010].
- The Illinois home inspector licence is quick to get: a 60-hour course, a field course of at least 15 hours, an exam and a $250 fee [A, WS18-S012]. The business is still agent-gated and cyclical.

## Looked good but fails
- **Capital-heavy models fail G1:**
  - **Express car wash.** Mister Car Wash is excellent at scale (32.8% adj. EBITDA, 76% of wash sales from memberships). But its growth capex was about $7.8M per greenfield opened (my calculation) [A, WS18-S022].
  - **Self-storage**, a **new laundromat**, a **child care centre** and **dog daycare/boarding** all need far more than $100k. Buying an existing laundromat or storage site is excluded by the mandate.
- **Family child care home:** licence capacity is capped at 8–12 children, so it is structurally the founder's own job (G4/G7).
- **Mobile grooming, party rentals and driver ed:** capital per unit or skilled-labor limits against low ceilings.
- **Dog walking, carpet cleaning and senior tech support** were screened out and not recorded. Dog walking is commoditized by Rover/Wag platforms, carpet cleaning is in decline and commoditized, and senior tech support faces Geek Squad with a low ceiling.

## Evidence gaps (largest first)
1. **Operating margins** for most categories. Only Ace Handyman, Junk King and A Place At Home disclose costs. Cleaning, lawn and pest margins are assumptions (E).
2. **Item 19 data I could not retrieve:** Mosquito Joe, Mathnasium, CertaPro/Five Star, DoodyCalls, Pillar To Post, Precision Door, College Hunks, Comfort Keepers, Mr. Appliance, Dogtopia, Primrose.
3. **Illinois rules not verified:**
   - IDPH 77 IAC 245 details (licence fee, manager qualifications, training hours).
   - Private Employment Agency Act (nanny agencies).
   - Amusement Ride and Attraction Safety Act (inflatables).
   - IDNR nuisance wildlife permit.
   - Plumbing-licence scope for drain cleaning (flagged G2 WARN).
   - Animal Welfare Act scope for daycare and grooming.
   - ICC fees and insurance minimums for movers.
   - Current amended Home Repair Act insurance amounts.
4. **Chicago municipal licensing:** chicago.gov returned 403, so none of it was verified.
5. **Chicago-area local prices and wages.** BLS OEWS metro tables load only in a JavaScript app.

## Research limitations
- The shared WebSearch budget ran out early in the run. After that I used only direct fetches of known primary URLs.
- State FDD databases returned 403: Minnesota CARDS and Wisconsin DFI. California DFPI redirected to an entity list.
- ilga.gov returned 503 throughout, so statute text came from the Attorney General's copy of PA 91-0230.
- SEC EDGAR started rate-limiting after the Mister Car Wash filing, so the Rollins, Addus and KinderCare filings were not retrieved.
- Item 19 excerpts came from a third-party PDF archive (genuine FDD pages, text extracted with pdftotext). They should be re-checked against the state database copies.
