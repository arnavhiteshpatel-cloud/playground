# SYN-001: Small motor-carrier compliance & back-office hub (Stage C, batch C4)

> **Verdict: ADVANCE to Stage D, with a changed spec.** The business should lead with insurance, aimed at established
> (3+ years old) carriers with 2–20 power units (PU). A compliance subscription is the way in, not the profit engine.
> Evidence level: **D** (was E).
>
> **Why.** Carriers already buy this exact bundle, from incumbents. OOIDA sells DQ-file compliance, a drug & alcohol
> consortium, permits/IRP/IFTA and truck insurance, and prices the bundle in tiers linked to placing primary liability
> [B, C4-S004, C4-S005]. Simplex Group bundles insurance, compliance, permits, IFTA and factoring, and claims 10,000+
> trucking clients [D, C4-S013]. So bundling is real, but it is the incumbent model, not an opening. The component
> prices show where the profit is:
> - consortium membership has collapsed to **$149 per company per year with unlimited drivers** [C, C4-S006];
> - self-serve DQ software costs **$5 per driver per month** [B, C4-S008];
> - done-for-you compliance sells for **$149–$400 per month** [D, C4-S011, C4-S012];
> - a 4-truck insurance account pays **about $5,000 a year** in commission (4 × ~$14k premium × ~9%, E).
>
> Churn is severe in the new-venture segment. Of the Chicago-area for-hire carriers registered in 2023, only 41.9% are
> still active three years later; of the 2021 cohort, 29.5% [A, C4-S001]. The bundle beats its best single component
> (the insurance agency) by roughly $40–130k of year-3 operating profit, and mainly through retention and lead flow,
> not price. It is worth a deep dive only in that insurance-led form.

## Industry structure
- **Buyer base (A).** The 8-county Chicago area has 25,049 active carriers that filed an MCS-150 in 2024 or later.
  - 11,781 run 2–100 PU; 9,622 of those registered before 2024 [C4-S002].
  - 18,502 carriers with 1–100 PU hold active MC (for-hire) authority [C4-S002].
  - Self-reported CDL drivers in the 2–100 PU band total about 110,800 [C4-S002].
- **The pool skews young.**
  - Single-truck carriers: 44.7% registered in 2024 or later.
  - 2–5 PU: 24.0%. 6–20 PU: 13.2%. 21–100 PU: 7.7% [C4-S002].
- **New-entrant formation has not collapsed despite FMCSA's 2026 vetting.** Comparing 1 Jan–27 Nov of each year, new
  MC-docket registrations went from 3,052 in 2025 to 2,967 in 2026 (−3%) [C4-S001].
- **Missing data.** Census SUSB/CBP counts for "carrier compliance services" (a NAICS 541611/561110 mix) are MISSING;
  the Census API needs a key.
- **Fragmentation (D).** The market runs from national firms (J.J. Keller with 600+ managed-services staff [C,
  C4-S010]; Foley; DOT Compliance Group with 1,253 Google reviews [D, C4-S014]) down to ethnic-community "trucking
  services" offices in the suburbs (Stage A search, D).

## Value chain & mechanism
The carrier pays:
1. its insurer a premium, from which the agency earns commission;
2. a monthly compliance fee;
3. a consortium fee plus per-test fees, which pass through to the lab, MRO and collector;
4. state IRP/IFTA/UCR fees plus a service fee;
5. optionally, a bookkeeping fee.

**The money is in insurance.** Illinois commercial-auto "selling expense" (commission, brokerage and other acquisition
costs) is 15.9% of premium [C, C4-S015]. That pool is shared between retail agent and wholesaler/MGA. Against roughly
$14k premium per truck, it dwarfs the other wallets: consortium is $149 per company, DQ software about $60 per driver per
year.

**Incumbent vs new-entrant economics.** Incumbent agencies and associations amortize brand and data across tens of
thousands of accounts. A new entrant has no carrier appointments; market access comes through aggregators or
wholesalers that take splits [D, WS02-S013–S015].

## Small-start evidence
J.J. Keller began in 1953 as a one-man consulting firm for motor carriers [C, C4-S010]. That is real, but 70 years old.
TWFG recruits start-up agencies and passes about 60% of commission to them [A, WS02-S012]. **No documented recent case**
of a Chicago start-up growing this bundle to a stated size: MISSING.

## Competition
- **Associations:** OOIDA (cheap and trusted).
- **National back offices:** Simplex, J.J. Keller, Foley, DOT Compliance Group. The last runs a referral programme with
  insurance brokers [D, C4-S014].
- **Insurance agencies** that give compliance away: free DOT monitoring for insureds [D, C4-S038], Stage A WS02 notes.
- **Telematics/ELD DQ modules.**
- **Local multilingual permit offices.**

**Weaknesses.** National firms are remote and phone-based; self-serve tools still leave the work with the carrier.
Local offices are said to be paper-based (E).

**Switching costs** are low for consortium and permits. They are moderate for compliance, because records are held,
and for insurance, because of mid-term rewrites and filings.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Who decides:** the owner, or the spouse or office manager.
- **When they buy (triggers):**
  - new authority, which brings 18-month new-entrant monitoring and a safety audit [A, C4-S035];
  - insurance renewal, when they shop 30–60 days ahead (E);
  - an audit letter or a crash;
  - a broker or shipper vetting them.
- **How they find vendors:** referrals, their insurance agent, factoring companies, community networks and Google.
  They are flooded with deceptive "FMCSA update" mailers.
- **Spend:** a 5-truck carrier's annual wallet is roughly $70k premium + $2.4–4.8k compliance + ~$150–850 consortium +
  ~$300–800 filing services (E) + bookkeeping (MISSING).

## Failure evidence
1. **Client mortality** [A, C4-S001]: of all 2021 registrants, 29.4% are active and filing today; of the 2023 cohort,
   39.8%.
2. **Price compression:** consortium is now unlimited drivers for $149 [C4-S006].
3. **Insurance capacity.** Commercial-auto liability lost 15.1 points of underwriting margin countrywide in 2024, and
   Illinois commercial auto lost 4.7 [C, C4-S015]. MGAs and carriers withdraw, and a start-up agency loses markets.
4. **E&O** from missed filings or expiries.
5. **Distrust** created by scam mailers.

## Regulation (precise)
- **Compliance administration:** no federal or Illinois licence found (absence not verified). Avoid legal advice.
- **Consortium / C-TPA:**
  - register as a service agent in the Clearinghouse, and the employer designates the C/TPA [A, C4-S037];
  - follow Part 40 limits, e.g. no intermediating lab-to-MRO results [A, C4-S036].
- **IFTA/IRP:** filed under a carrier power of attorney. Whether processing IRP plates for others needs an Illinois
  SOS remitter licence is MISSING; ilga.gov failed TLS this session.
- **Insurance:**
  - Illinois P&C producer licence: 20 hours per line, Pearson VUE exams, $215 [A, WS02-S001, S003];
  - agency entity licence $150 with a designated producer [A, WS02-S003];
  - about 1–3 months; age 18+;
  - E&O is not statutory, but carriers and networks expect it.
- **Outbound marketing:** TCPA limits autodialed calls and texts to cell phones.

## Entry wedge test
**Target:** about 9,600 carriers with 2–100 PU that are 3+ years old [C4-S002]. Start with 3–20 PU firms in one or two
language communities.

**Offer:**
- a free DQ-file and CSA audit, plus a pre-renewal insurance review;
- then done-for-you compliance at $200–450 per month, with consortium and filings included;
- then quote the insurance at renewal.

**Assessment:** the wedge is local presence, done-for-you service, language and speed. It is copyable (OOIDA and Simplex
already do it). Defensibility builds only from held records plus the renewal book.

**Does the bundle beat the best single component?** Insurance alone (WS02-001) is $25k–$130k. Compliance alone
