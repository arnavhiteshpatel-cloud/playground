# SYN-003 — Restaurant back-of-house recurring service route (bundle)

**Bundle components:** pest control (WS05-007), hood-filter exchange (WS05-032), dishmachine/chemical program (WS13-001), grease-trap service (WS05-035) and draft-line cleaning (WS13-010).

> **Verdict: DROP as a bundle.** Keep only its lead component, WS05-007. Revised evidence level: **D**.
>
> All four bundling tests fail:
> 1. **Joint purchase.** No evidence that restaurants buy these services together.
> 2. **Incumbent bundling.** Incumbents that sell several of them keep them in separate service organizations.
> 3. **One route.** Each component has a different licence regime, visit cadence and time-of-day window, so one technician route cannot carry them.
> 4. **Best single component.** The bundle adds $25k–80k+ of capital (filter pool, wash facility, placed dishmachines) and two unresolved legal questions (grease-trap plumbing scope; tied-house rules). It does not beat pest control alone.
>
> Low-capital, unlicensed add-ons (gaskets, strip curtains, drain-fly service) can be cross-sold later once pest density exists. That is a feature of WS05-007, not a separate opportunity.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Pest control.** The largest and best-documented piece: NAICS 561710, 13,603 US firms and $19.6B in receipts [C, C2-S012].
- **Warewashing.** About 13% of Ecolab's $16.1B 2025 sales [A, WS13-S005]. Auto-Chlor claims about 140 branches [D, WS13-S006].
- **Other components.** No industry data was found for hood-filter exchange, under-sink grease-trap service or draft-line cleaning. Stage A marked them MISSING or E.
- **Chicago demand triggers.** Analyst string-matching on inspection comments over 13 months (licences cited) [A, C2-S014]:

| Trigger in inspection comments | Licences cited |
|---|---|
| Pests (violation 38) | 2,147 |
| Gaskets | 702 |
| Dishmachines | 623 |
| Hood filters | 458 |
| Grease traps / interceptors | 291 |

Pest is by far the largest compliance trigger.

- **Addressable base.** 11,223 active Retail Food Establishment licences and 776 taverns [A, C2-S015].

## 2. Value chain and mechanism: is one route real?

| Component | Licence / permit | Visit cadence | Time window | Capital |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pest | IDPH business licence plus Certified Technician; insurance $100k/$300k/$50k [A, C2-S001, C2-S004] | monthly or biweekly | after close or before open | low |
| Hood-filter exchange | none found; wash facility must meet MWRD's FOG limit of 250 mg/L [A, WS05-S027] | weekly to monthly | before open | filter pool plus wash plant |
| Dishmachine/chemicals | none found (E); hookups may need licensed trades (unverified) | Auto-Chlor guarantees every 28 days plus 24-hr emergency, 2-hr response [D, WS13-S007] | during service hours | $3–8k per placed machine (E) |
| Grease trap | Plumbing License Law scope unresolved [C2-S031]; disposal via MWRD-approved haulers [A, WS05-S032] | monthly or more | off-hours | low |
| Draft lines | tied-house treatment unresolved (E) | ~biweekly (E) | before open | low |

The emergency-response promise in warewashing breaks a fixed route. Cadences differ (7, 14, 28 and 30 days). The skills differ too: pesticide certification, plumbing-adjacent work, chemical dispensing and draft mechanics. A single technician holding all of them would be a scarce, costly hire, which undercuts the delegation case.

## 3. Operator economics: do incumbents bundle?
- **Ecolab** sells pest elimination and warewashing to the same restaurant segments. It runs them as **separate operating segments**: Pest Elimination versus Institutional, which includes dishmachine lease programs. Its cross-sell is "One Ecolab," where one customer may use offerings of several segments [A, C2-S011]. That is account-level bundling, not route-level.
- **Rentokil NA** separates Pest Control Services (19.7% adjusted margin) from Business Services (about 9–11%) [B, C2-S009, C2-S010].
- **Auto-Chlor** sells warewashing only [D, WS13-S006].
- **Gasket Guy** is a gasket-only franchise [D, C2-S032].

Incumbent economics remain segment-specific. None of the scaled incumbents runs a multi-trade single-technician route. This is weak but consistent negative evidence.

## 4. Small-start evidence
No documented operator was found that started as a multi-service restaurant route and grew. The documented small starts are single-trade, e.g., local pest firms winning public work [A, C2-S016, C2-S018]. **MISSING** for the bundle.

## 5. Competition
- **By component:**
  - Pest: Rollins, Rentokil, Ecolab and locals.
  - Warewashing: Ecolab, Auto-Chlor and distributor programs.
  - Hood work: franchises such as HOODZ (Stage A noted entry costs of about $200–247k) [WS05 summary].
  - Grease: haulers.
- **Effect on the entrant.** Each component faces a specialist. The bundle competes against five specialists at once, each with lower cost in its own line.
- **Switching costs differ:**
  - Dishmachine leases tie in through placed equipment.
  - Pest contracts are mostly month to month (E).

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- In independent restaurants one owner/GM buys everything. That makes a single invoice plausible as a convenience.
- No evidence was found that buyers seek a single vendor. **MISSING**; no surveys found.
- **Purchase triggers differ by service:**
  - Pest: inspections and sightings [A, C2-S014].
  - Dishmachines: machine failure [WS13-001].
  - Filters and grease traps: fire-insurer or MWRD pressure.
  - Draft lines: beer quality.
- **Validation needed in Stage D or later:** interviews asking whether owners would pay for consolidation. No contact is allowed now.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Restaurant closures strand placed capital.** Chicago inspectors recorded 1,083 licences as "Out of Business" in 13 months [A, C2-S014]. That hurts placed dishmachines and filter pools far more than pest, which has no placed capital beyond stations.
- **Retention.** Rentokil's 80.7% retention [B, C2-S009] shows even a single-service relationship churns about 19% a year.

## 8. Regulation
See the table in section 2. Pest licensing is verified at A-level; the other four are unverified or ambiguous:
- **Grease traps:** a plumbing licence requires a 4-year apprenticeship [A, WS05-S029], so the founder could never hold one if the work counts as plumbing.
- **Draft lines:** tied-house rules (Liquor Control Act) unresolved.
- **Hood-filter wash water:** MWRD discharge rules apply; permit details MISSING.

These ambiguities add legal risk without adding moat.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The pitch:** "one vendor, one inspector-ready logbook for pest, grease, filters and dish."
- **The problem:** delivering it requires five capabilities and roughly $40k–120k of capital. A specialist competitor copies any single line more cheaply.
- **The workable wedge** is narrower: a pest route whose technicians also log and fix cheap, unlicensed defects that inspectors cite (torn gaskets, strip curtains, door sweeps).

## 10. Economics sanity check (year 3, before founder salary; E)
**Pest-only base (WS05-007):** about $412k revenue and about $138k operating profit.

**Bundle attempt:**
- **Uplift.** Add filter exchange at 60 accounts × 12 filters × $5 × 26 exchanges = $94k, and dishmachine programs at 40 accounts × $250 × 12 = $120k. That is +$214k revenue.
- **Added costs:**
  - 1.5 extra technicians: about $84k.
  - Filter pool and wash operations: about $35k/yr including labour and water treatment.
  - Machine depreciation: 40 × $5k / 4 yrs = $50k.
  - Chemicals at about 30% of $120k = $36k.
  - Vehicle: $12k.
  - Total about $217k.
- **Incremental profit: about −$3k to +$40k**, depending on pricing. On top of that comes about $25–80k of filter and wash capital plus about $200k of machines (40 × $5k).
- **Range.** Year-3 operating profit of about $30k–$200k, versus $45k–$245k for pest alone.
- **Ranking.** Pest alone has higher ROIC, a higher score on every criterion except possibly access, and lower risk.
- **Launch capital:** $40k–120k (E).
