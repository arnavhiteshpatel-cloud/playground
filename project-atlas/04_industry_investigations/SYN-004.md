# SYN-004 — Commercial energy cost-management bundle
Brokerage (WS11-007) + bill audit (WS11-010) + demand-response enrollment (WS11-008) + capacity-tag/PLC alerts (WS11-009)
Stage C · batch C7 · investigated 2026-11-28

> **Verdict: HOLD.** The bundle is real at the customer level and is the right way to run WS11-007 if this path is pursued, but it does not clear the bar for Stage D. Evidence: **D**.
>
> **Is the bundling real?** Yes, in a narrow sense. All four components:
> - sell to the same buyer (the C&I controller or facility manager);
> - use the same data (12–24 months of bills and 15-minute interval data);
> - sit under one licence (the ABC licence covers supply procurement; audit, DR referral and PLC alerts need none) [A, C7-S003, WS11-S023].
>
> **Does it beat its best component?** Only modestly. Revised evidence-adjusted composites: bundle **5.32** against brokerage alone **5.16**, DR alone ~5.01 and audit alone 4.90 (all Stage C or current values).
> - The audit is a free door-opener.
> - DR adds a second recurring stream on perhaps 10–20% of accounts.
> - PLC alerts are a retention feature competitors give away free, not revenue.
>
> The binding constraints are untouched by bundling:
> - a saturated broker field (392 ABCs) [A, C7-S007];
> - the one-year electric-industry experience requirement [A, C7-S001];
> - DR partner revenue shares that remain MISSING.
>
> Year-3 operating profit is plausibly $15k–$170k, base ~$85k.

## Industry structure (shared)
- **Retail choice.** Illinois non-residential choice is mature: 79% (ComEd) and 88% (Ameren) of usage is on ARES. 96 ARES and 392 ABCs [A, C7-S007].
- **Capacity value**, the DR and PLC driver:
  - PJM cleared at the cap for 2026/27 ($329.17) and 2027/28 ($333.44/MW-day; COMED cleared the most DR, 1,515.9 MW) [A, WS11-S020];
  - FERC approved a 2028/29 cap of $325 and floor of $175/MW-day [A, C7-S010].
  - Capacity is therefore worth **$63.9k–$118.6k per MW-year** through May 2029 (arithmetic).
  - COMED import capability fell ~60% [A, C7-S010], so if the collar lapses after 2028/29, COMED could price above RTO. That is a risk for customers and an opportunity for DR.
- **DR aggregators.** CSPs (Voltus, CPower/NRG, Enel X) run partner programs with revenue-share, fixed or upfront models [D, WS11-S021, WS11-S022]. Their percentages are not public.

## Value chain and mechanism (bundle)
1. **Free bill and interval audit.** Finds tariff errors (contingency fee) and identifies sites with curtailable load or capacity pass-through.
2. **Supply RFP.** The ABC earns a mils adder for the contract term [D, C7-S008].
3. **DR enrollment** with a CSP for sites with ≥100 kW of curtailable load. The partner earns a share of capacity payments each delivery year.
4. **PLC peak alerts** to cut next year's capacity tag. This is mostly a retention tool, because brokers, CSPs and suppliers give alerts free [E, WS11-009].

**Who pays whom:**
- suppliers pay the broker;
- CSPs pay the partner;
- clients pay the audit contingency.

There are three counterparties, and each controls its own terms.

## Operator economics
No pure bundle operator with disclosed financials was found.
- **Proxies:**
  - Schooley Mitchell's franchise contingency model [D, WS11-S047];
  - CPower's acquisition by NRG [D, WS11-S022], which shows DR aggregation has strategic value, but for the CSP, not the channel partner.
- **Incumbent advantage.** Large brokers already bundle procurement + DR + sustainability reporting (e.g., Constellation, Enel X, Edison Energy; not verified this session). An incumbent's advantage is supplier pricing access and DR program scale.

## Small-start evidence
None documented. MISSING.

## Competition
Bundled offers are standard among serious brokers and CSPs. The new-entrant differentiator would be hands-on curtailment playbooks for mid-size ComEd manufacturers (100 kW–1.5 MW), a segment too small for CSP direct teams but large enough for DR value.
- **Above 1,500 kW**, customers are exempt from ABC licensing (a gap for an unlicensed founder) [A, C7-S003], but these are the most heavily courted accounts.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decisions are sequential.** Audit acceptance takes days. Supply contracts turn over every 12–36 months.
- **DR is timed.** Enrollment follows PJM delivery-year deadlines and needs operations buy-in. All-hours DR availability since 2027/28 deters some sites [A, WS11-S020].
- **Rule disclosure.** Illinois rules require signed disclosure of total price including broker fees [A, C7-S005].

## Failure evidence
Risks across the components:
- supplier exits shrink quote pools (active ARES in ComEd −14% year over year) [A, C7-S007];
- DR non-performance penalties sour clients (E);
- capacity normalization after the collar (2028/29 floor is 47% below the 2027/28 cap) [A, C7-S010];
- partner terms set unilaterally by CSPs.

## Regulation
- **ABC licence** (83 Ill. Adm. Code 454): one year of management experience; **one year of electric-industry experience** (an employee or partner can qualify the entity); $5,000 bond; 90–180 days; annual report by March 31 [A, C7-S001–C7-S006].
- **Not needed for:** bill audit; DR referral (no ICC licence identified; PJM market participation is the CSP's); PLC alerts. Gas brokerage rules are MISSING.
- **Unlicensed founder path:** start with audit + DR referral (no licence), sub-agent under a licensed ABC for supply deals, and seek one's own ABC licence after a year in the industry (the sub-agent year may itself count as "experience in the electric industry", an interpretation not verified).

## Entry wedge test
"One analyst, all four levers" for ComEd mid-size manufacturers and cold storage, delivered with interval-data software and LLM bill parsing.
- **Cost.** Low ($5k–$20k incl. bond, tools, insurance).
- **Copyability.** Copyable by established brokers, but many brokers are telemarketing shops without DR or analytics depth.
- **Moat.** The relationship and residual book. Moderate.

## Economics sanity check (year 3; E)
- **Base:**
  - Brokerage: 80 accounts × 400 MWh × $0.002 = $64k.
  - DR: 8 enrolled sites × 0.4 MW × $250/MW-day × 365 × 10% partner share = $29.2k.
  - Audit contingency: $15k.
  - Gross $108k, less tools, insurance and bond of about $15–20k.
  - Operating profit ≈ **$85k**.
- **Low:** brokerage $15k + DR $5k + audit $5k − $10k tools = **$15k**.
- **High:** brokerage $170k (with a sub-agent) + DR 20 sites × 0.5 MW × $250 × 365 × 12% = $55k + audit $30k − $85k (analyst and overhead) = **$170k**.
- **Bundle uplift.** The bundle adds roughly **+30–50%** to brokerage-only profit in the base case, mostly from DR. It depends on two MISSING inputs: CSP partner share and the number of curtailable sites.
