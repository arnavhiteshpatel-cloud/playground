# SYN-005 — Workplace facility-services route, "Cintas-lite" (first aid + restroom hygiene + entrance mats + point-of-use water) (Stage C, batch C1)

| Verdict | Revised evidence level | Revised adj. score |
|---|---|---|
| **DROP the bundle as defined.** Replace it with a narrower "safety route" variant (extinguishers + emergency lighting + first aid/AED/eyewash), tested under WS05-001 in Stage D. | **E** for the economics. The bundle-structure test itself rests on A/B evidence (10-Ks, cooperative-contract documents). | 4.67 (Stage B placeholder 5.03) |

**Why:** I tested four questions.
1. **Do incumbents sell these together?** Yes, commercially. Cintas offers restroom and mat service, first aid with WaterBreak water and eyewash, and fire protection under **one cooperative contract as "one vendor"** [B, C1-S015], and public buyers purchase through it without competitive bids [A, C1-S016].
2. **Do buyers actually buy them together?** Only partly. Vestis says its existing customers use **only 30–40% of its full line** [A, C1-S013]. Even route incumbents with the relationship sell subsets, and Cintas grows by *gradual* penetration [A, C1-S011].
3. **Does one route serve them all?** No. The four components need **two different delivery systems:**
   - Mats and restroom service sit in Cintas' rental segment, delivered on routes "originating from rental processing plants".
   - First aid and water are delivered through a separate distribution network and routes [A, C1-S011].
   - UniFirst likewise runs first aid as a separate van business [A, C1-S012].
   - Mats require laundering plants: 86% of industrial-laundry receipts sit with $100M+ firms, and Illinois has only 14 such firms [C, C1-S020/S021]. WS13-006 was already rejected on this basis.
4. **Does the bundle beat the best single component?** Barely on profit, and it loses on capital and ROIC (§11).

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
The bundle combines two different businesses:
- **Rental-plant routes (mats, towels, restroom).** The incumbent owns the textile inventory and the laundry, and margin comes from reusing the asset. A plant-less entrant must pay contract laundering to a competitor's plant (supply unverified, WS13-006).
- **Replenishment van routes (first aid, water filters, safety).** Margin comes from consumable markup times stop density.

## 3. Operator economics
| Operator | Disclosed numbers |

| Cintas | Rental & facility services 50.0% gross / 24.1% operating; First Aid & Safety 57.7% / 25.4%; ~12,500 routes [A, C1-S011] |
| UniFirst | Uniform & facility 5.5% operating margin; First Aid & Safety loss of $(4.6)M [A, C1-S012] |

| Rentokil Initial | Hygiene 18.6% adjusted operating margin [A, C1-S014] |

- **Only the scale leader earns high margins on both delivery systems.**
- #2 and #3 (UniFirst, Vestis) earn 2–6% with full bundles. Bundling alone does not create returns; density and the plant network do.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- Single-line small starts exist: Green Guard in first aid [C, C1-S018]; ZEE distributors [C, C1-S017].
- No documented small start that built a multi-line first-aid + hygiene + mat + water route was found (MISSING).

## 5. Competition
- **Cintas** is the reference bundler, with the pending UniFirst merger [A, C1-S011].
- **Others:** Vestis (declining), Rentokil Initial (hygiene), Culligan/Primo (water), janitorial contractors (hygiene), and Grainger/Amazon (first-aid self-supply).
- **Switching costs:** low per line, but multi-line incumbent accounts are stickier.

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Who decides differs by line:**
  - Safety/plant manager: first aid, eyewash, AED, sometimes water.
  - Office/facility manager or janitor: restroom, mats.
  - At small firms one owner or manager may decide everything.
- **Contract terms:** rental lines run on multi-year (3–5-year) service agreements [A, C1-S012]. Public buyers use cooperatives [A, C1-S016].
- **What drives purchase:** existing vendor relationships, not separate searches per line.

## 7. Failure evidence
- Vestis' lost business and net loss show a full-line bundler failing on service [A, C1-S013].
- UniFirst's first-aid loss shows sub-scale route networks don't profit [A, C1-S012].
- The mat component has no viable plant-free model (WS13-006).

## 8. Regulation
- **No special licences** identified for first aid, hygiene, mats or water.
- **Water:**
  - Point-of-use plumbing connections may require a licensed plumber (Illinois Plumbing License Law; unverified).
  - Leak liability.
- **Hygiene waste:** non-medical [E inference from A definition, C1-S025].
- **Where the regulatory moat is:** only in the licensed alternative (extinguishers), which is why the safety-route variant is preferred.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **"One local vendor for everything"** is exactly Cintas' pitch [B, C1-S015], so it is not differentiated.
- **A credible wedge** is "local, no auto-renew, faster" aimed at Vestis or merger-disrupted accounts. It is temporary and copyable.

## 10. Economics sanity check (E)
**Bundle assumptions:**
- First aid at every stop ($70/month).
- Attach rates: hygiene 40% ($60), water 20% ($80), mats 15% ($100), bounded by Vestis' 30–40% line usage.
- So: $70 + 24 + 16 + 15 = **$125 per stop per month**.

**Year-3 low:**
- Revenue: 150 stops × $105 (lower attach) × 12 = $189k
- Blended gross margin 40% (mats at thin margin with outsourced laundering; water-unit depreciation) = $76k
- Less vans $14k, insurance $6k, admin/marketing $15k
- **Operating profit ≈ $41k** (founder on the route)

**Year-3 high:**
- Revenue: 350 stops × $130 × 12 = $546k
- Gross margin 45% = $246k
- Less route rep $60k, part-time installer/technician $30k, two vans $26k, insurance $10k, sales/admin/software $30k
- **Operating profit ≈ $90k**

**Range:** stated **$35k–100k**, against $20k–80k for first aid alone (WS13-004) and $25k–90k for hygiene alone (WS05-025).

**Capital:**
- Water units 70 × ~$1,000 = $70k [E]
- Hygiene units ~$20k
- Mat inventory (3–4 sets per placement) ~$10k
- Two vans
- Total **$40k–120k**, against $10k–30k for first aid alone. ROIC is therefore lower than the best single component.

**Variant to test in Stage D (under WS05-001):**
- Lines: extinguishers + emergency lighting + first aid/AED/eyewash.
- Same buyer (safety/plant manager) and same van.
- A licensed core service the buyer must buy annually.
- Precedent: UniFirst runs extinguisher inspection, testing and maintenance inside its First Aid & Safety segment [A, C1-S012].
