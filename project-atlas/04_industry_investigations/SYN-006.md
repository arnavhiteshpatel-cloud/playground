**Components:** WS02-028 (job-cost bookkeeping), WS15-023 (certified payroll), WS10-004 (lien-rights administration), WS02-007 (surety), WS02-026 (payroll). **Lead (Stage A):** WS15-023. Access date 2026-11-28.

|---|---|
| Revised evidence level | **D** |
| Reason | The bundle is real in one sense: the same owner needs job costing, payroll by job/class, certified payroll and WIP schedules for the surety, and the data flows from one to the next. Software incumbents prove the logic by bundling it. Procore owns Levelset lien management [A, C5-S030], and ADP sells certified payroll as an add-on [B, C5-S013]. The narrowed bundle beats its best single component (bookkeeping) modestly. Base year-3 operating profit is about $146k versus about $124k, through higher revenue per client and stickiness. The full five-part bundle is worse than bookkeeping alone: lien work carries documented unauthorized-practice-of-law (UPL) exposure, surety needs a producer licence plus carrier access, and in-house payroll adds fiduciary risk. The Stage A lead was the wrong lead, because certified payroll alone has a low ticket and is seasonal. |

## 1. Industry structure
- **Buyers** [C, C5-S002]: 29,457 Illinois construction establishments, including 18,685 specialty-trade establishments. About 5,200 specialty-trade establishments have 5–49 employees.
- **Public works subset:** the number of prevailing-wage filers is MISSING (search snippet about 1.5M submissions since 2020, unverified) [D, C5-S034].
- **Supplier industries** (Illinois, Census SUSB 2022) [C, C5-S001]:
  - Bookkeeping (541219): 1,673 firms.
  - Payroll (541214): 154 firms, only 91 of them with fewer than 20 employees.
- Both supplier industries are fragmented at the small end and offer no scale economy below large-enterprise size.

## 2. Is bundling real for Illinois construction subcontractors?
| Test | Evidence | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Same buyer and decision-maker | The owner/office manager buys bookkeeping, payroll and compliance; surety and bank requests flow to the same person. | Yes [E, consistent with WS02/WS10] |
| Shared data | Payroll by job/class feeds the job-cost report, certified payroll (per-project IDOL template [A, C5-S017]) and the WIP schedule. | Yes, and it is the strongest argument |
| Do incumbents bundle? | Procore acquired Levelset (lien rights) in 2021 [A, C5-S030]; ADP offers certified payroll via Points North [B, C5-S013]; Paychex attaches an insurance agency to payroll [A, C5-S011]; NEXT partners with QuickBooks and Gusto [B, C5-S025]. | Yes, but in **software** |
| Legal compatibility | Lien filing: Procore says Levelset "has been subject to claims of UPL" and "currently face[s]" similar claims [A, C5-S030]. Surety: producer licence plus carrier appointment needed (WS02-007). Certified payroll: the filing agent carries personal liability under SB1344 [C, C5-S019]. | Partly. Lien and surety fail the "no licence, low liability" premise. |
| Cross-sell rate | Paychex: more than 50% of clients come via referrals (CPAs, brokers, banks) [A, WS02-S022]. Specific bookkeeping→payroll attach rates are MISSING. | Unproven (E) |

**Conclusion:** bundling is real at the data and buyer level. Value comes from **sequencing** (bookkeeping first, add-ons later), not from launching five services at once. The incumbents' software bundles also mean the service firm competes as a *human layer on top of* QBO/ADP/Procore, not against them.

## 3. Value chain and mechanism
- The firm runs the client's books in QBO (or a construction ERP).
- From the same timecards it produces:
  - payroll (on a platform that impounds taxes);
  - monthly certified payroll for each public project;
  - a quarterly WIP schedule for the surety.
- Surety and insurance are *referred* to a licensed agency (later possibly the founder's own WS02-002 or WS02-007), never sold unlicensed.
- Lien deadlines may be tracked as a calendar service only. Notices and liens go through the client's attorney.

## 4. Operator economics (inherited)
- **Bookkeeping:** construction vertical list prices $500–$2,800/month [D, C5-S009]; generic AI tiers $65–$99 [B, C5-S004/S005]; Bench $199–$999 [B, C5-S003].
- **Payroll:** SaaS $50 + $7/employee [B, C5-S010].
- **Certified payroll software:** $175/month + $7.50/report [B, C5-S013].
- **Incumbent bundlers:**
  - Paychex: 38.6% operating margin.
  - Procore: software economics.
  - Neither describes new-entrant economics.

## 5. Small-start evidence

## 6. Competition
- Construction CPAs, which already bundle bookkeeping, payroll and WIP.
- Remote vertical bookkeepers.
- ADP/Paychex with add-ons.
- Construction ERPs.
- Procore/Levelset for lien management.

The bundle's differentiation is **one accountable human team and one monthly fee**. That is copyable by any construction CPA.

## 7. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Entry point:**
  - a surety/bank request (bookkeeping);
  - a first public award (certified payroll);
  - payroll pain at year start.
- **Expected entry fee:** $900/month for bookkeeping, plus about $300 for certified payroll on public jobs and about $375 for payroll [E].
- **Discovery:** CPA, surety agent, GC.

## 8. Failure evidence
- **Bench:** over-automation and service delays [C, C5-S006].
- **Certified payroll:** filer liability and debarment risk [A/C, C5-S016, C5-S019].
- **Lien services:** UPL claims against even the scaled leader [A, C5-S030].
- **Payroll bureaus:** fiduciary blowups (generic; specific Illinois cases MISSING).
- **Bundle-specific risk:**
  - complexity raises error rates;
  - the founder must master three bodies of rules (construction accounting, payroll tax, prevailing wage) before hiring.

## 9. Regulation
- **Bookkeeping:** no licence identified (225 ILCS 450 text unverified).
- **Certified payroll:** monthly by the 15th, per project, Class A misdemeanor for willful failure, SB1344 civil penalties, 4-year debarment [A/C, C5-S014, C5-S016, C5-S019].
- **Payroll:** Form 8655 and money-transmission questions unverified (WS02-026).
- **Surety:** Illinois producer licence of about $215 plus 20 hours pre-licensing per line [A, WS02-S001–S003], and the surety line is unverified (WS02-007). Paying referral fees to unlicensed persons is unverified (MISSING).
- **Lien:** attorney partner required; UPL risk [A, C5-S030].
- **E&O:** essential.

## 10. Entry wedge test
- **Offer:** "One back office for public-works-capable subs." A fixed monthly fee for close plus WIP, certified payroll, and payroll.
- **Why a buyer switches:** one accountable party. Errors in one function (payroll classes) otherwise silently break the others (certified payroll, WIP).
- **Cost:** a senior construction accountant plus one payroll/compliance specialist by year 2.
- **Copyability:** moderate. Construction CPAs can copy it, but many avoid low-ticket compliance work (E).

## 11. Economics sanity check (year 3) [E]
**Low:** 20 bookkeeping clients × $700 × 12 = $168k, plus 6 certified-payroll clients × $275 × 9 = $14.9k. Total revenue **$182.9k**.
- Costs: labor $95k; software $10k; overhead $28k.
- **Operating profit about $50k.**

**Base:** revenue **$528.8k**:
- 40 bookkeeping clients × $900 × 12 = $432k;
- 15 certified-payroll clients × $300 × 9.5 = $42.8k;
- 12 payroll clients × $375 × 12 = $54k.

Costs:
- payroll platform 12 × $110 × 12 = $15.8k;
- labor $279k (2 bookkeepers, a senior, a specialist);
- software $28k;
- overhead $60k.

**Operating profit about $146k.**

**High:** revenue **$995k** (65 clients: bookkeeping $780k, certified payroll $87.5k, payroll $127.5k).
- Costs: platform $33k; labor $546k; software $45k; overhead $100k.
- **Operating profit about $271k.**

**Comparison with the best single component (WS02-028, base about $124k):** the bundle gains about $22k (+18%) with the same client count, slightly lower churn (E), and a public-award lead list. It requires one more specialist hire and extra E&O exposure.

**Comparison with WS15-023 standalone (base about $48k):** the bundle wins clearly.

**Capital:** $3–15k.

**Plausible ceiling:** $500k/yr, through additional pods and later a licensed insurance/surety agency as phase 2.
