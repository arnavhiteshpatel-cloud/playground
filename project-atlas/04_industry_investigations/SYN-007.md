# SYN-007 — Chicago small-landlord operations bundle (2–6 flats: maintenance/code plan + unit turns + porch/exterior repairs + landlord insurance referral)

> **Verdict: DROP (as a bundle)** · revised evidence level **E** for economics (demand counts are A) · 2026-11-28
>
> The bundle fails both tests. **(1) It is not one buyer.** The plan, porch, masonry and insurance pieces sell to owner-landlords of Chicago 2–6 flats. The unit-turn piece (WS10-016) is defined for property managers of 50–2,000-unit portfolios. A 2–6 flat generates only about 1–3 turns a year, usually handled by the owner. **(2) It does not beat its best component.** The plan's recurring fee has no willingness-to-pay evidence. Violation demand converts slowly: 75% of 2025 porch/stair violations were still open in November 2026 [A, C8-S003]. Porch work already has a dominant specialist: one firm pulled 32% of 2025 express porch permits [A, C8-S002]. Insurance commissions need a producer licence plus carrier access the founder lacks (WS02-004). A bundle building yields about $2.3k/yr of gross profit (E), so ~130 retained buildings are needed to clear $150k of overhead. A focused masonry firm (WS10-010) reaches similar gross profit with about 25–30 jobs. Keep the public-data lead engine as a sales tool for WS10-010/WS10-014; drop the bundle.

## 2. Industry structure (the buyer base)
- **Building stock.** In tax year 2026, the City of Chicago has **119,948 class-211 parcels** (2–6 unit apartment buildings) and **10,802 class-212 parcels** (small mixed-use) [A, C8-S004]. That is a large, dense and fully identifiable owner base. The owner-occupancy share is MISSING; the assessor class-code PDF and the ACS API were both blocked.
- **Enforcement flow, 2025:**
  - 50,099 violations at 10,787 addresses across all building types [A, WS10-S011].
  - 2,214 porch/exterior-stair violations at 1,727 addresses [A, C8-S003].
  - 2,101 exterior-wall and 856 parapet violations [A, C8-S003].
  - Even if every violation address were a 2–6 flat, about 9% of the stock gets any violation in a year, and about 1.4% gets a porch/stair violation.
- **Trend is down.** From 2019 to 2025: porch violations 2,407 → 1,351, exterior stair 1,487 → 863, exterior wall 3,050 → 2,101 [A, C8-S003]. Porch permits 2,936 → 1,623 [A, C8-S002].
- **Enforcement is reactive.** 92% of 2025 porch/stair violations came from complaints, not periodic inspections [A, C8-S003].

## 3. Value chain & mechanism
The owner-landlord pays contractors per job. Tenants' rent funds it, and the City's enforcement and the housing court create the deadlines. The proposed bundle has four parts:
1. A monthly plan: inspection, a prioritized repair list and violation response.
2. Pull-through repair work, with licensed trades subcontracted.
3. Turns.
4. Insurance referrals.

Margin would be captured on repair labor (masonry and porch tickets of $10–15k median [A, WS10-S010]). The plan fee would mainly cover customer-acquisition and inspection overhead.

**Verdict on bundling.** Shared-buyer synergy is real for plan + porch + masonry: same owner, same building, same violation notice. It is weak for turns and adds regulatory weight for insurance. Lumpy capital repairs, not recurring fees, drive the economics, so the plan subscription adds churn risk without adding margin.

## 5. Operator economics and small-start evidence
- **Disclosed economics for small-landlord plan operators:** none found (MISSING).
- **Repair incumbents.** Census proxy operating margins: masonry 20%, framing ~27.7% before rent/other opex [C, WS10-S022]. Incumbent numbers.
- **Small-start evidence.** The porch specialist Frackiel Builders shows that a focused Chicago trade firm can reach 256 permitted porch jobs a year (~$6.7M reported cost) [A, C8-S002]. That supports single-trade focus, not bundles. Its founding history is MISSING.

## 6. Competition
- **Handymen and painters.** Abundant; 80% of Illinois painting firms have fewer than 5 employees [C, C8-S005]. Handyman counts are MISSING.
- **Specialists.** Porch firms (Frackiel; Mr Porch with 62 permits) and 333 tuckpointing contractors [A, C8-S001, C8-S002].
- **Property managers and insurance agents** for the other two pieces.
- **Switching costs:** nil. No subscription plan competitor was found, which may mean there is no demand rather than an opening.

## 7. Customer purchasing behavior
The decision-maker is the owner, often owner-occupant and self-managing (share MISSING). Owners buy reactively at a violation, a court date or a tenant complaint, solicit two or three bids, and pay a deposit plus completion. Three-quarters of porch/stair violations remain open many months later [A, C8-S003], which suggests deferral rather than urgency.

## 8. Failure evidence
The main failure mode is owners deferring work and buying only one-off repairs. Others are the liability of "inspection" plans that miss hazards, and plan churn. Declining violation counts reduce lead flow. No closures were documented (MISSING).

## 9. Regulation
- **City of Chicago GC licence.** Class E is $300/yr with $1M CGL [A, WS10-S004].
- **Mason contractor licence.** Requires a City-exam supervising mason and W-2 masons [A, WS10-S005]. Porch foundations are exempt [A, WS10-S005].
- **Other trades.** Electrical and plumbing licences [A, WS10-S006, WS10-S007]. EPA RRP for pre-1978 buildings [A, C8-S024]. Home Repair Act contract and brochure for consumer jobs of $1,000+ [A, WS18-S011].
- **Insurance.** The insurance leg requires an Illinois P&C producer licence: 20 hours of pre-licensing per line, an exam, and $215 [A, WS02-S001, WS02-S002].
- **Plan scope.** Plan inspections must not drift into unlicensed architecture or engineering advice.

## 10. Entry wedge test
The wedge is a "violation-to-plan funnel": clear the current violation, then retain the owner on a plan. Delivery is a letter or call to owners on public violation lists (founder-run later; not done here). The marketing cost is low. The data are public, so any contractor can copy the targeting. The retention step has no evidence.

## 11. Economics sanity check (year 3, E)
**Gross profit per plan building:**

| Item | Calculation | Gross profit |
|---|---|---|
| Plan fee | $100/mo × 12 × 60% margin | $720 |
| Repair pull-through | $2,000/yr × 35% | $700 |
| Turns | 1.5 × $1,000 × 35% | $525 |
| Insurance commission | if licensed and placed | $400 |
| **Total** | | **≈ $2,345/yr** |

**Overhead:** coordinator/inspector $60k, technician $50k, vehicle $10k, insurance $15k, software and marketing $15k, about $150k in total.

| Case | Calculation | Op. profit |
|---|---|---|
| Base | 100 buildings × $2,345 = $234k − $150k | **≈ $85k** |
| Low | 50 buildings, plan fees at $75, 30% churn: ~$100k GP − $120k overhead | **≈ −$20k** |
| High | 150 buildings: $350k GP − $230k overhead | **≈ $120k** |

Reaching 100 retained plan buildings at an assumed $500 CAC and 25% churn means signing about 130 plans in three years. Compare masonry: 30 jobs × $15k × 35% ≈ $158k gross profit with no subscription risk.
