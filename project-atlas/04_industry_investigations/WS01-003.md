# WS01-003 — Business-acquisition financing advisory (SBA 7(a) change-of-ownership packaging)

> **Verdict: HOLD**
>
> - **Revised evidence level:** D. Market volume and fee rules are A; realized fees and close rates are E or MISSING.
> - **Year-3 operating profit:** $20–220k (base ≈ $95k).
>
> **What is now A-level:**
> - **Volume.** SBA's loan-level data show **7,533** change-of-ownership 7(a) approvals in FY2025 ($8.82B; 4,639 at or above $500k) and **7,184** in FY2026 ($8.89B; median $765k). Illinois had **311** in FY2025 but only **240** in FY2026 [A, C9-S027].
> - **Fee rules.** SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective 2026-10-01) allows an agent to charge the buyer a packaging fee. That fee must be hourly or a percentage, capped at $30k, and *never contingent* on approval. The agent may also take a disclosed lender referral fee [A, C9-S026].
>
> **Three facts cut against it:**
> 1. SOP 8.1 now excludes green-card holders and requires **100% U.S.-citizen ownership** [A, C9-S026]. That shrinks the buyer pool.
> 2. The packager competes with lender business-development officers, who are free to the buyer. Live Oak alone made 517 of the ≥$500k change-of-ownership loans in FY2025 [A, C9-S027].
> 3. Non-contingent fees are a hard sell to first-time buyers, because many letters of intent die. Lender referral fees for $1M+ loans are unpublished (MISSING).

## Industry structure
- **Census proxy:** NAICS 522310 (mortgage and nonmortgage loan brokers) has 9,792 U.S. establishments, 92% with fewer than 10 employees, and 279 in Illinois [C, C9-S007]. That count is dominated by mortgage brokers.
- **Change-of-ownership 7(a) approvals** [A, C9-S027]:

  - Illinois ran 160 to 311 loans a year (FY2023–FY2025), then 240 in FY2026.
  - Change-of-ownership loans are about 9.6% of all FY2025 7(a) approvals (7,533 of 78,078).
- **Lender base:** 511 lenders made ≥$500k change-of-ownership loans in FY2025. The top lenders were Live Oak 517, Huntington 371, First Internet 177 and Byline (Chicago) 121.
- **Cyclicality:** highly sensitive to interest rates and to SBA policy swings.

## Value chain & mechanism
**The buyer's path:**
1. The buyer signs an LOI on a $1–8M business.
2. The buyer needs a lender-ready package: credit memo inputs, debt service coverage (DSC) model, equity-injection structure, and QoE-driven adjustments.

**What the packager does:**
- prepares that package;
- shops 7(a) lenders;
- coordinates the deal to closing.

**How the packager is paid:**
- **By the buyer:** a non-contingent packaging fee (hourly, or a percentage capped at 2% to $1M + 0.25% above, $30k maximum).
- **By the lender (optional):** a referral fee. Both must be disclosed on SBA Form 159 [A, C9-S026].

**Where margin is captured:** analyst hours per deal. Lenders capture loan economics, and their BDOs offer packaging help free.

## Competition
- **Lender BDOs** at Live Oak, Huntington, Byline and others: free to the buyer, and they control credit.
- **Established packagers and loan brokers.**
- **Business brokers,** who steer buyers to favored lenders.
- **Online marketplaces** (analyst knowledge: lender-paid models).

**Weaknesses of incumbents:**
- BDOs serve their own bank's credit box.
- First-time buyers get little structuring help.

**Buyer complaints:** slow lenders, surprise QoE findings, equity-injection confusion (D).

**Switching costs:** none before the term sheet.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** individual buyers and self-funded searchers acquiring $1–8M businesses; brokers and attorneys influence the choice.
- **Frequency:** usually once per buyer.
- **Timeline:** 60–120 days from LOI to close.
- **How they find help:** brokers, searcher communities, podcasts, search.
- **Contract terms:** written engagement with hourly or percentage billing, disclosed to the lender.

## Failure evidence
- **Deal mortality:** LOI fall-through is high (rate MISSING). Under the SBA rule the packager can bill hours whether or not the deal closes, but collection risk rises.
- **Policy swings:** the FY2026 Illinois drop (311 to 240) coincided with SOP 50 10 8 tightening. Causality is not proven.
- **Enforcement:** SBA can suspend or revoke agent privileges for unreasonable fees or undisclosed relationships [A, WS01-S012].

## Regulation (Illinois + federal)
**SBA:**
- 13 CFR 103 agent rules.
- SBA Form 159 for every agent not under a Lender Service Provider agreement.
- No contingent or flat standard fees.
- Fees above $2,500 must be itemized and documented.
- Ownership must be 100% U.S. citizens or nationals; LPRs, visa holders and PRC citizens are Ineligible Persons.
- Seller earnouts are prohibited.

All of the above: [A, C9-S026].

**Illinois Loan Brokers Act (815 ILCS 175):**
- It is unlawful to broker loans without registration [A, C9-S028].
- Registration requires a disclosure document, consent to service, and a fee set by Secretary of State rule (amount MISSING).
- A $25,000 bond applies only to loans subject to Truth-in-Lending, so not to business loans [A, C9-S028].
- Because SBA forbids contingent buyer fees, the Act's exemption for wholly-contingent fees likely does not apply. Registration is likely required (analyst reading; counsel review needed).

**Other requirements:**
- No exam.
- Time to obtain: 1–2 months (E).
- California Financing Law may apply to California borrowers [A, WS01-S017].

## Entry wedge test
- **Wedge:** lender-ready packages in days rather than weeks, with AI-assisted tax-return spreading, DSC models and credit-memo drafts. Add QoE-lite diligence, plus transparent SBA-compliant hourly billing.
- **Who would choose it:** a buyer would pay only when a free BDO is slow or declines. That is plausible for marginal deals, but marginal deals have the highest mortality.
- **Copyability:** the speed edge is copyable. Relationships with brokers compound slowly.
