Access date 2026-11-28.

| Verdict | **HOLD.** This is a credible *phase-2* adjacency once a construction back office (SYN-006/WS02-028) supplies the relationships. It is weak as the founder's first business. |
|---|---|
| Revised evidence level | **D**. Commission rates are B, valuations C, and carrier access and new-entrant volume D/E. |
| Reason | The book does compound into real wealth: small books trade at a "two-times commission … minimum" [C, C5-S021], and workers' compensation is mandatory for every Illinois contractor and subcontractor [A, C5-S020]. ## 1. Industry structure
- **NAICS 524210 Insurance agencies and brokerages** (SUSB 2022) [C, C5-S001]:
  - US: 120,434 firms with $207B receipts.
  - Illinois: 5,424 firms with $10.75B receipts.
- **Illinois size distribution:**
  - 4,093 firms (75%) have fewer than 5 employees, averaging **$302k receipts**.
  - 864 firms have 5–9 employees, averaging $938k.
  - 222 firms have 10–19 employees, averaging $2.26M.
- **Agency count trend:** independent agencies fell from about 39,000 to about 37,000 between 2024 and 2026 [C, WS02-S018].
- **M&A:** 695 deals in 2025 [C, WS02-S006].
- **Buyers:**
  - 18,685 Illinois specialty-trade establishments, 12,921 of them with fewer than 5 employees [C, C5-S002].
  - Construction is cyclical, and WC/GL premiums move with payroll and receipts.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- The contractor pays premium to the carrier (direct bill). The carrier pays the agency commission.
- **Published schedule** [B, WS02-S016, 2022]:
  - 16% on commercial lines except WC;
  - GL/property 20%, auto/BOP 15%;
  - WC 5–10%, tiering down to 5% on large premiums;
  - new and renewal rates the same.
- **Intermediated access:**
  - A start-up without direct appointments goes through aggregators, MGAs or wholesalers, which take a split.
  - Smart Choice case studies cite 70/30 until a production threshold [D, WS02-S014].
  - TWFG passes about 60% of commission income to its agencies [A, WS02-S012].
- Margin is captured by renewals and servicing efficiency.
- Workers' comp audits and certificates of insurance (COIs) are the servicing load.

| Small-book transactions | 2x commission minimum; 5–7x profit; 20–50% down plus a 3-year retention earn-out; profit 15–30% | C, C5-S021 |

**Incumbent vs new entrant:**
- **Incumbents** earn 23–36% margins on mature renewal books.
- **New entrants** have:
  - first-year operating losses;
  - aggregator splits;
  - 1–2 years before a CSR is affordable.
- Branch-platform evidence shows recruits are experienced agents, not newcomers.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- SIAA claims a 75% average 5-year CAGR for start-ups across 5,200 member agencies. The method is undefined [D, C5-S023].
- No named Illinois contractor-agency start-up case was found: MISSING.

## 5. Competition
- **Locals:** every local independent agency writes contractors.
- **Direct digital:** ERGO NEXT advertises "buy in less than 10 minutes" with instant COIs, GL from $19/month, and QuickBooks/Gusto partnerships [B, C5-S025].
- **Payroll-attached:** Paychex Insurance Agency sells WC and BOPs to payroll clients [A, C5-S011].
- **Others:** PE-backed roll-ups (WS02-S006) and wholesalers' retail arms.

**Weaknesses and switching:**
- Digital insurers handle micro-accounts well but struggle with multi-line WC, auto and umbrella for 10–50 employee firms (E).
- Switching happens at renewal.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the owner.
- **Cadence:** an annual renewal, plus ad-hoc COIs required by GCs and property managers.
- **Spend:** small contractor premiums of about $3–30k (E). Workers' comp is mandatory [A, C5-S020].
- **Sales cycle:** 30–60 days before renewal.
- **Discovery:** referrals, the incumbent agent, online quoting.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Ramp:** new agencies starve during the 12–24 month ramp. Carriers are "reluctant to negotiate contracts with smaller agencies" [D, C5-S024].
- **Aggregator lock-in:** departure fees of about 25% were cited anecdotally [D, WS02-S015].
- **Retention-based earn-outs** [C, C5-S021] show that buyers discount books with poor retention.
- **Workers' comp audit errors** create E&O claims (E).

## 8. Regulation (Illinois)
- **Licences** [A, WS02-S001–S003]:
  - Resident producer licence: 20 hours pre-licensing per line, Pearson VUE exams, $215 per 2-year term, age 18+.
  - Agency entity licence: $150, with a designated licensed producer.
- **Surplus lines:** a licence is needed only if placing E&S directly; otherwise use wholesalers [A, WS02-S004].
- **Clients' workers' comp mandate** [A, C5-S020]:
  - Penalties up to $500/day (minimum $10k).
  - Stop-work orders.
  - Class 4 felony for knowing failure.
  - Construction rates set by the Illinois work location.
- **Time to licence:** about 1–3 months. The binding constraint is carrier access, not the licence.
- **Gate:** G2_LICENSE WARN (obtainable).

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **Proposed wedges:** same-hour COIs, bonds plus insurance, and bilingual service.
- **Copyability:** COI speed is now table stakes from digital insurers (instant COIs) [B, C5-S025], so the wedge is easily copied.
- **Stronger wedge:** being the bookkeeper/payroll provider that already holds the client's payroll-by-class data. That data improves WC classification and audits, and it fits a phase-2 build on SYN-006.

## 10. Economics sanity check (year 3) [E drivers; commission rates B]
Assumes an average account of about $9k premium at a blended ~11% commission, or about **$1,000 commission per account**, with 85% retention.

**Base:**
- New accounts: 35 in year 1, 60 in year 2, 70 in year 3.
- In force at end of year 2: 35 × 0.85 + 60 ≈ 90.
- Written in year 3: 90 × 0.85 + 70 ≈ 146 accounts, or **$146k gross commission**.
- With a 70/30 aggregator split, net is **$102k**.
- Costs: CSR $50k; AMS/rater $6k; E&O $5k; fees and marketing $20k; total $81k.
- **Operating profit about $21k with the split, about $65k without it.**

**Low:**
- About 74 accounts × $900 = $66k gross; $46k net after the split.
- Costs $40k.
- **Operating profit about $6k.**

**High:**
- New accounts: 50, 100, 120; average $1,300; 88% retention; no split.
- About 247 accounts, **$321k gross commission**.
- Costs: 2 CSRs plus overhead, $140k.
- **Operating profit about $181k.**

**Other figures:**
- **Book value at year 3** (base, about $146k commission × 2x): about $290k, illiquid [C, C5-S021].
- **Capital at risk:** about $8–60k, mostly the year-1 operating loss.
