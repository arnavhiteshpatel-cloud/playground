# WS02-004 — Small habitational / landlord property insurance niche (Chicago 2–6 flats) (Stage C, batch C5)

Access date 2026-11-28.

| Verdict | **DROP as a first business.** It could later be one line inside an established agency (WS02-002 as a phase-2 build). |
|---|---|
| Revised evidence level | **D** |
| Reason | The demand is real and lender-mandated: 2-unit buildings are 12.7% and 3–4-unit buildings 19.9% of Chicago's rental stock [C, C5-S028]. But the niche combines the start-up agency's carrier-access wall with a capacity crunch. The Chicago Fed reports owners of older buildings "are finding it particularly difficult to secure affordable property insurance" and that insurer consolidation is limiting options [C, C5-S028]. ## 1. Industry structure
- **Buyers** (Chicago Fed from ACS 2020–24) [C, C5-S028]:
  - Buildings with 2+ units hold 69% of Chicago housing units.
  - 2-unit buildings are 12.7% of rental stock; 3–4-unit buildings 19.9%.
  - Two-unit rental stock fell 10.7% citywide between 2010–14 and 2020–24.
- **Premium trend:** Illinois homeowners premiums rose 32% from 2018 to 2022 ($1,366 to $1,809), versus 26% nationally [C, C5-S029]. Landlord/dwelling-fire data: MISSING.
- **Agencies:** 5,424 Illinois agency firms; 75% have fewer than 5 employees [C, C5-S001].
- **Cyclicality:** the insurance-market cycle (hard market) matters more than the economy. Real-estate transaction volume drives new-policy flow.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- Small investors or LLCs buy dwelling-fire/landlord or small commercial package policies. Lenders require evidence of insurance at closing and refinance.
- The agency earns commission:
  - about 15–20% on property under a published carrier schedule [B, WS02-S016];
  - lower when placed through an E&S wholesaler that keeps part of it (wholesaler split MISSING).
- Older frame and masonry stock often needs E&S markets (Stage A, E). E&S adds a 3.5% surplus-lines tax plus a 0.04% stamping fee, paid via the licensed wholesaler [A/C, WS02-S004/S005].
- Margin is captured by carriers and wholesalers with capacity. The retail agent's value is speed at closing and portfolio servicing.

- **New-entrant unit economics:** about $4,500 premium per building × 12–15% = **$540–675 per building per year** [E].
- **Customer-count implication:** a $100k commission book needs about 150–185 buildings. Portfolio owners help, but most small landlords own 1–3 buildings (E).

## 5. Competition
- **Digital landlord insurers selling direct:** BiggerPockets says Steadily is "leaning in" while generalists pull back [D, C5-S032]. Others (Obie, Hippo) were not verified.
- **Local agencies** with established habitational carriers.
- **Captive agents** (Country, State Farm, Allstate; not verified for appetite).
- **Lender-preferred agents.**

Incumbents' weakness is speed and appetite for older buildings. That weakness is also the start-up's weakness, because appetite belongs to carriers, not agents.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the investor/owner, or a property manager.
- **Triggers:**
  - closing or refinance deadlines (days);
  - non-renewal notices;
  - premium shocks at renewal.
- **Discovery:** the lender, the real-estate agent, investor networks, online.
- **Price sensitivity:** high. Owners report rents rising 4–6% a year while costs outpace them [C, C5-S028].

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Capacity crunch:** older buildings struggle to get affordable coverage, and consolidation limits options [C, C5-S028].
- **Non-renewals:** driven by building-age underwriting [D, C5-S032].
- **Thin per-policy commission:** a generic risk.
- **Concentrated claims:** catastrophic fire or freeze losses lead to carrier exits (generic, E).
- **Illinois FAIR Plan:** the residual-market option. Its site failed to load, so its role and producer terms are MISSING.

## 8. Regulation
- **Licences** [A, WS02-S001–S003]:
  - Illinois resident producer licence (property line): 20 hours pre-licensing, exams, $215 per 2 years.
  - Agency entity licence: $150 with a designated producer.
- **Surplus lines** [A, WS02-S004]: a surplus-lines producer licence plus bond only if placing E&S directly; otherwise go through a licensed wholesaler.
- **Carrier access:** carrier appointments are subject to minimum-volume rules [A, C5-S022].
- **Gate:** G2_LICENSE WARN.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **Proposed wedge:** a 24-hour binder or evidence of insurance for investor closings, plus portfolio specialization.
- **Deliverable?** Only if the agency holds markets that write older Chicago stock. A start-up does not, and must borrow them through wholesalers or aggregators at reduced commission.
- **Speed:** digital direct insurers already bind online.
- **Copyability:** a lender referral relationship can be copied by any agency with markets.
- **Conclusion:** the wedge is weak.

## 10. Economics sanity check (year 3) [E]
**Low:** 60 policies × $550 = $33k commission.
- Costs: about $30k (E&O, AMS, aggregator or wholesaler fees, marketing).
- **Operating profit about $3k.**
- Carrier access could fail entirely, leaving revenue near $0.

**Base:** 180 policies × $600 = $108k.
- Costs: CSR $50k plus $25k overhead.
- **Operating profit about $33k.**

**High:** 350 policies × $700 = $245k.
- Costs: 2 staff plus overhead, $110k.
- **Operating profit about $135k.**
- Requires both a steady lender referral flow and reliable habitational capacity.

**Other figures:**
- **Capital at risk:** about $6–40k, including year-1 losses.
- **Book value at base:** about $108k commission × 2x = about $216k (illiquid).
