Access date 2026-11-28.

| Verdict | **DROP** |
|---|---|
| Revised evidence level | **C**. Competitor list prices are B; new-entrant volume is E. |
| Reason | Stage A assumed **$3–$10 per tracked vendor per month**. A visible competitor publishes **full-service** COI tracking at **$17.80 per vendor per *year*** (about $1.48/month) with a $10,000 annual minimum. That price includes US analysts, deficiency correction and renewal outreach. The same vendor sells self-service at $0.95/vendor/month, offers a free tier up to 25 vendors, and runs AI certificate review on every tier at about 30 seconds per COI [B, C5-S027]. Market prices are therefore 2–7x below the Stage A assumption, AI extraction is given away free, and venture-funded SaaS (myCOI, TrustLayer, Jones; pricing not public) occupies the space. A 500-vendor general contractor is worth about $8,900/year to the full-service incumbent. A small service firm cannot reach $100k operating profit without dozens of mid-size accounts won from software companies. |

## 1. Industry structure
- **Buyers:** general contractors, property managers, franchisors and facilities teams that must collect vendor and subcontractor COIs.
- **Buyer pool:** Illinois has 29,457 construction establishments; about 1,933 have 20–99 employees and 286 have 100+ (CBP 2023) [C, C5-S002].
  - Exact counts of general contractors with large subcontractor bases: MISSING.
- **Supplier landscape:** dominated by software firms with service tiers.
  - bcs [B, C5-S027].
  - myCOI (Capterra: "contact vendor") [D, C5-S038].
  - Low-end tools such as SubDoc ($14/month for 100 vendors) and RiskPartner ($49/month flat) [D, C5-S038].
- **Market size and growth:** MISSING.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
1. The client (GC or property manager) pays per vendor tracked.
2. The service collects ACORD 25 certificates and endorsements.
3. It checks them against contract requirements (limits, additional insured, waiver of subrogation).
4. It chases deficiencies and renewals.

AI has moved the extraction and checking step to near-zero marginal cost (30 seconds per COI) [B, C5-S027]. What remains is vendor chasing (phone/email) and exception judgment. The incumbent bundles both into $17.80/vendor/year at scale.

## 5. Competition
- Software players have AI extraction, procurement integrations (Procore, Yardi, MRI per bcs [B]), and free tiers that capture small buyers.
- **Where they are weak:** service quality and local responsiveness (complaints not collected; reviews = D).
- **Insurance agents** sometimes provide COI tracking free as a value-add to large insureds (not verified this session).

**Switching costs:** moderate once vendor records are loaded.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-makers:** risk managers, controllers, project administrators.
- **Process:** annual subscriptions with a 1–4 month sales cycle (Stage A, E). Tools are often discovered through software review sites and procurement-platform marketplaces (B, integrations).
- **Spend:** for a 300–500 vendor GC, roughly $5–9k/year at incumbent full-service rates [B-derived].

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Liability:** a missed lapse that lets an uninsured subcontractor onto the job shifts losses to the client and creates E&O exposure (generic; no specific case retrieved).
- **Price anchoring:** the presence of a free tier plus AI at every tier [B] makes a premium-priced service hard to defend.

## 8. Regulation
- No licence is required for document tracking.
- **Licensing risk:** advising clients whether coverage is adequate, for a fee, may implicate Illinois producer or consultant licensing. The relevant Illinois provision was **not verified** this session (MISSING) [E].
- **Insurance:** E&O/professional liability required in practice.
- **Gate:** G4_CEILING WARN.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **Proposed wedge:** a done-for-you service tier for Chicago mid-size GCs and property managers, plus an insurance-agent reseller channel.
- **Result:** fails the price test.
  - The incumbent already sells done-for-you service at $17.80/vendor/year with US analysts and deficiency correction [B].
  - Local presence is the only remaining differentiator, and it does not justify a 2–5x premium.
  - The model is copyable and copied.

## 10. Economics sanity check (year 3) [E drivers; price anchor B]
Assumes a premium price of $25–30 per vendor per year, roughly 1.4–1.7x the incumbent's full-service price.

**Low:** 5 clients × 300 vendors × $25 = $37.5k revenue.
- Costs: about $40k for an analyst, software licence, E&O and sales.
- **Operating profit about −$2.5k.**

**Base:** 15 clients × 400 vendors × $30 = $180k revenue.
- Costs: 2 analysts $80k; software $30k; E&O $8k; sales $25k; total $143k.
- **Operating profit about $37k.**

**High:** 30 clients × 500 vendors × $30 = $450k revenue.
- Costs: about $330k.
- **Operating profit about $120k.**

**Benchmark:** at the incumbent's price, 500 vendors = 500 × $17.80 = $8,900/year, below bcs's own $10k minimum.

**Capital:** $5–40k, more if software is built.

The economics only work as a free lead-generation tool for an insurance agency. Even then, digital insurers' instant COIs [B, C5-S025] and incumbents' free tiers limit its pull.
