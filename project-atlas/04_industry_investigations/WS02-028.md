## 1. Industry structure
- **US, NAICS 541219 "Other accounting services"** (includes bookkeeping), SUSB 2022 [C, C5-S001]:
  - 45,390 firms, $29.0B receipts, 247,793 employees.
  - 79% of firms (35,754) have fewer than 5 employees, averaging **$191k receipts**.
  - Firms with 500+ employees hold only 21% of receipts, so the industry is extremely fragmented.
- **Illinois** [C, C5-S001]:
  - 1,673 firms with $1.04B receipts.
  - Under 5 employees: 1,286 firms averaging $189k.
  - 5–9 employees: 231 firms averaging $731k.
  - 10–19 employees: 66 firms averaging $1.71M.
- **Productivity and pay:** receipts per employee are flat at about $100–135k across size bands. Payroll is 35–44% of receipts in small firms, and the owner is usually not on payroll. No scale economy is visible.
- **Buyer pool** [C, C5-S002]:
  - Illinois has 29,457 construction establishments, 18,685 of them specialty trades.
  - About 5,200 specialty-trade establishments have 5–49 employees, the job-cost/WIP sweet spot.
- **Growth:** MISSING (BLS not retrieved).
- **Cyclicality:** the buyers are construction firms, so demand moves with construction volume.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- The owner pays a fixed monthly fee.
- The bookkeeper produces:
  - reconciled accrual books;
  - job-cost reports;
  - the WIP schedule that sureties and banks require;
  - a year-end package for the CPA.
- Software vendors take a rising share of the stack. QuickBooks Online Accounting revenue grew 23% in FY2026, partly on "higher effective prices" [A, C5-S008]. Those price increases land on the firm or the client.
- Where the margin is captured: senior judgment (WIP, revenue recognition, retainage) and AI-assisted junior labor.
- What the new entrant actually does: a monthly close by about day 10, plus a surety-ready WIP schedule. The client receives the package and can forward it to the surety agent or banker.

- **Incumbent economics:** small-firm payroll runs 35–44% of receipts, leaving roughly 25–40% pre-owner operating margin after software, rent and marketing [E, derived from C].
- **New-entrant economics:** lower prices to win the first clients, the founder doing production work in year 1, and no brand. Bench shows that a $113M scale play failed on quality, not on demand.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- The Census distribution itself shows a large population of 1–9 person firms surviving at $0.2–0.7M receipts [C].
- No named small-start-to-scale construction bookkeeping case was found this session: MISSING.

## 5. Competition
**Competitor tiers:**
1. **AI-first software:** Pilot, Digits, Intuit agents.
2. **Human online firms:** Bench, QuickBooks Live (price not disclosed: MISSING).
3. **Offshore managed bookkeepers:** Wishup lists $299 managed and $999/mo for a "construction-trained" virtual bookkeeper [D, C5-S035].
4. **Vertical online firms:** e.g., Remote Books Online, which saturates search results with programmatic city pages.
5. **Local CPA firms and construction-specialist CPAs.**

**Weaknesses and switching:**
- Generic and AI tiers are cash-basis or limited to transaction counts, and they lack WIP/surety literacy.
- Bench's documented failure mode was delay.
- Switching costs are low in both directions, because the client owns the QBO file.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the owner, or the owner's spouse/office manager.
- **Triggers:**
  - a surety or bank asks for a WIP schedule or a reviewed statement;
  - the CPA bills for cleanup;
  - the in-house bookkeeper quits.
- **Terms:** monthly, month-to-month, with annual prepay discounts common (Bench gives 20% off annual) [B, C5-S003]. A cleanup project is typical at onboarding.
- **How buyers find vendors:** CPA and surety-agent referrals, plus search.
- **Typical spend:** $500–1,800/month (D).

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Bench** (C5-S006): over-automation degraded quality, delays drove churn, and debt-funded growth collapsed.
- **Low-end price compression** to $65–$99 AI tiers (B).
- **Generic risks** (not specifically evidenced this session):
  - key-person turnover;
  - founder-as-reviewer bottleneck;
  - error liability.
- **Customer base fragility:** about 17–18% of payroll clients churn annually even at Paychex [A, C5-S011], and much of that is business failure, which hits contractors hard in downturns.

## 8. Regulation (Illinois and federal)
- No Illinois license for bookkeeping was identified.
- The CPA title and attest work are reserved under the Illinois Public Accounting Act (225 ILCS 450). The statute text was **not retrieved** this session (ilga.gov TLS failure; FindLaw 404), so the exact restrictions on terms such as "accountant" remain unverified [E].
- Paid tax preparation would need an IRS PTIN (not verified this session) [E]. The recommendation is to refer tax work to CPA partners.
- E&O and cyber cover are commercial necessities, not statutory ones [E].
- **Time to launch:** days.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **Wedge:** "Surety-ready close." Monthly accrual books with a job-cost report and a WIP schedule (over/under-billings, retainage receivable and payable) delivered by about the 10th, at a fixed fee.
- **Why a buyer switches:** their surety or bank is asking for it, and generalist or AI services do not produce it.
- **Delivery:** QBO plus a WIP tool (tools listed at $20–70/month) [D, C5-S036], AI categorization, and a senior construction-accounting reviewer.
- **Cost:** mainly the reviewer's salary.
- **Copyability:** high. Any construction CPA can do it.
- **Defensibility:** comes only from referral relationships with surety agents and CPAs, plus delivery quality.

**Checks:**

- The founder must personally be able to close construction books in year 1, which is a real skill hurdle.

## 11. AI threat verdict
- AI is cutting the cost of categorization and reconciliation for everyone, incumbents first.
- The AI benefit is real but not proprietary. It defends margin only in the accrual/WIP niche.
- Score impact: `ai` drops from 7 to 5, and `resilience` from 7 to 5.
