# WS03-001 — Contract-manufacturing sourcing broker for CNC machining & sheet-metal parts (Stage C, batch C3)

> **Verdict: HOLD (low priority). Revised evidence level: C.**
> **The incumbent is accelerating, not slowing.** In Q3 2026, Xometry reported:
> - marketplace revenue of $238.0M, up 43% year on year
> - a 36.0% marketplace gross margin
> - 93,812 active buyers, up 20%
> - its first GAAP net profit
> - full-year guidance of at least 37% growth [B, C3-S029]
>
> **What that means for a new broker:** the platform is taking the easy, instantly quotable work. Its scale lets it spend $35.6M a quarter on sales and marketing (about 15% of marketplace revenue). That leaves a human-led broker the complex, multi-process and production-program niche: higher value, but slower to win and riskier to deliver.
>
> **The supplier-side opening is real but anecdotal.** Shop owners say Xometry payouts are low and push rates down [D, C3-S030]. Whether those shops would quote a new broker reliably is untested.
>
> **No economics for a small human broker were found** (MISSING). The founder also has no machining or quality background, which matters because quality escapes are the broker's liability.
>
> **Bottom line:** year-3 operating profit of about $25–200k, base about $70k. Ultra-low capital and high ROIC are the reasons not to drop it.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Supply:** US machine shops shipped $44.7B in 2022 across 16,876 firms [C, WS03-S007].
  - Illinois: 829 machine shops and 182 sheet-metal shops [C, WS03-S006].
  - Shops keep only about 10% of shipments after materials, payroll, services, depreciation and rent [C, WS03-S007]. That is a thin margin to share with an intermediary.
- **Brokers and agents (NAICS 425120, all products):** Illinois has 1,482 establishments, 1,150 of them with fewer than 5 employees [C, C3-S014]. Many tiny brokerages exist, but how many specialize in machined parts is MISSING.
- **Platforms:**
  - Xometry (above)
  - Protolabs network: $116.2M revenue, up 15.7% [B, WS03-S002]
  - Fictiv: lost money three years running, then sold to MISUMI for $350M [C/D, WS03-S004/S005]
- **Cyclicality:** tied to OEM capital spending and new-product development, with tariff-driven reshoring tailwinds that are volatile [C, WS03-S009–S011].

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- **Money flow:** the OEM pays the broker; the broker pays the shop, plus finishing and freight. The spread is the broker's margin.
- **Benchmark take:** Xometry keeps 36% [B, C3-S029].
- **New-entrant take:** likely 15–30% (E). Its edge is DFM help, multi-process kitting, inspection, and a local, audited supplier base.
- **What the broker must do:** quote within 24 hours, place POs, chase suppliers, run first-article inspection, and absorb remakes.

**Incumbent vs. new-entrant economics:** platforms show buyers will pay intermediaries about 35% gross margin. But that margin funds heavy sales, operations and technology spending. A solo broker has no S&M machine. It must win a handful of deep accounts, with gross profit far above Xometry's ~$3.65k per buyer.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- **No documented human-led sourcing broker** with disclosed growth numbers was found (MISSING).
- **PartsBadger (Wisconsin)** positions itself against Xometry but describes owning equipment, so it is not an asset-light broker [D, search result].
- **Platform cases (Xometry, Fictiv, Hubs)** are venture-funded and not comparable.

## 5. Competition
- **Platforms:** Xometry, Protolabs network, Fictiv/MISUMI, Chinese platforms.
- **Shops selling direct:** shops with their own estimators.
- **Manufacturers' reps.**
- **Buyer view of platforms:** some Trustpilot buyers call Xometry overpriced versus local shops, while others call it fair [D, C3-S030]. This hints at a niche for local relationships, which shops themselves fill.
- **Switching costs:** low for prototypes, high for qualified production parts. Production parts are the broker's prize, and also the hardest to win.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-makers:** design engineers (prototypes) and buyers or supply-chain staff (production).
- **Process:** multiple quotes per RFQ.
- **Supplier approval for production:** ISO 9001, first-article inspection and PPAP-lite. Expect 3–12 months.
- **Payment terms:** net-30 to net-60. Shops may want net-30 or prepayment from a new broker (E). Platform buyers pay by card or terms.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Platform losses:** Fictiv's and Xometry's years of losses before Q3 2026 show how expensive this mechanism is even at scale [B/C].
- **Broker-level risks (E):**
  - quality escapes absorbed by the broker
  - disintermediation once buyer and shop meet
  - receivable gaps
  - a founder who is the only quoter

## 8. Regulation
- **Registration and tax:** Illinois LLC plus IDOR registration and resale certificates [A, WS16-S036].
- **Insurance:** product and general liability.
- **Defense work:** ITAR (DDTC registration) only if handling defense articles. Avoid initially; the fee was not verified this session (E).
- **No professional license is needed.**
- **Certifications:** ISO 9001 is a commercial, not legal, requirement for many buyers.

## 9. Entry wedge test
**The wedge:**
- engineer-led quoting for complex, tight-tolerance, multi-process parts and kits
- 24-hour quotes
- site-audited Chicago/Rockford shops
- documented domestic/USMCA origin for buyers exposed to tariffs

**Costs to deliver:**
- CMM/inspection access ($0–10k via a partner lab)
- quoting software
- the founder's sales time

**Copyability:** the wedge is copyable by any experienced manufacturing engineer and partly served by Xometry's own human-assisted quoting. The defensible part is accumulated supplier-performance knowledge on a given account.

**Gap:** the founder would need 6–12 months to gain credible DFM and quality judgment (E). That is the critical gap.

**Working capital:** a 15-day gap (net-45 receivables vs. net-30 payables) at $1M revenue is about $41k, plus supplier prepayments.

**Year-3 range used:** $25–200k.

**Gross-profit needed per account (base case):** $8.8k per account per year, about 2.4 times Xometry's average per buyer. Only plausible with production-program accounts.
