# WS03-012 — Overhead crane & hoist inspection, maintenance and repair (Stage C, batch C3)

> **Verdict: ADVANCE to Stage D (conditional). Revised evidence level: C.**
> This is the best-evidenced business in the batch. Regulation is A-level, prices B, incumbent margins B and wages C. But it is less attractive than Stage A claimed.
> - **No legal mandate to outsource.** OSHA lets employers inspect cranes with their own designated staff. Oregon OSHA says plainly that no rule requires outside inspectors [A, C3-S003, C3-S005].
> - **Inspection is cheap.** Published inspection prices are low: $108–$253 per crane for a periodic inspection [B, C3-S001].
> - **Profit comes from repairs.** The real margin is in repair labor at $115–$160/hour and parts.
> - **The founder cannot do the work at first.** A capable entrant must first recruit an experienced lead technician. The founder cannot become a certified inspector quickly: the NCCCO-track training recommends about 2,000 documented hours [D, C3-S006].
> - **Year-3 profit is modest.** Base case is about $75–125k operating profit, and the Chicago market is crowded.
> - **Why it still advances:** a mandated recurring need, a published price list, roughly 22% service margins at the global incumbent [B, C3-S007], and an active PE exit market [C, WS03-S016]. In Stage D, test it as the anchor of a "plant & warehouse inspection" bundle (cranes plus racks, WS03-013, and scales, WS03-017).

## 1. Industry structure
- **Size and fragmentation:** Crane service sits inside NAICS 811310, commercial and industrial machinery repair: 20,481 US firms with $53.5B of receipts [C, WS03-S008].
  - Illinois has 873 establishments. 503 have fewer than 5 employees and only 103 have 20 or more, so the industry is highly fragmented [C, C3-S014].
  - US firms with $1–2.5M of receipts average about $208k of receipts per employee, with payroll at 27.8% of receipts [C, C3-S015].
- **Buyer base in Illinois** [C, C3-S014; WS03-S006]:
  - 2,779 fabricated-metal and 1,266 machinery plants
  - 473 metal service centers
  - 77 foundries
  - 111 structural fabricators
  - The share of these that run overhead cranes is MISSING.
- **Cyclicality:** Service is steadier than new equipment. At Konecranes, Industrial Service earned a 21.8% comparable EBITA margin for January–September 2026, against 7.5% for Industrial Equipment [B, C3-S007].

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
The plant pays the service firm per crane for scheduled inspections, then pays hourly plus parts for repairs, load tests and modernizations.
- **Inspection is the door-opener.** Mazzella's list price is $151–$197 for a periodic inspection of a 5–30 t double-girder crane, and $101–$131 for a monthly frequent inspection [B, C3-S001].
- **Margin sits in repair labor and parts.** Labor runs $150–$160/hour [B, C3-S002]. Parts carry roughly a one-third gross margin, judging by Alta's dealer parts margin of about 33% [B, C3-S031].
- **What the new entrant does:** sell inspection contracts, dispatch a technician, write compliance reports, and quote the repairs those inspections uncover.

**Incumbent economics** run around 20%+ EBITA at scale. **New-entrant economics** are lower because utilization ramps slowly, there is no parts-volume pricing, and a dispatcher and insurance must be carried by 2–4 technicians.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- **Uesco:** Founded in 1921 when a GE armature winder bought a small Chicago motor-repair shop. It specialized in crane motors in the 1930s and is now a crane manufacturer in Alsip [D, C3-S008]. This is real small-start evidence, but it is a century old and began with a purchase.
- **Modern evidence:** No documented from-scratch crane-service start since 2000 with disclosed numbers was found (MISSING).
- **Roll-up activity:** Crane 1's platform was built by acquisitions [C, WS03-S016], which shows that small independents exist and sell.

## 5. Competition
- **Chicago is already served by:**
  - Konecranes
  - Mazzella (Chicago page)
  - Hoosier Crane (Chicago location)
  - Hoist & Crane Service Group (Chicago branch)
  - Uesco (family-owned, since 1921)
  - U.S. Crane (Batavia, family-owned, 30+ years)
  - Simmers Crane [D, C3-S009]
- **OEM distributors bundle parts and service.** Mazzella advertises master-distributor status for Harrington and CMCO hoists.
- **Weaknesses to exploit** (unverified, no reviews collected): slow response, post-consolidation account churn, opaque repair quotes.
- **Switching costs are low for inspection** (a monthly or annual service) and moderate for repairs (trust, parts familiarity).

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** a plant or maintenance manager, sometimes EHS; corporate procurement for multi-site firms.
- **Frequency:** monthly to annual inspections, plus breakdowns.
- **Spend:** about $1.5–2.5k a year in inspections for a 10-crane plant at list price (10 × ~$150–$200 annually) [B-derived, C3-S001]. Repair spend per plant is MISSING.
- **How vendors are found:** incumbent relationships, OEM distributors, insurer referrals, search. Fixed-price inspection quotes are the norm [B, C3-S002].

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Liability:** after a sign-off, the inspector's firm is exposed. Repairs must be done by "designated personnel," and rerating requires a qualified engineer or the manufacturer [A, C3-S005].
- **Technician scarcity:** Alta lists retaining "skilled technicians" as a risk factor [B, C3-S031].
- **Scare-selling:** Oregon OSHA documents out-of-state firms cold-calling about an "OSHA required annual professional inspection" that does not exist [A, C3-S003]. The tactic exists, and it erodes trust in new vendors.

## 8. Regulation (Illinois and federal)
**Federal OSHA, 29 CFR 1910.179** covers Illinois private employers.
- "Designated" means selected or assigned by the employer as qualified.
- Adjustments and repairs may be done only by designated personnel.
- Monthly certification records are required for hooks, hoist chains and running ropes.
- **No license or third-party inspector is required** [A, C3-S005]. OSHA does not define "complete inspection" and leaves the method to the employer [A, C3-S004].
- **The direct answer to the key question:** any person the employer designates as qualified may legally inspect. That includes an outside contractor's technician, so a non-technician founder may legally employ inspectors.
- **ASME B30.2** uses designated and qualified persons. The text is paywalled and not read (E). It binds through contracts, insurers and OSHA's General Duty Clause, not statute.

**Credentials and registration:**
- **Voluntary credential:** NCCCO overhead crane inspector certification. Training providers recommend about 2,000 hours of documented experience [D, C3-S006].
- **Illinois and Chicago:** no state or city license for in-plant crane inspection was found. This is MISSING rather than confirmed absent, because ilga.gov was unavailable.
- **Business setup:** LLC plus IDOR registration for parts sales [A, WS16-S036]; workers' comp and GL/umbrella (cost MISSING).

## 9. Entry wedge test
**Buyers' reasons to switch:** 48-hour repair response; photo/voice-generated digital reports with a compliance calendar; transparent repair quotes; one vendor for cranes, racks and scales.

Delivery cost is mostly software plus dispatcher discipline (about $10–20k/yr). The wedge is copyable: Mazzella already sells crane, rigging, rack and training inspections together.

**Durable advantage, if any:** corridor route density (e.g., I-55/I-88), the lead technician's reputation, accumulated inspection histories.
