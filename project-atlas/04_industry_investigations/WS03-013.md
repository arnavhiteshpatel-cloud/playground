# WS03-013 — Pallet-rack inspection, damage repair & compliance service (incl. merged duplicate WS13-013) (Stage C, batch C3)

> **Verdict: HOLD, as module #2 of the WS03-012 "plant & warehouse inspection" bundle. Revised evidence level: D.**
> - **Founder fit is better than cranes.** No license was found, and a trained founder could plausibly perform rack inspections personally.
> - **Price looks better than Stage A assumed.** A Mazzella table seen only as a search snippet lists a "2-Day Inspection" for larger or mixed-rack facilities at $7,800–$9,800 [D, C3-S023], roughly $3.9–4.9k per inspector-day. That would be well above crane or IR day rates if confirmed.
> - **Demand is not legally mandated.** ANSI MH16.1/RMI call for annual inspection by a qualified person [C, WS03-S029], but OSHA does not require it, and dealers can bundle inspections.
> - **Engineered repair designs may need PE sign-off.** WS13-013 flagged this; it remains unverified.
> - **Pricing rests on one D-level snippet**, and smaller tiers were not seen.
> - **Bottom line:** year-3 profit of about $30–190k, with economics mostly E.

## 1. Industry structure
- **No Census code isolates rack inspection.** It sits in 811310 (repair) and, for installers, in construction specialty codes.
- **Buyer density in Illinois (2023)** [C, C3-S014; WS03-S006]:
  - 976 warehousing-and-storage establishments (NAICS 4931), 430 with 20+ employees and about 269 with 50+.
  - Among them, 765 general warehouses (493110).
  - Plus storage inside 12,465 manufacturing plants and 14,000+ wholesale establishments.
- **Market size:** MISSING.
- **Demand drivers:** insurer and corporate-EHS pressure, collapse incidents, and new rack installs. Demand tracks warehouse construction and leasing, so it is cyclical.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- **Inspection:** paid per facility or per day [D, C3-S023]. It yields a red/amber/green damage report.
- **Repairs:** uprights, beams, guards and anchoring flow from the report. They are bought from the rack manufacturer or a dealer and installed by crews or subcontractors.
- **Margin split:** inspection is the high-margin, labor-only piece; repairs are lower-margin materials plus labor.
- **The new entrant's job:** inspect, report, quote repairs, and coordinate parts and installers.

## 5. Competition
- **Main competitors:**
  - rack dealers and integrators (see WS16-018)
  - multi-service firms such as Mazzella, which sells crane, rigging and rack inspections together [D, C3-S023; B, C3-S001]
  - material-handling dealers
  - insurer loss-control engineers who run free walk-throughs (E)
- **Dealer conflict of interest:** dealers both inspect and sell replacement rack. "Independent, non-dealer" reports are a plausible pitch, but untested.
- **Switching costs:** low.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** DC general manager, EHS manager or corporate risk at 3PLs.
- **Frequency:** annual or semiannual inspection; damage-driven repairs between inspections.
- **Spend:** about $2–10k per inspection depending on size [D, C3-S023]. Repair spend is MISSING.
- **How vendors are found:** dealer relationships, insurer recommendations, search.
- **Multi-site 3PLs:** may run RFPs for regional programs, which means a longer qualification cycle.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Dealers bundle inspections free with rack sales**, a risk flagged by both records. This is E, unverified.
- **Liability** for missed damage, and installation injuries at height.
- **Non-OEM repairs without engineering backing** create exposure [E, WS13-013].
- **No closures or enforcement data** were found (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation
- **Licensing and standards:**
  - No Illinois license for rack inspection was identified (E; ilga.gov unavailable).
  - ANSI MH16.1-2023 is a consensus standard calling for at least annual inspection by a qualified person [C, WS03-S029].
  - OSHA enforces only through the General Duty Clause and through material-storage rules (not re-read).
- **Engineering boundary:** load-capacity changes or engineered repair kits may require a licensed Illinois PE. Partner with or refer to the manufacturer; the Illinois PE Act boundary was not verified.
- **Permits:** municipal permit and high-piled-storage rules for installing replacement rack vary by suburb (MISSING).
- **Setup and insurance:** business registration and IDOR [A, WS16-S036]; general liability and workers' compensation for installers.
- **Can it be done this way:** yes. The founder can be the qualified inspector after training. The RMI or other course name and price were not verified (MISSING).

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The wedge:**
  - independent (non-dealer) inspection
  - photo-tagged digital damage history with trend dashboards
  - repair turnaround in days, using a stocked set of common components
  - a combined visit that also covers crane, dock and scale checks
- **Copyability:** the digital reporting is copyable. Independence is only credible if the firm does not also push rack sales, which conflicts with the repair pull-through it needs.
- **Cost to deliver:** $10–35k (training, lift rental, software, a small parts float).
