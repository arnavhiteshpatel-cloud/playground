# WS03-014 — Infrared thermography & electrical predictive-maintenance surveys (Stage C, batch C3)

> **Verdict: DROP as a standalone business; keep only as an add-on module for WS03-012/013. Revised evidence level: D.**
> - **Easy to enter.** The service is low-capital and the founder could personally perform it after Level I/II training.
> - **But the evidence points to a commodity add-on** that is sold by:
>   - utilities, e.g., Alabama Power at $225/hour with a $900 minimum [B, C3-S016]
>   - insurers' risk-engineering arms, e.g., Zurich through "trusted vendors" [C, C3-S018]
>   - electrical contractors, e.g., Delta Wye at $500–$1,500 for a small site [D, C3-S017]
>   - in-house electricians with their own cameras
> - **Demand driver is unconfirmed.** NFPA 70B's enforceability through AHJs and insurers is still unverified [C/D, WS03-S026/S027].
> - **Revenue is capped per person.** A thermographer can bill roughly $1,100–$1,400 per survey day, and survey days are seasonal.
> - **Bottom line:** the year-3 profit that can be delegated is small (about $30–100k), and most of it is the founder's own labor (G7 warning).

## 1. Industry structure
- **Census data:** Thermography sits inside NAICS 541380, testing laboratories: 5,226 US firms with $26.7B of receipts [C, WS03-S008]. Illinois has 292 establishments [C, C3-S014].
  - US firms with $1–2.5M of receipts average about $142k of receipts per employee, with payroll at 39.9% [C, C3-S015]. This is lower revenue per person than machinery repair.
- **Who actually does the work:** most IR work is done by electrical contractors, NETA testing firms, insurer vendors and in-house staff. None of these can be counted from Census codes. The size of the thermography-only niche is MISSING.
- **Buyer base:** Illinois has 4,371 manufacturing plants with 20 or more employees [C, WS03-S006].

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- **Who pays:** the plant or facility, for one to two survey days a year (sometimes twice a year).
- **Deliverable:** a ΔT-rated report.
- **Downstream work:** repairs go to an electrical contractor, who captures the larger ticket. Thermography is often their loss-leader.
- **Insurers' role:** they sometimes subsidize or require scans and contract their own vendors [C, C3-S018]. That places the insurer as gatekeeper rather than referral source.
- **New entrant's job:** schedule surveys around plant shutdowns, scan while the plant's electricians open covers (NFPA 70E), and write reports.

## 4. Small-start evidence
None found. There is no documented case of a thermography-only firm growing past a few technicians (MISSING). Stage A also cited none.

## 5. Competition
- **Insurers:** Zurich Resilience Solutions, and HSB (page 404 this session).
- **Utilities:** e.g., Alabama Power.
- **Electrical contractors and NETA testing firms.**
- **Do-it-yourself:** cameras that maintenance staff can buy (price not verified this session; Stage A assumed about $10–35k for professional units).
- **Switching costs:** near zero.
- **Customer complaints:** not collected.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** maintenance or EHS manager, often prompted by an insurer recommendation.
- **Frequency:** annual, sometimes twice a year for critical gear (vendor claim [D, C3-S017]).
- **Spend:** about $1–5k per site per year [D, C3-S017].
- **Procurement:** quotes from 2–3 vendors, or bundled into an electrical-contractor service agreement.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Commodity pricing** is visible in the published tiers.
- **Arc-flash exposure** while covers are open.
- **No-fault-found fatigue** after the first years of scanning.
- **Missed-fault liability.**
- No bankruptcies or enforcement actions were found specific to this niche (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation
- **No thermographer license was identified** in Illinois (E; ilga.gov unavailable).
- **NFPA 70B-2023** was converted to a standard with an inspection expectation of at least every 12 months. It binds only where an AHJ, insurer, contract or OSHA citation imports it (unverified) [C/D, WS03-S026/S027].
- **NFPA 70E** requires qualified persons for work near exposed energized parts. In practice the plant's own electrician removes covers.
- **Illinois has no statewide electrical license**, and Chicago licenses electrical contractors (E, not re-verified). A thermographer who opens panels or repairs equipment may cross into licensed electrical work.
- **Electricians are expensive:** the Chicago median is $102k [C, C3-S013]. Hiring licensed staff to make the service turnkey would cut margins.

## 9. Entry wedge test
**The wedge:**
- a fixed-price "NFPA 70B compliance program" for small and mid-size plants, covering an asset register, IR, condition ratings and an insurer-format report
- a 48-hour report
- AI-assisted hot-spot classification

**Cost to deliver and copyability:**
- About $15–40k: camera, Level I/II training, report software.
- Every electrical contractor can copy it, and insurers already package it.
- The wedge holds only for buyers whose insurer demands documentation and who have no electrical-contractor relationship. That group's size is MISSING.

## 10. Economics sanity check (year 3, before founder salary; E)
**Formula:** operating profit = people × billable days × day rate − loaded staff − equipment/vehicles − overhead.
- **Day rate:** $1,100–$1,400, consistent with $225/hour × 5–6.5 hours [B, C3-S016].

| | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermographers (founder counted) | 1 (founder) | 2 | 3 |
| Billable days each | 90 | 130 | 150 |
| Day rate | $1,100 | $1,300 | $1,400 |
| Revenue | $99k | $338k | $630k |
| Loaded staff (founder excluded; −$85k per employee) | 0 | −$85k | −$170k |
| Cameras and vehicles | −$15k | −$30k | −$45k |
| Insurance, software, marketing | −$25k | −$45k | −$65k |
| **Operating profit** | **$59k** | **$178k** | **$350k** |

**Skeptic's adjustment:**
- The base case needs 260 paid survey days, which is about 130–200 plant accounts in year 3. No evidence supports that volume for an unknown entrant competing with insurer vendors and electrical contractors.
- A more defensible base is the founder plus about 0.5 technician at 100–150 total days, which gives about $60–120k. Most of that is the founder's own labor; replacing it at market rate (about $85k) leaves roughly $0–35k.
- **Year-3 range used:** $30–150k. **Capital:** $15–40k.
