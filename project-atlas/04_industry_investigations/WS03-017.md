# WS03-017 — Industrial scale & weighing service (Illinois-registered service agency) (Stage C, batch C3)

> **Verdict: HOLD (fold into the WS03-012 plant-inspection bundle; do not deep-dive standalone). Revised evidence level: D.**
> - **The founder can do the work himself.** Illinois registration is cheap and quick: $30 per serviceperson, $100 per agency, and an open-book three-hour exam on NIST Handbook 44 plus an Illinois addendum, offered nine times in 2026 [A, C3-S019].
> - **The market is crowded.** The same agency's list shows 143 registered scale service companies [A, C3-S020]:
>   - 69 have Illinois addresses, and 33 of those carry "scale" or "weigh" in their name.
>   - Mettler-Toledo (Crestwood, IL), Fairbanks (Channahon, IL) and the PE-backed consolidator Michelli are among them.
> - **Prices are low.** The observed labor rate is about $125/hour [B, C3-S021].
> - **Heavy-capacity work is capital-heavy.** Test weights cost about $5,000 per 1,000 lb [B, C3-S022].
> - **Bottom line:** year-3 profit of about $20–150k, most of it the founder's own labor.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Classification:** Scale service sits in NAICS 811310, along with crane and forklift service. Illinois has 873 establishments in that code, most with fewer than 5 employees [C, C3-S014].
- **Registered firms** (IDOA list, January 15, 2026; 143 companies total) [A, C3-S020]:
  - 58 are in the Chicago-region 60xxx–61xxx ZIP codes.
  - About 18 are point-of-sale or food-equipment firms licensed for small retail scales (subcode A).
  - 37 Illinois firms are licensed for large scales (subcode B).
- **Market size:** Illinois scale-service revenue is MISSING.
- **Demand drivers:**
  - legal-for-trade devices under the Weights and Measures Act
  - ISO and quality-system calibration
  - inventory and billing accuracy
- **Cyclicality:** demand is mildly cyclical with manufacturing and agriculture.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- **Who pays:** plants, food processors, shippers, recyclers and elevators pay for calibrations and certificates (usually once or twice a year), repairs, load cells and indicators.
- **Equipment sales:** scale sales go through manufacturer dealer programs. Dealer access for a newcomer is unconfirmed.
- **Where margin is made:** in field labor, at roughly $115–$125/hour, and in parts and equipment sales.
- **Heavy-capacity work:** truck and railroad scales need a weight truck and certified weights, so it is a different capital tier.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- **Large field of small firms:** the 33 Illinois firms with "scale" names, many of them single-location (D-level inference from the list), show that small operators survive.
- **No growth story:** none with documented growth numbers was found (MISSING).

## 5. Competition
- **Nationals:** Mettler-Toledo and Fairbanks.
- **Roll-up:** Michelli.
- **Local specialists:** e.g., Grant Scale (Carol Stream), Automated Scale (Addison), Owen Scale (Chicago), D&M Scale (Bensenville), All Star Scale (Antioch) [A, C3-S020].
- **POS firms** for retail scales.
- **Competitor weaknesses:** unknown, since no reviews were collected.
- **Switching costs:** low for calibration, higher where an incumbent installed the scale.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** maintenance, quality or plant manager.
- **Frequency:** annual or semiannual calibration, plus breakdowns.
- **Spend:** a plant with 20 scales might spend about $5–10k a year (E).
- **How vendors are found:** often whoever installed the scale, or a dealer.
- **Contract terms:** service agreements are common (for example, Avery Weigh-Tronix's WeighCare, per search results; pricing not published).

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Consolidator pricing.**
- **Technician scarcity:** the Chicago median for the closest occupation is about $60k [C, C3-S012], below crane mechanics. That means lower pay and a lower billable rate.
- **Capital for weights:** $25k or more for a 5,000 lb set (E, from $5k per 1,000 lb [B, C3-S022]).
- **Liability** for legal-for-trade errors.
- **Firm failures:** no documented closures (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation (precise)
- **Statute and agency:** Illinois Weights and Measures Act (225 ILCS 470), administered by the IDOA Bureau of Weights and Measures. The Bureau "licenses anyone who sells, installs, or repairs commercially used weighing or measuring devices" (agency page).
- **Registrations required:**
  - **Serviceperson Certificate of Registration, $30.** Exam: General Code (Handbook 44, Section 1) plus the Illinois addendum, open book, 3 hours, in Springfield, with a device test for first-timers [A, C3-S019].
  - **Service Agency Certificate of Registration, $100.** The agency must employ a registered serviceperson [A, WS03-S028].
- **Paperwork:** place-in-service reports are required for legal-for-trade devices.
- **Unverified:** test-standard traceability rules (NIST Handbook 105 or 112) and renewal mechanics, because ilga.gov returned 503.
- **Can it be done this way:** yes, the founder can personally qualify within one to three months, and a registered employee also satisfies the agency rule.

## 9. Entry wedge test
**The wedge:**
- reliable scheduled service and fast breakdown response for small and mid-size plants
- digital certificates
- bundling with crane and rack inspections on the same plant visit

Cost to deliver: a truck with liftgate, $10–25k of weights, software.

It is copyable: Michelli, Mettler and the local specialists already offer contracts. The bundling angle is the only differentiator, and it rests on WS03-012.
