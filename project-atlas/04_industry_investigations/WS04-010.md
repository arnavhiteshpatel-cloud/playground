# WS04-010 — Specialty billing/RCM for small outpatient practices (behavioral health, ABA, PT/OT/SLP) · Stage C

> **Verdict: HOLD** · revised evidence level **E** (no price evidence retrieved).
>
> **Why it holds:** the customer base is real and growing fast. Illinois mental-health practitioner establishments rose 56% from 2019 to 2023, and PT/OT/speech establishments rose 26%. The work is asset-light and gets real AI leverage on eligibility checks, claim scrubbing and denial appeals.
>
> **Why it does not advance:** the most attractive niche is being platformized. CentralReach, the ABA practice-management system used by about 4,000 practices, now sells its own managed billing service (BillMax). Federal Medicaid rules bar paying a billing agent a percentage of collections when the agent receives the payment. No small-practice fee data were found. Year-3 profit therefore rests entirely on E-level assumptions ($5k–200k). It should advance only if Stage D finds published fee schedules and a niche without an embedded billing service.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Illinois establishments in 2023 [C, C6-S003; 2019 baseline C6-S004]:**
  - PT/OT/speech offices (621340): **2,545**, up from 2,023.
  - Mental-health practitioners (621330): **2,532**, up from 1,622.
  - Both are highly fragmented: 76% and 77% of establishments have fewer than 5 employees.
- **ABA** sits mostly in other codes. The Illinois count is MISSING.
- **Outsourced billing suppliers** are also fragmented. "Other accounting services" (541219) has 1,759 Illinois establishments, 77% with fewer than 5 employees [C, C6-S003]. That code includes billing services, but the split is MISSING.
- **Enterprise RCM** looks very different. R1 RCM had $2.25B revenue and about 27% adjusted EBITDA, but it serves hospital enterprises [B, WS04-S011].

## 2. Value chain & mechanism
- **Payment flow.** Payers (commercial plans, Illinois Medicaid MCOs, Medicare) pay the practice. The billing company is paid a percentage of collections or a per-claim or monthly fee.
- **Medicare constraint.** An agent's compensation must not be related to the amounts billed or collected if the agent receives the payment [A, WS04-S012].
- **Medicaid constraint.** 42 CFR 447.10(f) says agent fees must be "Not related on a percentage or other basis to the amount billed or collected" [A, C6-S035]. Percentage fees are workable only when payers deposit directly to the provider's account. The state-level interpretation is MISSING.
- **What the new entrant would do:**
  - Credentialing.
  - Authorizations, which are heavy for ABA.
  - Claim submission and denial work.
  - Patient statements.

## 5. Competition
- **EHR-embedded billing:**
  - CentralReach BillMax for ABA [C6-S037].
  - WebPT and similar systems for therapy (not verified).
- **Offshore RCM firms.**
- **Local billing shops.**
- **Therapist platforms** that bundle credentialing and billing.
- **Switching costs** are high once a firm is onboarded. That helps incumbents and hurts the entrant.
- **Customer complaints** (D, not retrieved): aged A/R, poor communication, and slow credentialing.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the owner-clinician or practice manager.
- **Triggers:** biller turnover, cash crunch, rising denials, new payer contracts.
- **Sales cycle and risk:** about 1–3 months. The perceived risk is high because the vendor controls the cash flow.
- **Contracts:** typically 12 months with 60–90 days' notice (E).
- **How buyers find vendors:**
  - Peers.
  - EHR marketplaces.
  - Consultants.
  - State associations.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Platformization:** when the EHR bundles billing, a standalone firm's addressable market shrinks [C6-S037].
- **Regulatory exposure:**
  - False Claims Act exposure from billing errors.
  - Percentage-fee compliance.
  - OBBBA Medicaid changes from 2027, which affect ABA and behavioral health payer mix [C, WS04-S007].
- **Concentration:** one large client is often more than 20% of revenue.
- **Direct failure data:** no bankruptcy or enforcement data for small billing firms were retrieved (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation
- **State licence:** none identified for billing services in Illinois (Stage A; not re-verified because ilga.gov was unavailable).
- **HIPAA:** a BAA and Security Rule compliance are required [A, WS04-S018].
- **Federal fee rules:** 42 CFR 424.73 for Medicare and 42 CFR 447.10 for Medicaid [A].
- **Insurance:** E&O and cyber insurance (E: $5–10k/yr).
- **Certification:** CPC/CPB is optional.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **Proposed wedge:**
  - ABA or behavioral-health authorization and denial specialization for Illinois Medicaid MCOs and commercial plans.
  - Transparent denial dashboards.
  - Credentialing as the door-opener.
- **Copyability:** the wedge is copyable. CentralReach can bundle it inside the software where the data already lives.
- **What could defend it:** a payer-specific playbook (IL Medicaid MCO rules) and referral relationships with BCBA owners. Both accumulate slowly.
