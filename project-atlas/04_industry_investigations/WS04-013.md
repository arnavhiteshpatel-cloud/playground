# WS04-013 — Medical records retrieval + AI-assisted medical chronologies for personal-injury law firms · Stage C

Merged duplicate read: WS12-024 (medical and employment records retrieval for PI and workers'-comp firms).

> **Verdict: DROP** · revised evidence level **D**.
>
> The product Stage A counted on for margin, the AI-drafted medical chronology, is already a commodity.
> - **Published AI pricing:** $0.07–0.15 per page (InPractice), from $39 per case (Chronos), and $0.25 per page from Lexitas, a national litigation-support firm.
> - **Stage A's assumption:** "several hundred to a few thousand dollars per case."
> - **What is left:** retrieval, which means chasing release-of-information desks by phone, fax and portal. That work is labor-heavy and has thin margins.
> - **Cash drag:** the firm must advance statutory copy fees of $36.68 handling plus $1.38 per page.
> - **AI shifts to customers:** AI now sits with the law firms and with vendors that already have scale. For a new entrant it is not an advantage.
>
> Year-3 profit is about −$37k to +$150k, with a base case near $20k.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Illinois law offices:** 6,918 establishments in 2023, 72% of them with fewer than 5 employees [C, C6-S003].
- **PI subset:** MISSING.
- **Document-preparation establishments (561410):** only 135 in Illinois [C, C6-S003].
- **Retrieval vendors:** mostly national firms (e.g., Lexitas) plus local shops (count MISSING).
- **Demand:** steady and non-cyclical, driven by injury case flow.

## 2. Value chain & mechanism
1. The law firm, holding a client's HIPAA authorization or a subpoena, asks the vendor to obtain records.
2. The vendor requests the records and follows up with the provider.
3. The provider, or its release-of-information vendor, charges copy fees. Illinois caps these at a **$36.68 handling charge, $1.38/page for pages 1–25, $0.92/page for 26–50 and $0.46/page beyond 50**, with electronic copies at 50% of the paper rate. Copies for SSDI/SSI/VA claims are free [A, C6-S029].
4. The vendor marks up its service fee and passes the copy charges through.
5. Chronologies used to be done by paralegals or nurses. AI tools now produce them for cents per page.

## 4. Small-start evidence
MISSING. No documented small-start retrieval firm with numbers was found. AI-native chronology vendors appear to be venture-funded software companies. That would be a G5-style path, not a service business.

## 5. Competition
- **Retrieval:**
  - National retrieval firms (Lexitas and others).
  - Release-of-information vendors on the provider side.
  - In-house paralegals.
- **Chronologies:**
  - AI-native tools (InPractice, Chronos, others).
  - Legal nurse consultants.
  - Offshore summarizers.
- **Customer complaints:** the main complaint is provider delay, which no vendor controls.
- **Switching costs:** low.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Buyers:** paralegals and case managers at plaintiff PI and workers'-comp firms.
- **Frequency:** every case, several times over its life.
- **Selection:** firms trial a vendor on one case.
- **Payment:** many contingency firms pay slowly or at case resolution (Stage A, E).
- **How vendors are found:**
  - Paralegal referrals.
  - Trial-lawyer association vendor halls.
  - Software integrations (case-management platforms).

## 7. Failure evidence
- **AI price deflation:** chronology prices now run at cents per page [C6-S030–S032].
- **Digitization:** Illinois requires providers to supply electronic copies of electronic records [A, C6-S029], so manual retrieval becomes less necessary.
- **Bad debt:** copy fees advanced on behalf of slow-paying firms may not be recovered.
- **Errors:** a single bad chronology loses the client firm.
- No enforcement or bankruptcy data were retrieved (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation
- **HIPAA:** requests require valid authorizations. Business-associate rules apply where the vendor acts for a covered entity [A, WS04-S018].
- **Copy fees:** set under 735 ILCS 5/8-2001 and adjusted annually by the Comptroller [A, C6-S029]. The statute text itself was not retrieved (ilga.gov 503; Justia 403).
- **Licensing and scope:** no licence identified. The firm must avoid unauthorized practice of law, and nurse reviewers must stay within scope.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **Proposed wedge:** faster turnaround plus nurse-verified AI chronologies at a fixed per-case price.
- **Weakness:** price is set by AI tools at $39 per case or $0.07–0.25 per page. Speed depends on providers.
- **Copyability:** high. National vendors already bundle retrieval with AI summaries (Lexitas).
- **What could accumulate:** relationships with Chicago-area release-of-information desks. These are weak and not exclusive.
