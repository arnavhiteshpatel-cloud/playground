# WS04-015 — Independent biomedical equipment service organization (ISO) for clinics, ASCs, dental, vet and dialysis · Stage C

> **Verdict: HOLD** · revised evidence level **D**.
>
> **In favour:**
> - Demand is compliance-driven. For example, Medicare-certified ASCs must keep emergency equipment "maintained by appropriate personnel".
> - Small sites are a fragmented customer base: 250 Illinois ASCs, 6,775 physician offices, 5,781 dental offices and 1,159 veterinary offices.
> - Regulation is light. FDA does not treat servicing as remanufacturing. IEMA radiation-machine service-provider registration costs $100 a year.
>
> **Against:**
> - **The founder has no BMET skill.** The business must hire an experienced BMET before revenue. That costs about $82k loaded at the Illinois median of $67,380.
> - **Small-site PM pricing is entirely MISSING.**
> - **Dealers already own the relationships.** Dental and vet equipment service sits largely with dealer technicians.
>
> **Year-3 economics:** profit is about −$15k to +$235k, with a $124k base case. That base case depends on an E-level assumption of about $180k billings per technician.
>
> **What Stage D needs before this can advance:** real price lists or awards for small-site PM, and evidence that a BMET hire is feasible.

## 1. Industry structure
**Illinois small-site customer base** [C, C6-S003]:

| Customer type | Count |
|---|---|
| Freestanding ASCs | 250 |
| Physician offices | 6,775 |
| Dental | 5,781 |
| Veterinary | 1,159 |
| Home health | 1,213 |

- **Hospitals** are served by OEMs and national ISOs: TRIMEDX, Agiliti (a $7.16M DoD award), and ServeStrong (a $3.86M VA base+4 award) [A, WS04-S035].
- **Repair shops:** NAICS 811210, electronic and precision equipment repair, is not separable for medical work (MISSING).
- **Demand pattern:** acyclical, accreditation-driven, with an annual PM cycle.

## 2. Value chain & mechanism
- **Who pays:** the facility pays per-device annual PM contracts, or time-and-materials repair plus parts markup.
- **What the ISO delivers:** documentation for surveys (CMS, Joint Commission/AAAHC, IDPH).
- **Where the margin is:** technician utilization and parts access.
- **What the new entrant does:** offers a multi-vendor single contract at a fixed per-device price with same-week response.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- ServeStrong and Laparoscopic Technologies are small private firms holding federal contracts [A, WS04-S035]. Their founding scale is MISSING.

## 5. Competition
**Competitors:**
- OEM service (GE, Philips, Stryker)
- National ISOs (Agiliti, TRIMEDX)
- Dental and vet dealer technicians (Henry Schein, Patterson, Benco). These are part of distributor relationships [B, WS04-S026, WS04-S013].
- Independent dental equipment technicians
- Local BMET shops (MISSING)

**Competitor weaknesses:**
- OEMs are slow and expensive at small sites.
- Dealers focus on their own product lines.

**Switching costs:** low to moderate. Contracts are annual.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:**
  - ASC administrator or materials manager
  - Clinic or practice manager
- **Purchase triggers:**
  - Annual PM contracts tied to accreditation surveys
  - Ad-hoc repairs
- **Procurement:**
  - A quote and a certificate of insurance
  - Sometimes technician credentials (CBET)
- **How buyers find vendors:**
  - Dealers
  - Accreditation consultants
  - Peer referrals

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Technician scarcity:** this is the binding constraint (Stage A). Turnover data is MISSING.
- **OEM parts and manual lockouts:** a right-to-repair dispute (E).
- **Liability:** a patient-harm event after servicing.
- **Utilization risk:** a tech who is 50% utilized loses money (see §10).
- No small-ISO bankruptcy data was retrieved (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation
**FDA:** servicing that restores equipment to OEM spec is not remanufacturing. Remanufacturing triggers registration [A, WS04-S014].

**IEMA X-ray servicing:**
- Radiation machine service providers register annually at **$100** [A, C6-S033].
- They must provide "appropriate training" to installers and servicers; no hours are specified [A, C6-S034].

**ASC equipment maintenance:** required under 42 CFR 416.44 [A, C6-S038].

**Not required:**
- No Illinois licence for general biomedical service (Stage A; not contradicted).
- CBET certification is optional.

**Insurance:** professional and general liability. Premium is MISSING.

## 9. Entry wedge test
**Proposed wedge:**
- One multi-vendor contract per small site.
- Fixed per-device price.
- Survey-ready documentation portal.
- 48-hour response.

**Is it real?**
- Plausible for ASCs, urgent care and dialysis.
- Weak for dental and vet, where dealer technicians are embedded.

**Cost to deliver:** an experienced BMET plus about $30–60k of test analyzers (E).

**Copyability:** moderate. Defensibility comes from technician skill and route density.

## 10. Economics sanity check — year 3
**Driver:** billings of $180k per BMET (E: about 1,400 billable hours at about $130/hr).

**Base case:**

|---|---|---|

| BMET labor | 2 × $82k ($67,380 × 1.22) | −$164k |
| Parts | | −$25k |
| Test-equipment calibration/depreciation | | −$10k |
| Vehicles | | −$20k |
| Insurance | | −$12k |
| CMMS software | | −$5k |
| Overhead/sales | | −$30k |
| **Operating profit** | | **≈ $124k** |

**Conservative:** 1 BMET at $110k plus $10k parts. Costs are $82k labor, $8k parts, $8k equipment, $10k vehicle, $10k insurance and $15k overhead. **Operating profit ≈ −$13k.**

**Optimistic:** 3 BMETs × $200k plus $50k parts, against about $414k of costs. **Operating profit ≈ $236k.**

**Capital:** $60–110k. This covers analyzers, a vehicle, and the first BMET before full utilization. G1 passes because revenue starts within months.
