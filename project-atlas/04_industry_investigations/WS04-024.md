# WS04-024 — Small-generator regulated medical waste (PIMW) collection route · Stage C

> **Verdict: HOLD** · revised evidence level **D**.
>
> **Reason.** Demand is mandated and recurring, and the permits are cheap ($2,000/yr plus $250 per vehicle). But two findings undercut Stage A's case.
>
> - **Treatment capacity is controlled by competitors.** Illinois has only five permitted PIMW treatment units. Stericycle runs two and Veolia/Onyx one. AbbVie's unit appears to be captive. The only independent autoclave is St. Francis in Rockford. Every Chicago-area transfer station belongs to WM, Stericycle, Clean Harbors or Onyx.
> - **Tickets are tiny.** A dental office pays roughly $40–180 a month. Each pickup needs its own $4 manifest, because multi-stop manifests are not allowed.
>
> **Economics.** Year-3 operating profit comes out at about −$15k to +$155k, with a base case near $50k. That base case uses an assumed treatment fee, because the real fee is unknown.
>
> **Condition to advance.** Stage D should take this up only if a non-competitor treatment outlet can be documented at ≤$0.50/lb within the 10-day transport limit.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Generator base.** Illinois has about 5,781 dental, 1,159 veterinary and 6,775 physician-office establishments, plus 250 freestanding ASCs [C, C6-S003]. Tattoo studios, funeral homes and labs add to this.
- **Hauler count.** NAICS 562112 (hazardous-waste collection, where RMW haulers sit) shows only **15 Illinois establishments**, with 407 employees [C, C6-S003]. The trade is concentrated.
- **Demand profile.** Demand is acyclical and set by regulation.
- **Market size.** Illinois small-generator market size is MISSING.

## 2. Value chain & mechanism
- The generator pays the hauler per stop, per container or per month.
- The hauler moves the waste on manifests to a transfer station or treatment plant and pays a tipping fee per pound.
- The IEPA collects a per-pound transporter fee. The IEPA page quotes both 1.5¢ and 3¢ per pound [A, WS04-S016].
- **Margin capture.** Margin goes to whoever owns treatment plus route density. Stericycle earned a 38.2% consolidated gross margin in 2023 [B, WS04-S028].
- **What the new entrant would be.** A collect-and-transport-only firm that buys treatment from the plants listed above.

**Incumbent vs new-entrant economics:**
- Incumbents own treatment and run dense routes.
- A new entrant pays retail tipping fees (MISSING) and runs sparse early routes.
- Pollution insurance cost is MISSING.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- Small regional firms such as NEIE and American Vet Solutions hold Illinois federal work [A, WS04-S031].
- Sharps Medical Waste Services markets Illinois service, including mail-back.
- None of these firms' founding scale or growth is documented (MISSING).

## 5. Competition
**National:**
- WM/Stericycle
- Daniels
- MedPro (national marketing; it is not clear whether it runs its own routes or subcontracts them — MISSING)
- Sharps mail-back providers

**Regional:** small permitted haulers.

**Customer complaints:**
- Auto-renewal and escalators. MedPro's own guide warns against 5-year auto-renew terms and uncapped increases [D, C6-S011].
- The Stericycle settlement [B, WS04-S028].

**Substitute:** USPS mail-back is often cheapest for generators producing less than one box a month [D, C6-S011].

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the office manager.
- **Pickup frequency:** monthly or quarterly.
- **How they switch:** at contract expiry, often after a price-increase shock.
- **Procurement:** an informal quote.
- **How vendors are found:**
  - Search
  - Distributor reps
  - Compliance-training vendors (WM bundles compliance [C6-S012])

## 7. Failure evidence
**Structural squeeze:**
- Treatment sites are few. Of the four non-captive units, three belong to rivals (Stericycle, Veolia) [A, C6-S010].
- Rivals can price tipping fees to squeeze collect-only haulers. This is an inference (E).

**Manifest cost:**
- Every pickup needs a $4 manifest [A, WS04-S016].
- On a $40–90 monthly ticket, that is 4–10% of revenue.

**Price war:** Stericycle itself calls the market price-competitive with low barriers [B, WS04-S028].

**Not found:** no Illinois enforcement actions were retrieved (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation
**IEPA PIMW hauling permit** [A, WS04-S016; re-accessed 2026-11-28]:
- $2,000/yr plus $250 per vehicle.
- $4 manifest for each pickup.
- Delivery within 10 days.
- Enclosed, secured vehicles.
- An emergency response plan carried in the vehicle.
- A quarterly per-pound fee.

**Facilities:** deliver only to permitted facilities [A, C6-S010].

**Not verified:**
- **DOT HMR:** Category B RMW (UN3291) packaging and hazmat employee training.
- **OSHA:** bloodborne-pathogen program for drivers.
- **Insurance:** auto and pollution liability amounts are MISSING. The IEPA page states no insurance requirement.

**Who can comply:** no professional licence is needed, so the founder can hold the permit.

## 9. Entry wedge test
**Proposed wedge:**
- Transparent flat monthly pricing with no auto-renewal or escalator.
- Compliance documentation (OSHA BBP training, a manifest portal).
- Same-week start.

**How it compares:**
- It is copyable, and MedPro already markets this exact message [D, C6-S011].
- Incumbents can match on price locally while also controlling treatment.

**Cost to the entrant:** wedge cost is low, but lower prices compress already-thin tickets.

## 10. Economics sanity check — year 3
**Base case: 220 customers × $85/mo × 12 = $224k revenue.**

Direct costs, totalling **$147k**:
- Driver-tech $52k (E)
- Vehicle $24k (E)
- Treatment: 66,000 lb × **$0.45/lb (E — MISSING actual)** = $30k
- IEPA fee: 66,000 lb × $0.03 = $2k
- Manifests: 2,640 × $4 = $10.6k
- Containers $12k
- Permit $2.25k
- Insurance uplift $15k

Results:
- Gross profit $77k (34%), consistent with Stericycle's 38%.
- Overhead $28k (software, sales, training, admin).
- **Operating profit ≈ $49k.**

**Conservative case: 120 customers × $75 × 12 = $108k revenue.**
- Direct costs $103k: part-time driver, treatment at $0.60/lb, manifests $5.8k.
- Overhead $20k.
- **Operating profit ≈ −$15k.**

**Optimistic case: 400 customers × $95 × 12 = $456k revenue.**
- Direct costs $261k: 2 drivers, treatment at $0.40/lb, manifests $19.2k.
- Overhead $40k.
- **Operating profit ≈ $155k.**

**Capital:** about $40–90k (van, permit, containers, insurance deposits, and driver cost before routes are dense).

**Sensitivity:** every $0.10/lb change in tipping fee moves base-case profit by about $6.6k.
