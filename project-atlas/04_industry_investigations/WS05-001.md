# WS05-001 — Portable extinguisher + emergency/exit lighting inspection route (Stage C, batch C1)

| Verdict | Revised evidence level | Revised adj. score |
|---|---|---|
| **ADVANCE to Stage D**, with profit expectations reset sharply lower. Treat it as the licensed core of a "safety route" (extinguishers, emergency lighting, later kitchen suppression and first-aid/AED checks), not as a standalone $500k/yr business. | **D** (unchanged). Licensing and market structure are A-level. One official per-unit price is now A, but it is a government volume bid. Small-commercial pricing and pull-through remain MISSING. | 5.54 (Stage B 5.77) |

**Why:** The mandate, the licence path and the public prospect lists hold up.
- **Mandate:** annual inspection is required by code [A, WS05-S010].
- **Licence:** reachable in weeks, via an open-book NAFED exam ($320) and fees of $900 plus $300 [A, WS05-S001/S002/S025].
- **Prospect list:** Chicago Department of Buildings citations [A, WS05-S035].

But the one primary price found is **$4.75 per extinguisher for annual inspection** [A, C1-S001], far below Stage A's $10–25 estimate. Money is made on pull-through (maintenance, hydrotests, replacements, systems), so small-account revenue is likely $250–400/yr. Separately, the licence pool has been flat at 173–184 firms for a decade [A, C1-S002; WS05-S014]. OSFM has opened about 22 complaints a year with **zero disciplinary actions** [A, C1-S002], so the licence protects incumbents only weakly.

A capable entrant's year 3 is about **$45k–125k** (base about $70k). Within this batch it still ranks first, because it has a real licence, mandated recurring demand and an active buyer market for the firm later (Pye-Barker, APi, Cintas).

## 1. Industry structure
- **No dedicated NAICS code.** Extinguisher service sits inside 811310, which in Illinois is 835 firms with $2.74B receipts (2022) [C, C1-S021]. That code is broad.
- **Licence roster:**
  - Today: 184 distributors and 858 employee licences, with about 75 Chicagoland locations [A, WS05-S014].
  - 2017–2021: 173–181 distributors and 626–700 employees [A, C1-S002].
  - The firm count is flat, which signals a mature, fragmented trade with acquirers active, not a growing field.
  - 88 of 177 locations have only 1–2 licensed employees [A, WS05-S014].
- **Growth comes from incumbents' acquisitions and pricing.** Cintas Fire grew from $728.6M (FY24) to $929.1M (FY26) [A, WS05-S024]. APi's Safety Services segment margin is 16.8% [A, WS05-S019].
- **Cyclicality** is low for inspection and moderate for new-unit and system sales.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- The occupant, owner or property manager pays a licensed distributor.
- The distributor buys units and parts from manufacturers or wholesalers. Wholesale terms for a new distributor are MISSING.
- Hydrotests are done in-house with a PHMSA RIN or subcontracted to a RIN holder [A, WS05-S001].
- **Where the margin is (Boone County 2025 tab, single bidder) [A, C1-S001]:**
  - Annual inspection: $4.75 per unit.
  - 6-year maintenance with recharge: $18.50–37.50.
  - 12-year hydrotest with recharge: $18.50–47.50.
  - New units: 5-lb ABC $80, 10-lb $110.
  - Other parts: 25% markup over cost.
  - Kitchen hood system: $125 per inspection, semi-annual.
  - Fire-department report filing: $34 each.
- So inspection is close to a loss-leader on government accounts.
- APi calls its approach "inspection-first", because inspection leads to service work [A, WS05-S019]. No pull-through ratio is published (MISSING).

- **New entrant:** no scale purchasing, and revenue per technician is limited by accounts sold, not labour hours (see §11).
- Revenue per employee across 811310 is about $256k [C, C1-S020]. This is broad and not specific to extinguishers.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- Persistence of about 88 one-to-two-technician licensed locations shows small firms survive [A, WS05-S014].
- No documented case of a small start growing, with numbers, was found (MISSING).
- Pye-Barker's acquisitions page solicits small owners [C, WS05-S020]. That shows small books are saleable, not that they grow.

## 5. Competition
- **Large players:**
  - Cintas, which bundles fire protection with first aid and facility services through cooperative contracts [B, C1-S015].
  - JCI, Pye-Barker (about 246 locations), Koorsen and Fox Valley [A/C, WS05-S014/S020].
  - UniFirst's first-aid segment also sells extinguisher inspection, testing and maintenance [A, C1-S012].
- **Weak enforcement:** industry members complain about "subpar service" by others, yet no discipline has resulted [A, C1-S002]. Low-price, low-quality operators are therefore not policed. That cuts both ways: the price floor is low.
- **Switching costs** are low (annual service). Customer complaints were not reviewed (reviews not sampled).

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Small occupancies:** the owner or office manager decides, annually, often prompted by an inspector or insurer citation. Public lists exist: the Department of Buildings logged 489 addresses with extinguisher violations in 13 months [A, WS05-S035].
- **Public bodies:** buy by bid or cooperative contract.
- **Typical spend:** modelled at $250–550/yr for small accounts [E]. Contract terms are MISSING (auto-renew practice unverified).

## 7. Failure evidence
- Low unit prices ($4.75) and wide price dispersion compress inspection-only books [A, C1-S001].
- The regulator has limited personnel and oversight [A, C1-S002].
- **Statutory sunset:** the Act is still scheduled for repeal on 1 Jan 2028 [D, C1-S003], and ilga.gov was unreachable to confirm.
  - Repeal would remove the licensing barrier altogether.
  - Code-driven demand (NFPA 101 / NFPA 10) would persist [A, WS05-S010; chain unverified].
- Business-for-sale proxies: BizBuySell was blocked (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **OSFM Class A distributor licence:** $900 for 3 years.
- **Employee Class 1 licence:** $300 for 3 years. Requires an ICC or NAFED certificate (open-book, $320), age 18+, and employment by a licensed distributor.
- **Insurance:** $1M liability plus workers' compensation.
- **Hydrotesting:** a PHMSA RIN, or a contract with a RIN holder.
- **One licence per location** [A, WS05-S001/S002/S003/S025].
- Pre-2022 fees were annual ($100 and $20) [A, C1-S002].
- **Kitchen suppression:** Class B $1,500 plus a 2K employee licence. Manufacturer training is unverified.
- **Chicago:** municipal extinguisher rules and electrical licensing for emergency-light repairs are unverified (chicago.gov 403).
- **Time to obtain:** about 1–3 months.
- A licensed employee can satisfy the employee-licence requirement.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The pitch:** a dense micro-territory, flat all-in annual pricing (extinguishers, emergency and exit lights, documentation), same-week response to citations, and digital tags and reports.
- **Cost:** founder canvassing time plus about $300/month of software.
- **Copyable:** yes, completely.
- **Real wedge:** responsiveness and density, which compound only slowly.

## 10. Economics sanity check (E, from drivers)
**Per-account revenue:**
- General account (8 extinguishers, 10 emergency/exit fixtures):
  - inspection 8 × $8 = $64
  - trip minimum $35 [E]
  - 6-year maintenance 8/6 × $22 = $29
  - hydrotest 8/12 × $32 = $21
  - parts and replacements $40
  - lighting test 10 × $6 = $60 [E]
  - **total ≈ $250/yr**
- Restaurant: add kitchen system 2 × $125 = $250 plus $40 fusible links, so **≈ $540/yr**.
- Blended (75/25 mix): **≈ $320/yr**. A richer mix reaches about $400.

**Year-3 low (founder is the only technician):**
- Revenue: 300 accounts × $320 = $96k
- Less: parts/COGS 22% $21k, van $12k, insurance $6k, licences/software/marketing $9k
- **Operating profit ≈ $48k**

**Year-3 high:**
- Revenue: 700 accounts × $400 = $280k, plus $40k replacements and projects = $320k
- Less: COGS $70k, one hired technician (loaded) $65k, two vans $24k, insurance $10k, overhead $25k
- **Operating profit ≈ $126k**

**Capacity and capital:**
- 700 annual accounts is only about 3 stops per working day, so selling, not labour, is the constraint.
- 700 accounts means about 4.5 net new accounts a week for 3 years, which is aggressive.
- Capital is $20k–50k, so ROIC is about 100–250%.

**Ceiling:** about $250k/yr within 5 years requires adding kitchen suppression and emergency-lighting repair, and later licensed sprinkler/alarm staff. $500k/yr is not evidenced for a new entrant.
