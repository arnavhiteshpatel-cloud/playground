# WS05-007 — Commercial pest control for food-handling and audited facilities (Chicago)

> **Verdict: ADVANCE to Stage D, with lower expectations than Stage A.** Revised evidence level: **C**. The economics rest on Census size-band receipts, BLS wages, public bid tabs and incumbent 10-Ks; restaurant ticket prices are still D.
> The licensing case is stronger than Stage A claimed. The founder can be certified within about 1–3 months, and uncertified technicians can legally work under supervision, so the business can be delegated. The demand case is weaker:
> - Chicago requires food establishments to keep pest-control logbooks, so most are already under contract. Selling means displacing an incumbent, not finding unserved buyers.
> - Even incumbents keep only about 81% of customers a year.
> - Two-thirds of US pest firms never reach $500k in revenue.
>
> Year-3 operating profit for a capable entrant (before founder pay): **about $45k–$245k, base about $135k**. The plausible ceiling is **$500k/yr**, not $1M. This is still the strongest idea in batch C2. WS18-007 (residential) and WS13-038 (wildlife) should be modelled as secondary lines of this one company.

## 1. Industry structure
- **US size.** NAICS 561710 had 13,603 firms, $19.56B in receipts and 139,917 employees in 2022 [C, C2-S012].
- **Concentration.** 15 enterprises with $100M+ hold 41% of receipts.
- **Long tail.** 8,945 firms (66%) take in under $500k. Only 2,441 (18%) exceed $1M, and 342 (2.5%) exceed $5M [C, C2-S012]. A pest firm becoming a $1M-profit company is a top-2–3% outcome.
- **Illinois.** 330 employer firms with 3,677 employees and $527M receipts (about $143k per employee) [C, C2-S013]. IDPH counts about 750 licensed locations, so many operators have no employees [A, WS05-S011].
- **Chicago demand base.** 11,223 active Retail Food Establishment licences, 396 wholesale food and 776 taverns [A, C2-S015].
- **Growth.** Rollins commercial revenue grew 10.5% (7.6% organic) in 2025 [A, C2-S008]. Rentokil North America's commercial growth slowed in H1 2026 [B, C2-S009].
- **Seasonality and cycle.** Demand is mildly seasonal: Rollins Q1 revenue was $822M against $1,026M in Q3 [A, C2-S008]. It is low-cyclicality, but exposed to restaurant closures.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- The food establishment pays the pest firm monthly. The service covers rodent stations, insect monitoring, treatments, exclusion and logbook records.
- Margin is captured through **route density** (minutes per stop) and **retention**.
- Chemicals and devices come from distributors, a minor cost.
- **What the entrant actually does.** The founder becomes the certified technician (CT), sells door to door, and services the first ~80–120 accounts. He then hires uncertified technicians who work under his supervision.

## 5. Competition
- **National players:** Rollins/Orkin, Rentokil-Terminix and Ecolab, plus Anticimex. Rollins itself describes "fragmented markets and low barriers to entry" [A, C2-S008].
- **Local players:** Smithereen, Rose and many small firms [A, C2-S016].
- **Consolidators:** Rollins spent $309.5M on 26 acquisitions in 2025 [A, C2-S008]. Exit demand is real.
- **Incumbent weaknesses:**
  - Rentokil says its Commercial and National Accounts retention "needs more focus" [B, C2-S009].
  - Rentokil opened 70 more small local branches in H1 2026, about 220 in total, to regain proximity [B, C2-S009]. The nationals are competing on locality too.
- **Price dispersion is large.** Four bids for the same three-year job ranged from $19,260 to $236,400 [A, C2-S018].

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Buyer:** the owner or GM of an independent restaurant, the store manager of a grocer, or QA for a plant. Decisions take days for restaurants.
- **Trigger events:**
  - **Failed inspections.** In 13 months Chicago cited 2,147 licences under violation 38 (1,419 restaurants, 257 grocers, 186 schools) [A, C2-S014].
  - **New licences.** 1,613 licences passed a licence inspection in the same period [A, C2-S014].
- **Typical ticket:**
  - Restaurants pay about $100–150 a month [D, C2-S020]. One California operator's published restaurant range is $50–$422 a month [D, C2-S019].
  - Public non-food buildings bid $30–$180 a month per building [A, C2-S017].
  - Grocers and plants are higher (E).
- **Terms:** monthly service with net-15/30 invoicing.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Churn is structural.**
  - Rentokil North America retains 80.7% of customers a year [B, C2-S009].
  - Inspectors found 1,083 Chicago food licences "Out of Business" in 13 months, about 10% of those inspected [A, C2-S014].
- **Liability tails.** Rentokil carries $392M in termite provisions [B, C2-S009], so the entrant should avoid termite warranties.
- **Insurance-market hardening** is flagged by Rollins [A, C2-S008].
- **Labour.** Rentokil's colleague retention is 82.7% [B, C2-S009].
- **Enforcement.** Operating unlicensed draws a $1,000 initial fine [A, C2-S006].

## 9. Entry wedge test
The defensible wedge is **"inspection-failure recovery."**
- Target the roughly 1,200 Chicago licences a year whose inspections note live pest evidence [A, C2-S014]. Comments show existing providers failing more often than establishments lacking one: 212 say "additional service needed" versus 98 with no provider.
- Offer a fast cleanout, then monthly service with digital logbooks ready for inspectors and auditors.
- Second channel: newly licensed establishments.

Costs and weaknesses:
- **Cost:** founder canvassing time plus $50–200 of devices per account (E).
- **Copyability:** fully copyable. The defensibility that does build is route density and inspector-ready documentation.
- **Price is not a wedge.** Low public bids show incumbents will price down.

## 10. Economics sanity check (year 3, before founder salary; E unless noted)
**Accounts.** Gross adds of 100, 120 and 130 a year with 20% churn [B, C2-S009] give about 261 accounts at the end of year 3, averaging about 220 during the year. The low case is about 128; the high case is about 356 (15% churn).

**Revenue (base).** 220 accounts × $130 a month × 12 = $343k. Add 20% for one-time and ancillary work (Rollins 25% is non-recurring [A]). Total **$412k**.

**Operating profit about $138k (33%).**

**Cross-check.** At $412k, 2 technicians plus a part-time office matches Illinois 5–9-employee firms at about $100k receipts per employee [C].

**Low and high cases:**
- **Low:** 128 accounts × $105 × 12 × 1.15 = $188k revenue, minus $141k costs (1 technician) = **about $47k**.
- **High:** 356 accounts × $160 × 12 × 1.25 = $816k revenue, minus $569k costs (4 technicians, 2 office) = **about $247k**.

**Capital.** Launch about $15–45k, covering a used van or lease, equipment, stations, the insurance deposit and 1–2 months of working capital. Base ROIC is about 300%.

**Validation (~$600; detailed in Stage D).** Pass the CT exam, then pressure-test retention and win rate. Key unknown: can the founder win about 2–3 food accounts a week?
