| Verdict | Revised evidence level | Revised adj. score |
|---|---|---|
| **DROP as a standalone opportunity.** Keep it as a possible phase-2 line inside the WS05-001 fire and life-safety platform once that firm has institutional references. | **E** (unchanged). The mandate is A; pricing and spend are MISSING. | 4.69 (Stage B 5.36) |

**Why:**
- **The mandate is real but not exclusive.** NFPA 101 (2015) as adopted in Illinois requires door inspections [A, WS05-S010; C1-S004]. But the Illinois State Fire Marshal states that **qualified in-house personnel may perform them**: the choice is "entirely up to the business", and OSFM will not review checklists [A, C1-S004]. The very buyers Stage A targeted (hospitals, universities, high-rise owners) employ facilities staff who can do this.
- **Credibility walls are high for an unknown entrant.** Federal solicitations reportedly require at least three years of hospital fire-door experience [D, C1-S029]. Institutional vendor onboarding and net-45/90 payment add to the wall.
- **No per-door price was found** in any public tabulation.
- **The money is in repair work,** which may involve locks and exit devices regulated under Illinois' locksmith licensing. That is unverified because ilga.gov was down.
- **Result:** year-3 profit for a cold start is about **$10k–100k**, with long institutional cycles.

## 1. Industry structure
- **No NAICS code** isolates door inspection; door hardware work sits in 238290/238350 and among locksmiths.
- **Illinois buyer pool with clear compliance pressure** [C, C1-S022]:
  - 208 general hospitals
  - 793 nursing facilities
  - 250 freestanding surgical/emergency centres
  - plus K-12 districts, universities and high-rises
- **Chicago enforcement:** the Department of Buildings logged fire-door violations at only **91 distinct addresses** in 13 months [A, WS05-S035]. The Fire Department's own enforcement is not captured, but this is a thin enforcement trigger compared with extinguishers (489).
- **Market size:** spend data MISSING. Fragmentation is unknown; competitors are hardware distributors, life-safety consolidators, and in-house staff.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- The facility pays either an inspector (per door) or an in-house technician's wages.
- Deficiencies (gaps, closers, latches, labels) lead to repair orders. These go to the inspector if trusted, otherwise to the facility's hardware distributor or locksmith.
- Federal scopes separate inspection from repair: repairs are quoted and approved separately [D, C1-S029].
- So inspection does not automatically capture repair margin. The new entrant is an inspector who hopes to win the repairs.

## 5. Competition
- **In-house facilities staff** are explicitly allowed [A, C1-S004]. This is the biggest competitor.
- **Others:**
  - Door hardware distributors and installers, who already own the repair relationship.
  - National life-safety firms bundling doors with sprinkler, alarm and extinguisher service: Cintas [B, C1-S015] and APi [A, WS05-S019].
  - Accreditation consultants.
- **Switching cost** is low for inspection, but trust is required for survey-critical work.
- Customer complaints were not sampled.

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Decision-makers:** the facility or plant-operations director, or the life-safety/accreditation coordinator.
- **Purchase:** annual; procured by RFP or vendor-credentialing portal at hospitals and public bodies.
- **Terms:** multi-year with option years (5-year VA structure) [D, C1-S029].
- **Vendor discovery:** incumbent life-safety vendors, hardware distributors, and associations such as the Illinois hospital engineering society (unverified).
- **Typical spend:** MISSING. Stage A's $15–40 per door is E.

## 7. Failure evidence
- Inspection gets commoditised, or done in-house, while repairs go to incumbent hardware vendors.
- Institutional sales cycles outlast the founder's runway.
- An accreditation survey citation after a vendor inspection creates liability.
- No bankruptcy or enforcement data were found (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **Inspection licence:** no Illinois licence identified. OSFM requires only that inspectors "demonstrate knowledge and understanding of doors and their components" and of the codes [A, C1-S004].
- **Third-party credentials:** commonly cited door-inspector certifications were not verified this session.
- **Repair work:** the Illinois Locksmith Act may require IDFPR licensing for lock or latch work. This is unverified (ilga.gov returned 503), so it must be confirmed before any repair line.
- **Healthcare:** CMS/Joint Commission door expectations were not verified this session (cms.gov captcha history).
- **Insurance:** general and professional liability (amounts MISSING).
- **Licensed partner:** a licensed partner could satisfy repair licensing, if it applies.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The pitch:** barcoded door inventory, photo-documented reports ready for surveys, and a fast deficiency-correction turnaround. Sold to mid-size facilities (nursing homes, ASCs, private schools) rather than hospitals that require 3-year track records.
- **Cost:** the founder's time plus software. A barcode/photo app is cheap.
- **Copyable:** yes. Hardware distributors can add it.
- **Fatal problem:** because in-house inspection is legal, the wedge must beat a salaried employee's marginal cost, not just a competitor.
