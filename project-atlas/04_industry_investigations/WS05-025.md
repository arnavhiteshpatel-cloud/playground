# WS05-025 — Restroom hygiene service route (incl. merged duplicate WS13-005) (Stage C, batch C1)

| Verdict | Revised evidence level | Revised adj. score |
|---|---|---|
| **DROP as a standalone opportunity.** Retain it only as an optional SKU on a denser route that already exists (see SYN-005: hygiene does not fit the safety route either). | **E** (unchanged). Incumbent economics are A; small-account pricing, unit costs and churn are MISSING. | 4.93 (Stage B 5.44) |

**Why:**
- **Strong incumbent margins come with a small US specialist market.** Rentokil Initial's Hygiene & Wellbeing earns an **18.6% adjusted operating margin on $1,205M** [A, C1-S014]. But North America accounts for only **$146M** of that.
- **Sticky, but not that sticky.** Rentokil's North American customer retention is 80.5% (pest and hygiene combined), roughly 20% annual churn [A, C1-S014].
- **In the US, restroom service is a bundled add-on:**
  - Cintas, inside its uniform/mat route segment (24.1% operating margin) [A, C1-S011].
  - Vestis, which says existing customers use only 30–40% of its line [A, C1-S013].
  - Janitorial contractors: 2,474 Illinois firms [C, C1-S021].
- **Waste rules are not a barrier.** Illinois defines regulated medical waste by medical generation (diagnosis, treatment, immunization) [A, C1-S025], so office restroom sanitary waste is very likely ordinary solid waste [E inference]. That keeps entry cheap, but it also means there is no moat.
- **Small tickets.** With accounts at about $40–80/month and no public price list, the standalone year-3 range is **$25k–90k**.

## 1. Industry structure
- **NAICS 561790** (other services to buildings) is a mixed code:
  - US: 17,342 firms, payroll 29.7% of receipts [C, C1-S020].
  - Illinois: 480 firms, $564M receipts; 346 of 463 establishments have fewer than 5 employees [C, C1-S021; WS05-S004].
- **Market size:** hygiene-specific spend is MISSING.
- **Rentokil** calls the global washroom market "broad and fragmented" [A, C1-S014]. Hygiene organic growth is only 2.3%, a mature category.
- **Cyclicality:** low; offices are exposed to occupancy changes.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- The site pays a monthly per-unit fee covering unit rental, service, liners, refills and disposal.
- The route company buys units and consumables from manufacturers or distributors. Wholesale costs and access are MISSING.
- **Margin** comes from consumable markup plus route density (stops per hour).
- **New entrant:** places and services units at 20–30 stops a day, in competition with janitors who can do the same task while already on site.

## 4. Small-start evidence
None documented with numbers (MISSING). Regional specialists exist, but no fetched source this session gives their numbers.

## 5. Competition
- **Nationals:** Cintas, Vestis, UniFirst (restroom supplies) [A, C1-S011/S013/S012] and Rentokil Initial [A, C1-S014].
- **Cooperative contracts:** Cintas sells restroom and mat service under one public-sector agreement with first aid and fire [B, C1-S015].
- **Janitorial firms:** they can add hygiene units at near-zero marginal labour. Stage A's proposed "white-label for janitors" channel therefore makes the janitor the customer, and a price-taker relationship.
- **Opening:** Vestis is losing business [A, C1-S013], and the Cintas–UniFirst merger is pending [A, C1-S011]. Both may free some accounts. That opening is temporary.

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Decision-maker:** office or facility manager, often delegated to the janitorial contractor.
- **Terms:** monthly service on multi-year agreements (incumbent terms MISSING). UniFirst cites 3–5-year service contracts for its rental lines [A, C1-S012].
- **Vendor discovery:** bundled through the uniform/mat vendor or the cleaner.
- **Typical spend:** MISSING. Stage A estimated $50–250/month [E].

## 7. Failure evidence
- About 20% annual churn even at Rentokil [A, C1-S014].
- Vestis' net loss shows bundled route services can deteriorate quickly when service quality slips [A, C1-S013].
- Small specialists fail to reach density, or are commoditised by janitorial contractors (WS13-005 failure modes, unrefuted).

## 8. Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **Medical waste rules don't apply:** potentially infectious medical waste (PIMW) is defined by medical generation and needs licensed haulers and treatment [A, C1-S025]. Non-medical restroom waste falls outside it [E inference; confirm with IEPA wording in 35 IAC 1420.102].
- **Required:** ordinary business registration, general liability insurance and workers' comp. No special licence was identified.
- **Medical offices:** waste from patient-care rooms may be PIMW and must not be co-mingled.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **Wedge options:**
  - No auto-renew and transparent per-unit pricing, aimed at accounts unhappy with Vestis or the merger.
  - A subcontractor role for independent janitors.
- **Cost:** low.
- **Copyable:** immediately.
- **Durability:** no durable reason to switch was found beyond price and service attention.

## 10. Economics sanity check (E)
**Unit economics:**
- Per account: 4 units × $15 = **$60/month**.
- Route capacity: 25 stops a day × 20 days = 500 monthly stops. At full density that is about $360k/yr per route.

**Year-3 low (founder runs the route):**
- Revenue: 150 accounts × $60 × 12 = $108k
- Gross margin 55% = $59k
- Less van $12k, insurance $5k, admin/marketing $10k
- **Operating profit ≈ $32k**

**Year-3 high:**
- Revenue: 350 accounts × $65 × 12 = $273k
- Gross margin 55% = $150k
- Less hired route technician (loaded) $55k, van $12k, insurance $6k, admin/marketing/software $20k, unit depreciation $12k
- **Operating profit ≈ $45k.** If the founder still runs the route instead, about $100k.

**Range and capital:**
- Stated range **$25k–90k**.
- Unit capex is about $150–300 per account (MISSING), i.e. $50k–100k at 350 accounts.
- That is why ROIC falls to 5.
- Ceiling is about $10k/month.
