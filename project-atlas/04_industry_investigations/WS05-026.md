# WS05-026 — Medical, dental & ambulatory clinic janitorial (OSHA bloodborne-pathogen-trained) (Stage C, batch C1)

| Verdict | Revised evidence level | Revised adj. score |
|---|---|---|
| **HOLD.** This is a real, scalable, nearly customer-funded labour business, but a commodity with thin margins. Move it to Stage D only if Stage D lacks a better labour-route candidate. | **D** (up from E). Cost structure is C-level (SUSB payroll share; IL firm counts). Price dispersion is A-level (an official bid tab). Clinic pricing and new-entrant margins remain modelled (E). | 5.66 (Stage B 5.73) |

**Why:**
- **Buyers are plentiful and easy to identify.** Illinois has 6,775 physician offices, 5,781 dentist offices, 2,545 therapy offices, 1,159 veterinary practices and about 1,000 outpatient and surgical centres [C, C1-S022]. Payroll runs about 41% of receipts in every firm size band [C, C1-S020/S021], so the model is labour pass-through with no capital.
- **It is a commodity bid market with a weak wedge:**
  - There are 2,474 Illinois janitorial firms, 1,594 of them with fewer than 5 employees [C, C1-S021].
  - One public health-centre bid drew 8 bidders with monthly prices from $1,950 to $29,825 [A, C1-S023].
  - At scale, ABM earns a 3.6% operating margin [A, WS08-S037].
- **The healthcare angle is a modest wedge.** OSHA bloodborne-pathogen compliance is a cost every competitor serving clinics must bear [A, C1-S024].
- **Year-3 economics:** about **$30k–110k**. The 5-year ceiling is about $250k/yr, supported by thousands of US firms with $1–5M in receipts [C, C1-S020].

## 1. Industry structure
- **US NAICS 561720** [C, C1-S020]:
  - 62,970 firms, receipts $74.0B, about $69k receipts per employee.
  - 137 firms with $100M+ hold 34.7% of receipts, so a long, fragmented tail.
  - 5,402 firms in the $1.0–2.5M band and 1,789 in the $2.5–5M band. These are the size classes a capable entrant could reach.
- **Illinois** [C, C1-S021]:
  - 2,474 firms, 49,606 employees, $3.705B receipts.
  - Payroll $1.503B, which is 40.6% of receipts.
- **Demand:** cyclicality is low (healthcare is non-discretionary). Growth tracks outpatient care and medical office construction (MISSING).

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- The practice, medical-office landlord or management/dental services organisation pays a monthly fixed fee.
- The contractor pays part-time cleaners, supervisors, supplies and insurance.
- **Margin** is captured through labour productivity (square feet per hour), scheduling density across clustered buildings, and low turnover.
- **New entrant:** wins buildings or practice clusters, recruits night crews, and documents the infection-control protocol.

- **Incumbent margins are thin** (ABM 3.6%) because big contracts are competitively bid.
- **New entrant:** a small owner-run firm on negotiated clinic accounts can plausibly earn 10–20% before owner pay [E]. No disclosed small-operator figure was found (BizBuySell blocked).

## 4. Small-start evidence
- The size distribution (thousands of firms at $1–5M) shows firms routinely grow to that scale [C, C1-S020].
- No named, documented small start with numbers was found this session (MISSING). Franchise FDD Item 19 data was not retrieved.

## 5. Competition
- **Players:**
  - Franchise systems selling "accounts" to unit franchisees (names not verified this session).
  - Independents (1,594 Illinois micro-firms).
  - ABM and other nationals for hospital systems.
  - Nonprofits on public work.
- **Switching:** buyers switch when quality slips. That is both the entry door and the churn risk.
- **Complaints:** reviews not sampled. The common industry complaint (missed tasks, crew turnover) is unverified (D/E).

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Decision-makers:**
  - Practice manager or office manager (single site).
  - Property manager (medical office buildings).
  - Procurement (management/dental services organisations, hospital systems).
- **Process:**
  - Private practices: walk-through plus quote, a 2–8-week cycle, monthly contracts, often 30-day termination (E).
  - Public buyers: bid (C1-S023).
- **Spend:** a 4,000 sq ft clinic is about $1,600–2,200/month [E, derived below].

## 7. Failure evidence
- The 15x price dispersion shows lowball bidders win public work [A, C1-S023].
- Vestis' and ABM's thin margins show labour-cost pressure [A, C1-S013; WS08-S037].
- Core risks:
  - Turnover and no-shows (quality slips, then the account is lost).
  - Wage floors: Illinois $15/hr [A, WS04-S025]; Chicago higher (chicago.gov blocked, MISSING).
  - Theft or damage claims.
- No bankruptcy data found.

## 8. Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **No Illinois janitorial licence** identified (unverified; ilga.gov unavailable).
- **OSHA bloodborne pathogens** applies wherever cleaners have reasonably anticipated exposure [A, C1-S024]:
  - a written exposure control plan
  - a written cleaning schedule
  - hepatitis B vaccine offered within 10 working days, at no cost to the worker
  - annual paid training
- **Regulated medical waste** (PIMW) must go to licensed haulers. Cleaners should not handle it as ordinary trash [A, C1-S025].
- **Insurance and bonding:** general liability, workers' comp and a janitorial bond (costs MISSING).
- **Wages:** Illinois minimum wage $15 [A, WS04-S025].

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The pitch:** a written infection-control protocol and the bloodborne-pathogen paperwork done for the practice, photo-verified task completion, a named supervisor, and higher pay to cut turnover.
- **Target:** clusters inside medical office buildings, where one property manager can deliver several practices.
- **Cost:** about $3k/year in compliance plus a wage premium.
- **Copyable:** yes. The wedge is execution, not structure.

## 10. Economics sanity check (E)
**Unit economics (4,000 sq ft clinic, 5 nights a week):**
- 2.0 labour-hours/night × 21.7 nights = 43 hrs/month.
- Labour: 43 hrs × $21.50 loaded (wage about $17.50 [E] plus taxes and workers' comp) = $935.
- Supplies: $90.
- Direct cost about $1,025. At a price of $1,800, gross margin is 43%.
- The payroll-to-receipts check (about 41%) is consistent [C, C1-S020].

**Year-3 low:**
- Revenue: 15 clinics × $1,600 (bid pressure) × 12 = $288k
- Gross margin 38% = $109k
- Less insurance/bond $9k, vehicle $10k, bloodborne-pathogen compliance $3k, admin/software/marketing $18k, part-time night supervisor $25k, turnover/training $14k
- **Operating profit ≈ $30k**

**Year-3 high:**
- Revenue: 40 clinics × $2,000 × 12 = $960k
- Gross margin 43% = $413k
- Less 2 supervisors $110k, office manager $55k, insurance $25k, vehicles $25k, sales/admin $40k, turnover $40k, bloodborne-pathogen compliance $8k
- **Operating profit ≈ $110k**

**Working capital:** payroll is biweekly and receivables are net-30, so working capital is about 6 weeks of labour ($15k–110k). It grows with revenue. This is the real capital need.

**Ceiling:** $250k/yr requires about $2.5M revenue at 10%, which is a 5–7-year path.
