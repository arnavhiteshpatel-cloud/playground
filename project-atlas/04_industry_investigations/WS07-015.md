# WS07-015: Licensed customs brokerage for small/mid importers, with the founder sitting the CBLE (Stage C, batch C4)

> **Verdict: DROP** as the first business. Keep the licence on the list as an optional credential.
> Evidence: **D** (unchanged). Licensing facts are A; per-entry prices B; volume E.
>
> **Why.** Three things work against it.
>
> **Slow, uncertain path to a licence.** The exam is held only twice a year and pass rates are low: 13% on 28 Oct 2026,
> 22% on 22 Apr 2026 and 12% on 22 Oct 2025, all before appeals [A, C4-S023]. The passing mark is 75% [A, C4-S026].
> The earliest sitting is April 2027. CBP then has to approve the licence, which takes an unstated time (MISSING). Even
> on the best path, first revenue is about 2028.
>
> **Low price anchors.** Online broker ClearIT publishes entry fees of **$89.95 (truck), $129.95 (air) and $149.95
> (ocean)**, each including 3 HTS codes. ISF filing is $50 and disbursement 5% [B, C4-S022].
>
> **Big licensed field.** There were 13,952 active individual licences in 2022, with about 430–450 new ones a year
> [A, C4-S025]. A corporation needs only one officer who holds a licence [A, C4-S026], so a licensed hire could get
> round the exam. That route still leaves an inexperienced owner competing on price.
>
> **Result:** year-3 profit of about $0–120k.

## Industry structure
- **Licensed population** [A, C4-S025]:
  - 13,952 active individual licences (January 2022);
  - CBP projected about 14,828 certifying by 2027;
  - about 39% of employers of individual brokers are small businesses.
- **Firm structure:** about 96% of firms in NAICS 488510 are small [A, C4-S025].
- **Demand drivers:**
  - De minimis is suspended indefinitely, with repeal in July 2027 [A/C, WS07-S032];
  - tariff complexity (Sections 232 and 301);
  - IEEPA refunds through CAPE (one-time) [C, WS07-S030, S031].
- **Who handles small parcels:** express integrators (UPS, FedEx, DHL) clear most small-parcel imports with their own
  brokerage. That claim is E, not verified this session.
- **Illinois:** Chicago is a major air-cargo and rail hub (WS07 summary). Local importer counts are MISSING.

## Value chain & mechanism
The importer pays the broker for:
- per-entry fees;
- per-line fees;
- ISF;
- partner-agency (PGA) fees;
- bond resale;
- a disbursement fee on advanced duties [B, C4-S022].

Government fees pass through: merchandise processing fee (MPF) of 0.3464% (minimum $33.58, maximum $651.50) and harbor
maintenance fee (HMF) of 0.125% [B, C4-S022].

Margin comes from entry-writer productivity, classification expertise and post-entry services, which ClearIT prices
at $200 each [B, C4-S022].

**What a new entrant can earn.** Total broker fees per entry are roughly $150–250 once lines, ISF and handling are
added (E, built from C4-S022). Reaching $500k in revenue therefore needs about 2,500 entries a year, roughly 10 per
business day.

## Small-start evidence
Most of the market is small firms [A, C4-S025]. No named start-small growth case with numbers was retrieved (MISSING).

## Competition
- **National forwarder-brokers.**
- **Integrators' in-house brokerage.**
- **Low-price online brokers** such as ClearIT.
- **Freight forwarders,** which can receive referral fees. Others cannot: 19 CFR 111.36 [A, WS07-S028].

**AI-assisted classification** tools reach incumbents too.

**Switching costs** are moderate: the broker holds powers of attorney, bonds and classification history.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the importer's operations or finance lead.
- **When they buy:** per shipment.
- **How they choose a broker:** most often through their forwarder.
- **Cost sensitivity:** high for small importers. ClearIT advertises flat prices.
- **Contract terms:** a power of attorney plus a continuous bond; ACH payment of duties.

## Failure evidence
- **Strict broker liability, audits and penalties.** A CBP audit of a broker averages 593 hours. Monetary penalties
  against individual brokers averaged $2,423 [A, C4-S025].
- **Exam failure:** 78–88% of candidates fail each sitting [A, C4-S023].
- **Policy risk:** tariff reversals shrink entry complexity.
- **Working-capital risk** if the broker advances duties for clients.

## Entry wedge test
**The pitch:** fast, tech-enabled service for Midwest manufacturers newly hit by complex tariffs, plus AI-drafted
classifications reviewed by the licensed broker.

**Why it fails as a wedge:**
- online brokers already advertise a $90–150 flat price [B, C4-S022];
- forwarders control the referral flow;
- it is copyable.

**What would make it stronger:** a niche such as drawback (WS14-013) or a specific industry's HTS chapters, not general
entries.
