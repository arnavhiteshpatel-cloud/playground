# WS07-023 — Online consignment auctions for business liquidations (restaurant/industrial/office assets)

> **Verdict: HOLD**
> - **Revised evidence level:** D (incumbent take rates A; small-operator volume E).
> - **Year-3 operating profit:** $20–200k (base ≈ $90k).
>
> **The licensing question is now answered.** Public Act 104-0145, effective 2026-01-01:
> - adds **online auctions** to the activities that require an Illinois auction license;
> - removes online auctions from the "Internet auction listing service" carve-out [C, C9-S032; A, C9-S029].
>
> A firm running timed online auctions for other people's assets therefore needs a licensed auctioneer. The online-auction exemption "with certain conditions" could not be read because ILGA was down (MISSING).
>
> **The license is obtainable** [A, C9-S030]:
> - PSI exam plus a $200 application.
> - Auction firm registration: $100 plus a licensed managing auctioneer.
> - Special (escrow) accounts.
>
> The gate is a feasibility cost, not a fail.
>
> **Economics, incumbents (A):** the market leader earns about **9.8%** of GMV on government consignments (GovDeals) and **18.6%** on capital assets (CAG) [A, C9-S033].
>
> **Economics, new entrants:** small-operator GMV and local competition are unmeasured. Consignment float is real, but the business is labor-heavy (cataloging, pickups).

## Industry structure
- **Size:** no official size for business-liquidation auctioneering.
- **Incumbent scale (Liquidity Services, FY2026)** [A, C9-S033]:
  - GMV $1.645B;
  - consignment and fee revenue $182.6M;
  - consignment was 86% of Q4 GMV.
- **Census proxy:** NAICS 561990 (all other support services, which includes independent auctioneers) has 12,567 U.S. establishments and 487 in Illinois [C, C9-S007]. It is a weak proxy.
- **Cyclicality:** mildly counter-cyclical. Closures and repossessions rise in downturns.
- **Illinois licensing reach:** licensing now also covers estate sales run for compensation (IDFPR FAQ) [A, C9-S029]. Estate-sale operators must become licensed. Some will likely move into online business auctions too (analyst inference).

## Value chain & mechanism
1. A seller (closing business, lender, receiver, landlord) consigns assets.
2. The auctioneer catalogs and photographs them, then runs a timed online sale on a platform (e.g., HiBid-type sites; fees MISSING).
3. Buyers pay the hammer price plus a buyer's premium before pickup.
4. The auctioneer remits net proceeds to the seller from a special account.

**Where margin sits:**
- buyer's premium plus seller commission;
- minus cataloging labor, pickup supervision, platform fees and marketing.

**Cash flow:** buyers pay first, so the business carries positive float.

## Competition
- **National platforms:** GovDeals/AllSurplus (government sellers) and the industrial auction houses.
- **Local operators:** Illinois licensed auctioneers and estate-sale firms running online sales (names and counts MISSING; the IDFPR license lookup was not queried).
- **Informal channels:** used-restaurant-equipment dealers who buy outright, and Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist for small closings.

**Incumbent weaknesses** (D, Stage A):
- large houses ignore sales under ~$50k;
- slow settlement.

**Seller switching costs:** none. Each sale is a new decision.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Sellers:** owners closing a business (one-time), equipment lenders, receivers and bankruptcy trustees (repeat), and landlords.
- **Selection:** based on speed, the net-proceeds track record, and referrals from brokers, lenders and attorneys.
- **Terms:** consignment contract with a commission schedule; settlement in days to weeks.
- **Buyers:** they aggregate on platforms; incumbent participation is growing [A, WS07-S036].

## Failure evidence
- **Supply risk:** too few consignments; recovery rates are falling in Liquidity Services' retail segment [A, WS07-S036].
- **Regulatory risk:** unlicensed operation post-2026. No enforcement data was retrieved.
- **Operational risk:** pickup no-shows and misdescription disputes (E).

## Regulation (Illinois + federal)
**Licensing — Auction License Act (225 ILCS 407):**
- An auction license is required to conduct auctions for others [A, C9-S031]. P.A. 104-0145 extends this to online auctions and estate sales from 2026-01-01; conditional exemptions are MISSING [C, C9-S032].
- **Individual auctioneer licensed by exam:**
  - PSI exam report;
  - **$200** non-refundable application fee;
  - high school diploma or GED;
  - personal history disclosures;
  - 45-day permit sponsor card (self-sponsorship allowed);
  - consent to audit special accounts.

  Pre-licensing course and exam fee: MISSING [A, C9-S030].
- **Auction firm:** **$100** initial fee and a named licensed managing auctioneer [A, C9-S030]. A licensed employee or partner can therefore satisfy the requirement.
- **Sunset:** the Act is scheduled for repeal 2030-01-01, so a sunset review is coming [C, C9-S032].

**Tax:** Illinois Retailers' Occupation Tax collection on auction sales.

**Time to license:** 1–3 months (E).

## Entry wedge test
- **Wedge:** fast (≤14-day) turnaround and transparent net-proceeds reporting for $10–100k closings that large houses skip.
- **Referral channels:** restaurant brokers, equipment lenders and receivers.
- **Delivery cost:** low (photo kit, platform, crew). AI drafts listings and pricing comps.
- **Copyability:** copyable by licensed local auctioneers. Durable edges are referral-partner density and buyer-list depth in one metro.
