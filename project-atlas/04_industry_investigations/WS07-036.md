# WS07-036: Managed transportation / outsourced logistics department for mid-size shippers (Stage C, batch C4)

> **Verdict: DROP as an initial entry.** It may be revisited later as an adjacency to a freight-agent or brokerage
> path (WS07-002/001). Evidence: **D** (unchanged).
>
> **Why.**
> 1. **The service is legally brokerage.** Stage A assumed a fee-based model could avoid broker status. It cannot. A
>    broker is anyone who, "as a principal or agent," negotiates for or arranges motor-carrier transportation for
>    compensation [A, C4-S028]. FMCSA's rule says brokerage "can be performed on behalf of a motor carrier, consignor,
>    or consignee" [A, C4-S029]. So tendering loads and negotiating with carriers for a shipper, for a fee, needs broker
>    authority. That means $75k of financial security, and since January 2026 BMC-85 trusts may hold only cash, ILOCs
>    or Treasuries [A, WS07-S001]. Only pure advice, where the shipper itself tenders, escapes, and that is worth much
>    less.
> 2. **Published pricing is far below the Stage A assumption.** It is $15–25 per shipment for a full program
>    [D, C4-S030]. A 100–250-load-a-month shipper therefore pays $1.5–6k a month, not the assumed $3–15k.
> 3. **Incumbents already target this exact segment.** Transportation Insight (Beon) manages $14.4B of spend, reports
>    97% retention and sells to SMB/mid-market shippers [D, C4-S032]. CHR Managed Solutions and RXO Managed
>    Transportation (being acquired by CHR) [A, WS07-S010] compete at the top.
>
> A founder without a logistics track record faces a CFO-level credibility wall. Year 3: about $0–100k.

## Industry structure
- **Freight market (A).** CHR's NAST segment billed $3.83B in Q3 2026 at about 13.5% adjusted gross profit (AGP)
  [A, WS07-S008]. RXO's brokerage gross margin was 12.6% [A, WS07-S010].
- **Consolidation.** CHR is buying RXO at an implied $5.8B [A, WS07-S010].
- **Cycle.** 2026 brought supply-driven tightening, with carrier cost per mile up 33.5% [A, WS07-S008]. Freight became
  a CFO problem.
- **Market size.** Managed transportation (MTS) has no clean Census code; it sits inside NAICS 488510 (transportation
  arrangement). About 96% of NAICS 488510 firms are small [A, C4-S025]. A precise MTS market size is MISSING.
- **Buyer base.** Midwest mid-size manufacturers and distributors with $1–15M freight spend (Stage A). Their count is
  MISSING.

## Value chain & mechanism
1. The shipper pays carriers directly, or through a pass-through account.
2. The MTS provider is paid per load, as a percentage of spend (sources disagree: 1–3% vs 3–7% [D, C4-S031]), or as a
   monthly retainer.
3. Some providers add a 5–15% "lift" on loads they broker themselves [D, C4-S030].
4. One-time setup runs $5k–45k (spreadsheet integration $5–10k; EDI about $35k) [D, C4-S030].

**Where margin sits:** in the analyst labor leverage of a TMS (transportation management system) and in brokering
spot loads. That is why large MTS firms are also brokers.

**Incumbent economics** rely on TMS platforms, carrier networks and brokerage float.

**New-entrant economics** are analyst-hours bounded. Each analyst (BLS logistician median $79,900 in Chicago; about
$100k loaded) [C, C4-S016] can serve roughly 3–5 clients (E).

## Small-start evidence
Transportation Insight has operated 25+ years and now manages $14.4B [D, C4-S032]. Its founding-stage size is not
disclosed (MISSING).

No documented case of a non-logistics founder building an MTS from zero was found.

## Competition
- **Large providers:** CHR Managed Solutions, RXO MT (merging into CHR), Uber Freight/Transplace, Echo, Transportation
  Insight/Beon.
- **Mid-market specialists:** InTek, Nuvocargo, regional 3PLs.
- **Brokers** that offer "dedicated account teams" for free inside their margin.

**Switching costs are high once a TMS is integrated.** That protects incumbents, and new entrants then face long
cycles.

**What shippers complain about:** gain-share "payment fatigue" is documented in the adjacent parcel market
[D, C4-S033]. MTS-specific complaints are MISSING.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Who decides:** VP Ops or CFO.
- **Process:** RFP or formal evaluation over 3–9 months (E).
- **Contracts:** 1–3 years, with implementation fees.
- **How they find vendors:** existing broker relationships, consultants, trade shows.
- **Proof required:** references and a TMS demonstration.
- **Fit for this founder:** poor. The founder brings no logistics reference accounts.

## Failure evidence
- RXO's gross margin fell 90 bp year on year [A, WS07-S010]. Contract margins compress when spot rates spike.
- Shippers revert to brokers in soft markets (Stage A blind rationale).
- The regulatory exposure: penalties for unauthorized brokerage under the dispatch-service guidance [A, WS07-S006].

## Regulation
- **Broker authority:** OP-1 via the Motus registration system, with identity proofing and at least 8 business days
  [A, WS07-S002].
- **Financial security:** $75k (BMC-84 surety bond, whose premium depends on credit (MISSING; founder's credit is
  unknown), or a BMC-85 trust in cash/ILOC/Treasuries) [A, WS07-S001].
- **Also required:** BOC-3 and UCR.
- **Pure-advisory model:** may avoid authority if the shipper's own staff tenders and the firm never negotiates for
  carriers. That requires a legal opinion (E).

## Entry wedge test
- **Wedge:** a freight-spend diagnostic using public rate data for $1–15M shippers below big-3PL thresholds.
- **Assessment:** an analytical founder can produce it. But it is a one-off project, and converting it into an
  outsourced department requires a TMS, carrier relationships, broker authority and references.
- **Copyable:** easily. Brokers give the diagnostic away free.
