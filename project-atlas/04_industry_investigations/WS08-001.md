# WS08-001 — DLA DIBBS spare-parts dealer (approved-source NSN quoting, drop-ship)

> **Verdict: HOLD** · Revised evidence level: **C** (revenue outcomes A; margins E) · Year-3 operating profit **$0–120k** (base ≈ $35k)
>
> I tested this with the A-level award data Stage A lacked. Buyers really are mechanically open. But the base rate for new entrants is poor. Of 523 small businesses that first won DLA hardware-spares awards in FY2023, only 34% were still winning in FY2025 (year 3). Only 23 of them (4.4%) received ≥$500k [A, C9-S002], and that cohort includes manufacturers. Dealer gross margin is still unmeasured [E]. Even a top-5% outcome ($500k revenue at 15–20% gross margin) yields only ~$75–100k gross profit. Keep it as a low-cost desk validation (a price-gap study on 50 NSNs), not a primary candidate.

## Industry structure
- **Scope.** FY2025 DLA awards ≤$350k to small businesses in ten hardware FSC groups (16, 25, 29, 30, 31, 43, 47, 48, 53, 59):
  - 336,313 awards worth $2.367B, averaging **$7,039** [A, C9-S002].
  - Vendor counts: 3,386 with positive obligations; median **$83k**; 702 at ≥$500k and 416 at ≥$1M.
- **Fragmentation.** Top-10 share is 19% and top-100 share 55%.
- **Growth.** The segment grew from $1.55B (FY2021) to $2.37B (FY2025) [A, C9-S002].
- **All DLA small-business product buying, FY2025:** 5,959 vendors, $22.5B, dominated by food and fuel prime vendors [A, C9-S001].
- **Wholesale proxy.** NAICS 423840 (industrial supplies wholesalers) has 9,605 U.S. establishments, 71% of them with <10 employees, and 442 in Illinois [C, C9-S007].
- **Cyclicality.** Low; DoD readiness demand is budget-driven.
- **Long-run headwind.** Common-goods buying is being consolidated into GSA vehicles (EO 14240, RFO; WS08 summary).
- **FY2026 data gap.** FY2026 shows only 245k awards because of DoD's 90-day publication lag. It is not a demand collapse [A, C9-S002].

## Value chain & mechanism
1. DLA posts automated RFQs on DIBBS for NSNs. Each NSN has approved-source CAGE/part numbers.
2. A dealer quotes the approved part, buys it from the OEM or an authorized distributor, packages and marks it to MIL-STD-129/2073, and ships to a depot.
3. Payment comes ~30 days after acceptance (FAR 32.904, WS08-S009).

**Where the margin sits:**
- On sole-source or approved-source items, margin sits with the OEM. DoD IG audits found DLA overpaid sole-source OEMs: up to 831% versus other suppliers for Ontic parts, and an average 392% for Bell [C, C9-S034].
- The IG also found exclusive-distributor arrangements lengthen lead times [C, C9-S034]. OEMs therefore control both access and price.
- The dealer's spread is whatever is left between the competitive award price and the distributor's price to an unknown reseller. That spread is **MISSING**.

## Small-start evidence
Several FY2023–FY2024 first-time vendors reached $1–3M within 2–3 years:
- Critical Procurement LLC: $0.55M → $2.16M (FY2024–FY2026).
- Cap 360 LLC: ~$1.1–1.5M.
- Hyrize Business Management & Consulting: $29k → $1.16M [A, C9-S002].

Whether these are pure dealers, and what they earn, is unknown. Some may be spin-offs of experienced operators.

## Competition
- Thousands of automated quoting dealers.
- Fast Auto Evaluation RFQs at or below the $15k micro-purchase threshold are **not set aside**, so large firms compete for small buys (WS08-S033).
- Incumbents already run price-history models and auto-quoting, so AI is table stakes.
- Main weaknesses of incumbents: quality defects (PQDRs), late delivery and packaging non-compliance. These rarely shift awards, which are price-led.
- Switching costs for DLA are zero. Every buy is re-competed.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** an automated evaluation, with contracting officers for exceptions.
- **Frequency:** daily postings, short response windows, and awards sometimes made before the return date (WS08-S033).
- **Typical ticket:** ~$7k.
- **Contract terms:** fixed-price POs; acceptance at destination; net-30.
- **Vendor discovery:** none needed. Quotes are solicited from anyone with a SAM/CAGE registration.

## Failure evidence
- Year-3 attrition of **66%** among first-year winners [A, C9-S002] is the dominant failure fact.
- Mechanisms (Stage A, D-level):
  - pricing errors on auto-awards;
  - supplier price increases after award;
  - PQDR/PIPRS score erosion leading to exclusion;
  - OEMs refusing to sell to resellers or quoting DLA directly;
  - cash strain when awards cluster.

## Regulation (federal; no Illinois license)
- **SAM.gov:** UEI plus CAGE, renewed every 365 days, free (WS08-S031).
- **Nonmanufacturer rule:** does not apply to set-asides between the MPT and the SAT (WS08-S012).
- **Joint Certification Program (DD2345):** needed for export-controlled drawings. MISSING verification.
- **Compliance obligations:** traceability-to-approved-source documentation, Buy American/Berry, counterfeit-parts rules. dla.mil returned 403, so clause text was not read.
- **Licensing:** no license, bond or exam. A new firm can register in weeks.

## Entry wedge test
- **Stated wedge:** FSC niche focus, accurate traceability, and fast automated quoting.
- **Why a buyer would "switch":** the buyer doesn't switch; the lowest acceptable quote wins. The wedge is therefore cost of goods (supplier pricing) plus quoting coverage.
- **Copyability:** quoting automation is copyable and already common.
- **The real wedge:** a distributor line at a better price. Whether a new reseller can get one is **MISSING** and cannot be tested without supplier contact (not authorized).
