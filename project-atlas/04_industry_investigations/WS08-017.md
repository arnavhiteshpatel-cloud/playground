# WS08-017 — E-Rate Category 2 network integrator for schools & libraries

> **Verdict: HOLD** (keep only as part of a broader K-12/library managed-network business, not as a stand-alone E-Rate play)
> **Revised evidence level:** C (market and new-entrant data A; margins C/E)
> **Year-3 operating profit:** $10–120k
>
> USAC open data gives an unusually clean view of how new entrants fare. Of 196 providers that first won Illinois C2 funding in FY2018–FY2022:
> - their median best year was **$43k** of pre-discount Illinois funding;
> - 35% ever reached $100k, and **7% ever reached $500k** [A, C9-S003].
>
> Two of the old fears are smaller than assumed:
> - **Cash-cycle risk:** USAC invoice review is fast (median 6 days) [A, C9-S004].
> - **Program risk:** the Supreme Court decided the USF challenge on 2025-06-27 [A, C9-S005].
>
> The binding constraints are instead small market size per provider, CDW-scale price competition, consultant gatekeeping, and prevailing-wage labor rules.

- **Concentration (FY2025):**
  - CDW Government: $13.3M (32%).
  - Only 9 providers topped $1M, 28 topped $250k and 55 topped $100k.
  - The 50th provider received $118k.
- **Who buys:** school districts placed 768 of the funded FRNs, private schools 89 and libraries 83.
- **Typical project:** the median Internal Connections FRN is **$21k** (mean $57k).
- **National market:**
  - $1.3–1.6B per year of funded Internal Connections [A, WS08-S042].
  - 1,907 providers; 522 of them at ≥$250k [A, WS08-S041].
- **Contractor universe:** NAICS 238210 (electrical contractors, which includes low-voltage firms) has 83,342 U.S. establishments and 2,689 in Illinois [C, C9-S007].
- **Demand pattern:** counter-cyclical to nothing. It is subsidy-paced, lumpy, and peaks in the first year of each five-year C2 cycle.

## Value chain & mechanism
1. The applicant posts FCC Form 470.
2. Bids run at least 28 days.
3. The applicant selects the most cost-effective bid (price is the primary factor).
4. The applicant files Form 471.
5. USAC issues a funding commitment.
6. The provider installs equipment (summer; service year starts July 1).
7. The provider invoices USAC for the discounted share (SPI) or the applicant pays and seeks reimbursement (BEAR) [A, WS08-S026].

**Where margin sits:**
- Equipment margin is set by manufacturer deal registration and distributor pricing.
- Installation labor.
- Multi-year maintenance or managed-broadband contracts.
- **Consultants** (51 firms in Illinois) sit on **76%** of FRNs. They write the 470s and steer evaluation, so they are the real channel [A, C9-S003].

## Operator economics
- **Incumbent benchmark:** CDW, gross margin 21.7% and operating margin 7.4% [A, WS08-S037].
- **Illinois regional integrators (FY2025 C2 funding):** Heart Technologies $1.81M, Low Voltage Solutions $1.70M, Tympani $1.63M, Quality Network Solutions $1.46M [A, C9-S003]. Their margins are not disclosed.
- **New entrants:** Forward Edge (first in Illinois in FY2023; $730k in FY2025) and Data Center Warehouse (first in FY2024; $595k) [A, C9-S003]. Both appear to be established out-of-state firms entering Illinois, not start-ups. This is analyst inference from names.
- **Year-3 new-entrant reality** (FY2018–22 cohort):
  - 38% still funded in Illinois;
  - median active funding **$32k**;
  - 11 of 196 at ≥$250k [A, C9-S003].
- **Revenue per employee:** MISSING.

## Small-start evidence
None verified. No Illinois provider that appeared after FY2017 has shown a documented ramp from zero to more than $1M without being an existing firm.

## Competition
- **Players:** national VARs (CDW, SHI, Presidio), Illinois low-voltage firms, and regional ISPs.
- **Bid intensity:** median 2–3 bids per FRN; 20–35% of FRNs received only one bid (FY2019–FY2026) [A, C9-S003].
- **Switching:** 52% of Illinois applicants with Internal Connections in both budget cycles changed their main provider between FY2016–20 and FY2021–25 [A, C9-S003]. Switching is real; loyalty is weak.
- **Incumbent weaknesses:** CDW-type firms are weak on small rural districts and on paperwork support (D, Stage A).

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-makers:** the district technology director or superintendent, usually advised by an E-Rate consultant.
- **Frequency:** annual 470/471 cycle. C2 budgets reset every five years.
- **Ticket:** ~$21k median FRN.
- **Discovery:** vendors find opportunities through Form 470 postings (public) and consultants' bidder lists.
- **Contract terms:** typically one year, with voluntary extensions. Maintenance contracts run multi-year.

## Failure evidence
- **Attrition:** about half of new Illinois providers had no funding two or more years after entry [A, C9-S003].
- **Invoice reductions:** USAC approves only 91.5% of requested SPI invoice dollars [A, C9-S004]. That is roughly 8.5% leakage before any resubmission.
- **Denied or late FRNs:** if an FRN is denied after work is done, the work is unpaid. Denial rates for new providers are MISSING.

## Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **E-Rate registration:**
  - FCC Registration Number;
  - FCC Form 498 (SPIN);
  - USAC EPC account;
  - Red Light rule, competitive-bidding and gift rules, CIPA (all free) [A, WS08-S026].
- **Illinois Prevailing Wage Act:**
  - It covers all fixed works constructed by any public body, and "construction" includes maintenance [A, C9-S006].
  - School cabling and installs are therefore very likely covered (analyst reading). That means certified payroll and prevailing-wage rates for technicians.
- **Local electrical and low-voltage licensing:** municipal in Illinois (no statewide license). Requirements by city are **MISSING**.
- **Certifications and insurance:** manufacturer partner certifications; general liability, workers' compensation, and possibly bonds for larger districts.
- **Timeline:** 1–3 months to be bid-ready. A licensed electrical subcontractor can satisfy local requirements.

## Entry wedge test
- **Proposed wedge:** small, rural and private schools plus libraries that national VARs under-serve, with full paperwork support and managed Wi-Fi.
- **Is it deliverable?** Yes. Form 470s are public, and 20–35% single-bid FRNs show thin competition in some districts.
- **Is it copyable?** Yes, by the 150 existing providers.
- **What would change the picture:** a relationship with one or two of the top consultants (Telesolutions, AJD and Infinite Connections handled 666 FRNs in FY2025) [A, C9-S003]. It is not testable without contact.
