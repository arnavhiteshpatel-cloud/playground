# WS10-010 — Chicago masonry restoration & tuckpointing contractor

> **Verdict: HOLD** · revised evidence level **C** (unchanged) · 2026-11-28
>
> This record has the strongest demand evidence in batch C8.
> - Chicago issues about 1,400–1,840 tuckpointing permits a year with no downtrend from 2019 to 2026, median reported cost $15,000 [A, C8-S001].
> - The trade is extremely fragmented: 333 named mason contractors, top firm 4.7% of express permits, median firm 3 permits/yr [A, C8-S001].
>
> Two things stop it advancing yet:
> 1. **Feasibility.** The founder cannot legally do the work. The firm must employ a supervising mason who passed the City exam, and all mason work must be done by W-2 employees [A, WS10-S005]. Whether such a person can be hired, and at what cost, is unverified.
> 2. **Ceiling.** The 15 busiest Chicago tuckpointing firms average ~30 permits and ~$0.87M of reported value a year [A, C8-S001]. Even a top-15 firm is a ~$1M permitted-revenue business.
>
> Rebuilt year-3 operating profit is **$0–$250k, base ~$90k**. Advance only after the supervising-mason hire and workers' comp/GL costs are confirmed by the founder (this requires contact, which this stage does not do).

## 2. Industry structure
- **Illinois firms.** 876 masonry firms (2022), 696 with fewer than 5 employees, $1.21B receipts [C, C8-S005]. In the U.S., 70% of the 18,359 masonry firms have fewer than 5 employees [C, C8-S005].
- **Chicago permit volume.** Tuckpointing permits per year, 2019–2026: 1,707 / 1,402 / 1,458 / 1,579 / 1,528 / 1,841 / 1,804 / 1,744 (2026 to ~Nov 27) [A, C8-S001]. Demand held through COVID and the 2023–26 rate shock: an aging masonry stock plus code enforcement.
- **Market value.** 2025 express tuckpointing reported cost, capped at $250k/job, sums to about $64M ($44M excluding the 79 jobs over $250k) [A, C8-S001]. Unpermitted and suburban work are excluded. Reported costs are applicant-stated and likely understated.
- **Seasonality.** December–February accounted for ~10% of 2025 permits [A, C8-S001]. Crews idle or shrink in winter.
- **Violations.** Exterior wall 2,101 and parapet 856 in 2025, down from 3,050 and 1,366 in 2019 [A, C8-S003].

## 3. Value chain & mechanism
The owner pays the mason contractor per project, usually a deposit plus progress payments (E). The contractor buys brick, mortar and lintels from supply houses and rents scaffolds or lifts. Expediters are common: 839 expediter contacts appear across the 1,804 tuckpointing permits [A, C8-S001]. Margin is captured by the contractor on labor productivity and estimating accuracy. The new entrant would sell (estimating, condo-board proposals, permit handling) and run crews led by a hired supervising mason.

- **Top-firm scale.**

- **New entrant vs incumbent.** No disclosed P&L for any of these firms (MISSING). A new entrant should expect a 10–15% operating margin early (E): price-taking against 333 bidders, insurance loaded for a new account, and idle winter capacity.

## 5. Small-start evidence
Firm size in the permit data points to a classic small-start trade. 90 firms pulled one permit, 158 pulled 2–5, 70 pulled 6–20, and 15 pulled 21+ [A, C8-S001]. No named case history of a firm growing from 1 to 3+ crews was found (MISSING).

## 6. Competition
- **Field.** No national player; hundreds of local masonry and tuckpointing firms, many micro-firms. Roofing and GC firms also do masonry under a licensed supervising mason (e.g., "Masonry & Roofing" names in the top 12).
- **PE roll-ups.** None identified (MISSING).
- **Customer complaints.** Not gathered; anecdotally, unpermitted low-bid crews and poor mortar matching (E).
- **Switching costs.** None between projects. Condo boards re-bid.

## 7. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Small buildings.** Owners of 2–6 flats (119,948 class-211 parcels in Chicago [A, C8-S004]) buy reactively after water intrusion, a violation, insurer demands or a sale. They take 2–4 bids and decide in 2–8 weeks (E).
- **Condo associations.** Buy through property managers and board votes on reserve projects. Cycles of 2–6 months (E).
- **Payment.** Typically a deposit plus progress payments; Home Repair Act formalities apply to residential jobs of $1,000+ [A, WS18-S011].

## 8. Failure evidence
No closures or bankruptcies specific to this trade were retrieved (MISSING). Structural failure modes:
- Fall and scaffold injuries drive workers' comp experience-mod spikes. Premium levels are MISSING; NCCI class rates were not retrieved.
- Silica exposure from grinding (the OSHA construction silica standard applies; not re-verified this session).
- Skilled-mason scarcity, underbidding, and warranty callbacks on mortar failure.

Violations falling about 30% since 2019 trims the violation-driven channel [A, C8-S003].

## 9. Regulation (precise)
- **City of Chicago mason contractor licence.** Required for brick, stone, concrete and terra-cotta work. The business must employ a supervising mason who passed the City written exam (age 21+). Exam fee $150; licence $150/yr; mason work by W-2 employees only [A, WS10-S005]. A licensed mason contractor needs no separate GC licence within its scope [A, WS10-S004].
- **Founder.** Could sit the exam if 21+, but the experience prerequisites are MISSING. Realistically the licence comes through a hire.
- **Suburbs.** Municipal registration varies (MISSING).
- **Insurance.** Home Repair Act minimums for residential work [A, WS18-S010]; GL/WC market pricing MISSING.

## 10. Entry wedge test
- **Why a buyer would switch:**
  - a photo- or drone-documented scope with fixed prices per linear foot or lintel
  - a permit handled in days
  - board-ready proposals with a reserve-plan schedule
  - targeting owners named in exterior-wall and parapet violations [A, C8-S003]
- **Cost:** estimating time plus a sales role (the founder).
- **Copyable:** yes. The data are public and the presentation is easy to imitate.
- **What accumulates:** a condo-PM referral network and a portfolio of buildings on 5–10-year inspection and repair cycles. A real but slow moat.

## 11. Economics sanity check (year 3, E unless noted)
**Crew cost.** One crew = supervising mason/foreman ($85k) + mason ($70k) + tender ($45k) = $200k wages. With taxes, WC and GL (~35%) that is about $270k a year. One crew handles about 35–40 median jobs per Mar–Nov season (E).

| Case | Jobs × avg ticket | Revenue | Direct costs | GP | Overhead | Op. profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low (price-taker) | 35 × $13k | $455k | crew $270k + materials/scaffold/permits 17% $77k | $108k | $90k (truck, equipment, office, marketing, winter carry) | **~$18k** (can be ≤0) |
| Base | 40 × $16k | $640k | $270k + $109k | $261k (41%) | $100k + $70k extra insurance/idle allowance | **~$90k** |
| High (2 crews) | 75 × $16k | $1.2M | $540k + $204k | $456k | $200k (estimator, office, 2 trucks, marketing) | **~$250k** |

- **Capital at risk:** $25–80k (truck, scaffold or lift deposits, tools, insurance deposits, first payroll before deposits clear).
- **Base ROIC:** ~110–350%.
- **Cash conversion:** deposits help, but a crew paid weekly in a bad-weather month burns cash.
