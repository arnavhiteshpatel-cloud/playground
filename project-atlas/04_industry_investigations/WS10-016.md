# WS10-016 — Multifamily unit-turn (make-ready) services for property managers (Chicago metro)

> **Verdict: HOLD** · revised evidence level **D** (unchanged) · Stage C batch C8, 2026-11-28
>
> Demand is recurring and the buyer base can be identified (1,176 Illinois residential property-management firms [C, C8-S005]). But none of the three numbers that decide the economics could be found in public sources: the price per turn, the share of turns PMs outsource rather than give to in-house maintenance techs, and payment terms. The proposed wedge (5-day SLA, fixed menu, photo QA) can be copied by any painter or handyman firm. Federal lead-safe RRP rules cover almost every Chicago turn: the stock is mostly pre-1978, and the rule names rental owners/managers [A, C8-S024]. That is a compliance cost and only a thin credential edge. Rebuilt from drivers, year-3 operating profit runs from **−$30k to $190k (base ~$85k)**. Validate pricing and outsourcing share with PM maintenance directors before any spend.

## 2. Industry structure
- **Buyers.** Illinois has 1,176 residential property-manager firms (1,487 establishments, $2.90B receipts). 725 firms have fewer than 5 employees and 28 have 500+ [C, C8-S005]. This is a long tail with a few large managers.
- **Suppliers (the core trade).** Illinois has 1,487 painting firms; 1,183 of them have fewer than 5 employees, and the trade takes $1.25B in receipts [C, C8-S005]. The Census proxy operating margin for painting is 25%, an incumbent average before owner draw [C, WS10-S022].
- **Macro.** A 7.21% 30-year mortgage keeps households renting [C, WS10-S019], and multifamily starts are up 17% y/y [C, WS10-S020]. New supply eventually raises turnover and concessions, but it also adds new buildings, which need fewer repairs per turn.
- **Missing.** Chicago renter-occupied units by building size (ACS) are MISSING: the Census API now requires a key. Annual turnover rate is MISSING (NAA survey not retrieved).
- **Cyclicality.** Turns happen in every cycle. Price pressure rises when vacancy climbs, because PMs squeeze vendors.

## 3. Value chain & mechanism
The owner pays the PM a management fee. The PM's maintenance director assigns each move-out to in-house techs or to an approved vendor through a vendor portal. Turn costs are billed to the owner, sometimes with a PM markup (E). The vendor earns a markup on labor and materials. The new entrant would be an outsourced, fixed-price turn crew covering paint, patch, caulk, minor carpentry, fixture swaps and cleaning, and would subcontract licensed plumbing and electrical work [A, WS10-S006, WS10-S007]. Margin sits mostly with whoever controls the labor. The PM holds the pricing power because it writes the menu.

## 4. Operator economics
- **Turn-specialist P&Ls.** None found (MISSING).
- **Labor-heavy home-service proxy.** Ace Handyman franchise units with $250k–$775k revenue kept only **2–11% as owner discretionary income** [B, WS18-S009]. Handyman-style mixed tasks earn thin owner margins after royalties.
- **Painting tickets.** Five Star Painting averaged $4,440 gross sales per residential job in 2025 [B, C8-S021]. That is consumer repaint work, not turns, so it gives no turn price.
- **Incumbent vs new entrant.** The Census painting proxy is 25% (incumbent, before owner pay) [C, WS10-S022]. A new entrant facing PM-set prices should plan on an 8–15% operating margin (E).
- **Revenue per employee.** MISSING.

## 5. Small-start evidence
No documented case of a Chicago turn specialist that started small and scaled (MISSING). Small painting firms are abundant (80% of Illinois painters have fewer than 5 employees [C, C8-S005]), which shows that starting small is easy, not that scaling works.

## 6. Competition
- **Main competitor: PMs' in-house maintenance staff.** Outsourcing is usually overflow at summer peak (E).
- **Other vendors:** independent painters, handyman firms, cleaning companies and general contractors.
- **No dominant national player** was identified.
- **Switching costs are low** in both directions. A vendor is dropped after one missed deadline (E). Incumbent vendors' weakness is reliability; the founder's offer would compete on that alone.
- **Reviews:** not gathered.

## 7. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the maintenance director or regional property manager.
- **Frequency:** every move-out, concentrated May–August (E).
- **Procurement:** COI (Chicago GC Class E requires $1M CGL [A, WS10-S004]), W-9 and portal onboarding, then trial turns.
- **Terms:** net 30–45 via portal (E). Some PMs use third-party vendor-credentialing services that charge vendors (E, not verified).
- **How vendors are found today:** referrals among maintenance staff (E).

## 8. Failure evidence
No enforcement actions or bankruptcies specific to turn vendors were retrieved. Generic failure modes:
- missed SLAs and lost accounts
- dependence on one or two PMs
- idle paid labor in winter
- 30–60-day receivables against weekly payroll
- RRP non-compliance in pre-1978 units, a federal violation [A, C8-S024]

## 9. Regulation (precise)
- **City of Chicago GC licence.** Required even for permit-exempt work. Class E is $300/yr for projects up to $500k, with $1M per-occurrence CGL. No exam, but a financial-solvency statement is required [A, WS10-S004].
- **EPA RRP.** Covers paid work that disturbs more than 6 sq ft of paint per room (more than 20 sq ft exterior) in pre-1978 housing. The firm must be certified, even as a sole proprietor. "Residential rental property owners/managers" are listed as covered [A, C8-S024]. The firm fee and training hours are not on the page (MISSING).
- **Trade licences.** Plumbing and electrical work goes to Chicago-licensed contractors [A, WS10-S006, WS10-S007].
- **Home Repair and Remodeling Act.** Written contract and consumer-rights brochure for consumer work of $1,000+ [A, WS18-S011]. Usually not triggered for B2B turns in large buildings.
- **Feasibility.** The founder can hold all of these personally within weeks.

## 10. Entry wedge test
A buyer would switch for guaranteed turnaround (vacancy days cost rent) and for documented RRP compliance, which some small painters lack. Delivery would be a fixed-price menu, photo before/after, and portal invoicing. The cost is a crew lead plus scheduling discipline. **The wedge is fully copyable**, and the PM's in-house team is the default. The only accumulating edge is a reliability record with specific PMs.
