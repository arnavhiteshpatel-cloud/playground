## Industry structure
- **Choice and switching.** Retail choice exists in ComEd, Ameren and MidAmerican [A, WS11-S025]. As of May 2026, ARES served **79% of ComEd and 88% of Ameren non-residential usage**. In ComEd, small (0–100 kW) customers are 51% switched, medium (100–400 kW) 75%, large (0.4–1 MW) 87%, and >1 MW 93% [A, C7-S007]. Utility fixed-price supply remains available only to ComEd customers <100 kW and Ameren customers <150 kW [A, C7-S007].
- **Volume.** ARES non-residential supply was 2.8B kWh/month in ComEd and 1.45B kWh/month in Ameren, about **51 TWh/yr** [A, C7-S007].
- **Suppliers.** 96 certified ARES; active in ComEd fell from 79 to 68 [A, C7-S007]. Supplier concentration is moderate (non-residential HHI 1,707 ComEd, 1,644 Ameren).
- **Brokers.** 392 licensed ABCs; 19 new licences and 10 exits in the year to May 2026 [A, C7-S007]. These are counts of licensees, not active brokers. The ICC licensee list page returned HTTP 500, so the size distribution is MISSING.
- **Market-level commission arithmetic [E].** 51 TWh × ~60% brokered (assumed) × $0.002/kWh ≈ **$61M/yr** of broker commissions statewide, or about $157k gross per licensee on average. The distribution is heavily skewed toward large brokers.

## Value chain and mechanism
The supplier embeds a per-kWh adder in the customer's contract price and remits it to the broker. Payment is usually monthly as the customer pays (residual); some suppliers offer discounted upfront payment [D, C7-S008, C7-S009].

Illinois rules make the economics transparent to the customer. Before signing, the ABC must give written, customer-signed disclosure of total price per kWh and total anticipated cost *inclusive of all fees or commissions*, and that it receives supplier compensation [A, C7-S005]. A broker with supplier contracts may not call itself independent [A, C7-S005]. Margin is captured on volume × mils × years retained. The broker's work is load and bill analysis, RFPs to suppliers, contract structuring (fixed, index, block, capacity pass-through) and renewal timing.

## Operator economics
- **Commission rates.** No Illinois-specific schedule was found. Industry examples cite a half-cent and 0.3¢ adders [D, C7-S008], and say compensation per kWh is lower on large accounts [D, C7-S009]. Working assumption for SMB/mid C&I: **1.5–3 mils** [E].
- **Disclosed financials.** None for any Illinois broker. Public suppliers' broker-commission expense was not obtained.
- **Incumbent vs new entrant.** An incumbent holds a large renewal book and supplier pricing-desk access. A new entrant has neither, and also faces supplier credit and approval steps that are MISSING.

## Competition
- **Field.** 392 licensees, many telemarketing-heavy national brokers, supplier direct sales teams, municipal aggregation for small loads, and the ICC's Plug In Illinois comparison site.
- **Broker weaknesses.** Undisclosed or high adders and pushy outbound sales. Illinois has a history of deceptive-marketing enforcement, mostly residential [E, WS14-021].
- **Switching cost.** Low at renewal. Customers routinely let several brokers bid.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker.** Controller, CFO or facility manager. Owners make the call at small firms.
- **Timing.** Decisions are tied to contract expiry every 12–36 months. Procurement is informal at SMBs; nonprofits and public bodies run RFPs.
- **How they find brokers.** Inbound solicitation, CPA or property-manager referral.
- **The capacity-price shock still supports the pitch:**
  - PJM 2026/27 cleared at $329.17/MW-day and 2027/28 at $333.44 [A, WS11-S020].
  - FERC approved a **2028/29 cap of $325 and floor of $175/MW-day** [A, C7-S010].
  - COMED's import limit fell ~60% [A, C7-S010].
  - Capacity therefore stays expensive through May 2029 at least.

## Failure evidence
- **Shrinking supplier pool.** Active ARES in ComEd fell 14% in a year [A, C7-S007]. Fewer suppliers means fewer quotes and supplier-exit risk for a broker's residuals.
- **Licensee exits.** 56 licences were withdrawn or removed in 2022–23 [A, C7-S007].
- **Commission clawbacks** when customers default (terms MISSING).

## Regulation (precise)
- **Statute and rules.** 220 ILCS 5/16-115C; 83 Ill. Adm. Code 454.
- **Requirements:**
  - 454.60: ≥1 year management experience with a business enterprise.
  - 454.70: ≥1 year electric-industry experience; resumes with month/year detail required.
  - 454.80: $5,000 bond from a Treasury-listed surety.
  - Good standing certificate and notarized verification.
  - No application fee [A, WS11-S023].
  - Decision in 90 days, extendable to 180.
  - Annual recertification report by March 31 listing RES relationships and customer counts [A, C7-S001–C7-S006].
- **Exemptions** [A, C7-S003]:
  - exclusive agents of a single RES;
  - deals for customers whose aggregate Illinois billing demand exceeds 1,500 kW.
- **Gas.** The ABC licence does not cover gas [A, WS11-S023]; Illinois gas-broker rules are MISSING.
- **Can a partner satisfy it?** Yes. The rule speaks of "persons ... used to satisfy" the requirement, so an employee or partner can qualify the entity [A, C7-S001]. Statute text on ilga.gov could not be read (TLS failure).

## Entry wedge test
The wedge combines three things:
- transparent flat or disclosed fees;
- capacity-tag (PLC) and pass-through analysis;
- bill audit as a free door-opener.

The ICC's own disclosure rule already makes fees visible, which blunts a "transparency" pitch. Analytics can be copied by any serious broker. Bundling with demand response is the more defensible part; see SYN-004.

## Economics sanity check (year 3; E)
Assume ~3 accounts signed per month from month 9, 25% lost at renewal, average 400 MWh/yr (a ~100–200 kW site), 2 mils.
- **Base:** ~80 active accounts × 400 MWh = 32 GWh × $0.002 = **$64k** gross residual. Less CRM/pricing tools $6k, E&O $3k and bond premium (MISSING, assume <$500).
  - Operating profit ≈ **$55k**.
- **Low:** 40 accounts × 300 MWh × 1.5 mils = $18k, less $8k = **$10k**.
- **High:** 150 accounts × 600 MWh × 2.5 mils = $225k, less a sub-agent split on 40% of the book (~$40k) and $15k overhead = **$170k**.
- **Experienced partner cost.** Hiring a licence-qualifying person ($70–90k) would erase base-case profit.
- **ROIC.** Very high on cash, but slow: the book takes 2–3 years to build.
