# WS11-010 — Utility bill audit and rate optimization on contingency
Stage C · batch C7 · investigated 2026-11-28

> **Verdict: DROP as a standalone business. Keep it only as the lead-generation layer inside SYN-004.** Evidence is revised from D to **E** for economics: hit rates, refund sizes, fee percentages and refund look-back are all still MISSING.
>
> Three structural facts weaken the standalone case:
> 1. **Most C&I supply is no longer on the utility tariff.** 79–88% of Illinois non-residential usage is on ARES contracts [A, C7-S007], so supply-side "billing errors" are mostly contract-compliance checks on competitive supply rather than tariff errors.
> 2. **No Illinois rule found guarantees refund look-back.** The ICC rule located governs *under*-billing: utilities may back-bill non-residential customers up to 24 months [A, C7-S024]. The rule for refunds of over-billing was not verified.
> 3. **The work is mostly one-off.** Contingency audits are one-time per client, with fees lagging months behind utility credits.
>
> Year-3 operating profit of $5k–$100k, high variance. Free to start, but the ceiling and cash timing are poor.

## Industry structure
- **Market size.** NAICS 541618 / cost-reduction consulting. No size data was obtained for utility-audit specialists; MISSING.
- **Delivery models:**
  - franchise networks (Schooley Mitchell claims 350+ offices; fee is a share of savings) [D, WS11-S047];
  - national expense-reduction firms;
  - energy brokers who offer audits free;
  - CPA firms (utility-tax refund studies) [D, WS14-S029].
- **Cyclicality.** Low. Demand rises when energy prices spike, and PJM capacity at the cap through 2027/28 and a $325 cap / $175 floor for 2028/29 keeps prices elevated [A, WS11-S020, C7-S010].

## Value chain and mechanism
The auditor gets 24–36 months of invoices or utility data authorization and looks for:
- wrong delivery rate class;
- demand-ratchet or meter errors;
- municipal utility-tax and sales-tax exemptions;
- water/sewer evaporation and irrigation credits;
- waste overcharges.

The client or auditor files a dispute, and the fee is a percentage of refunds plus a share of 12–36 months of savings. Margin is captured only on "hits". The auditor bears all the cost of non-hits.

## Competition
- **Brokers.** 392 licensed ABCs [A, C7-S007], many offering free bill reviews to win supply contracts.
- **Others:** franchise networks; CPA utility-tax studies; utilities' own key-account managers.
- **Wedge.** Software-driven tariff checks are copyable. Buyers' main complaints, inferred (E): savings disputes and long contracts claiming "future savings".

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker.** CFO or controller.
- **Easy yes, hard follow-through.** Saying yes is easy (no cost), but collecting complete bills and utility authorizations takes weeks.
- **Contract.** A savings-share agreement with a 12–36 month tail.
- **How they find vendors.** Mostly through cold outreach and CPA referrals.

## Failure evidence
- **Generic failure mechanisms:**
  - low hit rates (each audit consumes analyst time);
  - disputes over the "savings" baseline;
  - utilities crediting the account slowly.
- **Missing data.** No closures or enforcement documented in this session.
- **Price-volatility risk.** Elevated prices can reverse; the 2028/29 floor of $175/MW-day is ~47% below the 2027/28 cap [A, C7-S010], which shrinks "savings" baselines.

## Regulation
- **Licensing.** No licence is needed to audit bills [A, WS11-S023].
- **Supply procurement.** Recommending or procuring supply contracts requires the ICC ABC licence (83 Ill. Adm. Code 454) unless the customer is above 1,500 kW [A, C7-S003].
- **Billing rules.** ICC Part 280.100 sets back-billing limits of 24 months for non-residential customers [A, C7-S024]. Public Utilities Act limits on reparations for over-charges are not verified (Justia and ilga blocked).
- **Contracts.** Plain commercial contracts; contingency fees are lawful for non-CPAs.

## Entry wedge test
The wedge is LLM-based bill extraction plus Illinois tariff rules, giving a 48-hour free assessment. It is real and cheap (ordinary API costs), but copyable, and brokers give the same service away to win supply commissions. As a free door-opener for brokerage and demand response, it has value. As a fee business, it competes with "free".

## Economics sanity check (year 3; E)
- **Base:** 60 audits/yr × 30% hit rate × $6k average (refund + first-year savings) × 35% fee = **$37.8k** revenue, less data tools and filing time of $5–8k ≈ **$30k**. Cash arrives 3–9 months after the hit.
- **Low:** 40 audits × 15% × $4k × 30% = $7.2k ≈ **$5k**.
- **High:** 150 audits × 35% × $10k × 40% = $210k, less 1 analyst $70k and $40k overhead ≈ **$100k**. This needs a channel such as CPA or broker partners.
- **Founder dependence.** Each audit needs skilled judgment. Delegation is moderate.

## Bundling note
Its best role is the no-cost first meeting that converts to a brokerage contract (residuals) and, for curtailable sites, demand-response enrollment. See SYN-004.
