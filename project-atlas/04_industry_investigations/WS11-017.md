# WS11-017 — Commercial cooling-tower / boiler water-treatment service route (Stage C, batch C1)

| Verdict | Revised evidence level | Revised adj. score |
|---|---|---|
| **HOLD.** This is the most structurally attractive industry in the batch: recurring, technical and served by independents. Advance it to Stage D only if a path to an experienced Certified Water Technologist (CWT) partner or hire, and to private-label chemical supply, can be shown. | **D** (up from E). There are A-level transaction prices for specific public buyers, an A-level Illinois independent with large municipal awards, and a C-level trade-association count. New-entrant margins remain E. | 5.32 (Stage B 5.44) |

**Why: there is real evidence the independent model works.**
- **An Illinois independent wins large contracts.** H-O-H Water Technology holds City of Chicago contracts of **$3.88M (2016–21) and $4.32M (2022–27)** for boiler and HVAC water treatment [A, C1-S005].
- **Independents are numerous.** The Association of Water Technologies (AWT) represents **500+ companies** that apply treatment to cooling and heating systems [C, C1-S009]. Ecolab describes a market "led by a few large companies" with the rest served by smaller regional firms [A, C1-S010].
- **Healthcare buyers must run water-management programmes** under CMS QSO-17-30 for Legionella [A, C1-S008].

**Why not advance yet:**
- **Pricing is opaque, so trust is what wins.** The same multi-building city scope drew bids of **$29.4k, $66.6k and $78.0k** per year [A, C1-S006]. Nebraska's bid priced service labour at about **$25/hour** [A, C1-S007], which implies the margin sits in chemicals.
- **The newcomer's wall is credibility:** risk-averse buyers, liability for Legionella outbreaks or equipment damage, and a learning curve of 12+ months.
- **Result:** year 3 for a newcomer is about **$20k–120k**. Stage A's $60k–250k assumed an existing client book.

## 1. Industry structure
- **No clean NAICS code.** Independents file under chemical wholesale (424690) or services.
  - US 424690: 5,487 firms [C, C1-S020].
  - Illinois 424690: 320 firms [C, C1-S021].
  - Both codes are far broader than water treatment.
- **Players:** a few large firms (Ecolab/Nalco, Veolia, ChemTreat, Chem-Aqua/NCH, Garratt-Callahan) plus 500+ AWT members [C, C1-S009; A, C1-S010; A, C1-S006].
- **Illinois demand anchors** [C, C1-S022]:
  - 208 general hospitals and 793 nursing facilities (both covered by CMS QSO-17-30).
  - Universities, office towers, data centres and 12,465 manufacturing establishments.
- **Cyclicality:** low to moderate. Treatment is a consumable that runs while the HVAC system runs.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- The building owner pays either a monthly programme fee, or chemicals at a markup with service bundled in.
- The treater buys concentrated chemistry from blenders. Private-label blenders market to AWT members (sponsor banner on AWT homepage [C, C1-S009]), but terms for a newcomer are MISSING.
- **Margin** is chemical markup plus controller and equipment sales (Nebraska: controllers $3,958–5,816 each [A, C1-S007]) plus Legionella testing.
- **New entrant:** does monthly visits, tests and adjusts dosing, writes reports, and maintains feed equipment.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- H-O-H is an Illinois independent operating at multi-million-dollar contract scale [A, C1-S005]. Its founding history was not retrievable (site returned 503), so this is MISSING.
- The 500+ AWT member firms imply many small starts [C, C1-S009]. The typical origin (ex-major-company reps taking accounts) is not documented here [E].

## 5. Competition
- **Nationals:** Ecolab/Nalco, ChemTreat, Veolia, Chem-Aqua and Garratt-Callahan, plus independents.
- **National weaknesses:** price. Nationals bid 2.3–2.65x the independent [A, C1-S006], and they under-serve mid-size buildings (E).
- **Incumbent exits:** the Columbus incumbent (Zee Company) did not rebid [A, C1-S006].
- **Switching costs:** meaningful. Controllers, programme knowledge and liability make buyers reluctant to change without a failure.

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Decision-makers:** facility or chief engineer, hospital plant operations, or public procurement.
- **Terms:** annual or multi-year contracts by bid in the public sector (A, C1-S005/S006/S007); private sector relationship-based.
- **Spend examples:** about $29k–78k/yr for a multi-building city portfolio [A, C1-S006]; about $0.8M/yr ceiling for all City of Chicago HVAC water [A, C1-S005].
- **Triggers:** scale or corrosion failures, Legionella findings, contract renewal.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Treatment failure** (Legionella outbreak, equipment damage) creates liability. CMS documents outbreaks tied to building water systems [A, C1-S008].
- **Price competition** in public bids [A, C1-S006].
- Bankruptcy or enforcement data were not found (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **No Illinois water-treatment licence** identified [E; ilga.gov unavailable].
- **CMS QSO-17-30** requires water-management programmes at hospitals, critical access hospitals and long-term care, referencing ASHRAE 188 [A, C1-S008]. It requires a *programme*, not a vendor.
- **Biocides** are EPA/FIFRA-registered pesticides. Whether Illinois requires a commercial pesticide applicator licence for cooling-tower biocide work is MISSING; it must be verified before launch.
- **Transport:** DOT hazmat rules apply to some concentrates (materials-of-trade exception unverified).
- **Certification:** AWT's CWT credential is voluntary; cost and prerequisites MISSING.
- **Insurance:** professional liability (cost MISSING).

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The pitch:** for mid-size buildings, "half the price of nationals, with remote monitoring and plain-English monthly reports", backed by the public-bid evidence that independents price far lower [A, C1-S006].
- **Delivery:** a CWT-trained founder or hire, a private-label blender, wireless controllers, and documented Legionella plans.
- **Copyable:** yes, by other independents.
- **Defensible:** switching friction protects accounts once won.

## 10. Economics sanity check (E)
**Year-3 low:**
- Revenue: 15 accounts × $6k/yr = $90k
- Chemical and test COGS 40% leaves $54k gross profit
- Less vehicle $10k, insurance (general + professional) $8k, lab and test supplies $5k, software/admin $8k
- **Operating profit ≈ $23k** (founder as technician)

**Year-3 high:**
- Revenue: 40 accounts × $12k = $480k
- Gross margin 60% = $288k
- Less one experienced technician (loaded) $95k, vehicles $20k, insurance $15k, office/admin $40k
- **Operating profit ≈ $118k**

**Range, capital and ceiling:**
- Stated range **$20k–120k**.
- Capital: $20k–60k (test equipment, controller inventory, vehicle, training, insurance).
- Ceiling: about $250k/yr in 5 years if 60–80 accounts are won. H-O-H-scale municipal wins show the upside exists [A, C1-S005].
- **Key uncertainties:** chemical gross margin (MISSING) and the founder's credibility ramp.
