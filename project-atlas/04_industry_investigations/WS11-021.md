# WS11-021 — Construction stormwater (ILR10/SWPPP) inspections and BMP maintenance
Stage C · batch C7 · investigated 2026-11-28 · also covers merged duplicate WS15-021

> **Verdict: ADVANCE to Stage D, with conditions.** Evidence level is revised from E to **D**. Demand is now A-level and price is D. Illinois has **5,341 effective ILR10 coverages**, and 2,918 of them sit in Chicago-region ZIP codes [A, C7-S012]. The permit requires inspections every 7 days and within about 24 hours of a 0.5-inch storm, by "qualified personnel" whom the permittee supplies [A, C7-S011]. A non-engineer can legally do the work: a free USEPA course, or simply being a "knowledgeable person", qualifies. Two findings cut against the Stage A optimism:
> - The same broad definition lets general contractors inspect their own sites.
> - Inspections drop to **monthly** in frozen months.
>
> Price evidence is thin: $95 to about $240 per inspection from out-of-state proxies [D/B]. Year-3 operating profit for a new entrant is plausibly $25k–$180k, base about $90k. This is a sound, low-capital, delegable route business with a modest ceiling. Stage D must establish real Illinois prices and the share of sites that outsource.

## Industry structure
- **Demand pool.** EPA ECHO lists 5,341 Illinois ILR10 coverages with "effective" status [A, C7-S012], analyst tabulation, accessed 2026-11-28. Of these, 2,918 have ZIP3 600–608, which is roughly Chicagoland. Coverage requires disturbance of 1 acre or more [A, WS11-S039]. Caveat: ECHO "effective" counts include stale coverages where no Notice of Termination was filed. Active disturbed sites at any moment are therefore lower, and the true number is MISSING.
- **Permit term.** The 2023 permit runs five years from issuance and continues in force until it is reissued [A, C7-S011].
- **Firm structure.** NAICS 541620 (environmental consulting) is fragmented. Inspection work is delivered by three groups:
  - civil and environmental engineering firms that wrote the SWPPP (for example Fehr Graham of Aurora appears in Illinois municipal awards [A, C7-S035]);
  - erosion-control and landscape contractors;
  - specialist SWPPP-inspection firms. Silver Leaf SWPPP pays inspectors about $25–$28 per inspection [D, C7-S016], and ARCXIS sells fixed-monthly SWPPP packages to builders (price undisclosed) [D, C7-S036].
- **Cyclicality.** Fully tied to construction starts (housing, warehouses, solar, data centers). Winter is a structural lull: inspections may drop to once a month when work stops for frozen conditions [A, C7-S011].

## Value chain and mechanism
The permittee (owner or operator, usually the developer or GC) owes the inspections and may "provide" any qualified person. Money flows GC/developer → inspector, per inspection or per site-month. The inspector's deliverable is a signed report: names and qualifications, observations, corrective actions. Two parts of the permit create urgency:
- noncompliance must be reported to IEPA within 24 hours (ION report within 5 days);
- corrective actions are due within 7 days [A, C7-S011].

Margin sits in route density and in responding to storm triggers. The real operating constraint is that one 0.5-inch storm triggers every client site at once, all due within about one business day. That caps each inspector at roughly 20–30 sites, not the 35+ Stage A implied. Adjacent revenue at lower margin comes from SWPPP preparation, NOI/NOT filing, and BMP repair crews (silt fence, inlet protection, sediment-basin cleanout).

## Operator economics
- **Incumbents.** No public pure-play. Engineering firms bill inspections as hourly add-ons; margins are MISSING.
- **Price proxies:**
  - an Albuquerque vendor proposal at **$95 per inspection** [D, C7-S014];
  - Fort Wayne's municipal fee of **$1,440 for six monthly inspections**, about $240 each, with extra inspections at $220/hour [B proxy, C7-S013];
  - MWRD charges $250 per violation re-inspection [D, C7-S037].
- **Labor cost.** A specialist firm pays $25–28 per inspection [D, C7-S016], which implies a gross margin of roughly 60–75% at $100–150 prices before vehicle and overhead.
- **Not found.** No Illinois price list, no FDD, no listing.

## Small-start evidence
None documented with numbers. Specialist SWPPP-inspection firms (Silver Leaf, ARCXIS) exist as stand-alone businesses [D]. Their founding scale is MISSING.

## Competition
- **DIY.** The permit's "other knowledgeable person" clause lets a GC superintendent inspect. Builders with many sites likely do this, but the share is MISSING.
- **Engineering firms.** They bundle inspections with the SWPPP and the municipal permit.
- **Municipal rules.** Some municipal ordinances require the project's PE to inspect weekly. Hodgkins is one example [D, C7-S015]; prevalence is MISSING. These rules push work toward engineering firms in those towns.
- **Technology.** Drone and software platforms such as TraceAir market lower-cost monitoring (search-result title only; E).
- **What customers complain about** (inference; reviews not gathered): missed post-rain windows and generic reports.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Who buys.** Site superintendent or project manager at the GC or homebuilder, or the developer's civil engineer.
- **When.** Contracted at mobilization.
- **Frequency.** Weekly plus storm events, for the life of the project (months to about 3 years for subdivisions).
- **Terms.** Net 30–60.
- **How buyers find vendors.** Engineer referral, existing relationships, bids on public jobs.
- **Prospect list.** New coverages appear in ECHO and USEPA NeT, so newly permitted sites can be prospected weekly [A, C7-S012].

## Failure evidence
No bankruptcies were found for this niche. Documented risk mechanisms:
- enforcement falls on the permittee, so a missed inspection puts the client relationship at risk;
- construction downturns (2008–2011 style) and winter idle months;
- price undercutting by engineering firms that treat inspections as a loss-leader. This last point is inferred (E).

## Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **Who may inspect.** ILR10 Part IV.D.4 lists the qualifying categories [A, C7-S011]:
  - Professional Engineer (PE);
  - CPESC, CESSWI or CSI certification;
  - completion of the **USEPA Federal CGP Inspector Training**;
  - any "other knowledgeable person" with the skills to assess controls.
- **No state license.** None is required to inspect.
- **Permit facts.** NOI fee $250 (<5 acres) / $750 (5+ acres) [A, WS15-S028].
- **SWPPP authorship.** No PE stamp is required by the permit text reviewed, but local ordinances may require one (Hodgkins example [D]).
- **Insurance.** General liability and professional/E&O are expected; costs MISSING.
- **Time to entry.** Days to weeks (the free online course).

## Entry wedge test
The wedge is guaranteed storm-trigger response within the permit window, photo reports, and immediate corrective-action tickets. BMP repair crews close findings within the 7-day deadline.
- **Delivery.** Weather-API triggers, a mobile checklist, and AI-drafted reports from photos.
- **Cost.** Software about $3–6k/yr plus an inspector.
- **Copyability.** High; any engineering firm can match it.
- **Moat.** Route density and GC relationships, which build slowly. The wedge is real but thin, so entry is scored 4.

## Economics sanity check (new entrant, year 3; all E unless noted)
Per active site-year: 8 active months × 5 inspections (4.3 routine + ~0.7 storm) × $120, plus 4 frozen months × 1.25 × $120.
= $4,800 + $600 = **$5,400 per site-year**.
- **Base (2 hired inspectors, founder sells and manages):**
  - Revenue: 45 average active sites × $5,400 = $243k, plus 15 SWPPP/NOI packages × $1,500 = $22.5k, total **$265k**.
  - Costs: inspectors 2 × $62k loaded = $124k; vehicles $18k; insurance $12k; software/tablets $6k; marketing/admin $15k; total **$175k**.
  - **Operating profit ≈ $90k.**
- **Low:**
  - Revenue: 25 sites × $4,500 + $10k = $122.5k.
  - Costs: 1 inspector + overhead = about $96k.
  - Operating profit ≈ **$26k**.
- **High:**
  - Revenue: 80 sites × $6,000 + $45k SWPPP + $40k BMP gross margin = $565k.
  - Costs: 4 inspectors, coordinator, vehicles and overhead = about $384k.
  - Operating profit ≈ **$180k**.
- **Capital:** $5k–$15k. ROIC is very high.
- **Plausible milestones.** $100k/yr is plausible. $250k/yr needs a multi-crew BMP arm or neighbouring-state expansion. $500k/yr is not supported by evidence.
