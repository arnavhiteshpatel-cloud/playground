# WS11-026 — Roll-off dumpster rental (C&D, cleanouts, light commercial)
Stage C · batch C7 · investigated 2026-11-28

> **Verdict: HOLD, flagged G1 WARN.** Evidence: **D**. Revenue per truck is now B-level; costs and margins are E, and tipping fees are MISSING.
>
> **What holds up.** It is a proven, search-driven route model:
> - franchise Item 19 data show about **$264k–$289k average gross revenue per truck**, even for trucks in their first two years [B, C7-S028];
> - Chicago-area published prices are around **$460 for a 20-yard box with 2 tons included** [B, C7-S025].
>
> **What cuts against it:**
> - At those prices, disposal plausibly takes ~30–35% of each haul [E, gate rates MISSING].
> - Integrated incumbents (WM, Republic) own disposal, and Chicagoland has only 4 landfills [A, WS11-S037].
> - A two-to-three-truck operator clears only about $30k–$180k in year 3 (base ~$65k, with the founder driving) on $200k–$450k of mostly financed equipment.
> - ROIC is low, and the launch capital of $80k–$160k probably exceeds the founder's "low thousands" preference. Financing depends on unknown personal credit (F2).
>
> Keep it in reserve, behind the asset-light options.

## Industry structure
- **Incumbent margins (mature scale, owned disposal):**
  - WM FY2025 operating margin 17.1%; Republic 19.9% with capex at 11.4% of revenue [A, WS11-S008, WS11-S009];
  - adjusted EBITDA 30–32% [B, WS11-S011, WS11-S012];
  - Casella ~23% adjusted EBITDA but 0.4% net income [B, WS11-S010].
- **Disposal capacity.** Chicago metro has 4 landfills with ~12.4 years of remaining capacity [A, WS11-S037]. New transfer stations need local siting plus IEPA permits [A, WS11-S036].
- **Local roll-off market.** Fragmented: independents, franchise locations (redbox+ Northern Illinois [B, C7-S025]), and lead-gen brokers (Budget Dumpster) that, in our understanding, subcontract hauling [E, not verified]. Firm counts for NAICS 562111 roll-off in Illinois are MISSING; SUSB not pulled this session.
- **Cyclicality.** Tied to remodeling, roofing and construction. Winter is slower.

## Value chain and mechanism
The customer pays a flat haul price covering delivery, the rental period and a tonnage allowance, plus overage, extra days and item fees:
- Redbox+ 20-yd: $460, 2 tons, 3/7/14 days [B, C7-S025];
- Trash Daddy, Aurora: 2 tons, 10 days, $25/day extension, $50 per mattress [B, C7-S026];
- aggregator data: 14–20 yd $517–$789 with 2–4 tons in Libertyville; $75–100/ton overage in central Illinois [D, C7-S027].

The operator pays the landfill or transfer-station gate rate (MISSING; the state surcharge is only ~$2.22/ton [D, C7-S030]), plus driver, fuel, truck, container capital and insurance. Margin is captured through route density (hauls per truck per day), container utilization, and overage and extra-day fees. Integrated competitors set the disposal price the independent pays, which is a structural squeeze.

## Small-start evidence
Franchise cohorts show new units reaching ~$264k per truck within two years [B, C7-S028]. This is revenue, not profit. redbox+ itself grew as a franchise system from small operators (system count from D sources). No independent Illinois small-start case with numbers was found.

## Competition
- **WM and Republic.** They price aggressively on contractor accounts where they own disposal.
- **Local independents and franchises.**
- **Lead-gen brokers** (Budget Dumpster), which buy Google traffic and subcontract hauling.
- **Customer complaints** (D, general): late swaps, hidden overage fees, driveway damage.
- **Switching costs.** Near zero for homeowners. Contractors stay for reliability.
- **Wedge.** Same-day swap, transparent online pricing and clean bins. Copyable.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Homeowners** search online, buy once, and pay by card upfront.
- **Roofers, remodelers and property managers** order repeatedly on net-30 accounts.
- **Decision factors.** Speed and price.
- **Acquisition cost.** Google Ads CPCs in dumpster rental are high (not quantified; MISSING).

## Failure evidence
Failure mechanisms include:
- tipping-fee increases by integrated rivals;
- a single-truck operator's breakdown risk;
- price wars.

No Illinois closures were documented in this session.

## Regulation (Illinois)
- **IEPA hauling permits.** Apply to special waste only. Ordinary C&D and MSW hauling needs no IEPA hauling permit [A, WS11-S035].
- **Local licensing.** Municipal hauler licences and right-of-way permits vary by suburb; some suburbs franchise residential collection. Specifics are MISSING.
- **Chicago.** Requires permits for containers on the public way (chicago.gov blocked; MISSING).
- **Vehicle requirements.** USDOT number and commercial auto insurance. A CDL is required for trucks with GVWR over 26,000 lb under the federal rule, and most hook-lifts qualify [E, rule not re-fetched]. Driver age and CDL availability matter (F1).

## Entry wedge test
The wedge is exurban or underserved edges, contractor-account reliability, online booking and mixed box sizes. It is real but shallow. Integrated incumbents control disposal pricing, and Google-search acquisition is a bidding war. The founder's tech background helps only with booking, routing and ads efficiency.

## Economics sanity check (year 3; E unless noted)
- **Assumptions:**
  - revenue per truck $230k (new-entrant discount to the $264k franchise cohort [B]);
  - disposal 30% of revenue (2 tons × ~$70–90/t on a $460 haul ≈ 30–39%, gate rate assumed);
  - driver $65k loaded per hired driver; fuel $18k per truck; truck finance and depreciation $25k per truck;
  - containers 15 per truck × $5k ÷ 7 years ≈ $11k per truck; insurance $12k per truck; repairs $10k per truck; marketing $25k; office/software $15k.
- **Base, 2 trucks (founder drives one):**
  - Revenue $460k.
  - Costs: disposal $138k + 1 driver $65k + fuel $36k + trucks $50k + cans $22k + insurance $24k + repairs $20k + marketing $25k + office $15k = **$395k**.
  - Operating profit ≈ **$65k**.
- **If both drivers are hired:** ≈ **$0**. Thin margins at small scale are the key disconfirming result.
- **High, 3 trucks at $280k/truck, 27% disposal, founder drives one:**
  - Revenue $840k.
  - Costs ≈ $660k: disposal $227k, 2 drivers $130k, part-time dispatcher $30k, fuel $54k, trucks $75k, cans $33k, insurance $36k, repairs $30k, marketing $30k, office $15k.
  - Operating profit ≈ **$180k**.
  - With all 3 drivers hired and a full-time dispatcher, ≈ **$100k**.
- **Low, 1–2 trucks, $200k/truck:** ≈ **$30k**.
- **Capital:**
  - first truck (used hook-lift, ~$60–90k [E]) plus 12–15 cans ($3–7k each [D, WS11-S048]) = **$80k–$160k** before first revenue;
  - fleet by year 3 ≈ $300–450k, mostly financed.
  - ROIC ≈ $65k ÷ ~$350k ≈ 19% (score 3).
- **Milestones.** $100k/yr is plausible by year 4–5 with 3+ trucks. $500k/yr needs ~8–10 trucks and a yard (years 6+).
