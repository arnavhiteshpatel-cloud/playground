# WS13-004 — Independent first-aid cabinet restocking & safety-supply van route (Stage C, batch C1)

| Verdict | Revised evidence level | Revised adj. score |
|---|---|---|
| **DROP as a standalone opportunity.** Fold first-aid, AED and eyewash checks into the WS05-001 safety route as a cross-sell SKU, which is how UniFirst itself pairs them. | **D** (down from C). Incumbent economics are A, and they cut both ways. A new entrant's ticket size and wholesale costs remain MISSING. | 4.92 (Stage B 6.02) |

**Why:**
- **Stage A anchored on Cintas.** Cintas First Aid & Safety earns **25.4% operating margin on $1.39B** [A, C1-S011].
- **The second-largest player shows what a sub-scale route earns.** UniFirst's First Aid & Safety segment had **$127.0M revenue and an operating loss of $(4.6)M in FY2026**, after $0.85M profit in FY2025. This is despite "double-digit growth on our van business" and 27 years of ownership of Green Guard [A, C1-S012; C, C1-S018].
- **Vestis' first-aid supply revenue fell $5.2M** [A, C1-S013].
- **Density is everything,** and a newcomer starts with none.
- **The mandate is soft.** OSHA requires only that adequate supplies be "readily available". The ANSI kit list is non-mandatory and replenishment is the employer's own job [A, C1-S019]. So any plant can self-supply from a catalogue.
- **The bundle is strong.** Cintas sells first aid under one cooperative contract with uniforms, mats and fire [B, C1-S015], and public buyers buy it without competition [A, C1-S016].
- **Result:** year 3 is about **$20k–80k**.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Codes:** the activity spans NAICS 423450 and 424 wholesale codes. Illinois 423450 has 380 firms, mostly hospital-supply distributors [C, C1-S021]. No code isolates van first-aid service.
- **Disclosed segment revenue (A):**
  - Cintas $1,391.9M (+14.3%) [C1-S011]
  - UniFirst $127.0M [C1-S012]
  - Vestis' first-aid line (size not given) [C1-S013]
- **Concentration is high and rising:**
  - Cintas bought ZEE Medical (the van first-aid pioneer) in 2015 [C, C1-S017], reportedly for about $130M on $110–120M revenue [D, C1-S030].
  - Cintas' pending acquisition of UniFirst would combine #1 and a top-3 first-aid player [A, C1-S011].
- **Illinois buyer universe** [C, C1-S022]:
  - 12,465 manufacturing establishments (6,139 with 10–499 employees)
  - 765 warehouses
  - 18,685 specialty-trade contractors
  - 3,891 auto repair shops
  - 9,928 full-service restaurants
- **Cyclicality:** moderate (usage tracks employment).

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- The employer pays per item replenished (sometimes plus a service fee).
- The route rep buys from first-aid manufacturers or private-label kit makers. Independent wholesale costs are MISSING.
- **Margin** comes from item markup times visit density.
- **Cintas** reaches a 57.7% gross margin through scale buying and its own distribution network [A, C1-S011]. A newcomer buys at distributor prices (assumed 40–45% gross margin, E).

**The lesson:** margins are a function of national density. Owner-operator economics are a modelled route income, not Cintas-type returns.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- Green Guard grew from founding in 1984 to a book UniFirst bought in 1999 [C, C1-S018].
- Independent ZEE distributors persist [C, C1-S017].
- No disclosed small-operator profits were found (MISSING).

## 5. Competition
- **Players:** Cintas, UniFirst and Vestis, plus residual ZEE distributors and local van operators.
- **Weaknesses:** Vestis is losing business [A, C1-S013]. The Cintas–UniFirst merger may disrupt service (HSR review pending) [A, C1-S011].
- **Structural strength:**
  - One-vendor cooperative contracts [B, C1-S015], and non-competitive public purchasing through them [A, C1-S016].
  - Cintas' revenue growth comes from penetrating existing customers [A, C1-S011].
- **Switching cost:** low for cabinets, but multi-service accounts are sticky.

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Decision-maker:** plant, safety or office manager, usually a single decision-maker.
- **Process:** monthly visits, billed per item. Vendors are found through uniform/mat reps, catalogues (Grainger, Amazon) and cooperative contracts.
- **Spend per site:** MISSING. Stage A estimated $40–150/month [E].
- **Contract terms** for first aid are MISSING. UniFirst rental contracts run 3–5 years [A, C1-S012].

## 7. Failure evidence
- **UniFirst's segment loss** is the clearest evidence that a route network with $127M revenue can still be unprofitable [A, C1-S012].
- Vestis' decline [A, C1-S013].
- Additional modes: expired product and shrink, route-rep turnover, and dependence on a few large accounts (Stage A; unrefuted).

## 8. Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **OSHA 29 CFR 1910.151** [A, C1-S019]:
  - (b) adequate supplies readily available, plus trained responders where medical care is not near
  - (c) eyewash/drench facilities where corrosives are present
  - Appendix A is non-mandatory
- **No Illinois licence** identified for non-prescription first-aid supplies [E].
- **Avoid:**
  - repackaging OTC medicines (FDA drug-establishment rules; not verified)
  - selling AEDs without understanding medical-device distribution rules (not verified)
- **Insurance:** general and product liability.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The pitch:** no auto-renew, transparent item prices, and reliable visits. Target the Vestis and merger disruption [A, C1-S011/S013].
- **Cost:** low.
- **Copyable:** instantly, and incumbents can match price at the account level.
- **Better wedge:** pairing it with a *licensed* service the buyer must purchase anyway (extinguishers). UniFirst runs extinguisher inspection, testing and maintenance inside the same First Aid & Safety segment [A, C1-S012].

## 10. Economics sanity check (E)
**Year-3 low (founder runs the route):**
- Revenue: 150 sites × $60/month × 12 = $108k
- Gross margin 42% = $45k
- Less van $12k, insurance $4k, software/admin $8k, shrink/expiry $3k
- **Operating profit ≈ $18k**

**Year-3 high:**
- Revenue: 400 sites × $75 × 12 = $360k
- Gross margin 45% = $162k
- Less hired route rep (loaded) $60k, two vans $24k, insurance $6k, admin/software $15k, shrink $8k
- **Operating profit ≈ $49k.** About $90k if the founder runs the second route.

**Range and capital:**
- Stated range **$20k–80k**.
- Capital: van inventory $5k–10k, plus a vehicle and cabinets loaned to customers, about $10k–30k in total.
- The ceiling of about $10k/month reflects the UniFirst evidence that scale alone does not rescue margins.
