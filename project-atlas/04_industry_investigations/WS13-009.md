# WS13-009 — Restaurant back-of-house wear-parts route (gaskets, hood-filter exchange, strip curtains, knife exchange)

> **Verdict: DROP as a standalone. Keep it only as a cheap add-on line inside WS05-007.** Revised evidence level: **D**.
>
> **Demand is real and visible.** In 13 months Chicago inspectors flagged gaskets at 702 food licences [A].
>
> **But the business is small and fully contestable:**
> - A national franchise, Gasket Guy, claims more than 100 installers.
> - It also sells gaskets online direct to restaurants and repair technicians for $9.99 flat shipping, which sets a ceiling on the installation premium [D].
> - Gaskets are replaced every 1–3 years (E), so per-account revenue is episodic.
> - Hood-filter exchange needs a filter pool plus a wash facility with wastewater compliance. That is the opposite of asset-light.
>
> **Year-3 operating profit: about $25k–$80k.** Ceiling about $10k/month.

## 1. Industry structure

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- **Gaskets:** measure the door, order or fabricate to OEM spec, install. Margin sits in labour and the difference against OEM list price.
- **Knife exchange:** rent a rotating set of sharpened knives on a 1–2 week cycle.
- **Strip curtains and hardware:** product resale plus installation.
- **Hood filters:** swap a clean filter for a dirty one, then wash centrally.
- **The entrant's role:** route seller-installer who owns a small inventory.
- **Supply:** Gasket Guy positions itself as the low-cost supplier and installer. Its franchisees get factory pricing, and direct buyers can order online [D, C2-S032].

## 4. Small-start evidence
Gasket Guy itself began in refrigeration service, spotted overpriced OEM gaskets and grew into a franchise network. This is a company narrative, dated "over 20 years," with no figures [D, C2-S032]. It shows the niche can support a network, but the network already exists.

## 5. Competition
- Gasket Guy franchisees or installers (the find-a-location page did not render).
- Refrigeration repair contractors, who replace gaskets during service calls.
- OEM parts distributors.
- Online sellers that make DIY replacement easy, Gasket Guy among them.
- Knife services: local sharpening services and restaurant supply vendors.
- **Switching costs:** near zero. **Wedge:** bundling many fixes in one visit. That wedge is easily copied.

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Buyer:** the kitchen manager or owner. The purchase is impulse-like and often made on the same visit, triggered by an inspection finding or energy loss.
- **Spend:** gasket prices unverified, roughly $70–$200 installed (E, Stage A).
- **Frequency:** gaskets every 1–3 years (E). Knives and filters are the only recurring parts.
- **How buyers find vendors today:** the refrigeration technician, Google, distributors. Unverified.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Low ticket size.** Stage A's own failure mode was "too small per stop."
- **Closures strand rental inventory.** Knife sets and filter pools are lost when a restaurant closes [A, C2-S014].
- **Enforcement or bankruptcy data:** none found. **MISSING.**

## 8. Regulation
- **Gaskets, curtains and knives:** no Illinois licence identified (E). The Home Repair Act does not apply because these are commercial premises.
- **Hood-filter washing:** MWRD's local limit of 250 mg/L for fats, oils and grease applies to wash-water discharge [A, WS05-S027]. A pretreatment or interceptor setup may be needed; permit details are **MISSING**.
- **Refrigeration work beyond gaskets:** refrigerant handling requires EPA Section 608 certification. That is a known federal rule, but it was not fetched this session and is unverified here.
- **Feasibility:** near-trivial for gaskets and curtains.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **Pitch:** "Your inspection cited torn gaskets. We fix it today, plus curtains and door hardware, with a photo log for the re-inspection."
- **Delivery:** canvass using public inspection data [A, C2-S014]. Cost: founder time plus about $3–8k of tools and inventory (E).
- **Copyability:** completely copyable, and an established franchise already does it.
- **Better use:** fold it into a pest route. A pest technician already visits monthly and documents structural defects; adding gasket and door-sweep fixes raises revenue per stop with no new licence.

## 10. Economics sanity check (year 3, before founder salary; E)
**Base case: revenue $227k, costs $162k, operating profit about $66k.**

Revenue:
- Gasket installs: 1,200 doors × $110 = $132k.
- Strip curtains and hardware: $30k.
- Knife-exchange and filter add-ons: $65k.

Costs:
- Materials at 30%: $68k.
- One technician: $42k × 1.2 = $50k.
- Two vans: $20k.
- Insurance, software and miscellaneous: $15k.
- Marketing: $8k.

**Other cases:**
- **Low:** 600 doors with small add-ons gives $90k of revenue. With the founder doing installs, operating profit is about $27k.
- **High:** 1,800 doors plus $140k of add-ons gives $347k of revenue. With two technicians, operating profit is about $80k. The margin compresses because materials scale with volume.

**Volume check.** 1,200 doors a year means about 400–600 restaurant visits a year. That is plausible, given about 700 cited licences a year and replacement cycles.

**Capital:** $5k–20k, excluding any hood-filter pool, which would add $25k–80k (WS05-032).

**Return profile.** ROIC is high, but absolute dollars are capped.
