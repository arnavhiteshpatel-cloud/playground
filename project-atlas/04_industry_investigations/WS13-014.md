# WS13-014 — Private utility locating & concrete GPR scanning (incl. merged duplicates WS10-005, WS11-023) (Stage C, batch C3)

> **Verdict: ADVANCE to Stage D (conditional). Revised evidence level: D.**
> **Why it advances:**
> - **Best founder fit in the batch.** No license was identified, and the founder can personally be the first technician-salesperson.
> - **Modest capital.** A concrete-scanning kit costs about $15k; a full utility-locating setup about $65k [D, C3-S024].
> - **Short sales cycles** with frequent repeat buyers.
> - **Structural need.** JULIE does not locate private lines [B, WS11-S031].
> - **Clear exit path.** An active acquirer exists in GPRS (13 acquisitions) [D, WS13-S033].
>
> **Why it is conditional:**
> - **GPRS is not a template; it is the entrenched competitor.** It started in 2001 when the niche was nascent. It ran Trump Tower Chicago for Walsh Construction in 2004–05 and has a Chicago office and trucks [D, C3-S026; C, C3-S025]. It claims 400+ project managers across 54+ metros, uses a 400-hour training standard, and markets one-person-from-call-to-report service [D, C3-S024; C, C3-S025].
> - **Prices are unverified.** Day rates are D-level SEO figures ($800–3,000/day) [D, C3-S028].
> - **Demand is falling.** City of Chicago issued permits fell 34% from 2019 to 2025 [A, WS10-S010].
> - **Bottom line:** year-3 profit of about $15–250k, base about $90–170k, which includes the founder's own field labor.

## 1. Industry structure
- **No clean Census code.** Private locating and GPR scanning are spread across 541370 (surveying and mapping), 238910 (site preparation) and 541380 (testing).
  - Illinois counts: 164 establishments in 541370; 1,100 in 238910 [C, C3-S014].
  - US 541370 firms with $1–2.5M of receipts average about $138k per employee, with payroll at 45.9% of receipts [C, C3-S015]. That signals a labor-intensive business with modest revenue per head.
- **GPRS's size is uncertain.** Third-party revenue estimates range from $29M to $244M (IBISWorld $93M, 375 employees) [D, C3-S027]. If $93M with 400+ field PMs is roughly right, revenue per PM is about $200–250k (E).
- **Cyclicality:** construction-cyclical. Chicago permits went from 48,540 (2019) to 32,032 (2025), and 29,067 through about November 2026 [A, WS10-S010].

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- **Who pays:** contractors (electrical, plumbing, coring/sawing, telecom, fence, solar/EV) and facility owners. They buy half-day or day visits before they cut or dig.
- **Deliverables:** field marks plus PDF or CAD maps.
- **Where margin sits:** in technician utilization. Equipment depreciates at about $10–15k per kit per year (E, from C3-S024 prices).
- **No middleman layer:** the technician *is* the salesperson. GPRS PMs handle call, site walk, proposal, job and report [C, C3-S025].

- **Incumbent vs. new entrant:** incumbents carry national accounts, safety programs and training departments. A new entrant's advantage is responsiveness in suburbs. Its disadvantage is that GC prequalification often demands safety records and insurance limits (E).

## 4. Small-start evidence
**GPRS is the strongest small-start case in batch C3** [C, C3-S025; D, C3-S026]:
- Founded 2001 by Matt Aston, scanning for his father's concrete-coring firm in Toledo and Detroit.
- Utility locating added about five years later.
- Named to Inc.'s "Fastest Growing Company Under 10 Million" list in 2010, about nine years in.
- A formal sales department only from 2010.
- Grew "largely organic and word-of-mouth."

**Caveats:**
- It is survivorship evidence from a first mover.
- The founder had a captive customer: his father's coring business.
- GPRS itself now occupies the "responsive, one-contact" position a new entrant would pitch.

## 5. Competition
- **Main competitors:**
  - GPRS (Chicago office and trucks)
  - concrete cutting/coring firms with their own scanners
  - local private locators and surveyors (names not collected)
  - subcontractors that buy or rent GPR themselves (rental $165–380/day [D, C3-S028])
- **Switching costs:** low per job. Relationships with superintendents matter.
- **Customer complaints:** not collected.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** subcontractor foreman, project manager or GC superintendent; facility managers for campus mapping.
- **Timing:** booked days ahead of a pour, core or dig.
- **Ticket:** about $300–1,500 per visit (D).
- **How vendors are found:** phone, Google, word of mouth.
- **Contracts:** sometimes GC master service agreements with insurance requirements (E).

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Strike liability:** hitting a post-tension tendon or energized conduit.
- **Interpretation error:** GPRS markets its 400-hour certification and 99.8% accuracy as a quality bar [D, C3-S024, C3-S026].
- **Construction downturns:** permits down 34% [A, WS10-S010].
- **Underutilized kits.**
- **No documented closures** (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation
- **No Illinois license for private locating or concrete GPR was identified** (E; ilga.gov unavailable).
- **JULIE:** member utilities do not locate customer-owned lines, and the owner bears the cost [B, WS11-S031]. Statutory text of 220 ILCS 50 was not re-read.
- **Survey boundary:** producing *legal boundary or topographic surveys* is land-surveyor practice and would need a licensed Illinois PLS (E). Keep deliverables as "locate/scan reports."
- **Insurance:** general liability plus E&O; GC-required limits are MISSING.
- **Credentials:** NDT Level I training costs about $1,000 per level [D, C3-S024]. This is voluntary but expected.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The wedge:** same- or next-day suburban response, flat transparent half-day pricing, and one-click PDF/CAD maps for repeat subcontractors.
- **Copyability:** fully copyable by GPRS, whose model already centers on one contact.
- **Realistic reason to switch:** GPRS cannot cover a booking within 24–48 hours in an outer-collar county. That capacity gap is unverified, and it closes as GPRS adds trucks.
- **Exit:** sale to GPRS or a peer (13 acquisitions, 2018–2025 [D, WS13-S033]).
