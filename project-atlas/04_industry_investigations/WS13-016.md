Stage C · batch C7 · investigated 2026-11-28

## Value chain and mechanism
The HOA, property manager, park district, municipality or business park pays an annual program fee. Typical scope:
- biweekly or weekly visits;
- herbicide and algaecide treatment and dye;
- water-quality readings [A, C7-S031, C7-S034].

Add-ons are aeration and fountain installs and service, shoreline and native-buffer work, fish stocking and goose control. Margin comes from route density: many ponds per technician-day within a small radius. Chemicals are a modest share of cost (E). Basin vegetation work is a separate, landscape-type contract (Lombard: Bedrock Earthscapes, $29,350 for 9 sites) [A, C7-S034].

## Operator economics
- **Price points** (above): $1.1k–$2.9k per pond per year for biweekly or algae programs; ~$8k for weekly service with water testing.
- **Bid spread.** Low-to-high bids span 3× at Wheaton [A, C7-S031], so price discipline is weak and scope varies.
- **Margins.** No disclosed margins for any aquatic operator. Rentokil does not segment aquatic results (not checked). SOLitude publishes no prices [D, WS13-S024].
- **Incumbent vs new entrant.** An incumbent benefits from route density and purchasing scale on chemicals and aerators. A new entrant must build density from zero.

## Small-start evidence
McCloud Aquatics (operating as "BS&T dba McCloud Aquatic Services") is a local Elburn firm that beat SOLitude on price in 2026 and holds multi-year municipal work [A, C7-S031; C, C7-S032]. Its founding size is MISSING. This is evidence that small local operators win, not that a new one can.

## Competition
- **SOLitude (Rentokil).** The largest player and a likely acquirer.
- **About 20 regional firms.**
- **Landscape contractors**, which subcontract aquatic work because it requires the aquatic licence.
- **Mosquito and pest firms** with aquatic categories (Clarke, Rollins-branded entities appear in ILG87).
- **Customer complaints** (inferred): algae rebounds between visits, poor communication.
- **Switching costs.** Low; annual rebids are common.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker.** HOA boards via property managers, or park district / municipal staff, who take quotes or bid.
- **Budget cycle.** Fall and winter.
- **Terms.** One-year terms with renewal options [A, C7-S031].
- **Payment.** Monthly or seasonal billing; municipal net-30.
- **Buyers say they cannot self-perform:** "Park staff does not have experience/qualifications" [A, C7-S031].

## Failure evidence
Industry risks are documented in principle:
- chemical misapplication and fish kills;
- licence violations (IDOA);
- drought or heat algae events. Homewood approved up to $100k to test a pond after public "toxic" claims [A, C7-S035].

No closures were found.

## Regulation (precise)
- **Applicator licensing (IDOA).** Commercial pesticide applicator licence with the Aquatic category: General Standards plus category exam, $300 for 3 years (operator $240); each facility needs a licensed applicator; liability insurance minimums apply [A, WS13-S023].
- **Discharge permit.** The NPDES Pesticide General Permit (ILG87) is held by ~20 aquatic firms [A, C7-S012]. Coverage thresholds and NOI rules are MISSING (eCFR and IEPA permit text not pulled).
- **Wildlife and fish.** IDNR rules for fish stocking and goose control are MISSING.
- **Time to entry.** About 2–4 months (study plus exam scheduling).
- **Delegation.** A licensed employee can satisfy the facility requirement.

## Entry wedge test
The wedge is responsiveness plus measurable reporting: water-quality readings, before/after photos and algae alerts. The first route can be built by:
- subcontracting for landscapers who lack the aquatic licence;
- bidding municipal contracts, where prices are public.

Possible pairing with WS11-021 (stormwater and BMP inspections) for developers turning basins over to HOAs. The wedge is copyable, and the moat is local density and relationships.

## Economics sanity check (year 3; E)
A technician-route serves ~65–70 ponds on a biweekly cycle (6–7 ponds/day × 10 days).
- **Base, 2 routes:**
  - Revenue: 130 ponds × $1,900 = $247k, plus aeration/fountain installs and service $70k = **$317k**.
  - Costs: 2 licensed technicians (8-month season plus off-season install work) $110k; chemicals and dye ~12% of program revenue $30k; install COGS (60%) $42k; trucks, boats and fuel $25k; insurance and licences $12k; marketing/admin $20k; total **$239k**.
  - Operating profit ≈ **$78k**.
- **Low:** founder as the single applicator, 60 ponds × $1,600 = $96k + $20k installs, minus ~$55k direct costs ≈ **$60k**. This is founder-dependent. If a technician is hired at this scale, ≈ **$5–20k**.
- **High:** 4 routes, 260 ponds × $2,000 = $520k + $150k installs, minus ~$520k costs ≈ **$150k**.
- **Capital:** truck, jon boat, sprayers, test kit, first chemicals and insurance = **$15k–$45k**.
- **Milestones.** $100k/yr is plausible by year 3–4. $250k/yr needs about 6 routes or acquisitions.
