# WS13-018 — Multifamily doorstep ("valet") trash collection service (Stage C, batch C1)

| Verdict | Revised evidence level | Revised adj. score |
|---|---|---|
| **HOLD.** Advance only if Stage D can show Chicago-area garden-apartment adoption and pricing at or above about $10/unit. | **D** (up from E). Pricing proxies are D (trade publication, vendor claims). Adoption anecdotes are C (apartment-industry association). Illinois market size, fire-code limits and fee-law exposure are MISSING. | 5.18 (Stage B 5.48) |

**Why:**
- **The model is real:**
  - Camden runs doorstep valet at 95% of its communities and charges residents for it [C, C1-S027].
  - Operators describe it as "expected in class A and B+ garden-style communities" [C, C1-S027].
  - The national leader has operated since 1995 [D, WS13-S037].
- **What the vendor is paid is lower than Stage A assumed:**
  - Providers typically get **$8–15 per unit per month**, with the best rates at 300+ unit garden properties [D, C1-S026].
  - A vendor page reportedly lists $18–28 per door [D, C1-S028], so sources disagree and both figures are kept.
  - Stage A had assumed $15–35, which is closer to what *residents* pay ($25–35) [D, C1-S026].
- **The property owner keeps the spread,** so the vendor is a price-taker in a labour business.
- **Fit with Chicago is uncertain:**
  - Adoption evidence is Sunbelt-REIT anecdote.
  - Chicago high-rises use chutes.
  - Outdoor evening work in winter adds cost.
  - Illinois has only 40 firms in "other waste collection" (NAICS 562119) [C, C1-S021].
- **Result:** year-3 profit is about **$35k–130k**, capital is near zero, and the work is delegable.

## 1. Industry structure
- **NAICS 562119** (other waste collection):
  - US: 1,407 firms, $3.0B receipts [C, C1-S020].
  - Illinois: 40 firms, $87.6M [C, C1-S021].
  - Valet operators may also code as building services (561790), so counts are approximate.
- **Illinois buyers:** 1,464 residential property-manager establishments and 1,905 lessors of residential buildings [C, C1-S022].
- **Chicago-area garden stock with 150+ units:** MISSING (the Census ACS API redirected to key registration).
- **Growth:** tied to new Class A/B garden construction (MISSING).
- **Cyclicality:** amenity spend is cut when occupancy or rents soften.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- Residents pay a mandatory lease amenity fee of about $25–35 a month [D, C1-S026].
- The property pays the vendor $8–15 per unit.
- The vendor pays evening porters, supervisors and insurance.
- **Margin** is captured by the *owner* (the spread) and by the vendor through labour productivity (doors per hour).
- **New entrant:** recruits part-time evening labour, supervises it, and reports violations to management.

- **Example from the trade blog:** a 100-unit property paying $12/unit ($1,200/month) collects about $2,500 from residents [D, C1-S026].
- No vendor margin disclosures exist (MISSING).

## 4. Small-start evidence
- The vendor examples are small regional firms (Royal Trash Valet, National Doorstep Pickup) [D, C1-S026]. That suggests small starts are normal.
- No documented growth case with numbers was found (MISSING). Valet Living's scale is unconfirmed.

## 5. Competition
- **Players:**
  - Valet Living nationally.
  - Regional firms and franchise systems (names not verified).
  - In-house porters hired by property managers.
- **Contracts:** 1-year minimum, with 24–36-month discounts [D, C1-S026]. A property sale often triggers renegotiation (E).
- **Switching:** easy. A missed pickup is visible to residents immediately.

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Decision-makers:** regional or portfolio managers and owners. One management company can control dozens of sites.
- **Process:** the purchase is justified as an amenity plus ancillary revenue [C, C1-S027; D, C1-S026]. Pricing improves with property size.
- **Vendor discovery:** bid sites and property-manager networks [D, C1-S026].
- **Typical spend:** a 250-unit property at $10–12/unit is $2,500–3,000 a month.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Labour no-shows,** with evening part-time turnover. Camden also reports weekend self-dumping that generates complaints [C, C1-S027].
- **Lost contracts** at property sale.
- **Price pressure** from 300+ unit buyers.
- **Resident backlash** against mandatory fees (exposure MISSING).
- Bankruptcies or enforcement were not found (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **Hauling permits:** Illinois EPA hauling permits target special waste [A, WS11-S035]. Moving residents' bags to the property's own compactor appears not to be "hauling" [E, unverified].
- **Municipal hauler licensing:** not applicable if nothing leaves the property (MISSING per municipality).
- **Fire-code limits** on bags left in corridors or at doors: MISSING for Illinois and Chicago suburbs. Some jurisdictions restrict set-out windows (unverified).
- **Resident fee disclosure:** state and local junk-fee rules are MISSING. Chicago's Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance status is unverified.
- **Other:** general liability, workers' comp, and the Illinois $15 minimum wage [A, WS04-S025].

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The pitch:** photo- or GPS-verified pickups, a local supervisor on call, and a month-to-month pilot for suburban garden communities not yet offering the service.
- **Cost:** minimal.
- **Copyable:** immediately.
- **Compounding:** density inside one management company's portfolio is the only advantage that builds.
- **Real switching reason:** missed pickups by the incumbent.

## 10. Economics sanity check (E)
**Per property (250 units at $11/unit) = $2,750 a month:**
- Porter labour: about 2.5 hrs/night × 21.7 nights × $21 loaded = $1,140.
- Supervision, carts and insurance allocation: about $250.
- **Contribution ≈ $1,360 (49%).**
- Sensitivity: at $8/unit, contribution falls to about $610 (30%). At $15, it is about $2,360.

**Year-3 low:**
- Revenue: 6 properties × 250 units × $10 × 12 = $180k
- Contribution 40% = $72k
- Less insurance $10k, admin/software/sales $15k, vehicle $6k
- **Operating profit ≈ $41k** (founder supervises nights)

**Year-3 high:**
- Revenue: 25 properties × 250 × $12 × 12 = $900k
- Contribution 42% = $378k
- Less 2 supervisors $110k, insurance $30k, admin/office $60k, vehicles $15k, bins/carts $15k, claims reserve $15k
- **Operating profit ≈ $133k**

**Range and ceiling:**
- Stated range **$35k–130k**.
- Ceiling: about $25k/month, which needs 40+ properties.
- That requires Chicago-area demand that is not yet evidenced.
