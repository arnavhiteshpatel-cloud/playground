# WS13-028 — Pet cremation & aftercare serving veterinary clinics (asset-light route first, own crematory later)

*Also covers merged duplicate WS04-032 (pet aftercare for vet clinics).*

> **Verdict: ADVANCE** · revised evidence level **D** (Stage A D; blind E) · 2026-11-28
>
> **The main regulatory unknown is resolved in the founder's favor.** Illinois has **no pet-crematory licence**. The Companion Animal Cremation Act (815 ILCS 318) only requires providers to:
> - publish a non-misleading written explanation of services (no calling communal or partitioned cremation "private")
> - give it to referring vets
> - certify returned remains
>
> Violations are business offenses of $1,001–$2,500 [A, C8-S006]. Separately, 415 ILCS 5/22.49 keeps companion-animal cremation outside Illinois waste-facility rules [A, C8-S007]. An IEPA air permit and local zoning for an owned retort remain unverified (MISSING).
>
> **Prices.** Published Illinois retail prices are individual cremation **$200–$300**, group **$50–$180**, and pickup from a vet **$95** (Deiters, 2026 [B, C8-S014]). Chicago-area private cremation runs about $150–$200 for small and medium pets [D, C8-S015]. Clinic wholesale prices are MISSING.
>
> **Demand.** Recurring through clinics, non-discretionary and growing [C, WS13-S018].
>
> **Disconfirming evidence:**
> 1. A consolidator, Regency Pet, is rolling up Midwest pet aftercare [C, C8-S016], and corporate vet groups centralize vendors.
> 2. The asset-light phase depends on a competitor's crematory, and on our arithmetic it **barely breaks even**.
> 3. An owned retort (capex MISSING; estimated $150–300k all-in) is where the margin and moat sit. That pushes total capital at risk past $100k after revenue is proven (G1 WARN, not FAIL).
>
> Rebuilt year-3 operating profit is **−$10k to $200k, base ~$60k**; years 4–5 run ~$150–200k at ~70 clinics with an own retort.

## 2. Industry structure
- **Census.** Illinois has 181 cemetery & crematory firms (NAICS 812220), 111 of them with fewer than 5 employees [C, C8-S005]. Many pet crematories are coded elsewhere (e.g., 812910 pet care: Illinois 963 firms), so a pet-crematory count is MISSING.
- **Growth.** CANA calls pet cremation one of the fastest-growing deathcare segments, and 66% of U.S. households own a pet [C, WS13-S018]. The industry is largely self-regulated through voluntary IAOPCC accreditation [C, WS13-S019].
- **Consolidation.** Regency Pet bought three Ohio/Pennsylvania aftercare firms in 2021 and calls itself the leading U.S. pet aftercare provider [C, C8-S016]. Search summaries also link it to Gateway Services (PitchBook, 2022 buyout) and report a 2025 FTC consent order over Gateway's employee noncompetes. Neither was verified (MISSING).
- **Cyclicality:** low. Pets die regardless of the economy, though private-vs-communal mix may trade down in recessions (E).

## 3. Value chain & mechanism
The pet owner pays the clinic, typically at euthanasia. The clinic pays the crematory a wholesale fee and marks it up (markup MISSING). Owners can also go direct to a crematory: Deiters charges $250 for residence pickup and $95 for vet pickup [B, C8-S014].

The crematory captures the cremation fee, merchandise (urns $50–$295, keepsakes, paw prints [B, C8-S014]) and pickup fees; the clinic keeps its markup.

The new entrant would run scheduled freezer pickups (weekly or on call) and chain-of-custody tracking with Act-compliant disclosures. It would cremate first via a trade crematory, later in its own retort, and return remains to the clinic or family.

## 5. Small-start evidence
Regency's 2021 targets (Hummel Pet Services, Paws Awhile, Animal Friends) were local owner-run crematories worth buying [C, C8-S016]: small-start viability plus an acquirer market. Founding scale MISSING.

## 6. Competition
Consolidators (Regency/Gateway-type platforms; Illinois footprint MISSING); Chicagoland pet crematories (Cherished Pets Remembered in Justice; Forever Remembered in Woodstock [D, C8-S015]); human funeral homes with pet retorts; corporate vet groups with national aftercare agreements (which ones, and the corporate-owned share of Chicago clinics, MISSING); home-euthanasia vets as a channel. Typical clinic complaints: slow returns, lost or mixed-up remains, poor communication (E; reviews not gathered). Switching costs are moderate: routines and trust, but no long contracts at independent clinics (E).

## 7. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the practice manager or owner-DVM.
- **Frequency:** weekly pickups; a typical small-animal clinic produces an estimated 4–10 deceased pets a month (E).
- **Procurement:** trial, then informal standing arrangement. Corporate clinics follow head-office contracts.
- **Statutory role of referrers.** Clinics that refer more than 5 owners in an average month count as referring "on a regular basis". They must hand out the provider's written explanation [A, C8-S006]. A compliant, owner-friendly disclosure is therefore part of the sale.
- **Billing:** clinics are billed monthly (net-30, E); direct families prepay.

## 8. Failure evidence
Remains mix-ups/commingling (reputational catastrophe and a statutory false-certification offense [A, C8-S006]; IAOPCC accreditation exists to prevent them [C, WS13-S019]); losing clinics when a corporate group switches vendors; a trade crematory raising prices or refusing service; retort downtime. No Illinois enforcement cases retrieved (MISSING).

## 9. Regulation (precise)
- **815 ILCS 318:** written explanation (disclose any removal/use/sale of parts; no misleading "private/individual/separate" terms), free copies to owners, referring vets, the Attorney General (annually) and on request; certification with returned remains; fines $1,001–$1,500 first, $2,000–$2,500 subsequent; **no licence or registration** [A, C8-S006].
- **415 ILCS 5/22.49:** not a waste-management facility [A, C8-S007]. **Own retort:** IEPA air permit/registration, fees and timing MISSING; local zoning applies.
- **HB 4695 (2027)** covers human crematories; nothing found extending it to pets [A, WS13-S020]. Voluntary CPCO course $595–$695 [C, WS13-S018]. Commercial auto insurance. Everything is obtainable by the founder.

## 10. Entry wedge test
Why a clinic would switch: barcode/photo chain-of-custody with owner notifications, a guaranteed return time (e.g., 7 days), a statute-compliant explanation the clinic can hand out, and an online memorial/ordering portal that lifts the clinic's merchandise revenue. Cost: software plus disciplined SOPs. Partly copyable. What accumulates: weekly clinic routines, route density, and eventually a permitted retort site that zoning and air-permit friction make scarce (E).
