# WS13-038 (with merged duplicate WS18-033) — Nuisance wildlife control & exclusion

> **Verdict: HOLD.** Do not advance this as a standalone business. Treat it as a high-ticket secondary line of the WS05-007 pest company. Revised evidence level: **D**.
>
> **What favours it:**
> - Demand is urgent and customers pay on completion.
> - Rollins owns two wildlife brands, Critter Control and Trutech [A].
> - The Illinois permit is exam-based and quick to get [A].
>
> **What counts against it:**
> - The permit attaches to each **individual**. Every technician needs a Class A permit: age 18+, a closed-book exam scored at 80% or better, and a 45-day wait to retake.
> - Bat evictions are limited to two seasonal windows.
> - Leads come from paid search, where costs are rising.
> - Relayed Critter Control franchise data show mid-size territories averaging about $560k in gross revenue, with a low of about $83k [D].
>
> Year-3 operating profit: **about $30k–$145k, base about $95k**.

## 1. Industry structure
- No Census code isolates wildlife work. It sits inside NAICS 561710: 13,603 US firms and 330 Illinois employer firms [C, C2-S012, C2-S013].
- Consolidation is visible:
  - Rollins lists wildlife exclusion as ancillary work and wildlife removal as one-time work. It runs Critter Control as a franchise program [A, C2-S008].
  - One Chicago-area wildlife firm, ABC Humane Wildlife Control & Prevention (Schaumburg, about 40 years old per listings [D, C2-S034]), now holds city integrated pest management (IPM) contracts up to $4.374M [A, C2-S016].
- Demand is seasonal: spring litters, fall entry and summer bats (E). It is weakly cyclical (E).

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- **Who pays:** the homeowner or property manager.
- **What they pay for:** an inspection fee, trapping or eviction, exclusion repairs (sealing entry points, one-way doors, chimney caps), optional attic cleanup and insulation, and warranties.
- **Where the margin is:** exclusion labour and warranties. Trapping alone is a low-ticket job.
- **The entrant's position:** a permitted technician and salesperson who quotes at the inspection. Judgement and closing skill sit with the technician, which slows delegation.

## 3. Operator economics
- **Critter Control (franchise), relayed by third parties [D, C2-S025]:**
  - Investment $77,150–$222,475; franchise fee $59,875–$96,900; royalty 7%.
  - The 2025 FDD Item 19 covers 16 mid-size territories, with 2024 average gross revenue of **$560,517** (low $83,018, high $1,333,449).
  - Another relay of the same group reports an average of $629,293 and a median of $404,170. An older relay cites about $864k systemwide.
  - No cost data was found.
- **Rollins (incumbent economics):** consolidated gross margin 52.8% and operating margin 19.3%. Wildlife results are not disclosed separately [A, C2-S008].
- **Prices:** no verified Chicago wildlife prices were found. The Stage A estimate was $300–$1,500 for exclusion and $2–8k for attic restoration (E). A California operator reports average exclusion/removal projects of about **$635 per visit** [D, C2-S019].

## 4. Small-start evidence
- ABC Humane Wildlife began as a wildlife specialist. It later added insect control through Landmark Pest Management and won Chicago facility IPM contracts from 2015 (e.g., $2.06M for 2015–2020) and the airport IPM contract for 2024–2029 [A, C2-S016; D, C2-S034].
- This is the clearest local example of the **wildlife-to-pest pathway**. Its founding size and growth timeline are **MISSING**.

## 5. Competition
- **Players:** Critter Control franchises (including Illinois territories, not enumerated), Trutech (Rollins), pest firms adding wildlife, and many independent operators.
- **Switching costs:** low for one-off jobs. Warranties create some lock-in.
- **Complaints:** not collected (D, MISSING).
- **Weakness of franchises:** a royalty of 7% or more plus brand fees on a business funded by paid leads.

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- Homeowners decide the same day after noises or damage. They find providers through Google or Local Services Ads, referrals from pest firms, and property managers.
- One job per incident, with possible warranty renewals. Repeat purchasing is low unless gutters or attics are maintained.
- Commercial buyers (churches, schools, property managers) also buy bird and bat work. The IDNR Class D permit covers migratory birds [A, C2-S023].

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Lead-cost inflation.** Roto-Rooter, a comparable urgent home-service model, lost 477 basis points of EBITDA margin in Q4 2025 [B, C2-S028]. Its paid leads rose 9.4% [D, C2-S029].
- **Injury and safety.** Ladder and roof work, and rabies exposure (E). These push up workers' compensation and general liability costs. Premiums are **MISSING**.
- **Permit risk.** A violation of the Wildlife Code within 3 years can bar a permit [A, C2-S023].
- **Regulatory timing.** Bat evictions outside the allowed windows are a Class B misdemeanor [A, C2-S024].

## 8. Regulation (primary)
- **IDNR Class A Nuisance Wildlife Control Permit** [A, C2-S023], required for any individual who takes mammals or game birds, or excludes bats, for a fee:
  - Age 18 or older.
  - Closed-book exam, 80% to pass. A failed exam can be retaken after 45 days; a second failure means a 6-month wait.
  - Hunter safety course if guns are used.
  - The permit is annual and expires January 31.
  - The fee is **MISSING**.
- **Class D permit** for migratory birds [A, C2-S023].
- **Business practices** [A, C2-S024]:
  - Written landowner permission.
  - Cost estimates and receipts.
  - Records kept, and an annual report filed by January 20.
- **Bat eviction windows:** permanent evictions only from March 15 to May 15 and from August 5 to October 30, unless IDNR approves otherwise [A, C2-S024].
- **IDPH structural pest licence:** needed if rodenticides or insecticides are applied for hire [A, C2-S001].
- **Home Repair and Remodeling Act:** exclusion repairs on residences of up to 6 units probably fall within it. That means a written contract over $1,000, a consumer-rights pamphlet, and insurance of $100k/$300k bodily injury, $50k property damage and $10k for improper repairs, per the as-enacted text [A, WS18-S010].
- **Feasibility:** exam-based and obtainable in about 1–3 months (E for scheduling).

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **Wedge:** a pest company that already holds IDPH certification adds a Class A permit and sells exclusion with a multi-year warranty to its residential and commercial base and to property managers. Referral traffic is cheaper than paid search.
- **Standalone alternative:** responsiveness and a warranty. That is easily copied, and every competitor already claims it.
