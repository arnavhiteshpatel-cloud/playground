>   firms like it hold benchmark datasets a new entrant lacks.

> **Year-3 range:** about $20k–200k. That range is real but rests on assumptions; Stage D needs fee and savings
> evidence first.

## Industry structure
- **Who the buyers are.** Shippers spending roughly $250k–$5M a year with UPS, FedEx and regional parcel carriers:
  e-commerce brands, distributors and light manufacturers.
- **What we could not get.**
  - Parcel market size (UPS/FedEx 10-K figures were not fetched this session): MISSING.
  - Counts of firms in this segment by state: MISSING.
- **Fragmentation.** The field has dozens of consultancies and SaaS tools (D). No Census code isolates it.
- **Cyclicality.** Parcel volume moves with e-commerce. Savings opportunities grow when carriers raise general rate
  increases (GRIs) and surcharges (E).
- **Illinois.** Chicago has a dense distributor and 3PL base (E).

## Value chain & mechanism
1. The shipper signs a carrier agreement containing discounts, minimums, surcharges and earned-incentive tiers.
2. The consultant benchmarks those terms, coaches the shipper through negotiation, and audits invoices.
3. The consultant's fee is a share of verified savings against a baseline, usually paid monthly for the agreement term
   [D, C4-S033; WS14-S019].
4. Margin depends on three things: depth of benchmark data, analyst hours per client, and how disputes over the
   baseline get settled.

## Competition
- **National consultancies:** TransImpact, Shipware, Refund Retriever, CTSI-Global, LJM and others (search listings, D).
- **SaaS platforms:** Reveel and audit tools.
- **3PLs and aggregators** offering pooled rates to small shippers (E).
- **The carriers' own reps.**

**Weaknesses of incumbents:**
- gain-share terms locked to long periods;
- shipper fatigue [D, C4-S033].

**Switching** happens only at contract renewal.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Who decides:** the ops or finance lead.
- **When they buy:** triggered by an annual GRI, a surcharge shock or contract expiry.
- **How they evaluate:** a free benchmark first.
- **Contract terms:** a gain-share contract for 1–3 years.
- **How they find vendors:** carrier-rep frustration, LinkedIn, referrals from 3PLs and accountants (E).
- **Recommended client-side protections:** negotiate the contingency term, and exclude acquisitions from the savings
  base (shipper-side presentation, D, page did not load fully).

## Failure evidence
- The refund pool that once funded audit firms has largely disappeared [C/A, WS14-S016, S018].
- SaaS is substituting for consultants [D, C4-S033].
- Disputes over baselines are reported anecdotally (E).
- Closures of named firms: none found (MISSING).

## Regulation
- **Licences:** none specific. This is not freight brokerage, because no transportation is arranged. Note, though,
  that 49 CFR 371.2 defines brokerage as arranging transportation [A, C4-S029], and negotiating carrier *pricing* is
  not arranging shipments (E: interpretation).
- **Confidentiality:** carrier-agreement confidentiality clauses may restrict sharing pricing with third parties
  (MISSING, not verified).
- **Data security:** invoice data handling.

## Entry wedge test
**The pitch to switch:**
- a fast, free benchmark;
- month-to-month audit;
- a shorter gain-share term (e.g., 12 months), or a flat project fee instead of a 3-year tail. This targets the
  "fatigue" weakness.
- a vertical focus (supplements, auto parts) to build benchmark depth.

**What it costs to deliver:** analyst time plus data tools.

**Is it copyable?** Yes. Data depth is the moat, and incumbents have far more of it.
