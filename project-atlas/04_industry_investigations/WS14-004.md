# WS14-004 — Sales/use tax reverse audit (overpayment recovery) for manufacturers
Stage C · batch C7 · investigated 2026-11-28

> **Verdict: HOLD, downgraded.** Evidence: **D**. Illinois procedure is now A-level; fee levels, hit rates and recovery sizes remain D/E.
>
> The Stage A legal premise holds only in part. A non-CPA, non-attorney may act as an "other" representative on IL-2848; only hearings and the Tax Tribunal require an attorney [A, WS14-S008]. But Illinois refund mechanics are hostile to the purchaser-side model:
> 1. **Vendor-collected tax cannot be claimed by the purchaser.** Tax a purchaser paid *to a vendor* (ROT reimbursement) can be recovered only through the **vendor's** claim. IDOR will not approve it unless the vendor proves it bore the tax or unconditionally repaid the purchaser [A, C7-S021, C7-S022]. Purchasers may claim directly only for use tax they paid to IDOR themselves, i.e., self-assessed or direct-pay accruals [A, C7-S022].
> 2. **Claims are limited to roughly 3–3.5 years** [A, C7-S021].
> 3. **Approved claims are normally paid as credit memoranda**, not cash. Cash refunds depend on appropriations and hardship [A, C7-S023].
>
> So the work is narrower (self-accrued use tax plus vendor-cooperation requests), slower, and the client's "recovery" is often a credit against future tax. A contingency fee therefore costs the client cash today against a non-cash credit. Combined with a credibility wall against CPA SALT practices, year-3 profit is ~$5k–$200k (base ~$65k), with high variance and a 6–18 month cash lag.

## Industry structure
- **Who does reverse audits.** CPA-firm state and local tax (SALT) practices and specialist recovery firms. Marketing examples:
  - Cherry Bekaert claims manufacturers average $75k+/yr in overpaid sales/use tax [D, WS14-S009];
  - CliftonLarsonAllen markets reverse audits and utility refund studies [D, WS14-S029].
- **Fee basis.** Contingent fees on net tax recovered are common [C, WS14-S010].
- **Market size and firm counts.** MISSING.
- **Target pool in Illinois.** Manufacturers and heavy purchasers with Manufacturing Machinery & Equipment exemptions, production inputs and utility-tax exposure.
- **Cyclicality.** Low; refunds are counter-cyclically attractive.

## Value chain and mechanism
The analyst reviews AP invoices, fixed-asset additions and use-tax accrual workpapers for:
- exempt items taxed;
- double accruals;
- wrong local rates;
- tax on non-taxable services.

**Two recovery routes in Illinois:**
1. **Purchaser claim to IDOR** for use tax the client self-assessed and paid. This is direct, and the credit is issued to the client [A, C7-S022].
2. **Vendor refund request.** The vendor refunds the client (or issues a credit memo) and files its own ROT claim. The vendor must unconditionally repay first [A, C7-S021]. Vendors have little incentive to do this administrative work, so this route depends on the client's purchasing leverage.

The fee is a percentage of the recovered amount, payable when credited.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker.** CFO or controller.
- **Gating requirements.** An NDA and ERP/AP data access; IT and audit sign-off often needed.
- **Sales cycle.** 2–6 months.
- **Frequency.** A look-back once every 2–3 years.
- **Trust.** Buyers trust their CPA by default. A non-CPA firm needs referral partners (small CPA firms without SALT depth) or an engineering-style specialty such as M&E exemption expertise.
- **Constraint from fee restrictions.** Contingent-fee restrictions for some public companies and jurisdictions narrow the CPA-firm offering [D, WS14-S029], which is an opening for non-CPAs. Illinois-specific restrictions on non-CPA contingency are not found (MISSING).

## Failure evidence
Generic failure modes, not documented by named closures:
- long payout lags starve the firm;
- denied claims after the work is done;
- client audit exposure if aggressive positions trigger an IDOR audit;
- cash appropriation limits on refunds [A, C7-S023].

## Regulation (precise)
- **Representation.** IL-2848 allows "other" representatives with a witness/notary step. Only attorneys may appear at IDOR administrative hearings or the Independent Tax Tribunal [A, WS14-S008].
- **ROT claims.** Retailer files; unjust-enrichment proof required; 3-year window anchored on Jan 1/Jul 1, auto-extended 6 months if the limitation period would expire within 6 months [A, C7-S021].
- **Use tax claims.** Purchaser claims only for tax paid to the Department [A, C7-S022].
- **Refund form.** Credit memoranda, assignable only to other Illinois taxpayers with approval; cash refunds subject to appropriation [A, C7-S023].
- **Licensing.** No licence is needed to prepare claims. A contract tax attorney is needed for contested claims.
- **Other states.** Rules vary; not researched.

## Entry wedge test
The wedge is AI line-item classification of AP data against Illinois exemption rules, a no-cost assessment, and a focus on self-accrued use tax and M&E exemptions where the purchaser can claim directly.
- **Real:** extraction and classification costs fall sharply with ordinary APIs.
- **Copyable:** CPA firms have the same tools.
- **Hard parts:** credibility and data access, not analysis.

## Economics sanity check (year 3; E)
The assumed average net recoverable of $90k per engagement is a discount to the $75k/yr × 3-year marketing claim, reflecting the vendor-route friction and exemptions already captured.
- **Base:**
  - Revenue: 8 completed engagements/yr × $90k × 25% fee = **$180k** (recognized when credited).
  - Costs: 1 analyst $75k; contract tax-attorney review $15k; data/tools $10k; travel/admin $15k; total **$115k**.
  - Operating profit ≈ **$65k**.
- **Low:** 3 engagements × $40k × 25% = $30k, against ~$30k of costs ≈ **$0–5k**.
- **High:** 15 × $120k × 28% = $504k, minus 2 analysts, a senior reviewer and overhead (~$300k) ≈ **$200k**.
- **Cash.** Year 1 is likely negative. Working capital of $20k–$60k (founder runway) is needed before the first fee lands. Launch cash (tools, insurance, legal templates) is $10k–$40k including runway buffer.
