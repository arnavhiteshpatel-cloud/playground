# WS14-019 — Niche non-healthcare group purchasing organization (GPO)

> **Verdict: DROP.**
> - **Revised evidence level:** D (fee levels are B but healthcare-only; new-entrant economics E).
> - **Year-3 operating profit:** −$20k to $100k.
>
> The mechanism is real and incumbents value it highly. Premier sold its non-healthcare GPO book to OMNIA Partners for about **$800M** [B, C9-S008]. That price is the problem: non-healthcare group purchasing is a scale business already consolidated by OMNIA, foodservice GPOs and distributor programs.
>
> A newcomer needs roughly $10–20M of *activated* member purchasing before net fees cover one employee. The documented fee levels (volume-weighted ~1–2%, about 70% passed back) come from five large healthcare GPOs [B, WS14-S023]. No non-healthcare fee schedule or small-start case was found.
>
> The chicken-and-egg ramp, with vendors paying only on proven volume, makes year-3 profit near zero in the base case.

## Industry structure
- **Size and growth:** no official non-healthcare GPO size. The proxy, NAICS 561499 ("all other business support services," which includes GPOs among many others), shows 3,628 U.S. establishments (83% with fewer than 10 employees) and 136 in Illinois [C, C9-S007]. It is a poor proxy, and growth is MISSING.
- **Large players:**
  - OMNIA Partners, which expanded through the Premier deal (closed July 2023).
  - Premier itself, which keeps economic participation in non-healthcare purchasing through its contracts [B, C9-S008] and still reports net admin fees of $556.3M (FY2025), mostly healthcare [A, WS14-S024].
  - Foodservice GPOs (Avendra, Foodbuy, Entegra) and Buyers Edge. These names are analyst knowledge, unsourced.
- **Cyclicality:** low. Purchasing of consumables recurs.

## Value chain & mechanism
1. Members join free.
2. The GPO negotiates tiered contracts with manufacturers and distributors.
3. Vendors pay admin fees as a percentage of member purchases, reported quarterly in arrears.
4. The GPO shares part of the fees back with members.

Margin sits with whoever aggregates the most volume and owns the vendor contracts.

Healthcare benchmarks [B, WS14-S023]:
- vendor fees are about 92% of GPO revenue;
- the most frequent fee is 3%, with a volume-weighted average of about 1–2%;
- about 70% of fees are passed to customers or owners.

Premier's FY2025 decline in net admin fees was driven by a rising member fee share [A, WS14-S024]. Members are capturing more of the spread.

A new entrant would either:
- become an affiliate or sub-GPO of a national GPO, taking a split of fees (terms **MISSING**); or
- build vertical-specific local contracts from zero.

## Operator economics
- **Premier (incumbent):** net admin fees $556.3M; Supply Chain Services adjusted EBITDA $326.9M (FY2025) [A, WS14-S024]. That is a margin above 50% at enormous scale, and it is incumbent economics.
- **OMNIA Partners (private):** paid about $800M for Premier's non-healthcare operations [B, C9-S008]. Its revenue and EBITDA are undisclosed.
- **Small or regional non-healthcare GPOs:** no disclosed numbers found (MISSING). Business-for-sale listings were not searched.
- **New-entrant economics:** fully E. See the sanity check below.

## Small-start evidence
None found or verified this session. Buyers Edge Platform is commonly described as having started as a small restaurant buying group, but that is unsourced analyst knowledge and is not used as evidence.

## Competition
- **Broadline distributors** (Sysco, US Foods) run their own deviated-pricing and rebate programs.
- **National GPOs** offer free membership with instant access to thousands of contracts. A newcomer's contracts will be worse until volume arrives.
- **Customer behavior:** members' main complaint is low realized savings and compliance friction (D, Stage A).
- **Switching costs:** low for members, so they can belong to several programs, and very high for vendors.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the owner or operator (restaurant) or asset manager (multifamily).
- **Frequency:** weekly to monthly replenishment.
- **Spend:** contracted-category spend per member is MISSING. A restaurant buying perhaps $200–400k a year with 20–40% on-contract is analyst E.
- **How they find programs today:** distributor reps, trade associations, peers.
- **The activation problem:** joining is easy, but members must change ordering behavior. Activation rates are MISSING.

## Failure evidence
No specific GPO bankruptcies were found. The structural failure modes are:
- members join but don't buy;
- vendors refuse fees at low volume;
- distributors undercut.

Fee compression toward members is documented at the largest incumbent [A, WS14-S024].

## Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **Licensing:** no license outside healthcare.
- **Anti-kickback safe harbor:** the Medicare safe harbor (42 CFR 1001.952(j), via GAO) is irrelevant outside healthcare.
- **Antitrust:** buyer groups are generally permissible (analyst knowledge, E). Robinson-Patman price-discrimination questions can arise for vendors.
- **Fee disclosure:** disclosure to members is best practice.
- **Feasibility:** essentially nothing to obtain. Feasibility is high.

## Entry wedge test
- **The wedge:** community plus a vertical focus. For example, Chicago independent restaurants, or owners of 100–2,000 multifamily units.
- **Why members would switch:** a member would use the new firm only if its contracts beat what national GPOs give them free. That is unlikely early, because a newcomer starts with no volume.
- **The affiliate route:** it gives immediate contracts but hands most of the fees to the parent (split MISSING).
- **Copyability:** fully copyable, and incumbents already serve these verticals.
- **Verdict on the wedge:** weak.
