# WS15-020 — UST compliance management for independent gas stations (Illinois)
Stage C · batch C7 · investigated 2026-11-28

> **Verdict: HOLD. Evidence level stays at E for the economics; the legal path is now A.**
>
> The key legal question is resolved. Illinois requires the 30-day and annual walkthroughs to be done by the facility's designated **Class A or Class B Operator** [A, C7-S017]. A trained operator may be designated for more than one facility [A, C7-S018, C7-S020]. A service firm can therefore legally do the work, but only by having its technicians complete OSFM-approved A/B training (offered online by several providers) and be formally designated at each client site. The annual containment-sump inspection must be done by an OSFM-licensed contractor and has to be subcontracted [A, C7-S017].
>
> Pain is quantified: 83% walkthrough compliance and 76% technical compliance in FY2026 [A, WS15-S029]. No market price could be found. Every facility already must have a trained A/B operator, who is often the owner, so the service sells "assurance", not legally required labor. Willingness to pay is the binding unknown.
>
> Year-3 operating profit is plausibly $30k–$150k. Hold until a price test shows owners pay about $100+/month.

## Industry structure
- **Tank and facility counts.** Illinois had 17,822 active petroleum USTs, 2,858 OSFM on-site inspections, 244 confirmed releases and a cleanup backlog of 4,619 in FY2026 [A, WS15-S029]. A facility count is not published in the sources read. Using the national ratio of 532,613 tanks to ~186,000 facilities (2.86 per facility) gives about **6,200 Illinois facilities** [E].
- **Chicago region and independents.** An assumed ~40–45% in the Chicago region gives ~2,500–2,800 [E]. A large share are chain-operated (Casey's, Pilot, Thorntons, GPM, Speedway, among others). Several chains run their own OSFM-approved Class C training [A, C7-S019], which signals in-house compliance staff. The addressable independent/multi-site-jobber segment is perhaps 1,000–1,500 Chicago-area sites [E].
- **Demand.** Mandated, recurring and non-cyclical. Station counts are slowly declining (EV transition, consolidation; trend not quantified here).

## Value chain and mechanism
The owner pays for:
- monthly compliance labor (walkthroughs and checklists);
- annual inspections and tests by OSFM-licensed contractors (sump inspection, emergency-stop and shear-valve certifications on OSFM forms) [A, C7-S017];
- operator training;
- release-detection monitoring (ATG).

A compliance firm captures a monthly fee plus a markup or coordination fee on licensed-contractor work. The real value to the owner is avoiding:
- a notice of violation, which triggers mandatory retraining [A, C7-S018];
- a red tag, which stops fuel deliveries [A, C7-S018];
- release liability.

## Competition
- **Owner DIY.** The owner is often the designated A/B operator and can do the 30-day walkthrough in under an hour. Compliance is already 83% [A, WS15-S029].
- **Jobbers and branded-fuel programs.**
- **Software firms.** Remote ATG monitoring with alarm management.
- **Licensed contractors.** They offer "compliance packages" bundled with mandatory testing. This is the most dangerous competitor, because only they can do the sump inspections.

Switching costs are low.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker.** Owner-operator, often running 1–5 stations. Decides quickly.
- **Price sensitivity.** High; fuel margins are thin.
- **Purchase trigger.** Most likely an NOV, a red tag, a change of ownership, or an absentee or multi-site owner.
- **Contracts.** Monthly, autopay feasible.
- **Prospecting.** OSFM's public UST facility search, red-tagged-tank search and contractor search [A, OSFM database page] are usable prospect lists.

## Failure evidence
Documented failure modes are regulatory, not business-level:
- red tags and green-tag revocation for missing trained operators [A, C7-S018];
- 244 new releases in a single year [A, WS15-S029].

The business risk is liability: a designated operator who misses a leak indication could face claims. Pollution/E&O insurance is needed (cost MISSING).

## Regulation (Illinois + federal), precise
- **Operator designation.** 41 Ill. Adm. Code 176.615 requires A, B and C operators at every facility. 176.620 requires an OSFM-approved course plus exam, with A/B retraining every 4 years (or annually) [A, C7-S018].
- **Walkthroughs.** 176.655(b): every 30 days (release detection, spill buckets, fill caps, dispensers, cathodic protection logs) and annually (sumps, emergency stops, shear valves) [A, C7-S017].
- **Sump inspections.** 176.655(c): only by an employee of an OSFM-licensed contractor holding the installation/retrofit or inspection-testing module [A, C7-S017].
- **Onsite operator.** At manned facilities a Class A, B or C operator must be onsite at all times [A, C7-S017]. Clerks fill the C role, not the service firm.
- **Fees.** Training fee is a "reasonable fee" set by providers [A, C7-S020]. OSFM contractor licensing exam and fees are MISSING (not pursued, because it would be subcontracted).

**Feasibility.** Founder or technicians can be trained online in days. Licensed-contractor work is subcontracted. Feasibility is raised to 7.

## Entry wedge test
The wedge is "We are your designated A/B operator": monthly walkthrough, OSFM-format records binder, alarm follow-up, and coordination of all annual licensed tests, with a pass-your-inspection guarantee. It may need bilingual service for owner communities.
- **Cost to deliver.** Technician time of about 45–75 minutes per site plus driving.
- **Copyability.** Easy for licensed contractors to copy.
- **Moat.** Route density and trust. Liability exposure is the hidden cost.

## Economics sanity check (year 3; all E)
A technician can visit 4–6 sites per day, about 90 sites per month.
- **Base ($100/site-month, the realistic price unless validated higher):**
  - Revenue: 120 sites × $100 × 12 = $144k, plus coordination markup on licensed annual tests of about $7k, total $151k.
  - Costs: 1 technician $62k; vehicle $9k; insurance (pollution, E&O, GL) $15k; software $5k; admin/marketing $20k; total $111k.
  - Operating profit ≈ **$40k**.
- **At $150/month:** Revenue $223k, operating profit ≈ **$110k**.
- **Low:** 50 sites × $100 × 12 = $60k, founder doing the walkthroughs, minus $29k overhead ≈ **$31k**. This is capped by founder hours.
- **High:** 250 sites × $175 × 12 = $525k, minus 3 technicians, coordinator and overhead (~$318k) ≈ **$207k**. This would require ~20% penetration of Chicago-area independents [E].
- **Capital:** $5k–$20k (vehicle, testing tools, insurance deposit). ROIC is high, but profit scales only with route density.
