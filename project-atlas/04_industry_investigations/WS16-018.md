# WS16-018 — Warehouse storage & material-handling products dealer-integrator (incl. used rack) (Stage C, batch C3)

> **Verdict: HOLD. Revised evidence level: D.**
>
> **What holds up:**
> - **The dealer channel exists.** Steel King and Penco both run dealer or distributor programs [B, WS16-S043/S044].
> - **Margins are plausible.** Census reports a 31.6% gross margin for machinery and equipment wholesalers [C, WS16-S018].
> - **Tickets are large:** projects run from $10k to $500k.
>
> **What is missing or weak:**
> - **No disclosed margin data** for a small rack dealer or integrator.
> - **Dealer criteria and discounts are unknown.** Steel King's dealer pages could not be read this session.
> - **The project-deposit assumption is unverified (E).** It is what makes Stage A's 7 cash score work.
> - **Demand is cyclical** and tied to industrial leasing and construction. Chicago issued permits fell 34% from 2019 to 2025 [A, WS10-S010]; the industrial-suburb data is MISSING.
> - **Buyers choose integrators on references and engineering competence**, and a new firm has neither.
>
> **Bottom line:** year-3 operating profit of about $20–250k, with lumpy, project-by-project results.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Classification:** dealer-integrators sit in NAICS 423830 (industrial machinery and equipment merchant wholesalers).
  - Illinois has 1,328 establishments [C, C3-S014]. US firms with $1–2.5M of receipts average about $348k of receipts per employee, with payroll at 17.7% of receipts [C, C3-S015].
  - Rack is a sub-niche of this code, so the size of the Illinois rack market is MISSING.
- **Margin proxy:** independent machinery and equipment wholesalers averaged a 31.6% gross margin in 2022 [C, WS16-S018].
- **Cyclicality:** high. Demand follows warehouse leasing, expansions and 3PL contract wins. New-rack prices also swing with steel prices and Section 232 tariffs, which remain at 50% on steel [C, WS03-S011].

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
1. A manufacturer (Steel King, Unarco, Interlake Mecalux and others) sells to an authorized dealer at a dealer discount.
2. The dealer designs the layout, sells it, has the material drop-shipped, and subcontracts installation.
3. Engineering and permits are passed through.
4. Used rack, bought at closures and resold, earns a wider spread (30–50%, E) but needs a yard and trucking.

The new entrant is a salesperson and project manager first. Installation crews are subcontracted.

## 4. Small-start evidence
None documented with numbers (MISSING). Regional integrators are commonly founder-owned (E), but no Chicago example was sourced.

## 5. Competition
- **Who competes:**
  - Established Chicago-area rack dealers and integrators (names not sourced this session)
  - Material-handling dealers such as Alta, which has Illinois locations and recently divested its Dock & Door business [B, C3-S031]
  - Manufacturers' direct national-account teams
  - E-commerce sellers of shelving at the low end
  - Used-rack brokers
- **Switching costs per project:** low.
- **What actually drives the buying decision:** references, design competence and lead-time reliability.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the operations or facility lead at a 3PL, distributor or manufacturer. Industrial real-estate brokers and general contractors are influencers.
- **Purchase triggers:** a new lease, an expansion, or rack damage.
- **Frequency:** infrequent per customer, every few years.
- **Process:** bids from two or three integrators with layout drawings. Payment terms (deposits vs. net-30) are MISSING.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Cyclical project droughts.**
- **Mis-specified capacities and installation accidents.**
- **Steel and tariff price swings** between quote and order.
- **Permit delays.**
- **Underbidding** to win the first references.
- No documented bankruptcies were pulled (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation
- **Registration and tax:** Illinois LLC plus IDOR REG-1, with CRT-61 resale certificates for tax-free purchase of resale goods [A, WS16-S036].
- **Licenses:** none professional.
- **Permits and engineering:**
  - Municipal building permits and seismic/engineering calculations for some rack and mezzanine installs.
  - High-piled combustible storage permits under the adopted fire code.
  - Requirements by suburb are MISSING.
  - Chicago general-contractor licensing for installation work is unverified.
  - Engineering stamps must come from a licensed Illinois PE (partner or manufacturer).
- **Insurance:** general and product liability plus subcontractor certificates.

## 9. Entry wedge test
**Proposed wedge:**
- Speed from a mix of used and new rack (used ships in days)
- CAD layouts within 48 hours
- Bundled annual inspection programs (WS03-013), which create recurring touchpoints

**Cost to deliver:** CAD software, sales time, and optional used-rack inventory ($10–60k).

**Copyability:** high. Established dealers already sell used rack. The real moat would be relationships with industrial brokers and 3PLs, which take years to build.

- **ROIC:** high if deposits hold, low if the firm ends up financing projects.
