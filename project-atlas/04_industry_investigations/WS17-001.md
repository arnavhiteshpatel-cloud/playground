# WS17-001: Done-for-you DOT compliance management for small/mid motor carriers (Stage C, batch C4)

> **Verdict: HOLD.** Do not pursue it as a standalone business. Keep it as the entry wedge inside SYN-001.
> Evidence level: **D** (was E).
>
> **Why.** Prices are now observable, and they sit at or below the bottom of the Stage A hypothesis ($25–60 per driver
> per month):
> - Done-for-you plans run **$149/month for 1–2 drivers and $249/month for 6–10 drivers** (about $25 per driver at 10
>   drivers), plus a $99 setup fee [D, C4-S011]. Another provider quotes $150–400/month [D, C4-S012].
> - Self-serve DQ software costs **$5 per driver per month** [B, C4-S008].
> - Consortium membership is $149 per company per year with unlimited drivers [C, C4-S006].
> - Insurance agencies give monitoring away free [D, C4-S038].
>
> National incumbents add scale and trust. J.J. Keller has 600+ managed-services staff [C, C4-S010]. OOIDA sells
> DQ-file "Compliance Connection" to its members [B, C4-S004]. At evidenced prices, a capable entrant reaches roughly
> $40–210k of year-3 profit, and only by holding about 120 clients against 20–30% annual churn.

## Industry structure
- **Buyers (A, C4-S002).** In the 8 Chicago-area counties:
  - 11,781 active carriers with 2–100 power units (PU) and recent MCS-150 filings;
  - 9,622 of them registered before 2024 (more durable);
  - about 110,800 self-reported CDL drivers in this band.
- **National size.** No Census series isolates "carrier compliance services." It spans NAICS 541611, 561110 and
  software. SUSB/CBP access needs an API key: MISSING.
- **Demand is mandated and recurring:**
  - driver qualification (DQ) files (49 CFR 391.51) [A, WS17-S004];
  - annual Clearinghouse queries (382.701) [A, WS17-S005];
  - random testing at 50% drugs / 10% alcohol [A, WS17-S006];
  - periodic inspections [A, WS17-S002];
  - an 18-month new-entrant monitoring period with a safety audit [A, C4-S035].
- **Cyclical through buyer mortality.** Of 2021 Chicago-area registrants, only 29.4% are active and filing today
  [A, C4-S001].

## Value chain & mechanism
The carrier (owner or office manager) pays a monthly fee. Third-party pass-throughs follow:
- MVRs from $12.95 [B, C4-S008];
- PSP $19.99;
- FMCSA query $1.25 [B, C4-S003].

The provider captures a labor margin on document chasing, expiry tracking and audit-file assembly. The new entrant
would:
1. onboard the carrier's files;
2. run expiry calendars, the annual MVR review and Clearinghouse queries;
3. prepare the carrier for safety audits.

**New-entrant economics.** One coordinator (loaded ~$65k; BLS bookkeeping-clerk median $51,440 [C, C4-S016]) can
serve about 40 small clients (E). At about $3,000–4,200 per client per year, that is $120–170k revenue per
coordinator, a contribution margin of about 45–60% before sales and overhead.

## Small-start evidence
J.J. Keller started in 1953 as "a one-man consulting firm for motor carriers" [C, C4-S010]. It is the only documented
small start found, and it is very old. Recent small consultancies exist (Fiverr gigs, local offices), but their scale
is unverified (D).

## Competition
- **National:** J.J. Keller; Foley (DQ files, MVR/CSA monitors, consortium) [D, C4-S007]; DOT Compliance Group (1,253
  Google reviews; runs an insurance-broker referral program) [D, C4-S014]; Simplex Group [D, C4-S013].
- **Associations:** OOIDA.
- **Software:** DQ modules in ELD and telematics platforms; DOTDriverFiles.
- **Local:** Chicago-suburb "trucking services" offices offering consortium, Clearinghouse, IRP and IFTA (Stage A
  search, D).
- **Insurance agencies** giving compliance help away.

**Incumbent weaknesses:** remote phone support, self-serve tools that leave the work with the carrier, and English-only
service (E).

**Customer complaints:** MISSING; review text was not accessible.

**Switching costs:** low to moderate (re-onboarding the files).

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the owner, often doing compliance personally in a 2–10 PU firm.
- **Triggers:** a new-entrant audit, an insurance renewal tied to CSA scores, an audit letter, or a crash.
- **Contracts:** month-to-month ("no contracts" is the norm) [D, C4-S011].
- **Vendor discovery:** referrals from insurance agents, factoring firms, dealers and community networks.
- **Trust problem:** carriers are bombarded with deceptive mailers, so cold mail converts poorly (E).

## Failure evidence
- **Client failure** [A, C4-S001]:
  - 2023 for-hire cohort: 41.9% still active;
  - 2022 for-hire cohort: 30.7% still active.
- **Price compression** from software and consortia [C4-S006, C4-S008].
- **Liability:** a missed expiry that triggers fines or an out-of-service order damages reputation (Stage A).
- **Founder-as-only-expert risk.**
- Named closures of compliance firms: none found (MISSING).

## Regulation
- **Licence:** none identified for compliance administration (absence not verified). Avoid legal advice.
- **Consortium add-on:** Clearinghouse service-agent registration [A, C4-S037]; Part 40 limits [A, C4-S036].
- **IFTA/IRP:** filed under carrier power of attorney (POA). The remitter-licence question is MISSING (ilga.gov TLS
  failure).
- **E&O insurance:** advisable; cost MISSING.
- **TCPA:** governs outbound calls and texts.

## Entry wedge test
**Why switch:**
- local, in-person service;
- multilingual (Polish, Serbian, Ukrainian, Punjabi and Spanish trucking communities: E, not verified);
- truly done-for-you, with an audit-ready guarantee;
- a fixed price of $200–450 per month for 3–20 PU.

**Cost to deliver:** coordinator time of about 2–4 hours per client per month (E).

**Copyable?** Yes, and incumbents already sell it.

**Standalone verdict:** the wedge is weak.
