# WS17-004: Mobile preventive maintenance for light/medium-duty service and delivery fleets (Stage C, batch C4)

> **Verdict: ADVANCE to Stage D (conditional).** Evidence: **D** (was E). The small-start evidence is C; the
> new-entrant economics are E.
>
> **Why.** This is the batch's best-documented start-small-and-scale story.
> - **Dickinson Fleet Services started in Indianapolis in 1997** doing oil changes, PM and minor repairs. By Cox
>   Automotive's 2021 acquisition it had 700+ mobile units and about 800 technicians. Under Cox it passed 1,500
>   technicians and 14,000+ clients a year [C, C4-S021].
> - **The founders' sons launched Coltrain Onsite Fleet Care in August 2025** and reached about 70 mobile technicians
>   in 15 states within about a year. They price "roughly on par with a quality shop" [C, C4-S021].
> - **Demand is non-discretionary**, and labor-led service carries incumbent gross margins of 66–68% (parts 28–30%)
>   [A, WS17-S021].
> - **Tech labor has a priced market.** Chicago medians are $55,320 for automotive techs and $65,680 for diesel techs
>   [C, C4-S016].
>
> **Constraints.**
> - The founder cannot wrench, so hiring the first lead technician is the critical step.
> - Capital runs about $40–70k per equipped van.
> - Cox and Ryder (Torque) are national competitors.
>
> **Year 3:** about $10k–185k operating profit (Stage A had $80–350k).

## Industry structure
- **Incumbent scale (A, WS17-S021).** Rush's aftermarket revenue was $2.52B at about 36.9% gross margin. Rush also runs
  mobile service units.
- **Ryder.** Runs Torque by Ryder mobile maintenance and SelectCare ($680M with other) [A, WS17-S022].
- **Cox.** Fleet Services by Cox Automotive: 1,500+ techs [C, C4-S021].
- **National market size for mobile fleet maintenance:** MISSING (Census CBP NAICS 811111/811198 not accessible).
- **Chicago fleets under 10,001 lb** are unregistered [A, WS17-S016]. FMCSA counts the heavier units: 6,493 carriers
  with 2–5 PU and 3,477 with 6–20 PU [A, C4-S002]. The construction and utility fleets are also identifiable
  [A, WS17-S001].
- **Cyclicality.** Maintenance is mandated for units of 10,001 lb and up (49 CFR 396.17 [A, WS17-S002]) and needed
  for all units. Deferrals happen in freight recessions; Rush notes the 2025 freight recession [A, WS17-S021].
- **EV effect.** EVs reduce engine PM over the long run, but diesel and ICE vans persist (WS17 summary).

## Value chain & mechanism
The fleet pays a flat PM price plus labor hours and parts markup, net-30. The provider buys parts on account from
distributors (NAPA/GPC). Margin comes from three places:
- technician labor utilization;
- route density (more stops per night);
- parts markup.

The entrant replaces three things: shop visits, driver time and the second driver that a shop run needs. Coltrain
frames the main savings as planned rather than unplanned downtime [C, C4-S021].

**Incumbent vs new-entrant economics.** Incumbent gross margins assume shop lifts and high utilization. A new entrant
loses billable hours to travel and setup.

## Small-start evidence
Dickinson grew from a small Indianapolis operation (1997) to about 800 technicians over 24 years [C, C4-S021].

**Caveat:** that was a family with an industry background, and Coltrain's founders were raised in the business. A
non-mechanic founder is a different case.

## Competition
- **National:** Fleet Services by Cox Automotive; Ryder Torque; Coltrain (expanding, mostly east of the Mississippi);
  Rush mobile vans for heavy trucks.
- **Local:** dealers, quick lubes, independents.
- **FMCs** route work to network shops [B, C4-S019].

**Weaknesses of nationals:**
- technician turnover and quality variance. Coltrain's founders voice concern about hiring to headcount quotas at the
  scaled incumbent and built "the version they wanted to work for" [C, C4-S021];
- minimum fleet sizes (E).

**Customer complaints:** MISSING.

**Switching costs:** low to moderate, rising with held records and density.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Who decides:** fleet or operations manager, or the owner, at 10–150-vehicle service, delivery and contractor
  fleets.
- **Frequency:** PM every 5–10k miles, monthly yard visits.
- **How they start:** a trial on a few vehicles over 2–8 weeks.
- **Contracts:** typically no long-term contract; net-30.
- **Vendor discovery:** shop relationships, FMC networks, and Google ("mobile fleet maintenance").

## Failure evidence
**Documented risks:**
- technician retention (Rush calls it a competitive factor [A, WS17-S021]);
- underpricing travel;
- comebacks and warranty claims;
- weather.

**Closures:** no closures of mobile-maintenance startups were found this session (searched; nothing returned). This is
a search gap, not proof of safety.

**Illinois-specific limit:** Illinois annual inspections for intrastate units must happen at Official Testing Stations,
which caps any "mobile DOT inspection" product line (WS17 summary).

## Regulation
- **State licence:** no Illinois general mechanic licence identified. 625 ILCS 5/5-301 licensing covers rebuilders and
  recyclers (applicability to general repair is E).
- **Chicago licence:** a municipal licence for repair businesses is MISSING (chicago.gov not accessed).
- **Fluids:** used-oil and fluid handling on customer sites.
- **Insurance:** garage-keepers and GL insurance.
- **Certification:** EPA 609 certification if servicing A/C (E).
- **Workforce:** technicians carry the skill; ASE certification is optional but sells.
- **Timing:** all achievable in 1–3 months (E).

## Entry wedge test
- **Why switch:** overnight yard PM with photo-stamped inspection points and same-week DVIR defect repair, for 20–100
  vehicle fleets.
- **Price:** about quality-shop rates (Coltrain parity [C, C4-S021]).
- **Cost to deliver:** one equipped van plus a technician.
- **Copyable?** Yes, but route density and records build a modest local moat.
- **What it depends on:** the founder recruiting and keeping a capable lead tech, possibly with equity or profit share.
