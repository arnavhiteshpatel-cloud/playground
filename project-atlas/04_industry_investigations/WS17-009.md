# WS17-009: Outsourced fleet maintenance management for fleets of 15–300 vehicles (Stage C, batch C4)

> **Verdict: DROP as a standalone business.** At most it survives as a reporting feature inside WS17-004.
> Evidence: **D** (was E).
>
> **Why.** The Stage A pricing hypothesis does not hold, and the gap it assumed is not there:
> - **The fee is far lower than assumed.** Stage A assumed $15–40 per vehicle per month. Fleet management companies
>   (FMCs) charge maintenance-management fees "nearly always" per vehicle per month, "usually in the $5 range," and
>   the fee is negotiable [C, C4-S017, 2016]. The 2005 figure was $5–6 [C, C4-S018].
> - **Big FMCs already serve small fleets.** Enterprise Fleet Management sells a maintenance program backed by a
>   55,325-shop network with ASE-technician bill review. Its fleet-size selector starts at 0–10 vehicles [B, C4-S019].
>   Merchants Fleet serves "a few trucks to 20,000 vans" [D, C4-S020].
> - **Software covers the self-managers.** Fleetio prices at about $5–10 per vehicle per month and bought the Auto
>   Integrate approval network [D, C4-S040].
>
> A new entrant has neither national-account parts and labor discounts nor a network. To earn meaningful profit it
> would have to charge 2–3x the incumbent fee. Year-3 operating profit is about −$10k to $100k.

## Industry structure
- **Incumbent scale.**
  - Ryder's Fleet Management Solutions: $5.85B revenue, 8.6% EBT. "SelectCare and other," maintenance on vehicles
    customers own, brought in $680M in 2025 [A, WS17-S022].
  - Enterprise claims $65M of client maintenance savings in 2022 [B/D, C4-S019].
- **Chicago fleet base.** Fleets under 10,001 lb (most service vans) are not in FMCSA data [A, WS17-S016]. A count of
  15–300-vehicle service fleets in Chicago is MISSING.
- **Heavier carriers (A, C4-S002).** 3,477 carriers run 6–20 power units (PU) and 1,811 run 21–100 PU. Construction
  cargo is flagged by 3,325 carriers [A, WS17-S001].
- **Fragmentation.** Concentrated at the top (Enterprise, Element, Holman, Wheels, Merchants, Ryder). Local shops do
  the work.
- **Growth/cyclicality.** Maintenance is non-discretionary, but fleets defer it in downturns (E).

## Value chain & mechanism
The fleet owner pays repair shops either directly or through the FMC's consolidated billing. The FMC earns three ways:
1. a small per-vehicle fee;
2. national-account discounts, sometimes as a spread;
3. float on consolidated billing.

Bill review by technicians against labor-time guides is the core service. For a new entrant to add value it must:
- beat the fleet's own local negotiation;
- beat FMC network pricing.

**Incumbent economics.** Low-margin and scale-driven; Ryder's FMS earns 8.6% EBT.

**New-entrant economics.** Value capture is limited to an admin fee or savings share, with no discount network.

## Competition
- **FMCs:** Enterprise, Element, Holman, Wheels, Merchants, Ryder.
- **Telematics and maintenance software:** Fleetio/Auto Integrate.
- **Dealers' fleet programs.**
- **Mobile maintenance providers** that bundle the records: Fleet Services by Cox Automotive (1,500+ techs), Coltrain
  [C, C4-S021].

**The real competitor is self-management.** The owner calls a trusted shop.

**Incumbent weaknesses:** lease-tied sales motions and call-center service (E).

**Switching costs:** low.

## Customer purchasing behavior
- **Who decides:** owner or COO.
- **Fleet-manager role:** small fleets often lack one, but the office manager handles shop relationships.
- **Purchase type:** usually bought together with leasing (Enterprise and Merchants sell maintenance alongside the
  lease).
- **Sales cycle and contracts:** ROI-driven sale, 1–3 months; contracts often annual (E).
- **Vendor discovery:** FMC reps and telematics resellers.

## Failure evidence
**Stage A failure modes confirmed by pricing:**
- inability to prove savings;
- becoming a bill-pay service with credit risk.

**Fee compression:** the fee has stayed at about $5 for two decades [C4-S017, S018].

**Disconfirming for any savings story:** the 2005 source itself notes local direct negotiation often already saves
money [C4-S018].

## Regulation
- **Licence:** none identified (absence not verified).
- **Contracts:** disclose any vendor rebates.
- **Consolidated billing:** creates credit exposure; no licensing triggered.
- **Heavier units:** DOT inspection records for units ≥10,001 lb (49 CFR 396.17) [A, WS17-S002].
- **Illinois testing:** Illinois runs its own periodic-test (OTS) regime (WS17 summary).

## Entry wedge test
**The pitch:**
- a "guaranteed-savings audit";
- transparent pricing;
- local vendor vetting;
- telematics-triggered preventive maintenance (PM).

**Problems with the wedge:**
- Software automates the triggers.
- Enterprise offers ASE review at scale.
- A local entrant's achievable discounts are MISSING and likely below the national accounts' discounts.

**Copyable:** easily.

**Verdict on the wedge:** no wedge.

## Economics sanity check (year 3; E unless cited)
Pricing assumes an equivalent fee of $15 per vehicle per month (a $10 fee plus a ~3% admin fee on about $2,000 a year
of repair spend). That is already 3x the incumbent fee.

**Low case**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue: 600 vehicles × $15 × 12 | $108,000 |
| Coordinator, service-advisor profile (loaded; BLS auto-tech median $55,320 ×1.25) | $69,000 |
| Software | $10,000 |
| Insurance | $5,000 |
| Marketing | $15,000 |
| Overhead | $12,000 |
| **Total costs** | **$111,000** |
| **Operating profit** | **≈ −$3k** |

**High case**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue: 2,000 vehicles × $18 × 12 | $432,000 |
| 3 coordinators | $207,000 |
| Software | $25,000 |
| Insurance | $8,000 |
| Marketing | $35,000 |
| Overhead | $25,000 |
| **Total costs** | **$300,000** |
| **Operating profit** | **≈ $132k** |

**What the high case needs:** about 50 fleets of 40 vehicles each, paying 3.6x the incumbent fee. That is implausible.

**More realistic fee:** at $8 per vehicle per month, 2,000 vehicles bring $192k, which roughly breaks even.

**Stated range:** −$10k to $100k.

**Capital:** $5–25k, rising if the business does consolidated billing (receivables risk).
