# WS17-023 — Forklift planned-maintenance & repair route (multi-brand) with operator training (Stage C, batch C3)

> **Verdict: HOLD. Revised evidence level: D.**
>
> **What supports it:**
> - **A real price umbrella exists.** A parts-seller survey puts Chicago forklift labor at about $219/hour against a $180 national average, with dealers adding a travel fee of about one labor hour [D, C3-S032].
> - **Dealer service is highly profitable at the gross level.** At Alta, a lift-truck dealer with Illinois locations, service runs a 61.5% gross margin and parts about 33% [B, C3-S031].
> - **Wages are well below billing rates.** Chicago heavy-equipment mechanics earn a median $74k [C, C3-S011].
> - **No license is needed.** OSHA requires only that repairs be done by "authorized personnel" [A, C3-S034].
>
> **What argues against it:**
> - **Dealer gross margins do not translate into profit.** Alta's consolidated operating margin is about 1.7% and it posts net losses [B, C3-S031].
> - **The OSHA mandate is narrow.** It requires daily operator checks, not outsourced PM.
> - **Market share data is missing:** how many fleets are locked into dealer full-maintenance leases is unknown.
> - **The founder cannot do the work** and would depend on 2–4 scarce mechanics.
>
> **Bottom line:** year-3 profit is roughly −$10k to $250k, with a base around $100k.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Classification:** forklift service falls under NAICS 811310, which nationally has 20,481 firms. Illinois has 873 establishments, mostly with fewer than 5 employees [C, WS03-S008; C3-S014].
- **Related Illinois counts** [C, C3-S014]:
  - 404 machinery-rental establishments (532490)
  - 976 warehousing establishments (4931), 430 of them with 20+ employees
  - Most of the 12,465 manufacturing plants also run forklifts
- **Fleet size:** the Chicago forklift fleet count is MISSING.
- **Cyclicality:** demand tracks warehouse activity. Service is steadier than equipment sales. Alta's Q3-26 service revenue fell 2.6% year over year while equipment sales fell 3.4% [B, C3-S031].

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- **OEMs sell trucks through franchised dealers.** Dealers bundle service through:
  - planned-maintenance (PM) programs
  - full-maintenance leases
  - parts sales
- **Who an independent serves:** mixed-brand or owned fleets, paid per PM visit or hour, plus parts (OEM or aftermarket) and tires.
- **Where margin sits:** labor (direct labor costs far less than bill rates) and parts markup.
- **Operator training:** an add-on with low revenue. Evaluations are due every 3 years and may be given by persons with the "knowledge, training, and experience" [A, C3-S034].

**Incumbent economics** combine high gross margin with heavy SG&A, rental-fleet depreciation and floor-plan interest.

**New-entrant economics** have no rental fleet but must undercut dealers. Assume $125–$140/hour (E), well below the $219 survey figure, to win accounts.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- **No documented independent forklift-service start with numbers was found** (MISSING).
- **Weak sign that small independents exist:** a 2011 forum post by a Chicago-area independent technician cited $82/hour plus one hour of travel (D, from search results). It is old and anecdotal.

## 5. Competition
- **OEM and franchised dealers:** Alta (Illinois), Equipment Depot (Illinois forklift-service page), Crown (company branches, hiring technicians in Rockford), Toyota dealers. These come from search results [D] and are not exhaustive.
- **Other competitors:** independent shops and rental companies.
- **Dealer weaknesses:** high rates and travel fees [D, C3-S032] and slow response (unverified).
- **Dealer strengths:** OEM parts and diagnostics, warranty work, and lease-bundled maintenance.
- **Switching costs:**
  - High for fleets under full-maintenance leases (share MISSING).
  - Low for owned trucks out of warranty.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Who decides:** the operations or maintenance manager.
- **Frequency:** PM every 200–250 hours (E, OEM practice), plus breakdowns.
- **Spend:** about $1–1.4k per truck per year for PM [D, C3-S033] plus repairs.
- **How fleets source service:** often whoever sold or leased the truck.
- **Common pain point:** downtime.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Technician scarcity:** Alta names "skilled technicians" as a risk [B, C3-S031].
- **Parts access** for multi-brand fleets.
- **Comebacks and warranty disputes.**
- **Dealer price response on anchor accounts.**
- **No bankruptcies or enforcement actions** were found specific to independents (MISSING).

## 8. Regulation
- **Federal:**
  - **29 CFR 1910.178:** trucks must be examined daily; repairs by authorized personnel; operator evaluation at least every 3 years. No license for maintenance [A, C3-S034; WS17-S014].
  - **Waste fluids:** used-oil and battery-handling rules (IEPA/RCRA) apply. Specific Illinois requirements are MISSING.
- **Illinois:** registration plus IDOR for parts sales [A, WS16-S036], and workers' compensation (E).
- **Founder eligibility:** feasible. The founder could also become an operator-training instructor.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The pitch:** multi-brand service at $125–140/hour with no travel fee, guaranteed 24-hour response, and digital PM and inspection records.
- **Cost:** service vans, diagnostic tools and parts accounts.
- **Copyability:** the wedge is copyable, and dealers can selectively match prices.
- **Possible moat:** route density in one corridor (e.g., I-55 / Bolingbrook–Joliet).
