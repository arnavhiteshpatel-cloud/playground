# WS18-002 — Non-medical in-home senior care agency (IDPH Home Services licence) · Stage C

Merged duplicate read: WS04-001 (independent private-pay agency, Chicago suburbs).

> **Verdict: ADVANCE to Stage D** · revised evidence level **C**. Revenue and gross profit are documented at level B (FDD Item 19). Overhead and operating margin are still E.
>
> **Why.** Licensing turned out to be lighter than Stage A assumed. The IDPH rules set no education, experience or licence requirement for a home services agency manager. Entry costs are a $1,500 fee, liability insurance and 10 hours of worker training. The G2 WARN can therefore be cleared. Three things argue against it:
> - The economics are worse at year 3 than Stage A claimed. An independent, private-pay-only agency is near break-even in year 3. The profit shows up in years 4–5, when revenue reaches $1–1.9M.
> - The market is crowded: 854 licensed agencies in the 7-county Chicago metro.
> - Caregiver turnover runs at about 75%.
>
> It advances because it still has the best-documented demand, delegation and ceiling in this batch. Stage D must model it as private pay only and compare it against the franchise route.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Illinois size and growth.** NAICS 624120 (services for the elderly and disabled) had 1,137 establishments, 88,337 employees and a $2.126B payroll in 2023 [C, C6-S003]. That is up from 850 establishments and 61,083 employees in 2019 (+34% establishments) [C, C6-S004]. The NAICS code also includes Medicaid-funded agencies.
- **Licensed agencies.** The IDPH directory lists **1,244 licensed home services agencies**: 666 in Cook, 166 in DuPage, and **854 across the 7 metro counties** [A, C6-S020]. About 135 carry major franchise names, such as Visiting Angels (18), Home Instead (17) and BrightStar (12). The rest (≈89%) are independents or small brands, so the industry is extremely fragmented.
- **Demand.** Aide jobs are projected to grow 18% over 2025–35 [C, WS18-S021]. Cyclicality is low because demand is driven by need. Private-pay volume is somewhat sensitive to wealth effects (MISSING: no recession data).

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- Families (private pay), long-term-care insurers (reimbursement in arrears), the VA, and Illinois CCP (Medicaid waiver) pay an hourly rate.
- The agency recruits, trains, schedules and supervises W-2 caregivers. Its margin is the spread between the bill rate and the loaded wage, less office overhead.
- **Medicaid vs private pay.** CCP pays **$30.80/hr** (from 1 Jan 2026) [A, WS18-S020]. Against an Illinois personal-care-aide median wage of **$18.30/hr** (Chicago $18.36; 90th percentile $20.43) [C, C6-S024], loaded at ~1.2× (≈$22/hr), CCP leaves a gross margin of about 26–28% (E). That is consistent with Addus's "low 20%" incremental margin on Illinois hours and its 54.7-day Illinois DSO [B, WS04-S004]. Private pay at an assumed $36–40/hr (E) yields 33–42% after fully loaded labor. Medicaid also carries policy risk: OBBBA work requirements and the 80/20 rule [C, WS04-S007, WS04-S009].
- **What the new entrant does.** Sell to families and referral sources, hire caregivers and keep them, and fill shifts reliably.

- **New-entrant economics:** see §11. Year 3 is near break-even and years 4–5 reach roughly 10–15%.

## 4. Small-start evidence
- Both Illinois A Place At Home outlets started from zero in 2021–22 and passed $1.6M in sales within 3.4–4.4 years [B, C6-S022]. These are franchise-supported.
- The independent count (~1,100 licensed non-franchise agencies) shows that many small operators survive [A, C6-S020]. No named independent with disclosed growth numbers was found (MISSING).

## 5. Competition
- **National franchises:** Home Instead, Visiting Angels, BrightStar, Assisting Hands, Senior Helpers and Synergy each have 10–18 Illinois licences.
- **Medicaid specialists:** Addus and others.
- **Others:** about 1,100 independents, plus paid lead aggregators.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Missed shifts and caregiver churn (turnover median ≈75%) [C, C6-S028].
  - Minimum shift lengths.
  - Slow start-of-care.
- **Switching costs:** low for families and low for caregivers.

## 6. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Who decides:** adult children aged 45–70, usually in a crisis such as a fall, hospital discharge or progressing dementia.
- **How they find agencies:** Google search, discharge planners, senior-living staff, and elder-law or financial advisors.
- **Spend:** about $2,000–5,500/month (15–40 hrs/week, E).
- **Contracts and billing:** no contracts (at-will service agreements). Private pay is billed weekly by card or ACH. LTC insurance pays in arrears.
- **Purchase cycle:** days. Referral-source relationships take 6–18 months to build (Stage A, E).

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Licence attrition.** IDPH licence numbers run sequentially from 3000103 to 3003353 (~3,250 issued); only 1,244 are active. Survival falls with licence age: about 18% for the oldest number block versus about 76% for the newest [A data, D inference, C6-S020]. Changes of ownership can re-number licences, so this is only a proxy.
- **Weak franchise outlets.** A Place At Home has operational outlets at $5k, $50k, $159k and $229k in sales [B, C6-S022].
- **Common failure modes:** being unable to staff the hours sold, owner burnout from on-call duty, and wage inflation that cannot be passed through. The documented turnover rate is 75% [C, C6-S028].

## 8. Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **Licence.** IDPH Home Services Agency licence under 210 ILCS 55 and 77 IAC 245. The fee is **$1,500/yr, non-refundable** [A, C6-S018, C6-S021]. IDPH aims to decide on a provisional licence within about 90 days. The provisional licence lasts 240 days, with an on-site survey before it expires [A, WS04-S027].
- **Agency manager.** The agency must "designate a person to supervise" services. **No credential or experience is required** [A, C6-S019, C6-S017]. Attachment E asks only for experience and criminal-history disclosure [A, C6-S021]. The founder can be the manager.
- **Insurance.** Evidence of liability insurance is mandatory [A, C6-S019].
- **Worker training.** 10 hours initially (5 before the first assignment) plus a competency test, then 10 hours a year. The supervisor or trainer needs 2 years of relevant experience, so hire one. Supervision must happen on site every 90 days and cannot be billed [A, C6-S015, C6-S016].
- **Background checks.** Health Care Worker Background Check Act fingerprinting applies [A, WS18-S013].
- **Not verified:** Chicago and Cook County minimum wages (chicago.gov returned 403). CCP provider contracting is a separate IDoA process (MISSING).
- **Federal:** FLSA home-care overtime rules (not re-verified).

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **The wedge:**
  - Guaranteed start within 24–48 hours.
  - A language or culture niche (Polish, Spanish, Korean or South Asian elders).
  - Dementia specialization.
  - Pay at or above the 75th–90th percentile (about $20–21/hr) with guaranteed hours to cut turnover.
- **Delivery:** founder-led referral selling in 2–3 suburbs.
- **Cost:** about $1–2/hr of extra wage, roughly 3–5 points of gross margin (E).
- **Copyability:** fully copyable. What accumulates is the caregiver bench and the referral relationships.

## 10. Economics sanity check — year 3 (months 25–36), independent, private pay only
Driver assumptions:
- Bill rate $38/hr (E).
- Wage $19.50 (E, above the Illinois median [C6-S024]).
- Loaded labor $24.30/hr: ×1.20 for FICA, unemployment, workers' comp and paid leave, plus 4% non-billable time (E).
- Gross profit $13.70/hr (36%).

Year-3 cases:
| Case | Hours | Revenue | Gross profit | Overhead | **Operating profit** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 8,500 | $306k @ $36/hr | $101k (33%) | $175k | **−$74k** |
| Base | 15,300 (≈16 clients × 18.4 hr/wk) | $581k | $210k | $225k | **−$15k** |
| Optimistic | 24,000 | $960k @ $40/hr | $384k (40%) | $280k | **+$104k** |

Base overhead of $225k (excluding founder salary): coordinator/supervisor $58k, recruiter/scheduler $47k, marketing $30k, rent $18k, insurance $15k, recruiting/background checks $15k, accounting/legal $10k, misc $10k, software/EVV $9k, mileage/phones $8k, training $5k.

Year-5 base: 35,000 hrs × $39 = $1.37M; × 38% = $519k; less $360k overhead (now including an $85k GM) ≈ **$160k**. Optimistic ≈ $300k. Revenue is benchmarked to A Place At Home's 37–48-month cohort at $1.52M [B, C6-S022].

**Capital at risk:**
- Before first revenue: $25–40k.
- Year 1–2 ramp losses: $40–90k (the founder doing scheduling).
- Payroll float: $10–20k.
- Total: **≈$70–150k**. The high case exceeds the $100k conditional backing. G1 passes, because less than $100k is needed before first revenue.
