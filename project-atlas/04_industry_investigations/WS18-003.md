# WS18-003 — Junk removal and hauling (independent, Chicago metro)

> **Verdict: HOLD** · revised evidence level **B** (blind scorer had C) · 2026-11-28
>
> This is now the best-evidenced owner economics in the batch, and the evidence is sobering.
> - **College Hunks (2026 FDD, CY2025).** Franchisees 13–36 months old had **median sales $691k and median EBITDA $44k (6.4%)**. EBITDA excludes truck payments and owner pay. At 60+ months (about 3 territories) the medians are **$1.56M sales and $137k EBITDA (8.8%)** [B, C8-S017].
> - **Junk King** franchisees keep 56.3% gross profit after 13% fees [B, WS18-S001].
>
> An independent avoids the ~9–13% franchise and call-centre fees but must buy its own leads, and franchisees already spend ~12% of sales on local marketing [B, C8-S018]. Cash conversion and delegation are excellent. Defensibility is weak (three national brands plus independents) and the ceiling depends on truck count. Rebuilt year-3 operating profit is **$20k–$170k, base ~$65k**. It is a solid, safe small business, not a holding-company engine.

## 2. Industry structure
- **No clean Census code.** Junk removal sits partly in NAICS 562111 (solid-waste collection: 6,993 U.S. firms; Illinois 208 firms, $3.1B, dominated by large haulers) [C, C8-S005]. That code mixes municipal and commercial trash, so a junk-removal-specific firm count is MISSING.
- **Franchise systems:**
  - College Hunks: 144 franchised stores reporting, plus 5 corporate [B, C8-S017].
  - Junk King: 166 continuously operating U.S. units in 2024 [B, WS18-S001].
  - 1-800-GOT-JUNK: Item 19 not retrieved. A third-party summary cites a 2023 average of $1.68M per franchisee business (D, unverified).
- **Demand drivers.** Moves, estates, renovations and evictions. Mildly cyclical with housing turnover. Winter is slower in Chicago (E).

## 3. Value chain & mechanism
The customer (household, realtor, property manager, contractor) pays per volume fraction of a truck at completion. The operator pays the crew, transfer-station tipping fees, fuel and truck costs. It earns extra margin by diverting items to donation or recycling, which lowers tipping fees. The new entrant would run 2-person truck crews, take bookings online or by phone, and quote from photos.

## 5. Small-start evidence
- **College Hunks** was itself founder-started as a student hauling business and is now a 150-store system. This is general knowledge, not re-verified this session (D).
- **Franchise tenure data** show units growing from ~$0.7M (years 1–3) to ~$2.0M average (5+ years, multi-territory) [B, C8-S017]. That is the documented ramp.
- **Independent Chicago small-start case:** MISSING.

## 6. Competition
- **National brands:** 1-800-GOT-JUNK, College Hunks, Junk King, JunkLuggers. All compete in Chicagoland (presence assumed; not verified per territory).
- **Independents and marketplaces:** independents and marketplace haulers (Thumbtack/Yelp/LSA). Dumpster-rental firms substitute for DIY cleanouts.
- **Brand weaknesses:** price opacity (volume-based quotes) and franchise call-centre friction (E).
- **Switching costs:** none for consumers. Realtor and PM accounts are sticky only through responsiveness.

## 7. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Consumers.** Search at the moment of need and book same day to one week. Infrequent; repeat business is low. Tickets around $400, paid at completion [B, WS18-S001].
- **B2B (realtors, PMs, estate attorneys, senior move managers).** Generate repeat jobs and referrals. Usually no contract (E).

## 8. Failure evidence
- **Unit-level dispersion.** College Hunks shows wide dispersion: the lowest franchised EBITDA in each tenure band is −$65k to −$205k [B, C8-S017]. Junk King's bottom-quartile units average $194k sales, below a two-truck break-even [B, WS18-S001].
- **Failure modes:**
  - tipping-fee inflation and underpriced loads
  - truck downtime
  - lifting injuries driving up WC premiums
  - lead-cost inflation on Google LSA

## 9. Regulation (Illinois + federal)
- **No Illinois junk-removal licence identified.**
- **ICC Household Goods licence.** Required to transport household property for hire within Illinois: tariff filing, insurance on file, annual report [A, WS18-S014]. Applicability to disposal-only hauling is unclear, so legal review is needed.
- **Municipal hauler licences.** Chicago private scavenger/waste-hauler licensing is MISSING (chicago.gov blocked); suburbs vary.
- **Special wastes.** Appliances with refrigerant (EPA Section 608 recovery), electronics (Illinois landfill ban, not re-verified), tires and paint all need specific disposal routes.
- **Insurance.** Commercial auto, GL, WC.
- **Home Repair Act.** Not applicable; hauling is not home repair [A, WS18-S010].

## 10. Entry wedge test
- **Proposed wedge:** same-day service, a binding photo quote, a documented donation/recycling rate, and B2B accounts (property managers, realtors, estate attorneys).
- **Cost:** low.
- **Copyable:** yes. All three brands already advertise same-day service and eco-diversion.
- **Durable advantages:** an owned transfer or recycling yard (capital), or a dense B2B referral base. Neither is unique.
