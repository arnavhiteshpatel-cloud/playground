# WS18-004 — Lawn fertilization & weed-control subscription route (IDOA-licensed)

> **Verdict: HOLD.** Revised evidence level: **C**. Revenue at mature units is B-level; there is no cost data, and new-entrant numbers are E.
>
> **What supports it:**
> - Weed Man's Item 19 shows that mature franchise units are large: average $2.04M, median $1.69M [B].
> - Licensing is exam-based and cheap: $300 for a 3-year applicator licence, with online-proctored exams [A].
>
> **What counts against it:**
> - The same Item 19 shows a bottom-quartile average of $347k and a low of $96k.
> - Mature-unit tenure is not disclosed.
> - Illinois has 4,949 landscaping firms competing for the same lawns [C].
> - An IDOA lawncare containment permit is required for any area where spray rigs are loaded or rinsed [A]. That is a facility cost Stage A missed.
> - The Chicago season runs about 8 months, so labour is seasonal and the founder has a winter gap.
>
> Year-3 operating profit: **about $20k–$150k, base about $95k.** It is less durable than pest control: the spend is discretionary curb appeal, and churn is MISSING.

## 1. Industry structure
- **Market size and fragmentation.** NAICS 561730 (landscaping, which includes lawn-care application) has 114,842 US firms and $118B in receipts. The category is fragmented: the 82 firms with $100M+ hold about 15% of receipts [C, C2-S012].
- **Illinois.** 4,949 firms, of which 4,094 have fewer than 5 employees [C, C2-S013].
- **Lawn application alone.** There is no Census split for lawn-application-only firms (MISSING).
- **Major players.** TruGreen is private (no financials retrieved). Franchises include Weed Man, Lawn Doctor and Spring-Green, an Illinois-based franchisor whose Item 19 was not retrieved.
- **Demand drivers.** Demand is seasonal (roughly April–November in Chicago) and mildly cyclical, since homeowners treat it as discretionary.

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
- **Programme.** The homeowner buys an annual programme of 5–7 applications (fertilizer, pre-emergent, broadleaf, grub control) and often renews automatically.
- **Inputs.** The provider buys product from turf distributors.
- **Where margin comes from:** density (lawns per hour), retention and upsells (aeration, overseeding, mosquito, tree and shrub care).
- **The entrant's role.** The founder is the licensed applicator. He sells in spring through direct mail and door hangers, using quotes measured from aerial imagery.

## 3. Operator economics
- **Weed Man Item 19, 2026 FDD (CY2025)** [B, WS18-S007]:
  - 149 US units. Average $2,042,514; median $1,693,230; quartile averages $4.19M, $2.35M, $1.17M and $347k.
  - Revenue only; costs, tenure and margins are not disclosed.
- **Wages.** Chicago-area median wages are $49,550 for pesticide applicators (vegetation) and $45,280 for landscaping workers [C, C2-S022].
- **Incumbent margins** (TruGreen, Spring-Green): **MISSING**.
- **Product cost.** Assumed at 15–20% of revenue (E).

## 4. Small-start evidence
Weed Man's spread is the best evidence available. Its bottom quartile ($347k) and low unit ($96k) show that some units remain small even inside a strong system [B, WS18-S007]. No documented independent Chicago-area start-up history was found (MISSING).

## 5. Competition
- TruGreen (national).
- Franchises: Weed Man, Lawn Doctor and Spring-Green.
- Full-service landscapers who bundle mowing with fertilization.
- DIY retail.

**Weaknesses.** Complaints about large players and auto-renewal practices are commonly alleged, but were not collected this session (D, MISSING).

**Switching costs** are low: customers switch each spring.

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Who decides:** suburban homeowners, who decide in the February–May selling window.
- **Price:** about $50–$90 per application, or $350–600 a year, by lot size (E, Stage A; not verified).
- **Payment:** prepay discounts are common (D).
- **How buyers find providers:** direct mail, door hangers, neighbours, Google.

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Rinsate and spill liability.** The containment rule exists because loading and wash sites contaminate soil and groundwater. Non-compliance carries risk [A, C2-S027].
- **Drift and misapplication claims.** Plausible (E), but no enforcement data was retrieved (MISSING).
- **Weather.** Drought or late springs compress the application window (E).
- **Thin small units.** Weed Man's bottom quartile shows small units can stall [B].
- **Churn.** MISSING.

## 9. Entry wedge test
- **Wedge:** hyper-dense subdivision targeting, instant aerial quotes and a results guarantee. It could be combined with perimeter pest control under a separate IDPH licence, which the same founder could hold.
- **Copyability:** fully copyable. Aerial quoting is now standard among competitors, so the AI and quoting edge is small.
- **What does accumulate:** subdivision density.

## 10. Economics sanity check (year 3, before founder salary; E)
**Base case:** 650 customers × $380 + $40k upsells = **$287k revenue**.

| Cost (base case) | Amount |
|---|---|
| One seasonal applicator: $49,550 × 8/12 × 1.2 | $40k |
| Product (18%) | $52k |
| Trucks and equipment ×2 | $24k |
| Acquisition: 300 gross adds × $90 | $27k |
| Shop plus permitted containment | $14k |
| Insurance | $6k |
| Software and aerial imagery | $6k |
| Spring office help | $12k |
| Miscellaneous | $10k |
| **Total costs** | **$190k** |

**Base-case operating profit: ~$97k (34%).** The founder still does about half the applications.

**Low and high cases:**
- **Low:** 300 customers × $350 + $10k gives $115k revenue; operating profit **about $25k**.
- **High:** 1,100 customers × $400 + $90k gives $530k revenue, with 2.5 seasonal applicators; operating profit **about $160k**.

**Capital and cash:**
- Launch capital about $25–70k: trucks, spray rig, containment area, spring product inventory and marketing before the season.
- Product and marketing are paid in spring, before most revenue arrives.
