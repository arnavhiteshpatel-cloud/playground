# WS18-007 — Residential pest control quarterly service plans (IDPH-licensed)

> **Verdict: HOLD. Merge into WS05-007 as the residential and small-landlord line of one licensed pest company; do not advance it standalone.** Revised evidence level: **D**. Incumbent and regulatory facts are A/B, but residential pricing is D and acquisition cost is E.
> - **Licensing is strong.** It is the same IDPH path as commercial work, with exams held monthly near Chicago.
> - **The residential market is the largest pest line.** It is also the most marketing-intensive and has the highest churn:
>   - Rollins residential revenue is $1.69B, but organic growth is only 5.0% [A].
>   - Rentokil is spending on brands, lead flow and small local branches (70 opened in H1 2026, about 220 in total) to defend residential share [B].
>   - Roto-Rooter's paid-lead costs rose 9.4% while organic leads fell [D]. That is the same dynamic a residential pest entrant faces.
>
> Year-3 operating profit: **about $30k–$160k, base about $110k.** That is lower-quality profit than commercial because it depends on continuous lead spend.

## 1. Industry structure
- **US industry.** NAICS 561710: 13,603 firms and $19.6B in receipts. Two-thirds of firms take in under $500k [C, C2-S012].
- **Illinois.** 330 employer firms, $527M in receipts [C, C2-S013], and about 750 licensed locations [A, WS05-S011].
- **Residential is the largest line.** Rollins breakdown [A, C2-S008]:

| Rollins service line | Revenue | Organic growth |
|---|---|---|
| Residential | $1,693M | 5.0% |
| Commercial | $1,245M | 7.6% |
| Termite and ancillary | $782M | — |

- **Seasonality.** Rollins revenue runs $822M in Q1 against $1,026M in Q3 [A, C2-S008]. Rentokil's Q2/Q3 revenue peaks follow the same pattern [B, C2-S010].
- **Chicago.** Older housing stock and rodent pressure support winter mouse work. Not quantified (MISSING).

## 2. Value chain and mechanism
The homeowner or small landlord pays an initial fee, then quarterly, bimonthly or monthly autopay.

Margin depends on four things:
- route density;
- autopay retention;
- upsells such as mouse exclusion, wasps and bed bugs;
- **acquisition cost**, which is the decisive variable.

The entrant is a certified-technician founder who builds a residential book through Google and Local Services Ads, door-to-door canvassing, landlords and realtors.

## 3. Operator economics
- **Rollins** (incumbent economics): 52.8% gross margin, 19.3% operating margin, about $171k revenue per employee [A, C2-S008].
- **Rentokil NA Pest Control Services:**
  - 19.7% adjusted operating margin in H1 2026 [B, C2-S009].
  - 80.7% customer retention, with residential retention improving thanks to autopay and a "customer saves team."
  - Residential lead flow up 6% after brand investment; regional-brand lead flow up double digits.
- **Lloyd Pest (California operator):** publishes single-family monthly plans at $20–$67 per month [D, C2-S019], roughly $240–$800 a year, which brackets Stage A's $450–650 assumption.
- **Small Illinois firms:** about $186k average receipts for firms with fewer than 5 employees; about $673k for firms with 5–9 [C, C2-S013].
- **Not found:** Aptive and other door-to-door operators' economics, and franchise Item 19 data. **MISSING.**

## 4. Small-start evidence
No primary source was found documenting a residential start-up's growth path.
- The door-to-door model (Aptive and others) is widely reported to have scaled fast. That could not be verified this session. **MISSING.**
- Proforce Pest Control is reported at #56 on PCT's Top 100 with more than $23M in revenue. This comes from a press release seen only in search results [D, C2-S035]. Its founding scale is unknown.

## 5. Competition
- **National players:** Orkin and Terminix, both investing in local brands [B, C2-S009].
- **Other competitors:** door-to-door sellers and many small Illinois firms.
- **Switching costs:** low. Plans are often month to month (E).
- **Rentokil's churn reduction** relies on autopay and save teams, both available to an entrant.
- **Customer complaints:** not collected. Review data was not gathered (D, MISSING).

## 6. Customer purchasing behaviour
- **Who buys:** the homeowner, or the owner of a 2–6-flat building.
- **Triggers:** urgent infestations such as mice and roaches (same-day decision), or a preventive plan (days).
- **How they find a provider:** search, referrals and door-to-door.
- **Spend:** about $240–$800 a year (D, C2-S019).
- **Landlord accounts:** more stable, but price-sensitive. Rockford public bids for small buildings come in at $30–$100 a month [A, C2-S017].

## 7. Failure evidence
- **Churn.** Even Rentokil keeps only 80.7% of customers a year [B, C2-S009].
- **Lead-cost inflation in urgent home services** is documented at Roto-Rooter. Its adjusted EBITDA margin fell 477 basis points in Q4 2025 [B, C2-S028], with paid leads up 9.4% [D, C2-S029].
- **Termite warranty tails.** Rentokil carries $392M of termite provisions [B, C2-S009]. Avoid termite warranties early.
- **Labour churn.** Rentokil colleague retention is 82.7% [B].

## 8. Regulation (primary sources)
Licensing:
- **Business licence:** IDPH Commercial Structural Pest Control Business License, $250 per location per year. Requires at least one employed Certified Technician and a certificate of insurance [A, C2-S001, WS05-S012].
- **Certified Technician:** General Standards exam, 70% to pass, $75 fee per the 2017 form. No experience is required, and the application is due 15 days before the exam [A, WS05-S013, C2-S002].
- **Exam availability:** exams are offered monthly at Westchester, with 10 seats per session [A, C2-S005].
- **Unlicensed operation:** a $1,000 initial fine [A, C2-S006].

Insurance:
- At least $100k/$300k bodily injury and $50k property damage, or $350k combined single limit [A, C2-S004].

Staffing:
- **Supervision:** uncertified technicians may work after two supervised applications per pesticide type per site type. The supervisor must be reachable and able to reach the site within one hour [A, C2-S002].
- **Renewal:** the certified technician renews every 3 years with 9 classroom hours [A, C2-S007].

Other rules:
- **Restricted-use products:** require subcategory exams, and fumigation is separate [A, WS05-S011].
- **Home Repair Act:** whether it applies to exclusion work over $500 on residences is unclear. Treat it as applicable (written contract over $1,000; insurance) [A, WS18-S010].

**Feasibility for the founder: high.**

## 9. Entry wedge test
The plausible wedge is **rodent specialization for Chicago 2-flats and bungalows**: exclusion work with a guarantee, landlord portfolio pricing, and winter demand that complements commercial work.
- **Delivery cost:** founder time plus lead spend.
- **Copyability:** fully copyable. The nationals are explicitly moving local [B, C2-S009].
- **Strongest form:** this line shares a licence, a certified technician, vehicles and dispatch with WS05-007, which lowers its overhead.

## 10. Economics sanity check (year 3, before founder salary; E)
**Base case: revenue about $354k, costs about $244k, operating profit about $110k (31%).**

Revenue:
- 550 recurring accounts × $480 a year = $264k.
- One-time jobs: $90k.

Costs:

| Cost item | Annual cost |
|---|---|
| Technicians: 1.5 × $46,740 Chicago median [C, C2-S021] × 1.2 loading | $84k |
| Vehicles (2.5) | $30k |
| Materials (8% of revenue) | $28k |
| Acquisition: 250 gross adds × $175 CAC (E) | $44k |
| Part-time office | $25k |
| Insurance | $9k |
| Software | $6k |
| Storage | $8k |
| Miscellaneous | $10k |

Other scenarios:
- **Low:** 250 accounts plus $40k of jobs gives revenue of $153k and operating profit of **about $30k**.
- **High:** 900 accounts plus $150k of jobs gives revenue of $600k. With 3 technicians and about $70k of acquisition spend, operating profit is **about $157k**.

**Sensitivity.** Each $100 of extra CAC per gross add removes about $25k in the base case.

**Capital.** Launch costs about $15–45k. Unlike commercial work, CAC is paid upfront before revenue, which adds working capital.
