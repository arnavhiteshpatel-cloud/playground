> - **Five Star Painting 2025** (208 reporting units): **average $4,440 per job**; gross sales per capita averaged $2.12; bottom-quartile units did only **$0.35 per capita**; **11 franchised businesses closed during 2025** out of ~245 [B, C8-S021].

Within this batch it is dominated by WS18-035 (urgent need, higher ticket per tech-hour, paid on completion) and by WS10-010 (code-driven demand). Painting capability can live inside WS10-016 if that advances.

## 2. Industry structure
- **Illinois (2022).** 1,487 painting and wall-covering firms (1,490 establishments), $1.25B receipts, 6,178 employees. 1,183 firms have fewer than 5 employees and only 6 have 100–499 [C, C8-S005].
- **U.S.** 37,830 firms, 75% with fewer than 5 employees [C, C8-S005].
- **Incumbent margin.** The Census proxy operating margin for painting is **25%**, before owner draw and including many owner-operators whose own labor is unpaid [C, WS10-S022].
- **Cyclicality.** Residential repaints are discretionary and track home sales and remodeling budgets. They are hurt by the 2026 rate environment (30-yr 7.21% [C, WS10-S019]).

## 3. Value chain & mechanism
The homeowner pays a fixed bid (labor plus paint), usually a deposit and the balance on completion. The company buys paint on contractor accounts (Sherwin-Williams, Benjamin Moore dealers) and pays crews by the hour or the piece, or uses subcontractors (misclassification risk). The new entrant would be a sales-led owner: the founder estimates and sells while a production manager runs crews.

## 5. Small-start evidence
Student-painter models and franchise systems prove the trade can be entered with almost no capital. CertaPro began as a student painting business in Canada (general knowledge, not re-verified; D). Small starts are easy; growing past $1M is uncommon (Five Star bottom quartile).

## 6. Competition
- **Franchise brands:** CertaPro (FirstService), Five Star (Neighborly), Fresh Coat, Painter1 and others.
- **Locals:** thousands of local painters, many cash-paid and uninsured or not RRP-certified (E).
- **Weaknesses:** no-shows, slow estimates, inconsistent quality. Professionalism is the stated wedge of every franchise.
- **Switching costs:** none.

## 7. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Who and how:** homeowners solicit 2–4 bids and decide in 1–4 weeks.
- **Frequency:** repaint cycles of 5–10 years. Referrals and yard signs matter.
- **Realtors** buy listing-prep paint jobs, and small landlords buy turns.
- **Payment:** deposits subject to the 3-business-day cancellation right (15 days if the buyer is over 65) [A, WS18-S011].

## 8. Failure evidence
- **Closure rate.** Five Star: 11 closures in 2025 in a ~245-unit system (≈4.5%/yr), plus 12 transfers [B, C8-S021].
- **Failure modes:**
  - seasonal cash crunch (Chicago winters)
  - crew quality and callbacks
  - price competition from uninsured painters
  - RRP violations on pre-1978 homes [A, C8-S024]

## 9. Regulation
- **Home Repair and Remodeling Act.** Written contract and consumer-rights brochure for jobs of $1,000+; insurance minimums ($100k/$300k bodily injury, $50k property damage, $10k improper repair as enacted) [A, WS18-S010, WS18-S011].
- **EPA RRP.** Firm certification and a certified renovator whenever painting disturbs more than 6 sq ft per room interior (20 sq ft exterior) in pre-1978 homes. That is most Chicago-area housing, and it adds containment cost per job [A, C8-S024].
- **City of Chicago GC licence.** Applies to repair work in the city: Class E $300/yr [A, WS10-S004].
- **Suburbs.** Registrations vary (MISSING).

## 10. Entry wedge test
- **Proposed wedge:** fast photo-based estimates, a written warranty, and an RRP-compliant process. That is real against uninsured painters, but identical to every franchise's pitch.
- **Copyable:** yes.
- **What accumulates:** reviews and neighborhood yard-sign density, slowly.
