# WS18-030 — Senior move management & estate sale/cleanout service

> **Verdict: DROP as a standalone** (keep as an add-on idea for WS18-002 home care) · revised evidence level **D** (Stage A E) · 2026-11-28
>
> The niche's largest franchise system, **Caring Transitions**, published 2025 figures (2026 FDD, via a secondary summary). Across 223 full-year owners, median gross receipts were **$272,710** and the bottom quartile averaged **$86,313**. The top quartile averaged $964,873. Average "gross profit" was 65% (definition not given) [D, C8-S023]. The franchisor takes 6% royalty plus a 2% branding fee, and owners must spend about 4% on local marketing [D, C8-S023].
>
> The median owner therefore runs a business with well under $100k of operating profit, and much of the work is founder labor: valuation, sorting, family mediation and sale-day staffing. Negative working capital is real (the firm holds sale proceeds), but the ceiling is low. Deal flow depends on referrals from senior-living communities, elder-law attorneys and realtors, and scaling requires replicating trusted judgment.
>
> Rebuilt year-3 operating profit is **$30k–$140k, base ~$80k**.

## 2. Industry structure
- **Census.** No dedicated code. It sits in NAICS 812990 (all other personal services) and 484210 (used household goods moving: Illinois 389 firms, $1.31B receipts, a code dominated by movers [C, C8-S005]). A firm count specific to estate sales and move management is MISSING.
- **Caring Transitions:** 423 franchise territories owned by 296 owners at end-2025 [D, C8-S023]. Other operators are local estate-sale companies and online auction/consignment platforms.
- **Demand.** Demographic (aging owners downsizing, estates) and non-cyclical in need, though estate-sale proceeds track used-goods prices. Size of the Chicago-area market is MISSING.

## 3. Value chain & mechanism
The family or estate pays two ways:
- **Hourly or project fees** for move management: planning, sorting, packing coordination, set-up.
- **A commission withheld from estate-sale or online-auction gross,** typically assumed at 30–40% (E; no published Illinois rate sheet found).

Cleanout, donation and junk-removal fees are passed through or marked up. The new entrant would run in-home tag sales and online auctions and coordinate movers and junk haulers.

Inference (E): if the 65% gross profit is after direct labor, a median owner at $273k with ~12% fees and marketing and ~20% other overhead makes about $90k before owner pay. If labor is not in COGS, the figure is much lower. This is incumbent-system data, and a new independent saves the fees but has no brand.

## 5. Small-start evidence
The bottom quartile ($86k average receipts) and the lowest owner ($14,535) show that many start small and stay small [D, C8-S023]. No named case was found of an independent Chicago-area firm scaling past ~$1M (MISSING).

## 6. Competition
- **Rivals:** local estate-sale companies (dozens in Chicagoland, unverified count), online auction platforms (consignment), Caring Transitions franchisees, NASMM-member move managers (not retrieved), junk-removal brands offering estate cleanouts (WS18-003), and realtors' preferred vendors.
- **Customer complaints:** low sale proceeds, slow payouts, missing items (D, not gathered).
- **Switching costs:** none. Each job is one-time.

## 7. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-makers:** adult children or executors, often under a closing date or senior-living move-in date.
- **Frequency:** once per household.
- **How vendors are found:** referral by senior-living marketing directors, elder-law and probate attorneys, realtors, and home care agencies. Sales cycle 1–4 weeks.
- **Payment:** commission netted from sale proceeds, and fees invoiced at completion.

## 8. Failure evidence
- **Failure modes:**
  - deal flow that never stabilizes (the bottom-quartile dispersion above)
  - theft or valuation disputes and reputational damage
  - seasonality of tag sales
  - founder burnout, since the founder is the appraiser and the face of the business
- **Enforcement:** none retrieved (MISSING).

## 9. Regulation
- **ICC Household Goods licence.** Transporting household property for hire within Illinois requires an ICC Household Goods licence, a tariff filing, insurance on file and annual reports [A, WS18-S014]. Outsourcing moves to licensed movers avoids this.
- **Auctions.** Illinois auctioneer licensing (IDFPR, Auction License Act) is likely required to conduct auctions. Scope, exemptions for online platforms, fees and exams are MISSING; the statute was not reachable (ilga.gov 503).
- **Estate tag sales:** no licence identified.
- **Trust handling.** Client sale proceeds should be held in a separate account. Statutory trust rules are MISSING.
- **Insurance:** GL, bonding/crime coverage (prudent; not mandated per sources found).

## 10. Entry wedge test
- **Proposed wedge:** transparent itemized accounting with photo catalogs and online price discovery, plus a one-call bundle (move plan + sale + cleanout) for referral partners. A buyer's reason to switch is trust and speed before a closing date.
- **Cost:** low.
- **Copyable:** yes; franchisees already market the same bundle.
