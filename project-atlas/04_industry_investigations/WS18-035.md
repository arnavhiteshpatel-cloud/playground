# WS18-035 — Garage door repair & installation (independent, Chicago metro)

> **Verdict: ADVANCE (conditional)** · revised evidence level **D** (revenue and ticket now B; margins still E) · 2026-11-28
>
> New franchise data show unusually high revenue density for a two-skill trade. **Precision Garage Door Service** franchises in the smallest territory band (91k–230k single-family homes) averaged **$1.38M gross sales (median $858k)**. The next band averaged $3.78M. The system-wide average ticket was **$706 in 2024 and $1,213 in 2025** [B, C8-S019, C8-S020].
>
> Demand is urgent (broken springs, a car trapped inside) and customers pay on completion. Licensing is light (Home Repair Act formalities and insurance [A, WS18-S010]), and technicians can be trained in months. That gives the best combination of ticket, cash conversion and feasibility among the consumer services in this batch.
>
> Stage D has three things to resolve:
> 1. **Margin evidence.** No FDD in this trade disclosed costs.
> 2. **Lead costs.** Chicago paid-search and LSA costs, in a channel known for lead-generation fronts and upselling (D).
> 3. **Technician supply.** Whether techs can be hired and kept.
>
> Rebuilt year-3 operating profit is **$60k–$245k, base ~$130k**.

## 2. Industry structure
- **No clean Census code.** Garage door installers fall in NAICS 238290 "other building equipment contractors", which also includes elevators. Illinois has 312 such firms with $2.1B receipts [C, C8-S005]. A garage-door-only firm count is MISSING.
- **Precision network.** 147 U.S. units (all franchised) at end-2025, up from 132 a year earlier. In 2025, 15 units opened and 16 transferred [B, C8-S019]. The transfer rate (~11%) is high and could reflect exits or a resale market for units.
- **Demand base.** Chicago has large single-family suburbs plus a city with widespread alley garages (E). Replacement and repair demand follows the housing stock, not new construction. Mildly cyclical for installs; repairs are non-discretionary.

## 3. Value chain & mechanism
Homeowners pay the service company. The company buys springs, cables, rollers, openers and doors from door manufacturers and distributors. Distributor dealer terms are MISSING. Margin is captured in parts markup plus labor on short visits (a spring job takes about 1–2 hours, E). Installs are larger tickets with lower margin percentages. The new entrant would run 1–3 technician vans, with the founder doing sales, dispatch and marketing (possibly working as a technician in year 1).

**Skeptic's notes:**
- The per-job average jumped 72% in one year. That points to a definition change, price increases, or an upsell-heavy mix. The 2026 FDD also words its reporting period ambiguously ("January 1, 2024 through December 31, 2025" alongside "52 weeks") [B, C8-S019].
- Franchise revenue reflects a premium-priced brand. An honest flat-rate independent would plausibly average $450–650 per job (E).
- **Incumbent vs new entrant:** multi-truck incumbents with established reviews. A new entrant starts with 1–2 vans, no reviews, and an expensive search channel.

## 5. Small-start evidence
Precision's growth from 132 to 147 units in a year, mostly owner-operators starting with a van, is documented [B, C8-S019]. A named independent Chicago firm that grew from one van is MISSING. Large independents elsewhere are reported in trade press (not retrieved).

## 6. Competition
- **Franchises:** Precision Door and other franchise brands. Their Chicagoland presence is unverified.
- **Manufacturer dealer networks:** e.g., Overhead Door and Clopay dealers (not verified individually).
- **Independents and big-box:** many local independents; big-box install programs for new doors.
- **Lead-gen fronts:** paid-search lead generators posing as local firms. This is reported in Stage A as anecdotal (D) and not re-verified.
- **Customer complaints:** upselling of full spring/roller "tune-up" packages and surprise pricing (D, not gathered this session). This is exactly the trust gap an honest-pricing independent would target.
- **Switching costs:** none. Reviews and search ranking accumulate.

## 7. Customer purchasing behavior
- **Decision-maker:** the homeowner, often in an emergency (the door won't open).
- **How they buy:** search on mobile and call the first credible result the same day.
- **Repeat purchases:** occasional. Springs last years, openers about 10–15 years (E).
- **Realtor and builder channels** exist for inspections and new doors.
- **Payment:** at completion, with a deposit on installs. The Home Repair Act requires a written contract and the consumer-rights brochure on jobs of $1,000+ [A, WS18-S011].

## 8. Failure evidence
- **Precision:** 16 transfers in a year out of about 131 operating units [B, C8-S019]. Closures are not reported in the excerpt.
- **Failure modes:**
  - dependence on Google LSA/PPC with rising cost per lead
  - technician turnover (techs leave with skills and customers)
  - spring-tension injuries
  - consumer-fraud complaints if salespeople upsell
  - thin winter demand for installs

  No enforcement actions retrieved (MISSING).

## 9. Regulation
- **Home Repair and Remodeling Act** (garages included): written contract, consumer-rights brochure, and insurance minimums of $100k/$300k bodily injury, $50k property damage and $10k improper-repair coverage as enacted [A, WS18-S010, WS18-S011]. There is a 3-business-day cancellation right for contracts signed at home, 15 days if the buyer is over 65 [A, WS18-S011].
- **City of Chicago.** A GC licence applies to repair work even when permit-exempt (Class E $300/yr) [A, WS10-S004]. Whether garage door service needs it is unclear, so legal review is needed.
- **Opener wiring.** Low-voltage wiring in Chicago requires an electrical licence [A, WS10-S006]. Opener installs that involve new wiring need a licensed electrician; plug-in replacement is unclear.
- **Suburbs.** Municipal registration MISSING.
- **No state trade licence identified.**

## 10. Entry wedge test
- **Proposed wedge:** posted flat-rate prices, a photo/video diagnosis before arrival, a warranty, and no-upsell commitments. This exploits the reputation problem of premium-priced and lead-gen operators.
- **Cost:** reviews and search positioning take 12–24 months. LSA spend is the swing cost.
- **Copyable:** yes, but honest-pricing positioning plus review density compounds locally.
- **B2B adjacency:** property managers of townhome/HOA communities, home inspectors, realtors.
