# WS05-001 — Fire extinguisher + emergency/exit-lighting inspection route (with a narrow first-aid add-on test from WS13-004)

**Stage D deep dive · batch DA (Chicago commercial building services) · 2026-11-28**

> **Verdict: RESERVE.** Hold it as a phase-2 licence for the WS05-007 pest company rather than a standalone business. Revised evidence level: **D**. Licensing, the official Illinois prices and the licence roster are A; small-account pricing is D and emergency-lighting pricing is MISSING.
>
> **What holds up:**
> - Demand is code-mandated and visible in Chicago Department of Buildings (DOB) citations: 545 addresses in 13 months [A].
> - The licence takes weeks and about $1,520 in fees.
>
> **What fails:**
> - **Revenue per account is tiny.** In the only Illinois public price found (Park District of Highland Park, July 2025), Fox Valley Fire & Safety bid **$3 per extinguisher** for annual inspection and JCI bid $7 [A]. Stage C's "$4.75" anchor was **Boone County, Missouri**, not Illinois.
> - A small account yields about **$330/yr including pull-through**, or about $28/month.
>
> **Model result (base):** year-3 operating profit **about $34k** and year-5 **about $95k**, all founder-delivered with no technician hired. Peak funding is about $41k excluding owner draws and $104k including them.
>
> **The 2028 sunset is real but procedural.** The Act is scheduled for repeal on 1 Jan 2028. However, the last sunset review (2022) recommended continuation and the legislature extended it. The licence was never much of a moat anyway: 29 distributor licences were issued in 2025–26.
>
> **First-aid add-on:** worth about $34/yr contribution per account. Keep it only as an option.

## 1. Specific initial niche

**Service bundle:**
- Annual portable-extinguisher inspection and tagging.
- Monthly visual-check checklist.
- Annual 90-minute emergency/exit-light test, plus battery and lamp replacement.
- Replacements, 6-year maintenance and hydrotests as they fall due.

**Target customers:**
- **Small occupancies** on Chicago's Southwest and West side: corner groceries, restaurants that do not need hood-system work, gyms, auto shops, churches and halls.
- **Mixed-use and 4+ unit residential buildings**, reached through their property managers.

**Demand evidence (Chicago DOB building violations, 2025-11-01 to 2026-11-27 [A, DA-S020]):**
- **Volume:** 904 fire-safety violation records at 725 addresses.
- **By violation code:**

| Code | Violation | Records |
|---|---|---|
| CN031023 | Extinguisher recharge & tag | 300 |
| CN107035 | Maintain exit sign illuminated | 125 |
| EL1827 | Restore exit and emergency lighting | 93 |
| CN031033 | Extinguisher required, non-residential | 72 |
| CN031013 | Extinguisher required, residential | 52 |
| EL1825/1826 | Emergency-lighting systems | 61 |

- **By bureau:** 629 records came from the Conservation bureau (existing, mostly residential buildings). Property managers are therefore a core channel, alongside storefront owners.
- **Wider base:** every food licence (about 11,000 retail [A, C2-S015]) needs extinguishers.

**Licence scope:** start Class A only (portables). Kitchen hood systems need a Class B licence ($1,500), a 2K employee licence and manufacturer training. Add those in phase 2.

**Evidence, from lowest to highest:**

| Source | Price evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|

| Clinton CT (2025) | $3–4.50 per unit; $130/h labour | A |

| Simplex price list filed in Connecticut (2017) | $12 per unit plus a $120 minimum | D, search summary only |
| Florida operator guide | $15–22 per unit plus $75–125 service call | D |

**Strategy:**
- **Annual site-compliance fee:** $65 trip charge + $12 per extinguisher + $9 per emergency/exit fixture, with a **$150 minimum**.
- **Replacement units** at the Fox Valley public price levels ($85 for 5-lb, $110 for 10-lb), which are defensible to buyers.
- **6-year maintenance and hydrotest** at $28–35.
- **Battery replacement:** $25–45.
- **Positioning:** compete on bundling and response, never on unit price.

## 9. Regulatory path (Illinois OSFM; Act 225 ILCS 217)

1. **Pass the NAFED/ICC portable-extinguisher exam.** $320, open book [A, WS05-S025]. Allow 2–4 weeks of study.
2. **Form the entity.** An LLC registered with the Secretary of State (and an assumed-name filing if needed), in good standing with IDOR [A, DA-S003].
3. **Get insurance.**
   - **Required:** $1,000,000 liability plus workers' comp proof [A, DA-S003].
   - **Premium:** MISSING. As a floor, broker averages for small pest firms (GL $112/mo, auto $114/mo [C, DA-S010]) are used; the model carries $450/mo (E).
4. **OSFM Class A Distributor licence.** $900 for 3 years, **one per location**. It requires a licensed employee, the certificate of insurance, a list of officers, and access to compliant maintenance equipment [A, WS05-S001; DA-S003].
5. **OSFM Class 1 Employee licence.** $300 for 3 years. Requirements: age 18+, photo ID, the NAFED/ICC certificate, and employment by the distributor [A, WS05-S002]. Applications must be signed by a distributor officer.
6. **Operations rules.**
   - Keep 3 years of service records: technician licence number, date, location and equipment class [A, DA-S003].
   - Notify OSFM when a licensed employee leaves.
7. **Hydrotests.** Subcontract to a holder of a PHMSA RIN, or obtain your own.
8. **Emergency lighting.** Monthly 30-second and annual 90-minute tests are code duties [C, DA-S008]. Whether Chicago requires a licensed electrician for fixture repair or replacement (beyond battery and lamp swaps) is **unverified** (chicago.gov returned 403). Assume yes for anything hard-wired.
9. **Timing.** About 1–3 months to first sale. Processing time is MISSING.

**The 2028 sunset question.** The Act is listed in Regulatory Sunset Act §4.38 for repeal on 1 Jan 2028. This was seen via a search summary of P.A. 104-0296; ilga.gov returned 503.
- **History:**
  - The 2022 GOMB sunset review concluded the Act "should be continued", with a longer licence cycle [A, DA-S002].
  - P.A. 102-0715 then amended it, introducing 3-year licences, $1M insurance and per-location licences [A, DA-S003], and moved the sunset date from 2023 to 2028 (per search summary).
- **Most likely outcome:** another extension in the 2027 spring session (E).
- **If it lapsed:** the licence barrier would vanish, but demand would persist because occupancies must still comply with the fire code. The business case barely changes, because the moat is already thin.

## 10. Operations workflow (lead to cash)

1. **Weekly DOB and food-inspection pull** (automated script; AI-assisted prioritisation).
2. **Walk-in** (human): tag-date check and quote on a phone.
3. **Annual service visit** (human):
   - Inspect and tag extinguishers.
   - Run the 90-minute emergency-light test. This is scheduled as a two-pass visit or done with self-test fixtures, because lights must stay on for 90 minutes.
   - Swap batteries and replace units from the van stock.
4. **Paperwork:** barcode-tag record, digital report emailed, and fire-department report filing where required.
5. **Invoice at visit:** card or net-15.
6. **Monthly automated reminders** to customers for their own visual checks. This also produces retention touchpoints.
7. **Shop day each week** for recharges, 6-year maintenance and hydrotest pickups.

**Software and AI:**
- Inspection/tag app plus billing: about $150/mo (E). The AI line is $40/mo.
- AI's role is prospecting, routing and reports only.

## 11. Competitive position and defensibility over time

**What accumulates:**
- **Annual-renewal density.** Accounts are sticky; operators describe about 10-year relationships [D].
- **Property-manager portfolios** of multiple buildings.
- **An inspection-date database.**
- **A sellable book.** Pye-Barker, Hiller and APi are visibly acquiring Illinois locations [A, DA-S004].

**What does not:**
- **Price.** Public bids sit at $3–7 per unit.
- **The licence.** The OSFM opens about 22 complaints a year and has taken zero disciplinary actions [A, C1-S002], and new licences are issued every month.
- **Cintas's bundle:** uniforms, first aid and fire services sold on one cooperative contract.

**Strategic note:** the same restaurants and grocers buy pest control monthly (WS05-007). A second OSFM licence held by the pest company would roughly add $25–45/month per shared account at almost zero acquisition cost. That is the best use of this concept.

## 12. Key risks and pre-mortem

| Loss level | Failure path | Early warning |
|---|---|---|
| **$5k** | Licensed and insured, but walk-ins convert under 15% because owners already have "a guy" at $5–10 per unit | Average quote accepted below $200/account |
| **$25k** | A shop, recharge equipment and inventory are bought for a book of fewer than 150 accounts. Revenue under $50k/yr cannot cover a $700/mo space, van and insurance | Pull-through (replacements, maintenance) below $60 per account per year |
| **$50k** | A technician is hired at about 350 accounts. In the model, base year-5 operating profit falls to about $16k, or about $5k if a rep is also hired at 500 accounts. Plus a liability claim from a failed extinguisher | Revenue per technician below $150k |
