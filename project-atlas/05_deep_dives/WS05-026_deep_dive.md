# WS05-026 — Medical, dental and ambulatory-clinic janitorial (OSHA BBP-compliant), Chicago West/Southwest side

**Stage D deep dive · batch DA (Chicago commercial building services) · 2026-11-28**

> **Verdict: RESERVE.** Revised evidence level: **D**.
>
> Wages, buyer lists and contract terms are A/C. Clinic prices and win rates are E.
>
> **The business is real, scalable and close to customer-funded.** It has thousands of identifiable buyers and no licence barrier. But the driver model shows its economics are fragile.
>
> - **Wage floor.** Chicago's $17.05 minimum wage [A] is indexed annually. Payroll load comes on top.
> - **Thin gross margin.** With that floor and load, a $1,500/month clinic yields about **36% gross margin before supervision**.
> - **Base case.** Year-3 operating profit is about **$30k**; year 5 is about **$141k** on $2.6M revenue.
> - **Fragility.** Gross margin −5 points or labour +20% turns year 3 negative.
> - **Working capital.** It absorbs most cash. Net working capital is about $83k at 50 sites.
> - **Funding.** Peak funding is about $83k excluding owner draws and $195k including them.
>
> Keep it in reserve, as a scalable people-management business, for a founder who finds no better licensed route.

## 1. Specific initial niche

**What is sold:**
- Night janitorial, 3–6 nights a week.
- A written infection-control and OSHA bloodborne-pathogen (BBP) programme.
- Exam-room disinfection logs, with QR/photo task verification.

**Who buys it:**
- Outpatient sites of **2,000–15,000 sq ft**: federally qualified health centre (FQHC) sites, dental and specialty practices, physical-therapy clinics and small ambulatory surgery centres (ASCs).

**Where:**
- Chicago's West and Southwest side: Pilsen, Little Village, Back of the Yards, Humboldt Park, Austin.
- Near-west suburbs: Cicero, Berwyn, Oak Park, Melrose Park.

**Buyer universe:**
- **Cook County has 245 active HRSA-listed health-centre sites** (204 freestanding), run by about 30 organisations [A, DA-S022].
- **Illinois** has 6,775 physician offices, 5,781 dentist offices and 2,545 therapy offices [C, C1-S022].
- **CMS lists 129 Illinois ASCs**, including at least 15 at Chicago addresses [A, DA-S028].

**Why start with FQHCs:**
- **They run many sites.** Esperanza has 8 sites, Alivio 10, Erie 11 and PCC 11, so one relationship can add sites.
- **Long hours.** Sites average 39–57 operating hours a week, which means real cleaning load.
- **They procure competitively,** so bid outcomes are observable.

## 6. Customer acquisition

**Customer #1:**
- Start where the founder can clean: a 2,000–5,000 sq ft independent practice or a single FQHC satellite.
- Approach it through walk-in or LinkedIn outreach to the practice manager.
- Offer a written BBP/infection-control programme, a free walkthrough and scope, and a one-night paid trial clean.
- The founder works the first sites personally to calibrate hours.

**Customers #2–10:**
- Cluster small practices along one corridor, e.g. Cermak/26th Street or Harlem Avenue.
- Ask medical-office-building property managers for adjacent suites.
- Monitor FQHC RFPs for small sites.

**Customers #11–100:**
- Hire a sales rep at about 40 sites.
- Bid FQHC multi-site packages.
- Use references from the first 10 clients.
- Add ASCs once terminal-cleaning SOPs and training are proven.

|---|---|---|
| Qualified decision-maker conversations per month | 25 (conservative 18; optimistic 35) | E |
| Conversation → walkthrough and proposal | 40% | E |

**Why clinics switch:**
- **Quality lapses and personnel changes.** In L.E.K.'s survey, switchers cited delivery and quality issues, personnel changes and lack of proactivity [C, DA-S019]. That survey also ranks quality as the top criterion for janitorial.
- **Infection control.** In a 2021 survey, 54% of facility executives would pay more for increased infection-control procedures, and 49% rated certifications important [C, DA-S030].
- **Low switching friction.** With a 60-day termination right, switching is easy, so both the entry door and the churn risk are permanent.

## 7. Pricing strategy

**Price umbrella:**
- **Union-scale cost:** the Cook County prevailing janitorial wage for state-procured work is **$22.40/h, plus $949.87/month health & welfare and $56/week pension** [A, DA-S026]. That is about $31–32/h loaded. It sets the cost of union-scale competitors.
- **Non-union cost:** a firm paying $18.50 has a loaded cost of about $22.6/h.

**Pricing rules:**
- Price at **$38–42 per scheduled labour-hour**, which gives about 36–40% gross margin. That works out to about **$0.30–0.45/sq ft/month** for 5 nights a week (E).
- Set a $600/month minimum.
- Charge separately for the initial deep clean and floor strip/wax ($300–800).
- Include an annual escalator tied to Chicago's indexed minimum wage, which rose 2.7% in 2026.
- Do not chase the low bids that win public work. The Calvert spread was 15×.

## 9. Regulatory path

1. **Entity, registration and Chicago licence.** Form an LLC and register with the Illinois Department of Employment Security and the Illinois Department of Revenue. **No Illinois janitorial licence was identified.** This could not be verified, because ilga.gov was unavailable. A Chicago business licence for a cleaning contractor is also unverified (chicago.gov returned 403).
2. **OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030 (bloodborne pathogens)** [A, C1-S024]. Required elements:
   - a written exposure control plan and a written cleaning/decontamination schedule;
   - hepatitis B vaccination offered at no cost within 10 working days of assignment, with declinations documented;
   - annual paid training;
   - PPE, plus sharps and regulated-waste procedures.
3. **Medical waste.** Potentially infectious medical waste must go to licensed haulers. The janitor must not treat it as trash, and the contract should exclude it [A, C1-S025].
4. **Wages.**
   - Chicago: $17.05 from July 2026 [A, DA-S013].
   - Suburban Cook: $15.40 [A, DA-S027].
   - Illinois prevailing wage applies only to state-procured janitorial work [A, DA-S026].
   - Federal sites carry Service Contract Act wages.
   - Chicago paid leave and paid sick leave mandates are unverified (E).
5. **Insurance and bonding.** Broker averages for Illinois cleaners [C, DA-S009]:
   - GL $83/mo
   - bond about $10/mo
   - auto $234/mo
   - umbrella $87/mo

   WC is payroll-rated. The Illinois class 9014 rate is MISSING. Public buyers demand more: Winnebago's RFP requires WC $1M, GL $2M/$2M, auto $2M and a **$5M umbrella** [A, DA-S025]. The model carries $450/mo of overhead insurance and puts WC inside the crew load.
6. **Timing.** About 1 month to first service.

## 10. Operations workflow (lead to cash)

1. **Lead** (human, with AI drafting outreach).
2. **Walkthrough:** room-by-room inventory, with production-rate estimates from a template (AI-assisted).
3. **Proposal:** price per labour-hour, scope matrix, BBP programme.
4. **Contract:** 12 months, 30–60-day termination.
5. **Hire and onboard:**
   - background check;
   - BBP training and hepatitis B vaccine offer;
   - site-specific checklist in English and Spanish (AI translation).
6. **Nightly service:** QR check-in, photo of exam rooms and restrooms.
7. **Supervision:** supervisor audits each site at least weekly (1 supervisor per about 25 sites).
8. **Monthly QA report** to the practice manager.
9. **Billing:** net-30, with dunning at day 35.

**Software and tools:**
- Scheduling/QA app plus payroll service: about $300/mo (E; vendor price pages unreachable).
- Bookkeeping: $350/mo.
- AI assistant: $40/mo.

**What can and cannot be automated:**
- AI helps with SOPs, translation and proposals. The service is entirely human labour.
- AI cannot change unit cost meaningfully, so the AI score is 3.

## 11. Competitive position and defensibility over time

**What accumulates:**
- **Site clusters** cut travel time.
- **Multi-site FQHC and dental-group relationships.**
- **Clinic references.**
- **A trained, retained night workforce.** This is the real asset.

**What does not accumulate:**
- **Contracts are cancellable in 60 days.** Price is set by low bidders.
- **The BBP programme is a requirement every competitor serving clinics already meets,** so it is not a moat.
- **Healthcare-specific certifications** might help. 49% of executives value certifications [C], but the specific programmes were not verified.
- **Entry remains 3.**

## 12. Key risks and pre-mortem

| Loss level | Failure path | Early warning |
|---|---|---|
| **$5k** | No signed site after 20 walkthroughs; or actual hours exceed the estimate by 25%+, so the first site loses money | Proposals rejected on price below $35/labour-hour |
| **$25k** | Crew turnover causes missed cleans, and two of the first five sites cancel on 60-day notice. The founder is stuck working nights | Call-outs more than 1/week per 10 sites; client complaints |
| **$50k** | Growth to 30–40 sites on net-45 terms drains cash. Minimum-wage indexation (+2.7%/yr) outruns price escalators, or a workers' comp claim/BBP exposure incident hits | Days sales outstanding above 45; gross margin below 33%; any exposure incident without documented training |

**Model stress tests (base year-3 operating profit $30k):**

| Stress | Year-3 operating profit |
|---|---|
| Gross margin −5 points | −$17k |
| Labour +20% | −$20k |
| Competitor response | +$5k |
| Insurance and overhead +50% | +$16k |
| Customers pay 30 days later | Profit unchanged; peak funding about $433k |

- **Largest sensitivities:** gross margin and labour cost.
- **Caveat:** some stress lines (win rate −40%, recession) *raise* year-3 profit. That is an artefact of step-function hiring: fewer sites means no supervisor or rep is hired yet.
