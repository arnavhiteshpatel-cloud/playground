# WS12-012 — Dedicated offshore tax & accounting staff for Chicago-area CPA firms: Stage D deep dive (batch DG)

> **Verdict: FINALIST (conditional).** Evidence level **C**: prices B, wages B/D, churn and conversion E. Researched 2026-11-28.
>
> - **Vertical chosen: small and mid-size CPA firms.** The alternatives were insurance agencies, dental offices, property managers and freight brokers. CPA firms win on four counts:
>   1. **Price:** the seats are higher-skill, so they fetch higher prices. A CPA-focused Philippine provider lists talent cost **from $1,400 (accountant), $2,300 (tax preparer) and $2,400 (auditor) per month plus a management fee** [B, DG-S002].
>   2. **Prospects:** they are listed in an official register. There are **1,062 active Illinois CPA firm licences, 680 of them in the six Chicago-area counties** [A, DG-S001].
>   3. **Regulation favours a specialist.** IRC §7216 requires signed taxpayer consent before tax data goes to an offshore preparer, and an "adequate data protection safeguard" before SSNs do [A, DG-S009]. The FTC Safeguards Rule makes the firm contractually oversee its service provider [A, DG-S010].
>   4. **Labour supply is tight.** New Illinois CPA licences fell from ~1,800–1,900/yr (2016–2019) to ~1,300–1,600/yr (2020–2024) [A, DG-S001].
> - **Decisive fact: the legal structure decides the margin.** An employer-of-record costs **$599–699 per worker-month** [B, DG-S004/S005/S006], which takes a $2,400 seat from ~38% to ~9% contribution. The model only works with contractor engagement (platform fee $29–49) or, later, a Philippine entity.
> - **Model results:**
>
>   | Case | Year-3 operating profit | Notes |
>   |---|---|---|
>   | Base | **$231k** | ~36 firm accounts, ~59 seats, $1.33M revenue; peak funding $13k |
>   | Conservative | **−$4k** | |
>   | Optimistic | $2.0M | A ceiling, not a forecast |
>
> - **Main threats:**
>   - AI automation of 1040/bookkeeping tasks.
>   - India-based competitors.
>   - PE roll-ups. ORBA's domain now redirects to a Citrin Cooperman acquisition notice [D, DG-S015].
>   - The $99/seat marketplace floor [B, DG-S007].

## 1. Specific initial niche
**Dedicated, accounting-trained Philippine staff accountants, tax preparers and bookkeepers for 5–60-person CPA firms in Cook, DuPage, Lake, Will, Kane and McHenry counties.**

- **What the firm gets:**
  - Billed monthly in advance.
  - One seat minimum; three-month initial term, then monthly.
  - A US account manager.
  - A packaged "compliance kit":
    - §7216 consent templates the firm sends its clients [A, DG-S009];
    - SSN-masking workflow;
    - written security program;
    - Safeguards-Rule vendor-oversight file [A, DG-S010].
- **Sold nationally after the Chicago base.** The work is remote, but local presence (ICPAS chapters, in-person partner meetings) is the early wedge.

**Why CPA firms beat the other candidate verticals:**

| Vertical | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Insurance agencies | AMS software skill | Association channel already held (Agency VA endorsed by a state association [D, C9-S020]); producer-licence limits on what offshore CSRs may do |
| Dental offices | — | Insurance verification is a prime AI/API automation target; HIPAA BAA overhead; low seat prices |
| Property managers | — | Leasing-agent licence limits on offshore tasks; AI leasing agents |
| Freight brokers | Prospects easy to list | Highly cyclical; heavily targeted by AI voice/track-and-trace agents |
| **CPA firms (chosen)** | Highest documented seat prices [B, DG-S002; D, DG-S014]; firm register [A, DG-S001]; compliance-heavy buying [A, DG-S009/S010] | Busy-season seasonality |

  2. Offer: one trial seat starting in January, three-month minimum, with the §7216 consent pack done for them.
  3. Target the firms with visible staff-accountant openings first [D, DG-S016]. Their pain is current.

  - Plus ~10 hours of candidate vetting with the Philippine team lead.

## 9. Regulatory path
1. **Illinois LLC formation** plus an EIN. The Secretary of State fee was not fetched (MISSING; verify).
2. **No Illinois employment-agency licence.** The firm is paid only by employers and is the engaging party [A, C9-S022]. The Day and Temporary Labor Services Act does not apply, because no workers are at Illinois worksites (analyst reading).
3. **US tax treatment:**
   - Pay to Philippine residents for work performed abroad is not US wages and is not subject to withholding [A, DG-S011].
   - Collect W-8BEN forms.
4. **Philippine side — the critical legal risk:**
   - Contractors register and pay their own taxes; an EOR carries 13th-month pay and SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG (statutory add-ons 12–15% [D, DG-S014]).
   - A full-time, controlled "contractor" may be reclassified as an employee under Philippine law.
   - Mitigation: Philippine counsel review (~$1–3k, E); Contractor of Record at $325 for higher-risk seats [B, DG-S004]; a PH entity at ~20+ seats.
5. **Client compliance (firm obligations the product serves):**
   - §7216 consent before disclosure, with special SSN rules [A, DG-S009].
   - Safeguards Rule vendor oversight [A, DG-S010].
   - The Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 likely applies to workers handling personal data (not fetched; verify).
6. **Insurance (no quotes; E):**
   - E&O/professional ≈ $2–3k/yr.
   - Cyber ≈ $1.5–2.5k/yr.
   - GL ≈ $500/yr.

## 10. Operations workflow
1. Lead from the IDFPR list.
2. Discovery call (role, software, busy-season needs).
3. Profile and shortlist of three candidates. The PH team lead sources via OnlineJobs.ph and referrals, then runs a skills test on the firm's software (UltraTax, Lacerte, CCH Axcess, QuickBooks).
4. Paid trial week.
5. MSA, DPA and §7216 kit signed.
6. Laptop/VDI provisioning and MDM.
7. Monthly prepaid invoice.
8. Weekly QA check-in; quarterly business review.
9. Backfill within 10 business days.

**Human vs automatable:**
- **Human:** candidate judgment, partner relationships, escalations.
- **AI-assisted:** job descriptions, skills-test grading, onboarding SOPs, QA sampling of work papers. Running cost about $250/month (marked is_ai).
- **Automatable:** invoicing, contractor payouts (Deel/Wise) and the CRM.

## 11. Competitive position and defensibility
**What accumulates:**
- a bench of tested US-tax-literate Philippine staff;
- firm-specific SOPs;
- a security program that can later be SOC 2-audited (budgeted from month 13);
- referral density among Chicago partners;
- annual-prepay contracts.

**What does not protect the business:**
- price;
- the generic "VA" label;
- anything a PE-backed firm can build in-house.

**AI is a double edge.** Workers equipped with AI produce more per seat, which supports pricing. Fewer staff-accountant hours are needed per return, which erodes seat counts. The model's "competitor response" stress (no price growth, −3 pts margin, churn ×1.3) cuts year-3 operating profit to **$115k**.

## 13. Validation plan (cheapest real evidence first)
1. **Supply test ($99–198, 2 weeks).**
   - Post "US tax preparer — UltraTax/Lacerte" and "staff accountant — QuickBooks/CAS" on OnlineJobs.ph Premium [B, DG-S008].
   - **Success:** ≥20 applicants passing a 30-minute skills test, with asks ≤$1,200/month.
   - **Kill:** qualified asks above $1,500, which pushes margin below 30%.
2. **Paid pilot ($300 + founder time, by 15 January).**
   - Two firms from the 27, one seat each at $2,200–2,400 with a three-month minimum, prepaid.
   - **Success:** 2 paid seats.
   - **Kill:** <1 after 40 qualified conversations.
3. **Post-season retention (April–July).**
   - **Success:** ≥50% of pilot seats renew past 30 April at full price.
   - **Kill:** all cancel. That would mean a seasonal labour product, not recurring revenue.
4. **AI-displacement check with pilot firms.**
   - Log every task assigned to each seat for 8 weeks.
   - Classify each as client-facing/judgment, software-operation or data-entry.
   - **Kill:** >60% data-entry, which is the AI-replaceable share.
5. **Legal structure check ($1–3k).** Philippine counsel opinion on the contractor model, before the 5th seat.
