# WS18-003 — Junk removal & cleanouts: Stage D commercial deep dive (batch DF)

> **Verdict: RESERVE.** Evidence level **B** (franchise P&Ls plus official Chicago disposal and licence data). Researched 2026-11-28.
>
> This is the best-documented small-truck business in the batch, and the numbers are respectable but not exceptional.
> - **Franchise economics:**
>   - Junk King units average $554k sales at a $399 ticket. Labour is 20.6% of sales and dumping 7.2% [B, WS18-S001].
>   - College Hunks stores aged 13–36 months show median $691k sales and $44k EBITDA, after ~12% franchise, brand and call-centre fees and before truck payments [B, C8-S017].
> - **Independent owner economics:**
>   - A Naperville property-cleanout firm founded in 2020 lists $814k revenue and $220k cash flow but asks only 1.3× that cash flow [D, DF-S018]. Buyers clearly discount how durable such earnings are.
>   - A Chicagoland four-territory franchise founded in 2021 is listed with just $25k cash flow [D, DF-S019].
> - **Chicago specifics:**
>   - Public transfer-station disposal costs **$100/ton**, mattresses $75 and white goods $25 [B, DF-S016].
>   - Haulers hold a **per-vehicle "Private Scavenger Vehicle" licence**. 1-800-GOT-JUNK? Chicagoland holds 10 [A, DF-S015].
> - **Model results:** base year-3 operating profit **~$132k on ~$669k revenue (20%)**. The conservative case is about breakeven-to-negative (−$11k).
> - **Durability problem:** the moat stays weak, with three national brands and low exit multiples.

## 1. Specific initial niche
**B2B-first junk removal and cleanouts on Chicago's north and west sides and the near-west suburbs** (Oak Park, Elmwood Park, Park Ridge, Evanston, Skokie). Three repeat B2B channels:
- **Property managers and condo/apartment associations:** move-out, eviction and storage-room cleanouts.
- **Self-storage operators:** defaulted-unit cleanouts after lien sales.
- **Fire/water restoration contractors:** tear-out debris and damaged contents.

Consumer jobs come through Google LSA and search. Dumpster rental and light demolition are deferred.

These three jobs explain the strategy: paid-lead single items barely cover acquisition, and **repeat B2B full loads carry the business**. Chicago disposal at $100/ton is roughly 12–30% of a job's revenue, depending on how dense the load is. That is above Junk King's 7.2% national average, so the model uses ~11% disposal and 24% labour (E uplift on B base).

## 6. Customer acquisition
- **Customer #1.** A consumer job via Google Business Profile/LSA in week 1–2, or a restoration owner who needs same-day debris haul.
- **Customers #2–10.** LSA plus three B2B "trial" cleanouts at standard rates (property manager, storage facility manager, restoration PM).
- **Customers #11–100:**
  - building-manager rounds (10 buildings a week);
  - storage district managers (flat per-unit cleanout sheet);
  - restoration owners (insurer-friendly itemised invoices, photo documentation);
  - realtor office talks for estate cleanouts.
- **Funnel (base):**
  - at plateau (~month 24), ~280 leads/month;
  - 70% receive a photo or on-site quote, and 60% of quotes are booked;
  - result: ~118 jobs/month plus ~6 repeat B2B jobs per 100;
  - calibration: between the Junk King median ($436k) and the College Hunks year-1–3 median ($691k) [B].
- **CAC:** $55 per job (13% of ticket). College Hunks franchisees spend ~12% of sales on local marketing *on top of* brand-fund and call-centre fees [B, C8-S017].
- **LTV:** B2B accounts can generate 1–4 jobs a month at near-zero CAC; consumers are one-off.
- **Weekly sales work:** about 8–12 hours of founder field selling in months 1–12.

## 7. Pricing strategy
- **Volume-based menu** (⅛, ¼, ½, full truck) anchored on the $399 national average ticket.
- **Pass-through surcharges** for mattresses ($75 tipping), appliances with refrigerant and tyres, quoted from photos.
- **B2B rate cards:** per cubic yard or per load, with net-30 terms for approved managers.
- **Price evidence:** Junk King $399 average, $389 median [B]; Chicago-specific competitor menus MISSING. Validation must collect them before launch.
- **Model ticket assumptions:** $380 / $420 / $470.

## 9. Regulatory path
1. **Entity and insurance.** LLC, EIN and IDOR registration, plus commercial auto (box trucks), GL and workers' compensation. Lifting injuries make WC expensive; the rate is MISSING.
2. **City of Chicago licensing.** Haulers of refuse hold a **Regulated Business License — Private Scavenger Vehicle, one per truck**. The largest local junk brand does this (10 vehicles) [A, DF-S015]. Small operators use the **Junk Peddler** licence.
   - The fee, inspection and insurance requirements are MISSING (municipal code and BACP pages blocked).
   - Confirm which licence applies before the first Chicago job. Suburban villages have their own hauler rules (MISSING).
3. **ICC Household Goods licence.** Needed only if transporting customers' goods for hire, such as donation delivery or moving. Get a legal read before offering "move-outs" [A, WS18-S014].
4. **Special wastes.**
   - Refrigerant appliances need certified recovery before disposal (EPA Clean Air Act §608; general knowledge, not re-verified).
   - Electronics fall under Illinois' landfill ban (not re-verified).
   - Tyres, paint and batteries need routed outlets. Transfer stations charge per item [B, DF-S016].
5. **Home Repair Act.** Not applicable to hauling [A, WS18-S010].

## 10. Operations workflow
**Flow:** lead (call, web, B2B email) → photo quote (AI-drafted, human-approved) → booked window → 2-person crew → load, sweep, photos → donation/recycling sort → transfer-station tip → card or ACH payment → review request → B2B monthly statement.

| Type | Tasks |
|---|---|
| Human | Crew work, B2B relationships, safety, pricing exceptions |
| AI-assisted | Photo-volume estimates, quote drafts, call answering, route sequencing, donation-receipt paperwork |
| Automatable | Reminders, invoicing, review requests |

**Software:** Housecall Pro Essentials $149–189/mo [B, DF-S035] plus QuickBooks and Google Business Profile/LSA. Modelled at $350/mo including AI tools (E).

## 11. Competitive position and defensibility
What can accumulate:
- **B2B account density:** property managers and storage districts reorder.
- **Route density** in a few community areas.
- **Disposal know-how:** donation outlets and metal recyclers, which lower tipping costs.
- **Reviews.**

What cannot: price, brand and same-day service. All three national brands advertise same-day service and eco-diversion, and 1-800-GOT-JUNK runs a 10-truck licensed fleet in the city.

**Exit value is low.** A seller-claimed $220k cash-flow business is asking 1.3× [D, DF-S018]. That fits the weak-moat score (entry 3).

## 13. Validation plan
1. **Chicago price and disposal benchmark ($0, 1 week).** Record public price pages and review mentions for 10 Chicago haulers. Confirm gate rates at two private transfer stations and Chicago licence fees *(after founder authorizes contact)*.
   - **Success:** median half-truck price ≥$350.
   - **Kill:** under $300 together with disposal above $110/ton.
2. **B2B demand test ($100).** Pitch 15 prospects from the CSV (5 property managers, 5 storage, 5 restoration) for a first job at list price.
   - **Success:** 3 paid jobs within 45 days, using a rented truck.
   - **Kill:** zero after 15 meetings.
3. **Rented-truck pilot ($1,500–2,500).** Rent a box truck per job and run 20 jobs in 6 weeks with LSA and B2B leads.
   - **Success:** contribution of at least $120 per job after disposal, labour and leads.
   - **Kill:** under $60.
4. **Licence/insurance quote ($0).** Obtain written premium indications for one box truck plus WC.
   - **Success:** under $12k/yr.
   - **Kill:** over $20k/yr.

Buy the truck only after experiments 2–3 pass.
