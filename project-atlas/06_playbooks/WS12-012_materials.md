## 2. Customer discovery interview questionnaire (non-leading; past behaviour and spend) — DRAFT, not sent; requires founder review
**Before starting:** "I'm researching how Chicago-area CPA firms handle capacity. I'm not selling anything in this conversation. About 25 minutes."

1. **Firm basics:** how many professionals do you have, and roughly what mix of tax, audit and CAS?
2. **Last busy season:**
   - Where did the firm run short of capacity?
   - What did you do about it? Overtime, temps, extensions, turning work away?
3. **Last hire:** walk me through the last staff accountant or tax hire you tried to make. How long did it take? What did it cost? How did it end?
4. **Past outside help:** has the firm ever used outside or offshore help, for any role?
   - If yes: who? What did you pay per month or per return? What was included? How long did it last?
   - If you stopped: why?
5. **If you use offshore staff today:**
   - How many? Which tasks?
   - What share of their week is data entry, versus work inside your software, versus judgment calls?
6. **§7216 consents:** how do you handle them today? Who obtains them, and when? Has a client ever refused?
7. **Security questions:**
   - What do your engagement letters or your cyber insurer require of any provider that touches client data?
   - Have you been asked for a vendor's SOC 2?
8. **Software:** which tax and accounting software do you use? Have you tried, or do you plan to try, the new AI preparation features (CCH Axcess, UltraTax/SurePrep, others)? What happened?
9. **Decision process:** who decides on outside staff, and who else must agree?
10. **Payment terms:** how do you usually pay vendors (prepay, net 30, card)?
11. **Price:** if you have paid for offshore help, what was the actual monthly amount per person?
12. **Close:** is there anything else about capacity I should have asked? Can I follow up after I've spoken with a few other firms?

**Recording sheet:** keep facts and quotes separate from interpretation. Never record a commitment that was not made.

## 4. Sales call script with qualification questions (DRAFT — not sent; requires founder review)
1. **Agenda (1 min):** "I'll ask about your capacity needs, show how a dedicated seat works, and if it fits, we can discuss a 90-day pilot."
2. **Qualify the need.** Disqualify if no role is defined, if the firm wants per-return pricing only, or if nobody can supervise.
   - Which role (bookkeeping/CAS, tax prep, audit support)?
   - How many hours a week?
   - Which software?
   - Who would supervise?
3. **Qualify timing.** When would the seat start? Is the need year-round or seasonal? If seasonal, what work fills May–December?
4. **Qualify compliance.**
   - Will the firm obtain §7216 consents from clients before assigning their returns?
   - Do you provide remote access with MFA (VDI/VPN)?
   - Are you comfortable with masked SSNs by default?
5. **Qualify budget and authority.** "Seats are $1,950–3,100 a month, depending on role, prepaid monthly." Who approves?
6. **Present the solution:**
   - three pre-tested candidates within 10 business days;
   - a paid trial week credited to month 1;
   - a 90-day initial term, then 60 days' notice;
   - replacement within 10 business days;
   - a vendor security file;
   - a weekly 15-minute check-in.
7. **Objections:**
   - "Penbrothers or Sourcefit is cheaper." → "Yes, by a few hundred dollars. What we add: local partner-level account management, tested candidates on your software, a consent workflow and seat pairing so the person stays busy after April. If the price gap matters most, they're a reasonable choice."
   - "AI will do this." → "Possibly for some 1040 input. That's why we recommend starting with CAS or review-support work and training the person on your AI tools. Our pilot logs every task so you can see what's automatable."
8. **Close:** "Shall I prepare a role profile and shortlist? If you like a candidate, the first month is prepaid and we start [date]."

## 7. Pricing calculator spec (DRAFT — requires founder review)
**Inputs:**
- role;
- PHP salary (or USD);
- FX rate (default: latest Treasury rate, 62.658);
- share of hours from 10pm to 6am PHT (for the night differential, ≥10%);
- expected PH holidays worked per year (double pay);
- HMO;
- equipment amortisation;
- structure (contractor pilot / PEO fee / EOR fee / own entity);
- entity overhead per month and the number of seats sharing it;
- recruiting/backfill reserve;
- target contribution per seat;
- discount (annual prepay);
- setup fee;
- churn assumption.

**Calculations:**

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| 13th month | salary ÷ 12 |
| SSS employer | 10% × min(salary, PHP 35,000), plus EC (~PHP 30, verify) |
| PhilHealth employer | 2.5% × min(max(salary, 10,000), 100,000) |
| Pag-IBIG employer | 2% × min(salary, 10,000) |
| Night differential | 10% × salary × night-hour share |
| Holiday premium | salary ÷ 21.75 × holidays worked ÷ 12 |
| Entity overhead per seat | entity overhead ÷ seats |

**Outputs:**
- all-in monthly cost;
- minimum price for the target contribution;
- contribution $ and %;
- 12-month account value;
- break-even seats for the entity overhead;
- a warning flag if the price is below $2,100 or contribution below $550.

- [ ] Contractor pilot only: W-8BEN collected.

- [ ] Escalation contacts exchanged.
