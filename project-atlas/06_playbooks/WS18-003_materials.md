## 2. Customer discovery interview questionnaire (non-leading; past behaviour and spend)

**DRAFT — not sent; requires founder review.**

**Purpose:** learn how cleanouts happen today. This is not a pitch. Spend 25–30 minutes, take notes, and record only with consent.

**Opening:** "I'm researching how property managers handle cleanouts and bulk-item removal. I'm not selling anything today. Could I ask about the last few times this came up?"

**A. Role and scope**
1. What buildings or facilities are you responsible for? Roughly how many units?
2. Who decides which vendor removes junk or debris: you, the building engineer, the owner, the board?

**B. Recent behaviour**
3. Think about the last three times a unit, storage room or common area needed clearing. What happened each time?
4. Who did the work: maintenance staff, a dumpster, a junk company, the tenant? Why that choice?
5. How long from the request to the space being clear? Did that delay cost anything (vacancy days, a board complaint, a missed closing)?
6. Roughly how many of these jobs happened in the last 90 days? Is that a typical quarter?

**C. Spend and process**
7. What did the last job cost, approximately? How was the price set (per load, hourly, flat)?
8. How was it paid: card, invoice, net terms? Did you need a PO or a W-9? An onboarding portal?
9. What insurance certificate did the vendor have to provide (limits, additional insured)?

**D. Problems**
10. Tell me about a cleanout that went badly. What went wrong?
11. What have you done to avoid that since?

**E. Current vendors**
12. Do you have a go-to vendor now? How did you find them? What would make you call someone else?

**F. Close (behavioural commitment, not a hypothetical)**
13. "If a vendor could photo-quote within an hour and clear a unit within 48 hours with an itemised photo invoice, would you be willing to place one job at standard rates to try it?" Record yes / no / conditions.
14. "Who else in your company or at other buildings handles this?" (Referral ask.)

**Record afterwards:** jobs per 90 days, average price, payment terms, COI requirements, pain points (verbatim, short), trial yes/no.

**Storage-operator variant (replace B and C):**
- How many units went to lien sale or abandonment last quarter?
- After the sale, who empties units that don't sell?
- What do you pay per unit size?

**Restoration variant:**
- How often do jobs need contents or tear-out debris hauled?
- How fast do you need it?
- Is hauling billed to the insurer, and what documentation must the invoice show?

## 4. Sales call script with qualification questions

**DRAFT — not sent; requires founder review.**

1. **Permission and agenda (30 s).** "Thanks for the time. I'd like to understand how cleanouts work at your buildings, tell you briefly what we do, and see if a trial job makes sense. Fifteen minutes OK?"
2. **Discovery** (use §2 questions 3–10; listen 70% of the time).
3. **Qualification checklist** (score each 0/1; pursue if ≥4 of 6):

| Question | Qualifies if |
|---|---|
| Jobs per month across their portfolio | ≥2 |
| Decision-maker on the call, or name obtained | Yes |
| Current vendor problem (speed, no-shows, invoices, price surprises) | Yes |
| Can approve a trial job at list price without a board vote | Yes |
| Payment terms | ≤ net-30 |
| COI requirements | Within what we can carry |

4. **Offer (2 min).** Photo quote in an hour; 48-hour clearance; itemised photo invoice; monthly statement. Show the B2B rate card. **No discount on the first job**; offer a satisfaction guarantee instead: "If the space isn't clear to your standard, we come back free."
5. **Objections:**
   - *"We already have a vendor."* → "Great. Most managers keep a backup for weeks when their vendor is booked. Could we be that?"
   - *"Maintenance does it."* → "How many hours did that take last month, and where did the debris go?" (Listen; don't argue.)
   - *"Price?"* → Show the per-load card; compare to vacancy days, not to the other vendor.
6. **Close.** "Is there a unit or room this month we could quote from a photo?" Capture address, access, PO and COI requirements.
7. **Follow-up.** Same-day email summary. After the first job, send photos and the invoice within 24 hours and call within 3 days to ask for the next building.

## 7. Pricing calculator spec (inputs → outputs)

**DRAFT — not sent; requires founder review.** Build it as a spreadsheet tab or FSM price book.

**Inputs**

| Group | Inputs |
|---|---|
| Job | load fraction (⅛ … full, or cubic yards; 16 ft box ≈ [15] yd³, to be measured); est. weight (t) from category defaults (furniture 0.25 t/yd³-equivalent, mixed household, C&D/plaster); item counts (mattress, appliance with/without refrigerant, TV/monitor, tyre); crew size; est. on-site hours; drive minutes to site and to the tip; channel (paid lead / B2B account / referral) |
| Cost rates | loaded crew $/h [24]; disposal $/t [100] + item fees [mattress 75, white goods 25] (SWANCC 2026 schedule); fuel $/mile; card fee [3%]; paid-lead CAC [80], B2B servicing [25], referral [50] |
| Policy | target contribution $/job [≥120] and % [≥35%]; minimum ticket [149]; B2B rate-card overrides |

**Outputs**
- Recommended price = max(minimum ticket, cost + target contribution, menu price for the load fraction).
- Cost breakdown (crew, disposal, item fees, fuel, card, acquisition) and contribution $ and %.
- Flags:
  - contribution < $60;
  - disposal > 20% of price;
  - heavy-material warning;
  - single-item paid-lead job (suggest batching or a minimum).
- Quote text block for §5.

**Validation use.** After each pilot job, enter the actual hours and scale-ticket tons. The sheet shows the quote-vs-actual variance, which calibrates the weight defaults.
