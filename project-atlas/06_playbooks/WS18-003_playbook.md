# WS18-003 Playbook — Junk removal & cleanouts (independent, B2B-referral-led), Chicago north/west side + near-west suburbs

> **Stage E verdict: RESERVE / CONDITIONAL. It is safe to validate cheaply. It is not yet a holding-company engine.** Evidence level **B** for unit economics (franchise Item 19s), **E** for the new-entrant overlay. Researched 2026-11-28.
>
> This is still the cheapest finalist to test and the best documented. Customers pay at completion, every role is a standard labour-market job, and three franchise systems publish detailed P&Ls. Stage E re-checked those P&Ls line by line, and **the corrected base case is materially weaker than Stage D's.**
>
> **What changed**
> - **The Stage D plateau was calibrated too high.** It used College Hunks medians, which include moving revenue: 138 of 144 franchised stores sell junk and moving together [B, E-WS18003-S001].
> - **Junk-only brands ramp more slowly.** 1-800-GOT-JUNK franchisees, with the strongest brand in the category, averaged $220,855 in their first year and $511,647 in year two (CY2023) [B, E-WS18003-S003].
> - **Leads cost more than Stage D assumed.** College Hunks pays a median $32 per lead and closes 40%, or about $80 per paid-lead job. That is on top of a national brand and a call centre that books 48% of its junk sales [B, E-WS18003-S001].
>
> **Rebuilt model results**
>
> | | Base | Conservative | Optimistic |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Year-3 operating profit | **$49,282** | **−$28,507** | **$281,887** |
> | Year-5 operating profit | **$83,289** | **−$12,447** | **$390,575** |
>
> - All figures are before owner pay. The base case includes a second service zone and a field operations manager.
> - Base peak funding is **$142,264** with the standard $4k/month draw from month 13, or **$90,179** with no draw.
> - Multi-zone growth clearly raises the *revenue* ceiling. Mature 1-800-GOT-JUNK units have a median of $2.0–2.8M revenue [B/D, E-WS18003-S003/S005]. It does not reliably raise *margin*: in base economics a second zone plus the manager who frees the founder roughly cancel out.
> - Go further only if the B2B trial proves that repeat property-manager, storage and restoration accounts can supply roughly a third of jobs at near-zero acquisition cost. Only that moves the business toward the optimistic path.

## 1. Business explanation

**What it does.** A two-person crew arrives in a 14–16 ft box truck. They carry out whatever the customer points at and sweep up. They sort the load into donation, scrap metal, e-waste/appliances and trash, then tip the remainder at a transfer station. Nothing is resold as a core activity.

**What it sells.** The service saves the customer labour and time, and the urgency is real:
- a tenant moved out and the unit must be re-let;
- a storage unit was auctioned and must be emptied;
- a basement flooded;
- a house is being listed after a death;
- a renovation left debris.

**Who buys:**
- **Households.** Infrequent and price-shopping; they search Google at the moment of need.
- **B2B.** Property and condo-association managers, self-storage operators, fire/water restoration contractors, realtors and estate executors. These customers repeat.

**How it earns.** Price is set by volume: the share of the truck the load fills. Mattresses, refrigerant appliances, tyres and e-waste carry surcharges.
- **Ticket size.** National averages run about **$399/job at Junk King** (CY2024, median $389) [B, WS18-S001] and a **$443 median job at College Hunks junk-only stores** [B, E-WS18003-S001].
- **Published price ranges at 1-800-GOT-JUNK:** small loads $107–$396, medium $396–$600, large $600–$1,099; a sofa averages $125 [B, E-WS18003-S004].
- **Payment timing.** The customer pays at completion; B2B accounts are invoiced net-30. The operator pays crew wages, disposal at the scale, fuel, insurance, truck costs and lead costs.

**Why it can be attractive:**
- No inventory and near-zero receivables.
- No licence that the founder cannot get.
- It can be tested before buying a truck.
- Every operating role is a commodity labour-market job: helper, crew lead, dispatcher, field supervisor [C, DF-S020, DF-S033, E-WS18003-S011].

**Why it is hard:**
- It is a commodity. The three national brands and dozens of micro-haulers all advertise same-day service and eco-friendly disposal.
- Margins are thin once you pay for leads, Chicago disposal ($100/ton at SWANCC Glenview [B, DF-S016]) and insurance (about 7% of sales at College Hunks stores of every tenure [B, E-WS18003-S002]).
- **Franchised stores earn 6–12% EBITDA before truck payments and owner pay** (College Hunks medians: $44,031 at 13–36 months, $80,289 at 37–60, $137,005 at 60+) [B, E-WS18003-S002].

**The whole strategy in one sentence.** Win repeat B2B accounts, whose full-truck jobs contribute about $425 each, so you depend less on paid consumer leads, whose half-truck jobs contribute about $75 and single items lose money (section 3).

- **Property and association managers.** Move-outs, evictions, storage rooms and common areas. Illinois lists 183 active Community Association Management Firm licences, 101 in Cook County [A, DF-S023].

**Zone 2** opens only after month 24 and only once monthly revenue reaches $42k, meaning zone 1 is near plateau. The candidates are the north suburbs or the west suburbs.

## 3. Transaction economics

Four representative jobs follow (model `UnitEconomics` sheet). Crew cost is about $24/hour loaded per person: Chicago hand-laborer median $40,550 [C, DF-S020] plus taxes and WC (E).

| Line | A. Half-truck basement (consumer, paid lead) | B. Full-truck apartment turnover (PM account) | C. Single sofa (paid lead) | D. Estate cleanout, 2 loads (realtor referral) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $430 [B ticket] | $850 [B band] | $135 [B: sofa avg $125] | $2,400 [E] |
| Crew | −$120 (2 × 2.5 h) | −$200 (2 × 4 h) | −$48 | −$1,150 (3 × 2 × 8 h) |

| Acquisition | −$80 (≈$32 CPL ÷ 40% close [B]) | −$25 (account servicing) | −$80 | −$50 |
| **Contribution** | **$75 (17%)** | **$425 (50%)** | **−$28 (loss)** | **$525 (22%)** |
| Cash timing | Paid at completion; disposal paid at scale same day | Net-30 invoice; ~4 weeks' float on $400 of costs | At completion | 50% deposit advisable; balance at completion |
| Failure risks | Under-quoted volume; weight surprises (plaster, tile) | Slow payment; scope creep ("while you're here") | Loses money unless batched or minimum ≥$150 | Labour overrun; family disputes over items; hazardous finds |
| Repeat potential | Low (one-off household) | High: 1–4 jobs/month per active manager (E) | Low | Medium via the referring agent |

**Correction (Stage D transactions).**
- **Single item.** Stage D showed a +$11 contribution using a $55 lead cost. At College Hunks' documented $32 cost per lead and 40% close rate, the true paid-lead cost is about $80 per job. The single-item job is therefore a **loss** [B, E-WS18003-S001].
- **Labour.** Stage D modelled labour at 24% of revenue, yet its own half-truck example implied 28.6%. The base now uses 25%.

**What drives contribution.** The job mix sets contribution more than price does. A business with ~30% B2B full loads averages roughly $150–190 contribution per job. One living on paid half-loads averages ~$75–100. Disposal at $100/ton is 12–30% of revenue depending on load density. Validation must weigh every load (scale tickets) to see where it lands.

## 4. Validation

**Minimum viable commercial offering:**
- an LLC with GL insurance;
- a rate card and photo-quote workflow;
- one fulfilment route: a subcontracted insured hauler, a commercially rented truck once its terms are confirmed, or the founder plus a helper with a modest owned vehicle for small jobs.

**What validation must establish.** The three assumptions that most move the model are CAC, demand density and gross margin:
- **Staff/fixed-cost multiplier ±25%** moves year-3 operating profit from $76,846 to $21,718.
- **CAC ±25%** moves it from $68,783 to $29,781.
- **Ticket ±25%** moves it from $32,166 to $67,804 (model tornado).

- Ask for documents where appropriate: a vendor-onboarding packet, insurance-certificate requirements (COI limits, additional-insured wording) and a sample PO.

**Conditions to invest more (buy truck #1, ≈$45–55k).** All of the following:
- experiments 1, 3 and 5 pass;
- ≥3 B2B accounts have ordered at least twice;
- the pilot shows ≥$120 average contribution per job;
- the founder can commit ~60 h/week for 12 months unpaid. The draw does not start until month 13 in the model, and year-1 operating profit is −$22,511.

**Conditions to abandon or pivot:**
- **Abandon:** experiment 2 fails (no B2B pull), or insurance plus licensing pushes fixed cost above ~$3,500/month for one truck.
- **Pivot options:**
  - B2B-only cleanout coordination on subcontract, with no trucks;
  - folding the work into WS18-030 estate services as a referral add-on.

## 5. Operations

**Lead-to-cash workflow:**
1. **Lead arrives** by phone, LSA message, web form or B2B email. An AI receptionist answers 24/7 and collects photos and address.
2. **Quote.** AI drafts a volume estimate from the photos; the founder or dispatcher approves it and sends a binding range within 1 hour. On-site quotes are given for B2B or estate work.
3. **Booking.** A 2-hour arrival window goes out with automated reminders. B2B jobs get a PO number and access instructions (keys, elevator reservation, COI on file).
4. **Pre-job checks.** Truck inspection, PPE, tarps and dollies, route sequence.
5. **On site:**
   - before photos;
   - confirm price; any upsell needs written or text consent;
   - load, sweep, after photos;
   - signature.
6. **Sort.** Donation and resale go to partner outlets (receipts to the customer). Scrap metal and appliances go to a recycler with a refrigerant-recovery statement [A, E-WS18003-S010]. E-waste goes to a registered collector [A, E-WS18003-S009]. Hazardous items are refused under a written policy (paint, chemicals, asbestos suspects).
7. **Tip.** Remainder goes to the transfer station; keep scale tickets for job costing.
8. **Payment.** Card or ACH on site for consumers. Net-30 invoice with photos for B2B, plus a monthly statement.
9. **Close-out.** Review request (automated), a B2B follow-up call, and a KPI update.

**Function classification:**

| Function | Classification | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lifting, loading, driving, sorting, tipping | **Not automatable (human)** | ~25% of revenue; the core cost |
| Photo-volume estimate, quote drafting, call/text answering, review responses, route sequencing, invoice drafting, donation receipts | **AI-assisted** | Human approves price and exceptions |
| Reminders, invoicing, payment links, review requests, KPI roll-up | **Automatable** | Inside the FSM software |
| B2B relationship selling, pricing exceptions, damage claims, hiring, safety culture | **Human (founder → ops manager)** | Where the wedge lives |
| Insurance placement, entity/tax filings, licence applications, contract review (COIs, indemnities) | **Professional-dependent** | Broker, CPA, attorney (≈$1.5–3k/yr, E) |
| Hazardous/asbestos material handling | **Excluded** | Refer out; never accept |

**Suppliers and fulfilment.** Fulfilment means a truck plus a 2-person crew per ~55 jobs/month. The first extra truck comes at 60 jobs/month (model capacity trigger).
- **Disposal:** SWANCC Glenview, plus at least one private transfer station with long hours (rates MISSING).
- **Scrap:** a licensed scrap yard.
- **E-waste:** an IEPA-registered collector.
- **Donation:** charity partners (to be confirmed; pickup policies MISSING).

**Support and QA:**
- Photo documentation on every job.
- A damage-claim SOP: photograph pre-existing damage, set a $500 claims threshold before involving the insurer, resolve within 7 days.
- A weekly ride-along audit by the founder or ops manager.
- A 5-star review target ≥90%.

**Exceptions:** weight overruns (re-quote on site), refused hazardous items, no-access situations (trip fee), eviction disputes (crews never enter without manager present), and winter slowdowns (pull B2B backlog forward).

**Capacity limits:**
- **Truck:** about 2.5 jobs/day (55/month).
- **Transfer stations:** SWANCC hours are short [B, DF-S016].
- **Winter:** demand dips (E).
- **The real limit is management.** In base economics, a field ops manager is unaffordable on one zone. Engine variant: a single zone plus an ops manager earns year-5 operating profit of only $22,937. The manager becomes affordable only once zone 2 is producing.

**Can multi-truck, multi-territory growth raise the ceiling?** Yes for revenue; only partly for profit.
- **Revenue evidence:**
  - Mature 1-800-GOT-JUNK franchisees (>72 months) had a median of **$2,764,205** in CY2023, at a median 17 subterritories and about $148k per subterritory [B, E-WS18003-S003]. CY2024's median was $2,032,678 after affiliates bought out the largest units [D, E-WS18003-S005].
  - College Hunks stores grow from $691k (1 territory) to $869k (2) and $1.56M (3); 8-territory corporate stores reach $5.04M [B, E-WS18003-S002].
- **Margin evidence.** Margin does not expand with scale. College Hunks 60+ month stores earn a median 8.8% EBITDA after ~12% fees. The GM-run corporate stores earn about 4%, with insurance (8.5%) and office labour (8.4%) scaling linearly [B, E-WS18003-S002].
- **Our engine.** Each variant was re-run with the same cost structure (scratch runs via `/tmp/claude-0/atlas_scratch/E_WS18-003/scripts/variants.py`, not in `_results.json`):

The conclusion is that the ceiling depends on **unit quality, not unit count.** At base margins (contribution ~42% after crew, disposal, fuel and leads), a $1.85M three-zone business earns about $172k before owner pay. With a salaried GM it would earn roughly $60–80k. Only optimistic-grade units reach $280–390k:
- gross margin 68%;
- CAC $55;
- 170 jobs/month in zone 1 by month 20.

That is what multi-zone growth needs before it is worth funding.

**Adjacent services:**
- **Estate/senior-move cleanouts** (WS18-030 referrals: realtors, senior-move managers, estate sale firms). High ticket, labour-heavy.
- **Dumpster rental.** Junk King units offering dumpsters averaged $71,950 of dumpster revenue [B, WS18-S001]. In Chicago it requires the private-scavenger licence and container permits [A, E-WS18003-S017].
- **Hoarding cleanouts and storage-auction cleanouts.**
- **Light demolition** (shed, deck, interior tear-out). Triggers Home Repair Act duties [A, WS18-S010].
- **Appliance and e-waste recycling pickups** for property managers.

**Vertical integration.** An owned sorting/transfer yard would cut disposal (11% of revenue). But it needs IEPA siting and permits and more than $500k of capital (E). Incremental return: on $1M of revenue, 11% disposal is about $110k; halving it saves about $55k/yr against more than $500k invested, under 11% pre-tax. **Not worth it below about $3M of revenue.** A small covered sorting bay inside the yard (~$300–500/month extra) to raise donation and scrap diversion pays for itself if it saves 0.5 t/day.

**Management delegation:**
- **Months 1–12:** the founder drives truck #1.
- **Months 13–24:** the founder sells and dispatches while crew leads drive.
- **About month 26:** the dispatcher takes the phones.
- **About month 39:** the ops manager takes the field.
- The founder still owns accounts, pricing and finance. A GM can run day-to-day only once revenue is near $2M at optimistic margins; at base margins the GM's salary consumes the profit.

**Reinvestment slots:**
- trucks (~$42k each, supporting ~$250k revenue);
- zone launches (~$40–70k including losses);
- a dumpster fleet (MISSING costs).

The model's long-run incremental ROIC is 60% in base, 30% conservative and 90% optimistic (E).

## 9. Strategic wealth creation

**How it becomes a durable cash asset.** It becomes durable only through B2B account density. Managers who reorder monthly, storage districts with flat sheets, and restoration firms that call first turn a commodity search business into a route-like business:
- lower CAC per job;
- denser days (more jobs per truck-day, so lower labour %);
- higher full-load share.

That is the difference between base (about 9% operating margin) and optimistic (about 20%).

**How much capital it could generate:**
- **Base:** roughly $80–100k/yr operating profit before owner pay by years 4–10. The 10-year deployable surplus after the founder's draw is only $30,895, and year-10 EV at 2× is $192,778. That is a good job, not a capital engine.
- **Optimistic:** about $374–516k/yr operating profit in years 4–10 and a 10-year deployable surplus of $1,313,527. That would fund a holding company's second venture by about year 4–5.

**When management could take over:**
- An ops manager can run the field at about $600k+/yr revenue (base month 39).
- A GM can run the company at about $2M revenue, and only at optimistic-grade margins. Engine variant: a salaried GM in base turns year-5 op into −$31,513.

**Exit value is low.** A Naperville cleanout firm asks 1.3× its $220k seller-claimed cash flow [D, DF-S018]. Broader waste/recycling sold comps run 2.14–3.88× owner earnings, a category flattered by route-based haulers [D, E-WS18003-S014].

**Can it fund other ventures?** Only on the optimistic path, and probably not before year 4.

**As the first company for a diversified portfolio:**
- **Strengths:**
  - cash is collected at completion;
  - validation costs hundreds of dollars;
  - downside is capped by truck resale;
  - roles are standard;
  - AI dependence is trivial after December 2026.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - no moat (entry 3);
  - thin, cost-driven margins;
  - physical injury and vehicle risk;
  - the founder's tech/research edge buys little;
  - year-3 base return on peak funding is only 0.35×.

**Overall.** It is a strong fallback or "floor" company. It is a weak first holding-company asset unless the B2B test shows the optimistic path.
