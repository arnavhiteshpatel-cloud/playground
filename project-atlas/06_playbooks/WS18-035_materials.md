## 2. Customer discovery interview questionnaires
*DRAFT — not sent; requires founder review.*

Non-leading and focused on past behaviour. Never pitch during the interview. Take notes; record only with consent.

### 2a. Homeowner who had a garage door repair or replacement in the last 24 months (12 interviews)
1. Tell me about the last time something went wrong with your garage door. What happened?
2. What did you do first? Who did you call, and how did you find them (search, map listing, ad, neighbour, app, past company)?
3. How many companies did you contact? Why did you choose the one you used?
4. How long did you wait for the visit?
5. What was quoted at first, and what did you finally pay? What was included?
6. Did anything surprise you during the visit or on the bill? *(Do not suggest "upsell".)*
7. Did the technician recommend anything beyond the original problem? What did you decide, and why?
8. Was there a written estimate before work began?
9. Would you call the same company again? What would change your mind?
10. Have you ever had a company not show up, or a price change on site? What happened?
11. Who else in your household or street has had door work done recently?
12. *(Close)* Is there anything I should have asked?

### 2b. Community-association (CAM) portfolio manager (10 interviews)
1. Which of your communities have overhead or garage doors the association is responsible for: parking-garage doors, common garages, unit doors?
2. In the last 12 months, what door problems came up, and who handled them?
3. How did you choose that vendor? How long have you used them?
4. Roughly what did your communities spend on door repairs and replacements last year? *(Ranges are fine.)*
5. What happens when a parking-garage door fails after hours?
6. Do any of your associations have a scheduled maintenance agreement for doors? What does it cover, and what does it cost per door or per visit?
7. What does a vendor need to be added to your approved list (COI limits, W-9, background checks, references, licences)?
8. When did you last add or drop a door vendor, and why?
9. Who decides: you, the board, or a maintenance supervisor?
10. For unit garage doors, does the association specify style or brand? Who pays: owner or association?
11. *(Close)* Who else at your firm should I talk to?

### 2c. Light-commercial facility or property manager (flex, industrial, storage, auto) (2–5 interviews)
1. How many overhead doors do you manage (sectional, rolling, fire doors)?
2. What failed in the last 12 months? What did it cost, including downtime?
3. Who services them now? Under contract or on call?
4. Are any rolling fire doors drop-tested each year? By whom? When was the last test?
5. How do you buy service: PO, master service agreement, card?
6. What would make you try a second vendor?

### 2d. Home inspector (3 interviews)
1. How often do your reports flag garage-door items (auto-reverse failure, worn springs/cables, sensor misalignment)?
2. What do buyers or sellers usually ask you next?
3. Do you keep a "who to call" list? How does a company get on it?
4. What do you need to see from a repair company (insurance, written menu, response time)? *(Do not offer or discuss referral fees.)*

## 4. Sales call scripts with qualification
*DRAFT — not sent; requires founder review*

### 4a. Residential inbound call (founder, CSR or AI receptionist script; a human confirms every booking)
1. "Thanks for calling [Company], this is [name]. Is anyone hurt, or is a car trapped?" *(Emergency triage.)*
2. **"What is the door doing?"** Won't open / loud bang / cable hanging / off track / opener runs but door doesn't move / sensor light blinking.
   - **Safety line:** "Please don't try to open it by hand or pull the red cord if a spring is broken; the door can be very heavy."
3. **Qualify:**
   - Address and ZIP (inside the service area?).
   - Door size (single/double).
   - Number of springs visible above the door.
   - Opener brand and model if visible.
   - Owner or tenant? *(A tenant needs landlord approval.)*
   - Best phone number.
4. **Photos:** "Could you text a photo of the springs above the door and the opener label? It lets the technician bring the right parts."
5. **Price transparency:** "Our posted price for a standard two-spring replacement is [$375]. There's a [$79] diagnostic fee that's credited if you go ahead. The technician will give you a written estimate before doing any work, and nothing is added without your approval."
6. **Book** an arrival window; send a text confirmation with technician name, photo and vehicle description.
7. **Disqualify politely:**
   - Outside the area.
   - Commercial rolling or fire doors (until certified): "We're not the right company for that yet."
   - Requests for wiring work in Chicago (refer to a licensed electrician).

### 4b. On-site technician estimate script
- Show what's broken (photos).
- Present *repair-only* first; then optional items with plain reasons and prices.
- Get a signature on the written estimate before starting.
- Above $1,000: the written contract plus the consumer-rights brochure, and the cancellation-right notice if the sale is made at the home.

### 4c. B2B qualification checklist (after discovery)
- Doors under management: count and type.
- Last-12-month spend (range).
- Current vendor satisfaction (1–5) and the reason.
- Decision-maker and approval steps.
- Vendor requirements (COI limits, W-9, background checks).
- Pilot possible? First job type and timing.
- **Score** (each Yes/No): ≥ 10 doors · spend ≥ $2k/yr · dissatisfaction ≥ 1 reason · vendor-list path known. **Pursue if ≥ 3 Yes.**

## 5. Quote / estimate templates
*DRAFT — not sent; requires founder review*

### 5a. Residential written estimate (fields)
- Company legal name, address, phone; licence or registration number where required (Chicago: on estimates, ads and vehicles).
- Customer name, service address, date, estimate number.
- Description of the problem found (with photo references).
- Line items: each part (brand, size and specification, e.g., spring wire/ID/length, cycle rating), quantity, unit price; labour; trip/diagnostic fee and whether it is credited; any estimate-preparation charge (must be disclosed).
- Total; taxes as applicable (confirm Illinois treatment with an accountant).
- Warranty terms: labour [12 months]; parts per manufacturer.
- Statement: "No additional work or charges will be performed without your prior written approval."
- Contracts over $1,000: signature lines; consumer-rights brochure acknowledgment; cancellation-rights notice (3 business days if signed at the home; 15 business days if the buyer is over 65).
- Customer and technician signatures; date and time.
- Completion invoice: each part and service with its exact charge; payment method.

### 5b. Light-commercial PM quote (RFQ response)
- Site, contact, door schedule: location, type (sectional/rolling/fire), size, manufacturer, operator model, serial if visible.
- Scope per visit: visual inspection; lubrication; hardware tightening; balance/spring check; safety device test; operator force/limit check; written report with photos.
- Fire doors (only once certified): annual drop test per NFPA 80 and the manufacturer's instructions, two drops, reset, documented on a drop-test form; repairs quoted separately.
- Frequency: [semi-annual / quarterly].
- Price: [$ per door per visit] *(hypothesis $90; benchmark first)*; minimum per visit; repair labour rate; parts markup policy; emergency call rate.
- Terms: net-30; certificate of insurance attached; term [12 months], renewable; 30-day termination.

## 7. Pricing calculator spec (spreadsheet; build before 5 Dec)
*DRAFT — requires founder review*

**Inputs per job type:**
- Parts SKU and unit cost (from DDM public price or the SSC account price).
- Quantity.
- Hardware allowance.
- Technician minutes on site plus drive minutes.
- Loaded labour $/hour (default $40).
- Fuel + card % (default 6%).
- Lead cost (by source: LSA $/lead ÷ paid-conversion %; referral $0; account $0).
- Warranty reserve % (default 2%).
- Target contribution % and floor $.

**Outputs:**
- Minimum price at the floor.
- Recommended posted price.
- Contribution $ and % by lead source (paid lead vs referral).
- Break-even lead cost.
- Flag if contribution after a paid lead is < $60 *(e.g., opener at $750 → loss)*.

**Scenario toggles:** public vs dealer parts cost; single vs two-person job; emergency after-hours multiplier.

**Mix view:** weekly job mix → blended ticket and parts %, compared with the model's base ($575 ticket, 40% parts, $105 acquisition cost per job).

## 9. Weekly KPI sheet spec
*DRAFT — requires founder review*

| KPI | Definition | Green | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leads by source | LSA, GBP, search, referral, CAM, inspector, repeat | — | — |
| Valid-lead rate | Valid ÷ all leads (track disputed LSA leads) | ≥ 75% | < 60% |
| Booking rate | Booked visits ÷ valid leads | ≥ 65% | < 50% |
| Close rate | Paid jobs ÷ visits | ≥ 85% | < 75% |
| Paid jobs | Count, by job type | Plan ±10% | < 70% of plan |
| Average ticket | Revenue ÷ paid jobs | ≥ $575 | < $450 |
| Parts % | Parts cost ÷ revenue | ≤ 40% | > 48% |
| Cost per paid job | Paid-media spend ÷ paid jobs from paid sources | ≤ $90 | > $150 |
| Free-job share | Jobs with no paid lead ÷ all jobs | ≥ 30% (by month 12) | < 10% |
| Callback rate | Return visits within 30 days ÷ jobs | ≤ 3% | > 5% |
| Reviews | New reviews; average rating | ≥ 3/week; ≥ 4.8 | < 1/week; < 4.5 |
| Technician utilization | Billable jobs ÷ (60/month capacity) | ≥ 80% | < 60% |
| Contribution per technician-job | (Revenue − parts − variable − lead − tech cost) ÷ technician jobs | ≥ $120 | < $60 |
| Cash | Cash ÷ monthly cash costs | ≥ 2 months | < 1 month |
| B2B pipeline | Accounts in discovery, approved, active; doors under PM | +1 approved/month | 0 for 2 months |
| Safety | Incidents/near-misses | 0 | any |

**Weekly review questions:**
1. Did any channel exceed $150 per paid job?
2. Did any job type lose money?
3. Is the hiring rule met (≥ 60 jobs/month, > 90% booked, ≤ $100 per paid job, ≤ 5% callbacks)?
