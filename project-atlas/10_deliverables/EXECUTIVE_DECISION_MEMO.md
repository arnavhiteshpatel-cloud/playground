# Project Atlas — Executive Decision Memo

**Date:** 28 November 2026 · **For:** the founder · **Status:** recommendation after desk research and independent
adversarial review. Nothing has been tested in the market and no one has been contacted.

---

## 1. The recommendation

**Start an employer-paid contingency recruiting desk that places controls, automation and instrumentation (I&C)
engineers with Illinois control-system integrators, process plants and engineering firms.** Treat it as a 2–3-year
**cash engine**, not as the permanent core of the holding company. Hold a formal decision at month 24: either build it
into a boutique (recruiters, then contract engineering staffing), or harvest its cash into a more durable asset.

**Do not commit capital yet.** Run a **60-day, ≈$600 validation**. The decisive test is whether ≥5 of 30 Illinois
controls hiring managers sign an 18–20% fee agreement with a newcomer who brings a specific, vetted, consenting
engineer.

**Two serious alternatives** (each with a cheap, decisive test you can run in the same weeks):
- **Alternative 1 — the durable-asset choice: construction subcontractor back office** (surety-grade work-in-progress
  (WIP) bookkeeping + Illinois certified payroll for Chicagoland public-works subcontractors). Recurring, sticky,
  delegable, and sellable — but lower near-term cash and a real skill gap.
- **Alternative 2 — the high-upside choice: energy cost management for large Illinois commercial/industrial users**
  (>1,500 kW combined demand, which the statute exempts from broker licensing). The biggest structural tailwind found,
  but fees start only when customers' supply contracts expire (8–12+ months) and supplier onboarding of an unlicensed
  consultant is unproven.

Pick the primary unless its 60-day gate fails, or unless you decide you would rather build an organization than earn
fees personally for the first two years — in which case lead with Alternative 1.

---

## 2. What the research was, in one paragraph

18 independent discovery workstreams generated **630 opportunity records** (1,600+ sources). All 630 were re-scored
**blind** by separate scorers, which showed authors inflating their own ideas by 0.2–0.7 points. 74 went to industry
investigation, 25 to commercial deep dives with named Illinois prospects and suppliers and driver-based financial
models, and 11 to full playbooks. Six independent reviewers who wrote none of the material then attacked the 11
playbooks, opened 100+ citations, recomputed the models and found errors in every one. A final audit re-ran all 11
models under one common set of skeptical assumptions. Every financial model is a live Excel workbook whose formulas
were verified against an independent Python implementation (zero mismatches).

## 3. The honest headline

**No from-scratch option for this founder shows credible evidence of $500k–$1M a year of owner cash within five years.**
After review, the best corrected base cases produce roughly **$35–115k a year of operating profit by years 3–5**, and
most of that is the value of the founder's own labour. The cross-model audit found that once the founder's own delivery
work is charged at a market wage, **no finalist reaches $100k/yr** in harmonized year 5.

That does not mean the project failed. It means the wealth path is a **sequence**, not a single bet:
1. a cheap, fast cash engine that validates in weeks and risks little;
2. accumulated, deployable capital (realistically ~$100–300k over years 2–5 if the engine works);
3. redeployment into durable, delegable cash-flow assets (Alternative 1 with hired staff, or acquired recurring-revenue
   books — acquisitions are allowed later, just not as the first move);
4. only then, a holding company.

The recommendation is built for step 1 while keeping step 3 visible.

## 5. Why the primary wins

| Test | Primary (recruiting desk) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Capital at risk | **≤ $30k** in the post-review base (with a $4k/month owner draw from month 13); ≈$15k with no draw | Model + Stage F review [E on drivers] |
| Cost and speed of the decisive test | **≈$600, 60 days** | Playbook §4 |
| Legal entry | **No licence, bond or state forms** — Illinois' Private Employment Agency Act excludes a business "compensated solely by any employer", provided candidates are never charged for anything (including résumé help) | 225 ILCS 515/1.1 [A, read from a mirror; counsel to confirm] |
| Unit economics | One placement ≈ $21k fee at 20% of a ~$105k salary; incumbent perm gross margin 99.8% | Robert Half disclosures [A]; fee level [D] |
| Customer risk for a newcomer | Low: contingency means the employer pays nothing unless it hires — the easiest "try a newcomer" offer in the study | Mechanism [A]; win rate [E] |
| Robustness | **#1 in every variant** of the cross-model audit, including the original rankings, because its drivers were already benchmark-capped | `07_review/CROSS_MODEL_AUDIT.md` |
| Post-review base economics | Year-3 operating profit ≈ **$81k**, year-5 ≈ **$114k** (≈6–7 placements/yr), conservative year-3 ≈ $41k | `07_review/WS12-014_review.md` |
| Founder fit | Technical background helps a screen of PLC/SCADA/DCS engineers; work is sales-heavy, which the founder accepts | Judgment |

It wins on the founder's two heaviest-weighted criteria — free cash flow (18%) and return on capital (15%) — and on
speed to a first profitable transaction (a first fee is plausible by months 4–10).

**What it does not do:** it is founder-bound (in the corrected base it never hires), the most cyclical line in staffing
(Robert Half permanent placement revenue fell 57% in 2009 and 21.7% in 2023 [A]), and has low transfer value
(~2× operating profit). That is why it is framed as a cash engine with a month-24 decision, not as the holding company.

## 6. Why the alternatives were not selected — and when to switch

**Alternative 1 — Construction subcontractor back office (SYN-006).**
- *For:* lowest capital in the study (peak ≈ $10–12k); mandated recurring demand (Illinois certified payroll is due
  monthly by the 15th on every public-works job [A]); bookkeeping needs no licence (225 ILCS 450/8.05 [A]); public
  award records name every buyer; a surety program that bonds up to $1.5M on business statements plus a WIP [B];
  recurring revenue with standard, hireable roles and a sellable book.
- *Against:* reviewed base year-3 ≈ $50k, year-5 ≈ $83k (the playbook's $95k/$181k stacked four favourable
  assumptions); price evidence is D (~$950/client/month, unverified in Chicago); the founder has no construction-accounting
  track record, and the whole product is trust in accuracy; "no licence for certified payroll" rests on a statute that
  does not mention certified payroll; SB1344 adds penalties that can reach third-party filers.
- *Switch to it if:* the primary's fee-agreement test fails, or you prefer to build a staffed organization from year 1;
  and its own gates pass (≥4 of 8 surety producers name recent WIP failures; ≥3 paid $450 WIP checks in 60 days;
  the paid skill gate — two clean WIPs reviewed by a construction CPA).

**Alternative 2 — Large-customer energy cost management (SYN-004).**
- *For:* the licensing exemption for customers above 1,500 kW is real and statutory (220 ILCS 5/16-115C(b), read
  verbatim [A]); bill audit, capacity-tag work and demand-response referral need no licence for any size; PJM capacity
  prices cleared at the cap in the ComEd zone, making energy a CFO problem; long-term upside and enterprise value are
  the highest of the reviewed options.
- *Against:* reviewed base year-3 ≈ $33k (fees begin at contract expiry, 8–12+ months out); demand-response revenue
  share is likely near zero (providers now offer customers 100% pass-through); a major supplier's broker onboarding asks
  for state licence documents; peak funding with the owner draw ≈ $122k exceeds the backing; one 2023 amendment
  (P.A. 102-958) is unread.
- *Switch to it if:* ≥2 of ENGIE, Constellation and NRG confirm **in writing** that they will pay an unlicensed consultant
  serving only >1,500 kW customers, with fee levels (mils/kWh) and payment timing — a $0, ~3-week test — and counsel
  confirms the exemption.

**Reserve:** dental-practice managed IT + HIPAA (WS04-014) — prepaid recurring, cheapest test (~$150), but a $399/month
Chicagoland competitor and uncovered breach liability cut its reviewed base to ≈ $37k in year 3.
**Validation-only candidates:** commercial pest control (strongest demand data, weak economics after review), trucking
compliance hub (insurer access and a likely illegal inducement), GPR scanning (a one-person job on honest numbers).
**Failed in review:** offshore CPA-firm staffing, branded workwear, garage doors, junk removal as a first company.
Full reasons: `10_deliverables/INDEPENDENT_REVIEW_REPORT.md`.

## 7. The strongest evidence behind the decision

1. Statutory licensing exclusion for employer-paid recruiters (225 ILCS 515/1.1) [A].
2. Incumbent permanent-placement economics and cyclicality from public filings (Robert Half) [A]; PageGroup fee-earner
   productivity as the ceiling benchmark [B].
3. Live Illinois controls/I&C postings with salary bands at named employers [D, but directly observable and refreshable
   weekly with the role-map scripts].
4. The cross-model audit showing the ranking survives every harmonization variant.

## 8. The weakest important assumptions — what could invalidate it

1. **Conversion of a newcomer:** that ~22% of hiring-manager conversations become signed fee agreements and ~15% of
   agreements fill (reviewed base). The playbook's gate (17%) tests exactly this.
2. **Candidate supply:** that a founder with no network can reach movable controls engineers at ≥$100k
   (test: ≥20% reply, ≥5 open to move, ≥2 presentable within 3 weeks).
3. **No severe downturn in years 1–3:** a 2009-type year cuts reviewed year-3 profit to ≈ $19k and pushes peak funding
   toward ≈ $140k.
4. **Founder temperament:** sustained cold outreach and rejection. "Founder dislikes cold calling after 60 days" is a
   listed abandon trigger.
5. **Portals/MSPs:** if most target employers buy only through vendor portals, the year-1 market shrinks sharply.

## 9. Required confirmations (direct contact needed — none performed)

- **Customers:** signed fee agreements (the decisive test); whether integrators accept 20% vs 15–18%; payment terms
  and guarantee expectations.
- **Candidates:** consent to be presented; current compensation and motivation to move.
- **Legal:** an Illinois employment attorney reviews the fee agreement and confirms the 225 ILCS 515 exclusion, the
  Illinois Freedom to Work Act limits on non-competes, and pay-transparency rules (≈$1,500, after the gate).
- **Suppliers/tools:** LinkedIn Recruiter Lite price and terms at checkout (third-party figure $170/month); a recruiting
  CRM trial; E&O/GL quotes; optional split-network (Top Echelon) terms.

## 12. Conditions for further investment

- **Go (spend the ≈$2–5k launch budget):** ≥5 signed fee agreements at ≥18% and ≥2 candidates in active processes.
- **Continue to month 10:** ≥1 final-round interview by month 4; ≥1 accepted offer by month 6.
- **Stop before any hire:** no collected fee ≥ $15k by month 10.
- **Hire recruiter #1:** trailing 6-month revenue ≥ $20k/month with ≥8 open searches and a 3-month reserve.
- **Month 24 decision:** boutique (recruiter #1 producing ≥$60k collected within 9 months, postings stable) — or harvest
  the cash and launch/acquire a durable recurring-revenue business (Alternative 1 with hired bookkeepers, or an acquired
  bookkeeping/payroll practice or insurance book).
- **Abandon at day 60:** <2 signed agreements, or managers buy only through portals/MSPs, or you dislike the work.
