## 1. Executive summary

**Decision.** Start an **employer-paid contingency recruiting desk placing controls, automation and instrumentation
(I&C) engineers with Illinois integrators, process plants and engineering firms (WS12-014)**, as a 2–3-year cash
engine, not as the permanent core of the holding company. Commit no capital until a **60-day, ≈$600 validation**
passes: at least 5 of 30 Illinois controls hiring managers sign an 18–20% fee agreement with a newcomer who presents
a specific, vetted, consenting engineer. Hold a formal boutique-or-harvest decision at month 24.

**Alternatives.** Alternative 1, the durable-asset choice: a **construction subcontractor back office** (surety-grade
WIP bookkeeping plus Illinois certified payroll, SYN-006). Alternative 2, the high-upside choice: **energy cost
management for Illinois commercial and industrial users above 1,500 kW** (licence-exempt by statute, SYN-004).
Reserve: **managed IT plus HIPAA for independent dental practices (WS04-014)**.

**Why the primary.** After independent review it has the best corrected base (Y3 $80,698, Y5 $114,468), the second
smallest capital at risk ($29,277 with draw, $15,180 without), a statutory licence exclusion for employer-paid
recruiters (225 ILCS 515/1.1 [A, E-WS12014-S001]), and it is the only finalist ranked first in every variant of the
cross-model audit (`07_review/CROSS_MODEL_AUDIT.md` §5). It wins the two heaviest criteria, free cash flow (18%) and
return on capital (15%).

**What it does not do.** It is founder-bound (it never hires in the corrected base), the most cyclical line in staffing
(Robert Half permanent placement revenue fell 57% in 2009 [A, E-WS12014-S011] and 21.7% in 2023 [A, E-WS12014-S009]),
and has low transfer value. That is why the plan is a sequence: cash engine, then accumulated capital, then a durable,
delegable asset (most likely acquired in years 4–6), then a holding company.

**The honest headline.** No from-scratch option for this founder shows credible evidence of $500k–$1M a year of owner
cash within five years. Corrected base cases produce roughly $33–115k of operating profit in years 3–5, and once the
founder's own delivery labour is charged at market wage, no finalist reaches $100k in harmonized year 5. The best
are WS12-012 at $87k, WS12-014 at $84k, SYN-004 at $57k and WS04-014 at $55k (audit §5). At least $65k of the
conditional $100k should stay uncommitted (`10_deliverables/CAPITAL_ALLOCATION_REPORT.md` §1).

## 2. Strategic objectives

The mandate (`00_governance/MANDATE_AND_FOUNDER_BRIEF.md`) asks one question: what is the strongest business this
founder can establish **from scratch, today**, that becomes a substantial, profitable, cash-generating operating
company and the first asset of a future diversified holding company.

| Objective | Operational meaning used in Atlas |
|---|---|
| Owner cash, not revenue or valuation | Sustainable free cash flow and distributable cash; "a conventional business producing $500k/yr sustainable FCF beats a speculative startup" |
| From scratch | Acquisition is not an allowed first entry (gate G6); franchises allowed but judged on owner economics after fees |
| Not venture-style | Software earns no bonus; G5 warns where success needs venture funding or technology |
| Not passive at first, not capped later | The founder sells and delivers early; the business must eventually escape the founder's hours (G7 warns) |
| Capital discipline | Up to ~$100k conditional backing, explicitly not a spending target; validate for hundreds, launch for low thousands |
| Founder constraints | Illinois; no professional licences; age and credit unknown; heavy AI access only until 5 December 2026 |
| Milestones assessed, never manufactured | First profitable transaction → $1k → $5k → $10k → $25k/month → $100k → $250k → $500k → $1M+/yr |

Scoring weights (`00_governance/SCORING_FRAMEWORK.md`, v1.0, unchanged): fcf 18%, roic 15%, access 12%, scale 12%,
resilience 10%, entry 10%, delegation 8%, cash 5%, feasibility 5%, ai 5%. Composites are shrunk toward 5 by evidence
level (k = 1.00 A, 0.95 B, 0.85 C, 0.70 D, 0.55 E), so a weakly evidenced 8 cannot outrank a strongly evidenced 7.
Anti-gaming rules: a large market cannot offset inaccessible customers; automation cannot offset weak economics; high
margins cannot offset extreme risk; rankings are inputs to judgment, not the decision.

## 3. Research methodology and funnel

### 3.1 Design

Breadth before depth; founder-specific scoring; owner cash as the objective; shrinkage of weak evidence; independence
(separate workstreams, blind re-scoring, adversarial reviewers who wrote nothing they reviewed); everything persisted
to files (`00_governance/METHODOLOGY.md`). Authorization boundary: research and drafting only.

### 3.3 Calibration result (Stage B)

Authors scored higher than blind reviewers on every criterion. Mean raw composite fell from 4.81 to 4.41 (−0.40);
blind was lower on 554 records (88%). Inflation by workstream ran from +0.17 (WS12) to +0.70 (WS02). The most
over-claimed criteria were ai (4.15 → 3.41) and fcf (3.58 → 2.90); authors gave 148 records fcf ≥5, blind scorers 53
(`03_screening/calibration.md`). Bias was systematic, not erratic: gaps above 2 points occurred on ≤1% of records per
criterion. Limitation: the bundles SYN-001/004/006 were never blind-scored.

**A notable funnel fact.** The eventual primary, WS12-014, was **not** in the Stage B core: it entered Stage C as the
recruiting-family judgment pick at adj 5.23 (rank 10 of the 11 finalists at Stage B). The judgment-pick rule, adopted
because core scores were bunched within sub-0.2-point noise around a ≈5.40 cut (`DECISION_LOG.md`), is the only reason
it was investigated.

## 7. Major findings (cross-cutting)

1. **No idea has an evidenced cold-start path to large owner cash.** Blind scorers gave only 53 of 630 records fcf ≥5
   (≈$100–150k owner FCF in years 3–5) and none fcf 7 (≈$250–400k) (landscape §1.1). Review then lowered all 11
   finalists' year-3 base profit.
2. **Strong evidence mostly kills ideas.** Rules can be verified from a desk; prices cannot. Only 3 of 18 A/B-economics
   records reached Stage C. Economics evidence across Stage A: E 68.3%, D 18.9%, C 10.0%, B 1.6%, A 1.3%.
3. **Permanent state and local compliance cycles last; federal deadline surges do not.** CTA filing (U.S. companies
   exempted [C, WS15-S004]), the FinCEN residential rule (vacated 2026-03-19 [A, WS15-S031]), WOTC (lapsed
   [A, WS12-S025]) and several energy credits ended or moved during 2025–26; OSHA crane inspections, ILR10 stormwater,
   AHERA reinspections, UST walkthroughs and Illinois certified payroll did not.
4. **In Illinois, who may legally do the work decides entry more than demand does** (landscape §2.3). The primary and
   both alternatives each rest on a statutory exclusion, which is why counsel review is in every plan.
5. **Private-equity roll-ups cluster where the attractive ideas are.** APi Group (140 acquisitions since 2005
   [A, WS05-S019]), Rollins, Cintas, Pye-Barker, Bunzl and 695 insurance-agency deals show exit demand, not entrant
   profitability; consolidators are also the price competitors.
6. **Founder unpaid delivery labour was embedded in profit.** In 10 of 11 models the founder performs a priced
   $4.8–6.9k/month delivery role; capacity-triggered hiring made lower demand look more profitable (audit §2, §6).
7. **Optimism stacked, and template defaults stood in for evidence.** Four models used the engine's default 0.40 ×
   0.25 = 10% conversion; founder conversations ranged 9–45/month against a full-time SDR median of 10 meetings held
   [D, F6-S004]. Capping both cut Y5 by 55–77% for five models (audit §6).
8. **Real prices lowered the economics in 10 of 11.** Wishup at $999 [D, C5-S035], Siotek at $399 [B, F4-S003], Raynor
   opener prices, Chicago junk tickets, the 4imprint desk test, the compliant offshore market, GridBeyond's 100%
   pass-through, and the cohort-correct insurance attach. The primary's fee level was verified and kept; it fell on
   its funnel instead (`INDEPENDENT_REVIEW_REPORT.md` §9).
9. **AI commoditizes information and clerical work; it is a durable edge only where something else anchors the
   deliverable** (a licence, a seat-time mandate, a physical service or filing liability) (landscape §2.6; §15 below).
10. **Illinois labour law constrains every people-heavy model.** The amended Day and Temporary Labor Services Act
    (equal pay after 720 hours; equal-benefits clause enforceable [A, WS12-S001]) pushes away from light-industrial
    staffing and toward direct hire and W-2 route models.

### 10.1 WS12-014 — Controls/automation/I&C contingency recruiting desk (PRIMARY)

- **What it is.** An employer-paid contingency desk placing engineers at $90k–$160k base with integrators, mid-size
  process plants and engineering consultancies; Chicago metro first. Fee 20% (floor 18%), 90-day replacement
  guarantee. Offer: a specific vetted candidate with a written technical-screen summary. 27 named prospects, none
  contacted (`09_databases/prospects/WS12-014_prospects.csv`). Pay bands $72k–$157k, engineer midpoints median ≈$107k
  across 11 postings [D, E-WS12014-S025].
- **Post-review economics.** Reviewer base: conversion 30% → 22%, fill 20% → 15%, ramp 12 → 15 months. Y3 **$80,698**,
  Y5 **$114,468**, ≈6–7 placements/yr, peak **$29,277** ($15,180 no draw), never hires. Conservative Y3 $41,471.
  Stage E base ($170,012 / $259,002) becomes the optimistic case. 2009-type year: Y3 $19,110, peak $140,717.
- **Decisive test.** ≈$600, 60 days: ≥5 of 30 hiring-manager conversations sign ≥18% agreements; plus a candidate-supply
  test (≥20% reply, ≥5 movable at ≥$100k). Stop if no collected fee ≥$15k by month 10.
- **Verdict.** Survives with major revisions; recast as a low-capital personal cash engine with a month-24 decision.

## 15. AI leverage

**Calibration lesson.** The ai criterion was the most over-claimed in Stage B (author 4.15 → blind 3.41; lowered on 399
records). The mandate's rule holds: AI cannot rescue weak economics, and heavy access ends 5 December 2026.

**Durable after December 2026 (cheap at ordinary prices, or no AI at all):**
- **Prospecting from public data.** The ATS role-map scripts, permit and award pulls and food-inspection pulls run in
  plain Python with no AI dependency. These are the most valuable research by-products.
- **Drafting and notes.** Outreach drafts, interview notes, redaction, proposals: ~$60/month in the primary model; ~$25
  per seat in SYN-006. AI/software cost ×3 moves the REV primary Y3 from $81k to $69k; SYN-006 from $50k to $40k.
- **Document extraction.** SYN-004's bill extraction is its only recurring model call and has a rule-based parser
  fallback.
- **Templates built now.** The WIP workbook, IDOL certified-payroll mapper, technical-screen scenario bank and SOPs are
  durable assets if built before 5 December.

**Not durable, or not an edge:**
- **AI as the product.** Transcription, answering services, outbound SDR, exam prep, directories and grant writing were
  rejected for substitution (landscape §2.6).
- **AI equally available to clients and incumbents.** LinkedIn Hiring Assistant in in-house recruiting [B]; agentic 1040
  preparation in CCH Axcess and Thomson Reuters [C/B, WS12-012 review]; QuickBooks agents aimed at reconciliation. In
  the back-office and staffing models AI is as much a substitution threat as a cost lever.
- **AI in hiring decisions.** Illinois bars AI with discriminatory effect in recruitment (IHRA amendment, P.A.
  103-0804, effective 2026-01-01 [C, E-WS12014-S007]) and regulates AI-analysed video interviews (AI Video Interview Act
  [A, E-WS12014-S005]). The primary must use AI for drafting only, never for ranking or rejecting candidates.
- **Heavy build-out work.** Anything that needs Claude-scale effort after 5 December must be finished, packaged and
  runnable on a laptop before then.
