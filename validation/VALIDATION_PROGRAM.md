# OsteoQuant Validation Programme — Stages A–F

No stage beyond A has started. Every physical stage needs founder authorisation, facility access, OTM/IP answers and,
for animal work, a partner's IACUC-approved study; OsteoQuant never conducts animal work itself. Acceptance criteria
are pre-registered in `docs/science/PREREGISTRATION.md` and are frozen before each stage's data are seen.

| Stage | Question | Design | Truth source | Exit criteria | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Computational** | Are the algorithms correct, and where can separation work in principle? | Analytic objects, phantoms (generator v2), public real data | exact (synthetic); Scanco IPL maps; manual bone labels | Morphometry exact on analytic objects; in the **pre-registered held-out** test S1H3 (`docs/science/PREREGISTRATION.md` §9), the advisor refuses ≥95% of failures within the pre-registered precision bound (the in-sample runs S1/S1b/S1c are development checks, not exit criteria); ROI propagation (F2), registration QC (L4, L5) and granule tracking (L3) behave | **Run; items open (updated 2026-11-28).** Met so far: morphometry (G), ROI propagation (F2), registration QC detection (L4; fails closed on unverifiable directions after L5). Held-out advisor validation, quoted only in the standard wording: In the pre-registered held-out simulation S1H4 (advisor 1.4.3; 720 synthetic specimens from 36 phantom structures, knowledge-base and Unspecified material paths; a failure is a volume error above 20%): residual graft volume supported in 10% of specimens (HA only), every failing measurement refused and none of the supported ones failed (95% upper bound on the supported-failure rate 0.14 counting phantom structures as the unit; 0.052 if the 69 supported measurements were independent); supported graft volumes: median error 2.2%, maximum 11.4%; total mineral volume supported in 2% of specimens (β-TCP at PSF 40 µm only); supported mineral volumes: maximum error 16.9%; new-bone volume never supported; no volume endpoint supported for 89% of specimens. An earlier held-out test of advisor 1.4.1 (S1H3, 9 phantom structures) is recorded in the experiment registry. This test used the shipped advisor 1.4.3, frozen before the run. A further pre-registered held-out test was limited to sintered HA residual graft on condyle and segmental defects: in the held-out simulation S1H5 (advisor 1.4.3; 192 synthetic specimens from 48 phantom structures; a failure is a volume error above 20%), residual graft volume supported in 70% of specimens and none of the supported ones failed (95% upper bound on the supported-failure rate 0.074 counting phantom structures as the unit); supported graft volumes: median error 2.2%, maximum 12.3%. No residual-graft measurement in that scope failed at all, refused or not, so S1H5 shows precision for this narrow case, not that the advisor refuses failing HA measurements. The simulator and the advisor share attenuation physics, so this is evidence of internal safety, not of accuracy on real scans; no physical validation has been run. **Not met:** the precision bound with phantom structures as the unit (0.34 against ≤0.10); coverage; day-0 propagation under granule motion (L3, SYNTHETIC: naive day-0 footprint graft Dice — no motion: 0.80; 50 µm motion: 0.535; 100 µm motion: 0.35); the first tracking version produced false matches that the advisor accepted, so tracking was made conservative and L3 was re-run (`EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.md`). Still open: S2 parts b and c (running), U1 (queued) |

| **C — Material characterisation** | Can the advisor be driven by measured material intensity instead of assumptions? | Scan each material alone (and in saline/agarose) on the same protocol; measure relative-unit intensity and granule size distributions | Physical sample | Measured profiles replace assumption profiles in `osteoquant/knowledge`; advisor decisions stable across repeated scans | Not started — can run in the same scanner session as B |
| **D — Retrospective partner data** | Does the software agree with expert analysts on real in vivo scans, and how much time does it save? | De-identified scans from a partner study; ≥2 blinded analysts; timed manual vs software-assisted review | Analyst consensus (and histology if available) | ICC(2,1) vs consensus ≥ analyst–analyst ICC; measured hands-on time reduction (no assumed constants) | Needs M0, a data-use agreement, counsel and Gate 1; whether to accept partner data before then is founder decision #18 |
| **E — Prospective pilot with histology** | In a real graft study, are residual-graft and new-bone measurements right? | Partner's planned study adds end-point histology on matched planes; pre-specified analysis (no day-0 scan: the software has no day-0 workflow) | Histomorphometry by partner pathologist (blinded) | Pre-registered agreement criteria met for at least one material class; refusals concordant with histology-defined overlap | Needs partner study (founder) |
| **F — Independent / multi-site** | Does it transfer across scanners and sites, and can another lab reproduce it? | ≥2 sites, ≥2 scanner vendors, same SOP; independent analyst runs the software | as D/E | Between-site bias within pre-specified limits; independent reproduction of key results | Future |

**Gate mapping.** Stage A ⇒ Release Gate 1 eligibility; A + B (+C) ⇒ Gate 2 (pilot-ready research tool) for the
supported materials only; D–F are required before any claim of validation for a defined context of use (Gate 3,
never self-declared).

**What each stage cannot show.** B/C contain no biological new bone (bone chips are surrogates). D lacks ground truth
unless histology exists. E is limited to the materials and species studied. Every report states the stage of evidence
behind each endpoint.
