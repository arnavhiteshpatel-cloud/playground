Services & pricing

Services, and indicative ranges for pilots

OsteoQuant is pre-revenue and has no customers yet. The ranges below are hypotheses we are testing in conversations with prospective users. They are indicative ranges for pilots, not a price list, a quote or an offer. Scope and terms are agreed in writing for each engagement.

Read the ranges as hypotheses

Every figure on this page is an untested pricing hypothesis. They will change as we learn what the work actually costs and what it is worth to you. Nothing on this page is a GLP service or a validated measurement.

Pilot

Retrospective pilot

$2,500–5,000
Indicative range · hypothesis under test

10–20 retrospective scans from a completed study, analysed blinded and compared with your own analysis under acceptance criteria written down before the data are seen. Deliverables: agreement statistics (ICC, Bland–Altman), measured time per scan and a separability assessment for your material.

Not a validation of your material.

Study analysis

Non-GLP study analysis

$8,000–15,000
Indicative range per study · hypothesis

Up to 40 scans, up to two timepoints, one defect model: reviewed three-class segmentation where supported, morphometry and a DefectFill report that lists every endpoint not measured and why.

Expect refused endpoints. In our pre-registered held-out simulation (S1H3, synthetic), new-bone volume was never supported, and no volume endpoint supported for 58% of specimens. A feasibility check before any contract would say which endpoints your design can support.

Not GLP, and not for a regulatory submission without your own QA review.

Before a study

Material characterisation

$5,000–15,000
Indicative range · hypothesis · lower end under review (it does not cover the work)

Can new bone and your graft be told apart at your scanner settings? Today: a simulation-based separability assessment, using scans you supply (for example a scan of the material alone), and a scan-protocol recommendation. Physical phantom characterisation only after validation stage B, which has not started.

On the evidence so far the answer for new bone is usually no: in our held-out simulation, new-bone volume was never supported for any material.

Not a promise that separation will work. The answer may be “not separable by micro-CT alone”.

How a pilot works

Agree the yardstick first

A pilot is designed to tell both of us, quickly and honestly, whether OsteoQuant is useful on your data. If it is not, you will know which endpoints failed and why. This is how a pilot is planned to work; none is offered yet (see below).

  1. Conversation. Your defect model, material, scanner, timepoints and current analysis.
  2. Written protocol. Scans, endpoints, comparison method and acceptance criteria, fixed before analysis.
  3. Data agreement. A data-processing agreement before any scan changes hands. Scans stay on the analysis computer; your data are not used to train models unless you opt in in writing.
  4. Analysis. Blinded to your results; every correction and decision recorded in the audit trail; analyst time measured, not estimated.
  5. Read-out. Agreement statistics, endpoints the advisor refused and why, and a separability assessment for your material.

Not offered

What we do not sell

  • GLP study packages or GLP-validated analyses.
  • A 21 CFR Part 11 validated system.
  • Regulatory submission support or claims of regulatory acceptance.
  • Animal studies. OsteoQuant does not conduct animal work.
  • Clinical or diagnostic use of any kind.
  • Studies with metal hardware in the field of view (plated or nailed segmental defects, osseointegration / bone–implant contact around metal implants): metal artefacts are not modelled.
  • Day-0 or longitudinal analysis, including large-animal day-0 designs: there is no desktop day-0 workflow, and the method is fragile under granule motion in simulation.
  • Physical-phantom characterisation of a material: no physical phantom work has started.
