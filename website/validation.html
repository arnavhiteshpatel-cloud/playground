Validation status

Only the computational stage has run

Validation is planned in six stages, from simulation to independent multi-site studies. Stage A (computational) has run on synthetic phantoms and public real data, with open items. No physical, animal or partner stage has started. Nothing on this site should be read as validation on real graft materials.

Research product — not validated on real graft materials; not a medical device; no GLP/Part 11 compliance claimed.

OsteoQuant never conducts animal work itself. Every physical stage needs facility access, ownership clearances and, for animal data, a partner’s approved study. Acceptance criteria are pre-registered and frozen before each stage’s data are seen.

Programme

Stages A to F

Source: validation/VALIDATION_PROGRAM.md; acceptance criteria in docs/science/PREREGISTRATION.md.

  1. A

    Computational

    Question. Are the algorithms correct, and where can separation work in principle?

    Truth. Exact synthetic labels; Scanco IPL reference maps; manual bone labels on public scans.

    Exit criteria. Morphometry exact on analytic objects; advisor refuses ≥95% of failing measurements; ROI propagation, tracking and registration QC behave.

    Open items. In the pre-registered held-out simulation S1H3 (advisor 1.4.1; 216 synthetic specimens; a failure is a volume error above 20%): residual graft volume supported in 37% of specimens, every failing measurement refused and none of the supported ones failed (95% upper bound on the supported-failure rate 0.045); supported graft volumes: median error 4.2%, maximum 13.0%; total mineral volume supported in 10% of specimens (sharp imaging only); supported mineral volumes: maximum error 3.2%; new-bone volume never supported. Coverage is the open problem; earlier versions supported some measurements that failed, which led to the 1.4.x rules (EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.md). The day-0 method is fragile under granule motion (L3). Ensemble/conformal (U1) and synthetic-to-real (S2) experiments have not completed.

    Run, items open
  2. B

    Ex vivo physical phantom

    Question. Can real commercial grafts be separated from bone tissue on a real scanner, and are volumes accurate?

    Design. Bovine bone cores with weighed graft fills, 10/20/40 µm voxels, scan–rescan, HA calibration. Truth: gravimetric mass and material density.

    Exit criteria. Where the advisor says supported: graft-volume bias ≤5%, 95% limits of agreement within ±15%; advisor decisions match observed separability.

    Protocol drafted

    Not approved; not started

  3. C

    Material characterisation

    Question. Can the advisor be driven by measured material intensity instead of assumptions?

    Design. Scan each material alone on the same protocol; measure intensity and granule-size distributions.

    Exit criteria. Measured profiles replace assumed ones; decisions stable across repeat scans.

    Not started

    Can run in the same scanner session as B

  4. D

    Retrospective partner data

    Question. Does the software agree with expert analysts on real in vivo scans, and how much time does it save?

    Design. De-identified scans from a partner study; at least two blinded analysts; timed manual versus software-assisted review.

    Exit criteria. ICC(2,1) against consensus at least the analyst–analyst ICC; measured hands-on time reduction, with no assumed constants.

    Needs a partner

    Needs a data-use agreement

  5. E

    Prospective pilot with histology

    Question. In a real graft study, are residual-graft and new-bone measurements right?

    Design. A partner’s planned study adds a day-0 scan and end-point histology on matched planes, read blinded by the partner’s pathologist.

    Exit criteria. Pre-registered agreement criteria met for at least one material class; refusals concordant with histology-defined overlap.

    Needs a partner study
  6. F

    Independent, multi-site

    Question. Does it transfer across scanners and sites, and can another laboratory reproduce it?

    Design. At least two sites and two scanner vendors under one SOP; an independent analyst runs the software.

    Future

What each stage cannot show: B and C contain no biological new bone (bone chips are surrogates); D lacks ground truth unless histology exists; E is limited to the materials and species studied.

Shown so far

What the evidence supports

  • Morphometry is exact on analytic objects. Analytic
  • Trabecular thickness agrees with Scanco IPL on 8 public cubes, with a convention chosen post hoc. Real public
  • Real vendor files (2.1 GB Zeiss TXM, 829-slice DICOM) are read within bounded memory. Real public
  • Intensity-only separation fails once graft density overlaps new bone. Synthetic
  • The day-0 footprint works in simulation only without granule motion. Synthetic
  • Results are bit-identical across runs. Synthetic

Not yet shown

What has not been demonstrated

  • Accuracy of residual-graft or new-bone measurements on any real graft material.
  • That the advisor refuses the measurements that fail on real scans: it has been tested only in simulation, where the simulator and the advisor share attenuation physics.
  • That the advisor’s decisions match real separability (its material profiles are assumptions).
  • Volume bias and limits of agreement against physical truth.
  • Agreement with expert analysts on in vivo scans, and any analyst time saving.
  • Micro-CT versus histology agreement.
  • The day-0 method under real granule motion, fragmentation and remodelling.
  • Transfer across scanners and sites on real data.
  • Windows and macOS builds working on real machines.
  • Compliance with GLP or 21 CFR Part 11 (not claimed; a gap analysis exists as a draft).
